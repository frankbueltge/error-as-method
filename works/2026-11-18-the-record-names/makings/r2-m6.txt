Bottom of a vessel with foot. Egypt, Kharga Oasis, 4th–early 5th century. Glass
This fragment is only 2.3 centimetres across. It is the bottom of a small glass vessel, seen from below. The white ring is the vessel's foot, a thread of opaque white glass applied to the very dark, almost black body while both were hot. The hollow inside the ring is the underside of the vessel itself. Everything above it has broken away. The vessel was made in the Kharga Oasis, in Egypt's Western Desert, during the 4th or early 5th century, when Egypt was under Roman rule. A pale, gritty deposit, probably soil, still clings inside the foot. The fragment entered the museum in 1908.

=== object-D ===
Pin with animal. Moche, Peru, 2nd–7th century. Cast copper
This pin is 18.2 centimetres long. A Moche metalworker cast it in copper on the north coast of Peru. Its openwork head shows an animal, perhaps more than one, but the details are worn and hard to read. A small hole pierces the shaft just below the head, and a cord may once have passed through it. The museum's record gives the title "Pin with Animal" but lists the object type as "knife." The round, pointed shaft shows no cutting edge, so we describe it here as a pin. A touch of red remains on the lower shaft.

=== object-H ===
Edward I, from the English Monarchs series. Jean Dassier (Geneva 1676–1763 Geneva), 1731–32. Bronze
Edward I ruled England from 1272 to 1307. He is shown here as a crowned, draped bust, with wavy hair falling to his shoulders and a beaded chain clasping his cloak across his chest. The legend reads EDOUARD·I·D·G·ANG·ET·HIB·REX, "Edward I, by the grace of God, King of England and Ireland." The French spelling of his name reflects the Genevan engraver Jean Dassier. In 1731–32 Dassier issued a series of medals of English monarchs for collectors. The portrait is imagined, not taken from life. The medal is 41 millimetres across. On medals in this series the reverse usually carries a Latin summary of the reign, but it is not shown here.

=== object-M ===
Rattle. Probably Bribri, Talamanca, Costa Rica, about 1873. Gourd, bone, cord
When this rattle was shaken, whatever lies inside its hollow, egg-shaped gourd made the sound. The photograph does not show what is inside. The handle is a bone, apparently an animal long bone, with its knobbed joint end left at the base. It fits into the gourd's narrow end and is lashed in place with cord threaded through a ring of small holes pierced around the opening. The rattle is 18.5 centimetres long and weighs only 41 grams. It was made about 1873, probably by Bribri people of the Talamanca region of Costa Rica. It entered the museum in 1889 as part of the Crosby Brown Collection of Musical Instruments.
