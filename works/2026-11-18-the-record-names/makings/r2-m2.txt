A Distant View of Yokohama Honmura, the Licensed District, Noge Bridge, and Yoshida Bridge, from the Entrance to Yokohama near the Mountain Pass at Noge Village. Japan, Edo period, 3rd month, 1860. Utagawa (Gountei) Sadahide (Japanese, 1807–1873). Woodblock print; ink and color on paper.

Framed by the steep walls of the cutting at Noge and by leaning pines, a small party of travelers pauses above the new port of Yokohama. Two men walk ahead. One wears a green jacket with a white crest and the other carries a bundle on his back. Two women follow below, and a fan lies at the lower left. Beyond the bridges and the licensed quarter, square-rigged foreign ships crowd the bay. Yokohama had opened to foreign trade only the year before, in 1859, and Sadahide pictured it many times for buyers eager to see it. The signature at lower right reads Gountei Sadahide.

Cuirassier. France, 1872. Edouard Detaille (French, Paris 1848–1912 Paris). Watercolor on paper.

A cuirassier, one of the heavy cavalrymen of the French army, stands at ease in full dress. His gloved hand rests on his sword hilt and his black horse waits behind him. Detaille painted the helmet with its red plume and horsehair mane, the gold aiguillettes, the red sash and the tall boots with the near-documentary care that made his name as a painter of soldiers. He signed and dated the sheet at lower left. It is small, under 26 centimeters high, yet the figure has a portrait's presence. Only a cast shadow interrupts the bare brown ground, which keeps attention on the uniform.

=== object-G ===
Worker Shabti of Henettawy (C), Daughter of Isetemkheb. Egypt, Thebes, Deir el-Bahri, Tomb MMA 60. Third Intermediate Period, Dynasty 21, about 990–970 B.C. Faience.

Shabtis were placed in Egyptian tombs to do, in the deceased's place, any labor demanded of them in the afterlife. This "worker" figure, under 12 centimeters tall, was made for a woman named Henettawy, daughter of Isetemkheb. It comes from a family tomb at Deir el-Bahri in western Thebes. It has the mummiform shape: a wrapped body with feet on a low base, arms crossed on the chest, and a long wig falling over the shoulders. The bright turquoise glaze has worn through in pale patches, and dark traces near the hands may be remains of painted hoes. Burials of this period often held hundreds of such figures.

=== object-H ===
Edward I, from the English Monarchs series. Geneva, 1731–32. Jean Dassier (Geneva 1676–1763 Geneva). Bronze.

The legend around this crowned bust reads, in part, EDOUARD I D G ANG ET HIB REX, which means "Edward I, by the grace of God, King of England and Ireland." The portrait is not a true likeness. Edward died in 1307, and this idealized face, with long curled hair, a jeweled crown and a mantle fastened with a jeweled clasp, is an eighteenth-century invention. The medal belongs to the Geneva medalist Jean Dassier's series of English monarchs, which was sold as sets to collectors. It is only 41 millimeters across. The photograph shows the obverse only. In this series the reverse carries a historical inscription.
