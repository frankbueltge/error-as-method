Cuirassier. France, 1872. Edouard Detaille (French, Paris 1848–1912 Paris). Watercolor on paper
Detaille was twenty-four when he painted this cavalryman, and he would go on to become France's leading painter of military life. The trooper has dismounted and stands at ease beside a dark horse, one gloved hand resting on his sword. He wears a brass helmet with a red plume and a long horsehair mane, a blue coat with red facings, a red sash, white breeches and tall jackboots. There is no setting, only a plain brown ground, so every piece of equipment reads clearly. The sheet is signed lower left and dated 1872, the year after the Franco-Prussian War, in which Detaille served.

Twelve-Pointed Star-Shaped Tile. Iran, attributed to Khargird, dated 846 AH/1442–43 CE. Stonepaste; polychrome glaze within black wax resist outlines (cuerda seca technique)
This star tile is dated 846 AH (1442–43 CE). It is attributed to Khargird in northeastern Iran, where the Ghiyathiyya madrasa was being completed in those years for a vizier of the Timurid ruler Shah Rukh. A white strapwork rosette lies over a cobalt ground framed in turquoise, with red-brown and yellow blossoms and white buds in its twelve points. Each colour was fired in its own field, kept apart by lines of wax resist. This is the cuerda seca ("dry cord") technique. The tile, about 40 cm across, would have been set into a wall among other tiles. The glaze is crazed and the edges are chipped.

Couteau de Brèche (staff weapon). France, ca. 1500. Steel, wood (beech)
A couteau de brèche is a staff weapon with a long, knife-like blade mounted on a pole. Infantry carried it in France around 1500, and guards carried it later. This example has a slender steel head 61 cm long. One edge of the blade steps in sharply about a third of the way up, and from there the head narrows to a long, sharp point for thrusting. A faceted socket fixes the head to a beech shaft, of which only the top shows here. The complete weapon is nearly two metres long, so it could cut and thrust from well beyond the reach of a sword.

=== object-G ===
Worker Shabti of Henettawy (C), Daughter of Isetemkheb. Egypt, Thebes, Deir el-Bahri, Tomb MMA 60; Third Intermediate Period, Dynasty 21, ca. 990–970 BCE. Faience
This worker shabti, 11.9 cm high, was made for Henettawy (C), daughter of Isetemkheb. It dates to Dynasty 21, about 990–970 BCE, and comes from Tomb MMA 60 at Deir el-Bahri in Thebes, where she was buried. The figure is mummiform, with arms crossed over the chest and a long tripartite wig. The bright turquoise-blue faience is typical of the period and is now spotted with dark patches. At this time a single burial could hold hundreds of such figures, ready to stand in for the dead when work was demanded in the fields of the afterlife. No tools or inscription can be seen from this side.
