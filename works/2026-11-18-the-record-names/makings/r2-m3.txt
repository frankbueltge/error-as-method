=== object-J ===
Fragment of a kylix (drinking cup), Greek, Attic, Late Archaic, 490–480 BCE; terracotta

This sherd, about 2 cm long, was broken from an Athenian kylix, a shallow two-handled cup used for wine. The orange areas are bare clay. The dark areas were painted in a glossy black slip that turned black in firing. The tongue-shaped reserved areas set against the black suggest the red-figure technique. Red-figure was then the leading style in Athenian workshops, during the generation of the great cup painters. The fragment is too small to show what the scene was or who painted it. It was given to the Museum in 2011 by Dietrich von Bothmer, a curator who built a large study collection of such fragments.

=== object-K ===
Twelve-pointed star tile, attributed to Khargird, Iran, dated 846 AH/1442–43 CE; stonepaste, polychrome glaze within black wax-resist outlines (cuerda seca)

This tile, about 40 cm across, is dated to the reign of the Timurid ruler Shah Rukh. It is attributed to Khargird in eastern Iran, where a madrasa was completed for him in these years. Tiles like it were set into walls together with complementary shapes. Its design has six-fold symmetry. White strapwork forms pointed lobed medallions on a cobalt-blue ground, and in each one sits a red-and-yellow blossom around a central rosette. A turquoise band runs along the edge. The black wax-resist lines kept the coloured glazes apart during firing, the cuerda seca technique. The glaze is crazed, and the body shows at the chipped points.

=== object-L ===
Bottom of a vessel with foot, Coptic, made in Kharga Oasis, Byzantine Egypt, 4th–early 5th century; glass

This tiny fragment, only 2.3 cm across, is the base of a small glass vessel. The dark glass rises into a pushed-in dome at the centre. Around it, an opaque white trail was laid on while the glass was hot, forming a ring foot for the vessel to stand on. Soil is still caught in its hollows. The piece was made in the Kharga Oasis in Egypt's Western Desert. It was acquired in 1908, the year the Museum began excavating there. Small, everyday glass vessels with contrasting applied trails were common in late Roman and early Byzantine Egypt.

=== object-M ===
Rattle, probably Bribri, Talamanca, Costa Rica, ca. 1873; gourd, bone, cord

This shaken rattle is 18.5 cm long and weighs only 41 grams. Its head is a dried gourd, and the seeds or pebbles inside sound when it is moved. Cord threaded through a row of holes pierced around the gourd's narrow end binds the head tightly to a bone handle, which still has its joint at the end. The rattle was probably made by a Bribri maker in the Talamanca region of southern Costa Rica. Among the Bribri, rattles are associated with the chants of ritual healers. It came to the Museum in 1889 with the Crosby Brown Collection of Musical Instruments.
