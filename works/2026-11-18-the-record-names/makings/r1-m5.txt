Cavalryman in Full Dress Standing beside His Horse. France, late 19th century. Édouard Detaille (French, 1848–1912). Watercolor and gouache on toned paper
A mounted soldier has dismounted and stands at ease beside a dark horse, one gloved hand resting on his sword. He wears a crested brass helmet with a red plume, a blue coat with red facings and a red sash, white breeches and tall jackboots. Detaille was France's leading painter of military life and uniform, and he made many careful studies like this one. The plain brown ground has no setting, so the eye goes straight to the accoutrements, which are painted in exact detail. The sheet is signed lower left. The regiment and the period of the uniform are not identified here.

=== object-K ===
Star-Shaped Tile. Central Asia or Iran, probably 15th century (Timurid period). Stonepaste, polychrome glazes in cuerda seca technique
This ten-pointed star is edged in turquoise and filled with cobalt blue. A white strapwork rosette is laid over the blue, with turquoise stems, red-brown and yellow blossoms, and small white buds in the points. Each colour sits in its own field, kept apart by thin, slightly recessed lines. This is the cuerda seca ("dry cord") technique, which let potters fire several glaze colours on one tile without them running together. A tile like this would have been set with others in a wall revetment of a Timurid mosque, shrine or palace. The glaze is finely crazed and the edges are chipped.

Spearhead. Probably Europe, 16th–17th century. Steel, wood
This long, slender steel head narrows to a needle point. About a third of the way up from the base the blade steps in sharply, and above that point it continues as a narrower, stiffer section built to thrust and to pierce. Below the blade a faceted socket fits over a wooden shaft, part of which survives. Heads of this form were mounted on infantry spears and pikes, which could reach well beyond a sword's length. The photograph shows only the head and the top of the shaft. The full length of the weapon, and any maker's mark, cannot be seen. The place and date given here are an estimate from the form of the head.

=== object-G ===
Shabti. Egypt, probably Third Intermediate Period (ca. 1070–664 B.C.). Faience
This small figure is mummiform. Its arms are crossed over the chest and it wears a long tripartite wig. A bright turquoise-blue glaze covers it, now spotted with dark patches where the glaze has worn or discoloured. Shabtis were placed in tombs in great numbers to stand in for the dead person when the gods called on them to work in the fields of the afterlife. The figure probably held farming tools in its hands, but these have worn away. No inscription can be read in this view. The bright blue glaze and simple modelling suggest a date in the Third Intermediate Period.
