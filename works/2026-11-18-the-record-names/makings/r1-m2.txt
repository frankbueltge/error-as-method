Travelers Above Yokohama Harbor, Seen through a Hillside Cutting. Japan, Edo period, about 1860. Gountei (Utagawa) Sadahide (1807–1878/79). Woodblock print; ink and color on paper.

Framed by the steep walls of a cutting and by leaning pines, a small party of travelers pauses above a wide bay. Two men walk ahead. One wears a green jacket with a white crest and the other carries a bundle on his back. Two women follow below, and a fan lies at the lower left. Square-rigged foreign ships crowd the harbor. The scene is almost certainly Yokohama, opened to foreign trade in 1859, which Sadahide pictured many times for buyers eager to see the new port. The signature at lower right reads Gountei Sadahide. The photograph is too small to read the full inscription in the red cartouche at the top.

=== object-F ===
Cavalryman Standing beside His Horse. France, dated 1872 (reading uncertain). Édouard Detaille (1848–1912). Watercolor and gouache on tan paper.

A heavy-cavalry guardsman in full dress stands at ease. His gloved hand rests on his sword hilt and his black horse waits behind him. Detaille, whose signature is at lower left, built his reputation on painting the French army with near-documentary exactness. The steel helmet with red plume and horsehair mane, blue sleeves, gold aiguillettes, red sash and tall boots appear to be the uniform of the Cent-gardes, Napoleon III's mounted bodyguard, which was disbanded in 1870. If the date beside the signature reads 1872, this is a remembrance of a vanished corps. The bare brown ground, with only a cast shadow, keeps attention on the uniform.

=== object-G ===
Shabti. Egypt, probably Third Intermediate Period (about 1070–664 B.C.). Faience.

Shabtis were placed in Egyptian tombs to do, in the deceased's place, any labor demanded of them in the afterlife. This small figure has the mummiform shape: a wrapped body with feet set on a low base, arms crossed on the chest, and a long wig falling over the shoulders. Its bright turquoise glaze, typical of Egyptian faience, has worn through in pale patches. Dark traces near the hands may be what remains of painted details such as hoes. No inscription is visible in this view, so the date rests on style alone. Figures like this were made in large numbers, sometimes hundreds for a single burial.

=== object-H ===
Medal of Edward I, from a Series of the Kings of England. Probably Geneva, about 1731. Jean Dassier (Swiss, 1676–1763). Bronze.

The legend around this crowned bust reads, in part, EDOUARD I D G ANG ET HIB REX, which means "Edward I, by the grace of God, King of England and Ireland." The portrait is not a true likeness. Edward died in 1307, and this idealized face, with long curled hair, a jeweled crown and a mantle fastened with a jeweled clasp, is an eighteenth-century invention. The medal appears to come from the series of English monarchs issued by the Geneva medalist Jean Dassier around 1731 and sold as sets to collectors. Only the obverse is shown here. In that series the reverse carries a historical inscription.
