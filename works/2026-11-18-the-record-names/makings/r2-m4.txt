Side chair, Gothic Revival style. United States, 1850–60. Maker unidentified. Wood, with silk damask upholstery and brass casters.

The chair applies the architecture of a Gothic church to a piece of furniture. Its upholstered back rises to a pointed arch that stands more than four feet tall. The arch carries carved crockets and ends in a fleur-de-lis finial. Ring-turned posts, cut like stalks of bamboo, stand at either side and end in slender pinnacles. Pieces like this were made for the Gothic villas and libraries that were fashionable in mid-century America, and they were meant to look picturesque more than to be practical. The red damask, a pattern of lozenges and medallions, is probably a later replacement. The casters let the heavy chair be moved across the room.

A Distant View of Yokohama from the Mountain Pass at Noge Village. Japan, Edo period, 3rd month, 1860. Utagawa (Gountei) Sadahide. Woodblock print; ink and color on paper.

Travelers climb the steep cutting through the hill at Noge. Two men walk ahead, and behind them come women and children. Between the cliff walls, which are framed by pines, the view opens onto Yokohama: the town, its licensed district and its bridges, with a harbor full of ships beyond. The port had been opened to foreign trade only the year before, in 1859, and Sadahide became one of the most prolific artists to record it. Prints like this one sold to a public curious about the new port. A red cartouche at the top holds a descriptive inscription. The red cartouche at lower right holds the artist's signature, and seals sit beside it.

Fragment of a kylix (drinking cup). Greek, Attic, Late Archaic period, 490–480 B.C. Painter unknown. Terracotta with black gloss.

This sherd, about 2 centimeters long, comes from a kylix, the broad, shallow wine cup used at the Greek symposium, or drinking party. It was made in Athens in the decade when red-figure cup painting was at its height. On the orange clay surface, a band of lustrous black gloss is broken by a thin strip of bare clay. To one side are tapering black strokes, which may belong to a figure, a garment or a plant. The fragment is too small to show the scene. Fragments like this one are studied because they keep the painter's brushwork and the quality of the gloss.

=== object-B ===
Cylinder seal with animals, serpent and tree, shown with a modern impression. Cyprus, Late Cypriot period, ca. 15th–13th century B.C. Carver unknown. Steatite.

A cylinder seal was rolled across wet clay to leave its owner's mark, and it was usually pierced lengthwise so that it could be worn on a cord. This seal is just over 3 centimeters tall. The impression beside it shows the full design. Horned animals, probably goats or gazelles, stand in a row. Among them are a rearing serpent, a long-necked bird, a branching tree and small star-shaped motifs. The figures are cut in the soft stone with simple drilled and linear strokes. Cypriot carvers took both the seal form and its animal imagery from the Near East and adapted them in their own way.
