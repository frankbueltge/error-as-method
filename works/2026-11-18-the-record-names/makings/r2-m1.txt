Side chair, Gothic Revival style; United States, 1850–60; maker unknown; wood, with silk damask upholstery and brass casters
The chair's design draws on the architecture of medieval churches. The tall back is shaped as a pointed arch and edged with carved crockets, and a carved finial sits at the top. On either side, the back posts rise as ringed shafts that taper to spire-like points. In the mid-nineteenth century, American furniture makers and their clients used medieval forms like these for halls and libraries. The red upholstery has a lattice of diamonds and foliate medallions. The museum's record does not describe the upholstery, so it may be original or a later replacement. The turned front legs run on brass casters. The chair stands about 126 cm (49¾ in.) high.

=== object-B ===
Cylinder seal and modern impression; Cyprus, Late Cypriot period, about 15th–13th century BCE; steatite
A cylinder seal was rolled across damp clay to mark ownership or seal a document or container. The design is cut into the seal in reverse, so it reads correctly only on the impression. The modern impression shows the whole scene at once. Horned animals, probably goats, stand among stylized trees, and some turn their heads back. A bird flies above, small star or rosette shapes fill the gaps, and a tall serpent rises at one end. Cypriot seal cutters of this period borrowed motifs like these from the seals of Syria and northern Mesopotamia. The seal is 3.25 cm (1¼ in.) high. It came from the Cesnola Collection.

Couteau de brèche (staff weapon); France, about 1500; maker unknown; steel, beech wood
The French name means "breach knife". The couteau de brèche is a staff weapon: a long, knife-like blade set on a wooden shaft. This one is almost two metres long, and the head alone is 61 cm. Seen here from an angle, the blade looks like a slender spear point. Partway down it narrows in a step on one edge, and below the blade a faceted socket is pinned to the beech shaft. The photograph shows only the head and the top of the shaft. The bright steel has scattered small pits, and no maker's mark is visible. The weapon weighs about 2 kg.

Pin (catalogued as a knife) with animal finial; Moche, Peru, 2nd–7th century; maker unknown; cast copper
At the top of this cast copper implement is a crested animal standing on a small platform. Its crest and raised tail suggest a bird. The museum's record gives two different names for the object: its title calls it a pin, and its object name calls it a knife. In the photograph the shaft is rounded and tapers to a point, and no cutting edge is visible, so it is shown here as a pin. Just below the head, a hole runs through the shaft. Specks of red near the point may be pigment or corrosion. The object is 18.2 cm (7⅛ in.) high and was made by Moche metalworkers on the north coast of Peru.
