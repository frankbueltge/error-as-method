## F-229 — a pool that assumed verifiers explain themselves

**What happened.** The first draft of the pool required every entry to carry a verifier's note of at least 20
characters, so that the reader would always see the reason for the outcome. Run on the dump, that pool held 670 entries.
12 of them were *Verified*. The quotas (20 verified per group) could not have filled. The rule was changed before
`PREDICTIONS.md` was first committed: a *Rejected* entry must carry a note, a *Verified* one need not. The change is
recorded in the file itself.

**Why it is an entry.** The held term was my picture of the register: that a verifier writes a reason either way.
The register's verifiers write reasons when they refuse, and rarely when they agree. That asymmetry turned out to be the
night's subject (F-230), and I had met it as an obstacle an hour before I met it as a finding. **Holder of the
reference:** the practice. **What would repair it:** count a register's fields by status before writing a rule over
them.

## F-230 — a refusal booked as an error on the other side

**What happened.** I coded 57 errata before two fresh readers did. On 27 rejected entries I coded `R` (the
reporter's held reference was wrong) eleven times. The readers coded `R` once and twice. In ten of my eleven they coded
`X` (the entry calls neither term wrong). Read again after the answers, two of the eleven entries do say the reporter
was wrong (E01, E02). The other nine say the published text is right, or that the matter is not one for an erratum.
The prompt asked what *the entry* says.

**Why it is an entry.** The reference was the prompt's rule, *"from the entry's own text"*, and it was held by the
practice: I wrote that rule into Session 113's prompt and carried it here. My codes went past it. The norm that decided
them was not in the prompt. It came from the frame the prompt uses, a held term and a judged term with one of them at
fault, and from eleven seventh nights of using that frame on a register that records only failures. On a register of
corrections, most of which are declined, the frame booked declines as faults. This is the night's finding, and also
an error by the practice's own declared rule. **What would repair it:** the rule of writing adopted in
`works/position-2026-10-14.md` (a refused correction is recorded as a refusal), and `S127.DECLINE`, which sends the
claim to a third register.
