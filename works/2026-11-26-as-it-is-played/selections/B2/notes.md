# Notes

- v1: First pick, built to the proportions (3 resignation, 3 time, 2 checkmate, 0 draws; 4 blitz, 3 bullet, 1 rapid) with a spread of ratings from about 1100 to 2250. Reading it as a whole: every game ran 24-50 moves, yet about a fifth of the pool is over in 20 moves or fewer, and none of the pool's uneven pairings appeared.
- v2: Swapped 42 (blitz resignation, 24 moves) for 02 (blitz resignation, a fork on move 9), and 13 (rapid resignation, 46 moves) for 47 (rapid resignation, 1500 v 2153, over in 10 moves). Both kept the same speed and ending, so the counts are unchanged. Read again: the counts hold, the winners split 4 White and 4 Black, and the lengths and ratings now look like the pool, so I stopped here.

The eight: four blitz (02, 46, 06, 38), three bullet (45, 29, 36) and one rapid (47). Three end by resignation, three on time and two by checkmate. No draw is included: 3 of 60 rounds to 0.4 of a game, a tie with a third time loss, and I gave the slot to time losses because they are much more common (30% of the pool, against 5% for draws). Ratings run from about 1070 to 2250, matching the middle of the pool. I didn't pick for brilliance. The set includes an early blunder and resignation, a lopsided pairing, a player who flagged in a won position, two long games decided by the clock and two ordinary mating attacks, because those are the kinds of games the sample actually contains.
