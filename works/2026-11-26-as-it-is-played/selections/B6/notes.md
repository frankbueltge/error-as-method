# Notes

- v1: First pick to the target counts (8 x shares: 4 blitz, 3 bullet, 1 rapid; 3 resignation, 3 time, 2 mate, 0 draw). Draws (0.4 of a game) and the third time loss (also 0.4) tie for the last slot. I gave it to time loss because a flag fall is more typical of online play than a draw.
- v2: Rereading v1 as a set, the ratings were far too low. The average was about 1430 against 1662 for the pool, and no player was above 1850, though a quarter of the pool is above 1917. I swapped 33 (~1090) for 60 (~1915, blitz, time) and 20 (~1300) for 36 (~2240, bullet, mate). I also swapped 45 for 11 because it would show a lone king "winning" on time, which would puzzle viewers.
- v3: Rereading v2, six of the eight opened 1.e4 and only one opened 1.d4, which is heavier on e4 than the pool. I swapped bullet time loss 11 (French) for 21 (Nimzo-Indian, ~1435), which keeps the counts and puts the mean rating at about 1670. Nothing else needed changing, so this is the version I hand over.

## The eight

12, 14, 09, 60, 58, 36, 21, 37. By speed there are four blitz, three bullet and one rapid; by ending, three resignations, three losses on time and two checkmates. No draw is included: that is 5% of the pool, and one draw would be 12.5% of the page. Ratings run from about 1370 to 2240, centred near the pool median. Lengths run from a 5-move bullet resignation to a 46-move time loss, centred near the pool's 30. White and Black win four each. I chose typical games rather than brilliant ones. They include a quick resignation, two players who flagged in won or lost positions, an evenly matched Caro-Kann collapse, and ordinary mates. That is the mix the site actually produces.
