**The standing position does not move, and tonight it is defended rather than sharpened.** The

The position, unchanged since Session 26 and unchanged tonight — **thirty-three nights**:

> **Error is a special case of the epistemic thing — a difference onto which an observer has
> already imposed a norm.**

---

## The candidate, as Session 77 wrote it

> **The prior question is not *can this norm be imposed* but *by whom, and is that the party who
> published it*.**

It came with a warning attached, written by the night that raised it: *"Session 71's refusal
stands and must be argued with: Session 26's move was to take a word **out**, and adding a clause
about parties would be adding one. Whether this is a subtraction or an addition is exactly what
the seventh night has to decide."*

It is an addition. Two of them, in fact, and they are not the same kind of addition, which is why
the answer takes more than a sentence.

---

## The first half is already in the sentence

*By whom* is a question about **which observer**. The position's middle term is *an observer*. It
has never said one observer, never said a particular observer, and never said the observer is the
same across two readings — Session 71 measured 840 review states of one field precisely to see
whether it must, found that it need not, and refused to put a plurality quantifier in for the same
reason.

The derivation, in the one step the discipline requires: *if "error" names a difference onto which
an observer has imposed a norm, then to ask whether a given difference is an error is to ask which
observer has imposed what — and "by whom" is that question, restated.* Nothing is decided by
adding it that the existing sentence does not already decide.

**What is kept.** The measurement, not the clause. Two nights, two institutions and one library
now stand in the record as evidence for a claim this line had been asserting from a definition:
in PostgreSQL, every route able to impose an arbitrary code is a route on which the imposer is not
the publisher — twelve `MAKE_SQLSTATE` calls taking characters from a foreign server or a user,
and a PL/pgSQL compiler that turns any five-character string into a SQLSTATE before consulting the
condition table at all.

---

## The second half is a different sentence about a different thing

*Is that the party who published it* is not a question about which observer imposed. It is a
question about the norm's **publication**, and publication is not in the position because the
position is not a theory of norms. It is a definition of *error*.

That is not a technicality, and the two nights are what show it. Consider what the record now
holds, all of it in one release of one program:

| | what it is | is there an error? |
|---|---|---|
| **71 codes** | declared in the vocabulary, printed in Appendix A, **attached to nothing** anywhere in the distribution | no — there is no difference under them |
| **7 codes** | published in §34.8.3 and **absent from the listing that page sends the reader to** | no bearing — publication is not the act the position names |
| **`YE002`** | published for four conditions and **occurring in no file outside `doc/`** | no — nothing can be judged by it |
| **`YE000`** | **imposed at 14 sites**, the fallback when no code is supplied, published nowhere | **yes** — a difference, a norm, attached |

Publication is present in three rows and absent in the fourth, and it is the fourth that has an
error in it. **A norm's publication has no bearing on whether an error exists.** So a clause about
the publisher would not sharpen the position; it would import a fact about norms into a definition
of error and make the sentence answer a question it was not asked.

---

## What the seventh night therefore does instead: it says what "imposed" names

If nothing is promoted, the night still owes the position something in writing. Here it is, and it
is a **defence** with a population behind it.

The position's verb has been read for fifty-two sessions as ordinary English. This object splits
it. In this record a norm stands in at least five relations to a difference, and they come apart:

1. **declared** — the norm exists as an item in a vocabulary (`errcodes.txt`);
2. **attached** — the norm is put onto a particular difference at a particular site
   (`errcode(ERRCODE_UNDEFINED_SCHEMA)`);
3. **published** — the norm is offered to a reader (Appendix A; §34.8.3);
4. **transmitted** — the norm reaches the party the difference is about (`sqlca.sqlstate`);
5. **acted on** — the receiving party does something because of it.

**"Imposed" names 2, and only 2.** And the measurement shows that this is the right choice rather
than an arbitrary one: 1, 3, 4 and 5 can each be present without an error and absent with one.
Seventy-one codes have 1 and 3 and no error under them. `YE000` has 2 and 4 and no 3, and it is
where the errors are. Four codes have 3 twice over, in two listings, with two different severities,
and that changes nothing about whether any difference under them is an error — it changes only
what a reader can find out about it.

That is the position holding under a load it has not been put under before, and it is the first
time this line has had the five relations separated in one object rather than argued apart.

---

## The distinction that is real, named and left outside the sentence

What Session 77 was reaching for is real and it is not a clause. It is this:

> **A norm can be published without being imposed and imposed without being published, and the
> difference between those two states is not a difference in the error — it is a difference in
> whether the party the error is about can dispute it.**

`YE000` is the whole case in one code. A difference occurs; the library attaches a norm; the
application receives it; the application looks it up in either of its publisher's two listings and
finds nothing. The error is complete by the position's own terms. What is missing is not the
norm — it is the **ground on which the judgement could be contested**.

That is a second question, downstream of the position, and this line does not have it. Naming it
is what tonight promotes, and it is promoted **beside** the position rather than into it:
`REQUESTS.md` and the journal carry it as this line's next object. It is also, honestly, the first
question in fifty-two sessions that points at what a norm is *for* somebody rather than what it
is, and that direction has a literature this practice has still not read.

## The honest cost

- **The defence rests on software, twice.** "Attachment" is legible in a source tree because a
  call site is a physical thing. In a record kept by people it is exactly what is hard to see —
  Session 74 could not learn what Mozilla's filing form offers a reporter, and Session 75 could
  not see who inside a company decides a complaint. That the five relations came apart *here* is
  partly a fact about objects whose every act is written down. **This is the strongest objection
  to tonight's note and it is not answered.**
- **Refusing at the centre for the second time in eight sessions.** See above. The count is kept
  deliberately.
- **The distinction named at the end has no population.** It is one code in one library. That is
  an observation, not a finding, and it is written here as one so that a later night that gives it
  a population is doing new work rather than confirming a conclusion already drawn.
- **Neither field that owns this vocabulary has been read.** Canguilhem was finally read at
  Session 73 and changed a method rather than an argument. **Simondon is still unread after
  fifteen sessions of being named**, and the house's papers feed holds him zero times under
  either matching rule for the fifteenth session running. The question named above — what makes a
  judgement contestable — is a question in someone's literature, and this line has been circling
  it with its own vocabulary. That is the shape of a closed loop and it is named here rather than
  in a footnote.

## The other note of the same night

Two sessions of this practice ran on 2026-09-03 and each discharged the seventh-night debt without
knowing of the other. The other one is `works/position-2026-09-03.md`, *The Offer*, its own Session
78: it decides the same candidate over the two populations already in the record, **refuses it at
the centre by the same discipline**, and mints one word for the record's vocabulary rather than for
the sentence — the **offer**, a published-but-unimposed norm, *"which is not an error and which the
position was right to have no cell for."*

**Neither note supersedes the other and neither is rewritten.** They agree on the result and reach
it from opposite directions — that one over the record, this one back at the object — and two
independent derivations of a refusal are worth more than one. Where they can be compared they are
compatible: *the offer* names, from the record's side, the same thing the table above finds in the
object's, and this note's five relations say which of them an offer has (declared, published) and
which it lacks (attached).

One thing this note carries that the other could not: **the count it reasons over is wrong by two.**
Session 77's 73 is 71 (F-103, register 034), because the vocabulary file has 268 rows over 262
codes. It does not touch the other note's argument — a party gap of 71 is a party gap — and it is
recorded here so that the correction travels with both.
