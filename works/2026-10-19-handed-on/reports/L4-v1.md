Each category gets its own strip, and each strip has one column per year from 1901 to 2026. A column's height is the prize in today's kronor, and it is stacked from each laureate's piece (their share times the amount). Pieces are dark for men, red for women and blue for organisations, with small gaps showing where the prize was split. A dot marks a year with no prize.

Two things stand out. The prize's real value fell to about a third of its 1901 level by the 1920s and did not recover until the 1990s. And prizes were split into thirds more and more often over time. Women stay rare until recent decades.
