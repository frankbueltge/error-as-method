I made v4. In v3 most of the picture's red came from the way the lines were drawn, not from the data. Each year's row is about 5 px tall but holds up to 15 laureates. The red and blue lines were thicker than the grey ones, almost opaque and drawn last, so each one covered two or three men. As a result the lower right looked heavily red, although only 70 of the 1,036 awards went to women.

What changed in `index.html`:
- **Equal slices:** every laureate now gets an equal slice of its year's row that no other line covers, drawn fully opaque. The amount of red, blue and grey is now in proportion to the counts, and the slow arrival of women is shown at its true size.
- **Lighter grey for men:** men are drawn in a lighter warm grey so the slices stay distinguishable, and the legend swatch matches.
- **"age 60" label moved:** the mid-century label sat on the median curve, so I moved it from 1930 to 1922.
- **Birth-year dots removed:** the slices made the small dots at the left end of each line unnecessary.

One cost: the organisation slivers are now quite thin, so the 1963 Red Cross line is faint. Everything else (the form, the equal-age diagonals and the annotations) is unchanged.

Before changing the code I added the `## v4` heading and paragraph to `NOTE.md`. I looked at `current.png` first, then rendered the new version with the viewer (`renders/01.png`, no page errors). In that render the women now show as thin red slivers and the labels stay readable. I did not zoom in to check the moved "age 60" label up close (the crop failed); in the full render it sits clear of the curve.
