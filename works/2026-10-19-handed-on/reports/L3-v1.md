I made "The Long Lean", a still canvas picture in which every Nobel award is a thread. Each thread starts at the laureate's birth or founding year on an upper timeline and ends at the prize year on a lower one. Both timelines use the same calendar, so the further a thread leans, the longer the laureate waited. Seen together, the threads lean further over the century: the median age at award goes from 56 in 1901–25 to 68 in 2001–26.

- **Colours and weight:** men are faint grey, women are red and organisations blue. Thread thickness shows the laureate's share of the prize, and small red ticks on the lower timeline let you count the women.
- **Labels:** four threads are labelled: Malala (17, the steepest), Goodenough (97, the longest wait), Marie Curie's two prizes, and the Friends Service Council (founded 1647). The Council's thread is cut at the left edge and enters from off the page.
- **What's left out:** one award has no birth year and isn't drawn, so the key counts 30 organisations rather than 31. The median ages count people only.

I rendered and looked at it three times. After the first view I toned down the women's red lines, which made them look more numerous than 70, and moved two labels that were cut off or sitting on top of the threads. In the final render nothing is cut off and the page had no errors.
