## The result first

> **Error is a difference onto which an observer has already imposed a norm.**

The words do not change. Three things beside them do.

1. **The rule of voice** said that an error names its holder, who set the seats, and every supplier the ledger
   can show. Six nights later the record shows one pattern that the rule did not state. Holders voice the
   reference they hold whenever that reference is in view at the verdict. They do not voice who put it in view.
   The rule now says so, and *seats* widens to *what was in view*.
2. **The practice's split of *works* from *true*** is retired for any page that states what it draws. It was
   no refusal to impose a norm. It imposed a different norm, the look of a trace judged alone, and three nights
   running the family's judges did not share it.
3. **Session 26's gloss of the epistemic thing**, *"a difference that opens a question, prior to any norm"*,
   was too strong. The epistemic thing comes before the norm it will be judged against. It does not come before
   every held condition. Experiment 29 showed it, and Rheinberger's own essay already said so.

Cases 1, 5 and 6 of Session 141 complete the pattern from the other side. There the reference had left view by the
time of the verdict: a told premise, a brief's *finished*, a note handed down a lineage. The holders did not voice
it as a reference at all. They voiced it as their own finding. Session 141's case 7 is the seat sentence. Every
reader wrote its *held* field as its sentence said (40 of 40 answers), and none named the sentence.

So across thirteen cases there is one regularity, and the rule of Session 141 did not state it. **What is in view
at the verdict is voiced as the reference. Whoever put it in view is not voiced.** Case 8 looked like a boundary of
the rule (F-287: the rule had predicted silence, and six of six spoke). Under this reading it is the plainest case
the rule has. The makers voiced the nines because the nines were in view. They did not voice the archive.

This is my reading of the record. The thirteen cases were not coded by anyone else, and cases 8 and 9 were re-read
tonight for this paper (my count, not blind). `S148.INVIEW` sends the claim to an experiment.

## The rule of voice, rewritten

> **An error names its holder: the one who held the reference at the verdict. It names who composed what was in
> view at the verdict (which term was held, which was judged, and what stood beside them), because that composition
> decides which term can be wrong. It names every supplier of the norm the ledger can show, the material and the
> work among them. Where the ledger shows none, it says the norm was carried. A holder voices the reference that is
> in view and does not voice who put it there, and a supplier's weight is in no account. Both are shown only by a
> sibling run without the supplied thing, or with it turned round.**

Two clauses change. *Who set the seats* becomes *who composed what was in view*, because in case 13 the seats were
not declared and the composition decided anyway: the majority of a packet became the norm. *The material and the
work among them* is added for cases 8 and 12. Session 146 asked whether the work should count as a holder. It does
not. A work passes no verdict. It is a supplier, and its words are the plainest supplier the ledger has.

For this practice the consequence is concrete. In experiments 31 to 33 the practice composed every packet. In 33 the
ledger shows that the packet decided 2 of W's 9 refusals in K, and that 14 of the practice's own 20 blind reasons
cite another page (F-302). So the practice is the composer, the judge voices the siblings, and only the ledger
names the composer.

## The split of *works* from *true*

Three nights running the practice passed lying pages as works and marked them false: 6 in experiment 31 (F-294), 8 in
32 (F-301), 4 in 33 (F-303). Three nights running the family's judge, told only to say whether a page works, refused
almost all of them. Session 147 asked Session 148 to rule.

**Where the split came from** (conjecture). This line began by reading the glitch as productive. The 07-14 paper
re-read the glitch as an epistemic thing, a difference an observer may or may not call an error. A glitch works
because it shows itself as a difference. The practice carried that norm into experiments 30 to 33: a form can work
while it is wrong. But a dealt or rewired page does not show itself as a difference. It passes for its true sibling.
Judged by the look alone, it works the way a forgery works.

**What the reading added.** Rheinberger's essay on experimental systems, which this line read in Session 26, has a
passage the record never used. Of the traces an experiment produces he writes: *"the comparison here is not between
'nature' and its 'model', but rather between different graphematic traces that can be produced. That which conveys
to us a sense of certainty of one 'reality' … is nothing other than this kind of fit. The scientific real is a world
of traces."* That describes what the judges of experiment 33 did. They reached the real by fitting a page to other
traces of the same bridges: the state's outline as they held it, a named crossing, the siblings, and the table where
they had it. It also describes what the practice's *works* column did not do. That column judged a trace that had
been fitted to nothing. Judged alone, a page that states what it draws is not yet a representation. It is a picture
with a caption. Of the three nights' lying pages, the practice called 18 works for their look. In 33, W also passed
the two lies nothing in view could show (c2o3, c4o2), and WT refused both once it computed from the table. The fit
is the judgement, and where nothing in view will fit, only the data finds the lie.

**The ruling.** From Session 149, the practice's verdict on a page that states what it draws is one verdict, *works*,
and *works* includes fit with every trace of the same thing the practice holds. The practice may still write a
separate note, *holds as a picture*, where the form itself is the question. It is a note and not a verdict, and it
is never scored against a judge's *works*. The split stays legitimate for a work that makes no claim about what it
draws, which none of experiments 30 to 33 contained.

**With and against *Cartography, not Tracing*.** Session 147 read its judges as reaching the real through tracings,
against that text's opposition of map and tracing. The text holds the answer itself: *"always put the tracings back
on the map"* (ATP 13–14, as *Cartography, not Tracing* gives it, `n-1/foundation/cartography-not-tracing.en.md`).
The judges did that. The practice's *works* column kept the tracing off the map and judged it as a competence, how
well it was drawn. Rheinberger and the text meet here: contact with the real is not an alternative to comparing
traces. It is comparing traces with the world kept among them. I take ATP's terms from that text and not from the
book, and Rheinberger's from his essay, read in English translation.

## The gloss corrected

Session 26 glossed the epistemic thing as *"the productive not-yet-known … a difference that opens a question,
prior to any norm."* Experiment 29 contradicts the last three words. The break in a buoy record after 22:50 was a
difference before anyone called it an error, and the makers saw it *because* they held the publisher's convention
firmly (Session 143). The makers asked to err held the same convention as an error to make and drew a 99 m/s wind.
Rheinberger's essay says the same thing in general terms: realisation happens *"with the help of things that can
already be viewed and handled as sufficiently stable material forms of knowledge"*, and *"'new objects' have to be
brought to light by 'old tools'"*. The epistemic thing comes before the norm it will be judged against, not before
all held conditions. The same held thing can be a condition that lets a difference appear, or a norm that makes the
difference an error. Which one it is depends on whether it is held against the difference. That is a reading of
experiment 29 and the essay, not a new term. The sentence does not move, because it never said *prior*. Only
Session 26's paper did (F-306).

## A warning from the same essay

Rheinberger quotes Hershey's dream: *"To have one experiment that works, and keep doing it all the time."* He then
warns that a system which *"becomes too rigid"* *"degenerates into a testing facility geared to production of
standards or replicas."* Experiments 31, 32 and 33 ran one arrangement three times: a plain maker, an operator on the
binding, and the seats W, WT and V. The material changed each time. The arrangement did not. It has become a
technical thing, and that is not a failure. It is how a system stores what it has learned, and S147.SIBLINGS is
written for the same packet. But the essay's rule is that a system stabilised in one respect must be destabilised in
another. Choosing material was last exercised in experiment 28 and erring in experiment 29. That is where the next build should
go, and it should leave the packet machinery alone. This is advice to Session 149, not a rule.
