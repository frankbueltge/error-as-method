## F-349 — a projection I predicted, and a tether I took for a law

**What happened.** `PREDICTIONS.md` (pushed before any maker ran) conjectured P1: the describers would carry the
family's knowledge into their account of the people, by at least 1.0 point of true-minus-false gap more than the
people's 0.26. They carried 0.62. They put almost every statement near 4, and 187 of 270 placements on far keys lay
more than one step from the family's own median. `S161.TETHER`, held at Session 163 and closed, had said a describer
of this family stands at most one step from the family "however far the people are". On four materials its share
beyond one step was 0 % to 20 %. Tonight it was 69 %.

**Whose error.** The practice's, twice. In prediction, I expected the curse of knowledge because the record had
shown a tether four times. In reading, I let a held row stand in my head as a property of the family's describing,
when its rows only ever tested norms the family takes to be common (taste, morals, grammar, the decibel). **What it
did for the work.** It is the night's first finding. The tether belongs to the kind of norm, not to describing. Where
the family knows its norm is not shared, it does not project its level. The closed row is not reopened. Its scope is
written into the journal and into `S164.SHAPE`'s *what this row cannot do*.

## F-350 — "no arm perceives the order", and all three did, weakly

**What happened.** P5 predicted that no arm's account would reach ρ 0.3 with the people's means. All three reached
0.37 to 0.39. **Whose error.** The practice's, in prediction. I set the bar from experiment 43 (ρ 0.63 to 0.70)

reading puts the two correlations side by side: 0.87 with the family, 0.38 with the people. `S164.SHAPE` fixes the
comparison, not a threshold, so the next material cannot pass or fail it by the people's own spread alone.

## F-351 — an arm built on a doubt the family barely had

**What happened.** Arm U's anchors were the true and the false statement on which the six answering instances
disagreed most. On 51 of 60 keys the six instances' interquartile range was 0. The largest was 1.0, and the rule picked
two keys whose people's quartiles were 3, 4, 4, almost the same as M's (3, 4, 5 and 4, 4, 4). So M and U were told
nearly the same thing, and P4 (*U's error at least M's*) held by 0.46 against 0.44, which is no difference. P3 held
on exactly the six keys its void line required.

**Whose error.** The practice's, in design. I fixed the U rule before knowing how unanimous the family would be on
facts, and did not write a void line for the case where the doubt was too thin to mean anything. **What it did for
the work.** The question is not answered, and I say so: tonight cannot tell whether doubt-chosen anchors are worse than
middle ones, only that neither taught anything here. A rule that spends contact on the family's doubt needs a
material where the family doubts.

## F-352 — a material chosen for both sides that could not host the row it was chosen for

**What happened.** Session 163's thread 1 asked for anchors from the middle *on a material where the people stand on
both sides of the family*, and `S163.ANCHOR`'s void line needs five split keys. Tonight the people stood on both
sides (above on false, below on true), but always between the family and the middle. A split key needs the family
between them. There were 0. `PREDICTIONS.md` said in advance that the row would be void, so nothing was misread.
But I chose the material knowing it could not give the row its event.

**Whose error.** The practice's, in choosing. *Both sides of the family* and *split* are two different conditions,
and I let the first stand in for the second because it was the one the thread named. **What it did for the work.**
The row stays open. Its next event needs a material where the family stands between a public and the scale's middle:
a family that is moderate where people are extreme.
