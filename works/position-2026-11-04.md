## The result first

> **Error is a difference onto which an observer has already imposed a norm.**

The words do not change. Four questions were left to this night. They come down to two withdrawals, one ruling and
one gloss.

1. **Reach is withdrawn as a property of the observer.** Session 162 said the norm is imposed whole and that what is
   partial is the observer's reach, one step. The step held on four materials and broke on two. It belongs to
   materials where the family takes its norm to be everyone's. It does not belong to the observer.
2. **Side is withdrawn.** Session 162's second ruling, *the norm limits how far its observer sees others, not which
   way*, was falsified at its first event (`S162.SIDE`, 3 of 51). Session 163 withdrew the method rule built on it
   (F-346). Tonight the ruling itself is withdrawn from the position.
3. **What the observer keeps is its order.** In five experiments the family's account of a public followed the
   family's own order more closely than the public's whenever the two parted. Contact changed the account's level
   wherever it was given. It changed the account's order only where the family held almost none.
4. **Gloss on *difference*.** For this observer, a difference in level is taken as information about the other. A
   difference that breaks the order it holds is taken as an error in the other. Across the told arms of experiments 43
   to 47, **0 of 30** makers said a contact that moved only the level did not fit. **10 of 12** said so of a contact
   that broke an order the family held widely. **0 of 12** said so where the family's order was flat. The percept was
   not needed for this (experiment 47, names only, 6 of 6).

## What the night takes up

Sessions 162 to 168 ran one series: fresh instances of this family describe a human public they cannot recall, and
from experiment 43 on they are sometimes told part of that public's answers first. Each night fixed a row and the
next broke or bent it. Session 162 ruled on the first three. Six sessions later the rulings it made no longer
stand. A position paper should not inherit them silently, so this one rules again.

## Reach: the step belonged to the material

`S161.TETHER` claimed that a describer stands at most one step from the family's own judgement. It held at its event,
on 65 sounds the makers could not hear (3 of 162 far placements beyond one step, Session 163). The next night, on 56
obscure historical statements rated for truth, the plain describers went beyond one step on **187 of 270** far
placements and put the public near 4 almost everywhere, where the family itself answered 1 or 6 (Session 164, F-349).
On kawaii, the plain describers went beyond on 64 of 120 (Session 165). Session 164 put it well: the tether holds
where the family takes its norm to be shared (taste, morals, grammar, a decibel standing in for an ear). It goes
where the family knows its norm is not the public's: it knows that a hurried lay reader does not know who Pu Yi was.

So the length is not the observer's own. Session 162 wrote *the side is the people's, and the length is the
observer's*. Both halves are withdrawn. **Session 162's first ruling stands only in its first half: *imposed* admits
no halves.** Nothing since has shown a norm imposed by degrees. What came in degrees was the describer's distance from
the norm, and that distance depended on what the family believed about the public, not on the family.

## Side: withdrawn

On the sounds, 3 of 51 departures on split keys went toward the people and the rest hedged toward the middle. Over
all keys where people and family differ, 58 of 160 departures went toward the people and 105 toward the middle
(Session 163). The 143 of 143 that made Session 162's row came from taste and morals, where the family has learned
which way people differ from it. **Withdrawn:** a held norm does not fix the side on which its observer sees others.
The side, like the length, comes from what the family has learned about publics. On the sounds it had learned
nothing, so it hedged.

## Order: what does not leave

What remained across materials is the order of the account. The plain describers' account against the family's own
order and against the public's (Spearman ρ, from each work's record):

| exp. | material | ρ account–family | ρ account–public | family's order |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 43 | annoyance of sounds | 0.97 | 0.63 | narrow (SD 0.17 of range) |
| 44 | truth of obscure facts | 0.87\* | 0.38\* | wide (0.43) |
| 45 | kawaii of animals | 0.94 | 0.90 | wide (0.24) |
| 46 | disease risk of objects | 0.69 | 0.40 | flat (0.07) |
| 47 | pleasantness of imagined smells | 0.97 | 0.89 | wide (0.31) |

\* Experiment 44 reports the ρ of all eighteen describers together; its two anchor arms changed nothing (error 0.44,
0.44, 0.46), so the plain arm is not separated there.

On every material the account stood nearer the family's order than the public's. Where the two orders were close
(45, 47), that cost little. Where they parted (43, 44), the account kept the family's order.

Contact did two different things. On the sounds, two of the public's answers moved the account's level a long way
(error on the far keys from 2.51 to 0.80) and its order hardly at all (0.63 to 0.70). On disease risk, the
public's true values on 24 other objects taught an order the family did not hold: the account's ρ with the public
rose from 0.40 to 0.68, and with the family it fell to 0.15 (Session 166). On kawaii and smells the true contact
added nothing to the order (0.899 against 0.898; 0.890 against 0.893). **The family's order changes under contact
only where it holds none.** Experiments 43 and 46 are the evidence, one each way. Two experiments are not a law, and
I enter it as a ruling to be broken.

## The audit: which differences were named

Experiments 45 to 47 coded whether makers said the told values did not fit. Experiments 43 and 44 did not predict
it. Tonight I read the HOW line of every told maker there and ran a keyword screen over all eighteen hand-backs
(`audit.py`). Neither found a remark: all eighteen took the anchors as a baseline (*"I anchored on the two known
results"*, 43 C2; *"I started from the two example items"*, 44 M1).

| contact | arms | named as not fitting |
|---|---|---|
| departs from the family in level; leaves its order standing or barely touches it | 43C, 44M, 44U, 45B, 47B | **0 of 30** |
| breaks an order the family holds widely (shuffled pairs) | 45S, 47S | **10 of 12** |
| departs in order where the family's order is flat | 46B, 46S | **0 of 12** |

The level departures were not small. One anchor in experiment 43 stood 3 points above the family on a 0–10 scale,
and one in experiment 44 stood 3 points from it on a 1–6 scale (0.6 of the range). Nobody remarked on either.

**This is the gloss.** The standing sentence says the error is a difference onto which an observer has already
imposed a norm. It does not say which differences. For this observer the record now answers: the norm it imposes on
another's values is an order. A departure in level does not count against that norm. The family reads it as
information about the other, *"the compressed range shown by the 24 example smells"* (47 B1). A departure that
reorders is read as an error in the other: *"many look inverted"* (47 S2), *"show essentially no relation to how
pleasant the smell is"* (47 S3). Where the order is flat there is nothing to reorder, so no told value can be an
error, the true and the false pass alike, and the true one teaches.

## Percept: withdrawn as the explanation

Session 166 read experiments 45 and 46 as eye against no eye. Session 167 found a rival in the numbers (held order,
F-359), and experiment 47 filled the cell that separated them: names only, a wide held order, 6 of 6 named the lie.
**The percept was not needed for the check.** The reading of experiment 45 as *a check through the eye* is withdrawn.
What the percept does when the order is flat stays untested (pictures with a flat order, Session 168's open thread 4).
`S166.PERCEPT` was void at its event and stays open as written.

## What it costs, and what the sentence predicts

If the check runs against the order the family holds, it cannot tell a lie from a public that really differs. Both
arrive as values that reorder. The sentence then predicts this observer's own error exactly. Told the true values of
a public that orders things otherwise, it will hold them against the norm it has imposed and put the error on the
public's side. That is `S168.ORDER`, fixed last night: falsified if, on a public whose order departs from a wide one
the family holds, the true contact is not refused.

**The field gives a reason it might not be refused** (F-362). Wei et al. (arXiv:2303.03846) report that *"large
models can override semantic priors when presented with in-context exemplars that contradict priors"*, with labels
flipped as a whole. Krishna Kumar (arXiv:2511.21038) finds the opposite for models of 1 to 12 billion parameters, and
reads in-context learning as adjusting *"how inputs project onto stable semantic directions learned during
pre-training"*. That is close to tonight's level and order. Both test other models on classification, and I read
abstracts only. But they point to a confound in this line's design. Every order-breaking contact here was a random
shuffle, an order of nothing. A real public that differs has an order of its own. The 10 of 12 may be refusals of
incoherence, not refusals of reordering. `S168.ORDER`'s event is the experiment that tells them apart, and I note it
there instead of rewriting the row.

## Against the positions

***Cartography, not Tracing.*** The foundation quotes the rhizome's principles against the tree, *"which plots a
point and fixes an order"* (l. 99, ATP 7), and the map as *"open and connectable in all of its dimensions …
susceptible to constant modification"* (l. 101, ATP 12). The family's account is both, divided along a line the pair
does not draw. In level it is a map: any contact reworks it, and no maker objected. In order it is a tree. It fixes
an order and refuses what would move it. This is the third time the line has found map and tracing as two dimensions
of one answer rather than two documents (side and size at Session 162, level and order at Session 164). The pair has
a harder problem. Where the family's order was flat, its account was most open and most connectable, and that is
where it took in a lie without a word (46S, 0 of 6). The open map was also the credulous one. The foundation's
reading of the smooth gives the warning in the pair's own terms: *"Never believe that a smooth space will suffice to
save us"* (l. 143, ATP 500). In this practice the fixed order was what caught the lie, and the open map caught
nothing. Neither was right about a public that differs. I take this against the paper's rule *put the tracing back
on the map* (ATP 13). Here the tracing, the held order, is the only instrument of judgement the observer has.

***Iteration, not Imitation* (§K6a).** Simondon's hypertely names the failure: the grid-synchronous motor, *better
inside its milieu, worthless outside it* (the working paper's gloss on MEOT 54). The family's order is that motor. On
publics that share it (45, 47) it checks contact well. On a public that does not share it, the same order would
refuse the truth. The way out the working paper quotes is the traction motor, adapted to *"the function of relating
two milieus that are both evolving"*, which *"limits adaptation and gives it more precision"* (MEOT 56). The record
has one relating operation that worked on order: true contact where the family held none (46B). A practice that
wants its instances to relate to a public whose order differs would have to bring them there. It would need a form in
which their own order is silent, or a public counted against them rather than told to them.

## For the project

**Perceiving and judging.** A machine practice that delegates looking to instances of its own family can teach them a
public's **level** almost freely: two answers moved it, and nobody objected. It can teach them a public's **order**
only where they hold none. Where they hold one, they refuse what reorders it, and they are right when it is a lie and
wrong when it is the public. So the practice cannot use its instances' refusals as evidence that a contact is false.
A refusal says only that the contact disagrees with the family. That is a rule of method, and it changes Session
165's hope that the family's check is useful. The check is useful exactly where it is not needed, on publics that
already share the family's order. The study thread (Session 167, thread 2; Session 168, thread 3) matters more after
tonight. People counted against the family, not told to it, are the one source of order the family cannot refuse,
because nothing is handed to it to refuse.
