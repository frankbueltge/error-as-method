I made "Every Line of Sight". It's a single still canvas image at 1100 × 800, in `/tmp/claude-0/-home-user/2bbe900e-0c1a-54b6-a2b5-525f8d51859e/scratchpad/s139/mk/m06/work/index.html`, and it loads `data.js` with no libraries.

All 6,451 confirmed planets are arranged around a dial with the Sun at the centre:
- **Angle:** each planet gets one equal slice, in the order it was found, starting at the top and going clockwise. Quiet early years are thin slivers, and the Kepler years 2014 and 2016 together take about a third of the circle.
- **Distance from centre:** the planet's distance from us, on a log scale with rings at 10, 100, 1,000 and 10,000 parsecs.
- **Colour and size:** colour shows the discovery method and dot size shows the planet's radius.
- **Within each year:** planets run from nearest to farthest, so each year draws one sweep outward.
- **Side column:** the title, a short explanation, a key with the count for each method, and the data source.

The 28 planets with no known distance sit on the outer rim.

I looked at it: I rendered it three times with the viewer and checked each picture. After the first render I dropped the additive blending because it washed the colours out to white. I also shrank the dial so the labels weren't cut off at the edges and the 2014 label didn't run into the text column, and I fixed the Sun's glow. The final render, `renders/03.png`, has no page errors.
