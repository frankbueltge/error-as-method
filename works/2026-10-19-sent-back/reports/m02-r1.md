I made "The Shape of Looking", a still image drawn on a canvas. It plots 6,421 confirmed exoplanets as glowing points on a dark field. Left to right is how long each planet's year lasts, and bottom to top is its mass, both on log scales. Colour shows how each was found (transit, radial velocity, microlensing, imaging, other), and dot size follows the planet's radius. Planets with no known orbital period sit in a separate column on the right. Our own eight planets are white rings, so you can see the near-empty region around Earth.

The picture is meant to show how the instruments are biased. Transits crowd the short orbits and radial velocity finds the heavy planets. A few short notes and a legend explain this.

Yes, I looked at it: I rendered it twice with the viewer. After the first render I fixed overlapping labels (Venus/Earth, Uranus/Neptune), put a dark outline behind the notes so they read over the dots, and made the legend box solid. The second render had no page errors and looked right.

30 planets without a mass are left out of the plot. The 5 with years longer than 2,700 years sit on the right edge. The small print at the bottom says both.
