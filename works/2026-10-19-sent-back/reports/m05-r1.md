I made "The Length of Their Years", a still image of a dial drawn on a canvas. Every one of the 6,451 confirmed planets is a dot. Going clockwise from the top, each dot sits at the year the planet was found (1992 to 2026). Its distance from the centre is how long its year lasts, on a log scale from about two hours to a million days. Colour shows how it was found and dot size shows its radius. Faint rings mark the years of Mercury, Earth, Jupiter and Neptune. Planets with no known orbital period, mostly microlensing finds, sit as a loose band of dots just outside the rim. A short text, a key with a count for each method, and the data source are on the right.

I looked at it twice with the viewer. The first render was too big: the year labels at the top and bottom were cut off at the edges, and the transit dots blended into a near-white blur. I made the dial smaller and the transit dots fainter. The second render, `renders/02.png`, fits the frame with no page errors. In it, transits crowd the inner rings, the large Kepler batches of 2014 and 2016 show as bright spokes, radial-velocity planets fill the middle, and imaging and microlensing sit at the edge.
