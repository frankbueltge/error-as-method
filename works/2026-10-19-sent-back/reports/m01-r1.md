I made "One Sky of Orbits", a single still image drawn on a canvas, and I looked at it in three renders while working.

**What it shows:** all 6,451 confirmed exoplanets are placed around one imagined star at the centre.
- **Distance from the centre** is the planet's orbital period, on a log scale running from hours to millennia.
- **Angle** is the order of discovery: going clockwise from 1992 at the top, each planet gets an equal slice. The bulk releases of 2014 and 2016 therefore open into wide fans.
- **Colour** is the discovery method, and **dot size** is the planet's radius.
- **Rings:** a faint ring is drawn for every orbit, and a dashed ring marks Earth's year.
- **Unknown periods:** the 354 planets with no known period sit on an outer rim.
- **Text:** a title, a short explanation and a key with counts sit in a column on the right.

**What changed after looking:**
- At first the year labels at the top ran into each other. I now label only seven years.
- The labels for the period rings overlapped along the horizontal. I cut them to three (1 day, Earth's year, 100 years) and put a dark outline behind them.

The last render loaded with no page errors. Two things in it could still be better: the "1 day" label is faint against the dense dots, and the year ticks are crowded near the top.
