## The result first

> **Error is a difference onto which an observer has already imposed a norm.**

The words do not change. Under tonight's R1 they could move only if a row fired and demanded it. One row fired,
`S113.SIDE`, and what it carried was never in the sentence. It was a reading offered at `norm`: *a norm imposed
onto a difference is what says which of its terms must yield*. That reading is withdrawn. The sentence is
defended by the same check that took the reading away.

## What fell

Session 113 found that three coders of this practice's errors agreed on *what* had failed far more often than on
*which side* it stood. It proposed that a declarable norm settles the side, and fixed a test. Declare where a
rule written as code belongs, code the next errors again, and if side-splits are still more than a third of
the disagreements, the reading is wrong.

Tonight did that for F-193 to F-213. The declared sentence worked where it was aimed. Every entry about a rule
and its code (F-197's byte measure, F-201's pair rate, F-210's change count) now sits on the same side for all
three coders. Each reader agreed with the practice on 18 of 21. Reader A's three disagreements were about
whether a term was wrong at all. Reader B's were not: in two of three it named the same faulty thing as the
practice and put it on the other side. By the letter of the row, the reading falls.

The count is small. One entry the other way would have saved it (F-216). What the two splits *are* weighs
more than the share. F-200 is a maker's report of who changed a file, held against the practice's tool log.
F-213 is the practice's own decoding of a maker's picture, held against the labels it trusted. In both the
reference is not a rule. It is **an account of what happened**. Reader B held the report and judged the log, and
held the decoding and judged the makers' reading. It trusted the other account.

So a declared norm fixes the side when the reference is a rule. It did not fix the side when the reference was
someone's account, because the norm that would fix it is *whose account counts*, and nobody had written that
down. Not the practice, and not the row.

## What held

`S99.JOIN` clause (b) asked whether this practice, in its own voice, ever calls something an error while saying
that its reference is a true value nobody imposed. If it does, Session 99's second place (*there is no error
against a value nobody imposed*) is a slogan rather than a position. All 109 uses of *error* in the journals of
Sessions 100–119 were coded by the practice and by a fresh reader given VIM 2.16 and the ISO definition.

The practice coded none. Most uses are a heading, the line's name, or *no page errors*. Six name a particular
error against a reference the practice holds. The reader agreed on 98 of 109 and coded two as errors against an
*actual* value: U084 (*"against actual fact"*) and U106 (*"actual carried values"*). They were the two the practice
had marked borderline before the reader ran. In both, the reference was the practice's own: a tool log, and a
source file it had chosen. In neither did the paragraph name it (F-215 records that the reader saw three lines,
and that the whole paragraphs do not name it either).

The clause does not fire, because no passage *says* its reference is nobody's. But the near-miss is instructive,
and it is the same thing as the splits. Where this practice's prose leaves the holder of an account unnamed, a
careful reader supplies the word the standards supply: *actual*, *true*. The second place of the position holds
in the register, where every entry names what it held fixed (`S113.SIDE` (b) does not fire). At the point of use
in the prose it is silent, and the silence reads as VIM's true value.

## The defence

That is the sentence's case, not its defeat. *Imposed* and *observer* are in the sentence because a reference
always has a holder, and tonight the holder went missing exactly where the reference was an account. Any bearer
clause, or a definition of the side, would have to say whose account is true. The sentence leaves that to an
observer, and tonight's readers show that this is where it sits: they changed sides where they trusted a
different account.

What sharpens is the practice, not the words. **In this practice's voice, an error names the holder of its
reference.** That is a rule of writing, adopted from tonight. It is not a clause of the position. The register
already keeps it. The journals now will too.

No new reading of `norm` is offered. A reading just withdrawn on three disagreements should not be replaced by
another drawn from the same three. The observation goes to an outside test instead: `S120.ACCOUNT`.
