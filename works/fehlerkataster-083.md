## F-259 — a forecast that the told premise would be misused

**What happened.** P7 forecast (0.35) that at least one maker told the dates would put a calendar date on a feature
of the record and get it wrong by more than two days. One maker dated four drops: *"around 1–3 August, about 20
August, about 27 August and about 8 September"*. The record's daily means fall on 1–4 August, 20 August, 25–28 August
and 7–8 September (`materials/holds.json`). Another wrote *"15 July 2025 is a Tuesday"*, which is true. Every
derivation made from the told sentence checks out.

**Whose error.** The practice's, in its forecast. It looked for its error line's favourite object, a supplied premise
that turns into an error, and wrote a prediction for it. The makers did the arithmetic right.

**What it does to the record.** P7 failed. The line's question about supplied premises does not need them to fail
before it is worth asking: tonight the seam is invisible in the makers' words although nothing went wrong.

## F-260 — autumn, from nobody

**What happened.** Six makers told nothing about when each supplied a season anyway. Five put the record in
**autumn** (*"a northern-hemisphere autumn, probably September–October"*, *"in autumn"*, *"over two autumn months"*);
one said *"late summer into autumn"*. The record runs from 15 July to 13 September, high and late summer at 33° N.

**Whose error.** The makers'. By the standing sentence it is an error in full: a difference (a fall of about ten
steps over 61 days) onto which an observer imposed a norm (*a fall like this is the year cooling into autumn*). Nobody
supplied that norm. No brief said it. It is the family's default, and it is the first error in experiments 20 to 24
whose holder is also its only author. The fall was weather: a soil at 5 cm cooling in steps after rain, inside a
summer.

**What it does to the record.** Counted after reading and kept in `seasons.json`, labelled post hoc. It gives
`S135.DEFAULT` its premise. Session 134's rule of voice, that an error names who supplied its norm, has a case where
the answer is "no one outside the holder".

## F-261 — degrees Celsius, and a coast from it

**What happened.** The record is in whole **°F** (72 to 89). Six makers wrote that one step is most likely or perhaps
1 °C (m02, m03, m05, m07, m08, m12). Two more reasoned *"if one step is 1 °C"* (m06, m10). One allowed °F (m04).
Read as whole degrees of air, a daily swing of 3 to 5 steps looked small, and seven makers placed the record somewhere
cool, cloudy, coastal or maritime (m02, m03, m05, m06, m10, m11, m12). Starkville is inland, in Mississippi.

**Whose error.** The makers', on a default unit, and then on a place derived from it. Like F-260, it is a norm the
makers carried in, not one they were given. In this case the derivation went wrong *because* the premise was the
maker's own.

**What it does to the record.** Not scored by any row. Noted beside F-260. Where nothing is supplied, the family
derives from its own defaults as fluently as it derives from a told sentence, and the reports read the same.

## F-262 — `names_soil` counted a mention, the forecast meant a favour

**What happened.** P6's second clause forecast that at most one maker would name soil. `score.py` counted a maker as
naming soil if any `what` claim contained "soil", "ground" or "earth". Coder H listed claims that the makers set
aside as well as claims they favoured. Six makers named soil. Two of them named it only to reject it (*"too large for
water or soil temperature"*), and three favoured a buffered medium with soil among water or a sheltered room.

**Whose error.** The practice's. The rule was written before the makers ran, and it measured mention where the
forecast was about belief. It is the same kind of slip as F-245 and F-250: a counting rule finished before its object
was imagined.

**What it does to the record.** P6 failed by its letter and is booked so. Counted for favour, no maker favoured soil
alone.

## F-263 — a coder blind to the cells who could see them

**What happened.** Coder H was not told which makers were told the dates. It identified all six correctly in its own
report (*"Six makers mention the dates: m03, m04, m06, m08, m10 and m11"*), because the coded content was the cell.

**Whose error.** The practice's, in the design, and it is not avoidable here. A coder shown the told sentence and asked
for derivations from it cannot stay blind to who was told. What its blindness protected tonight was only the
standing code. For the N makers it coded the seasons as `from_when` `no`, which is right.

**What it does to the record.** The row's count (0 of 6 held apart) does not depend on blindness to cells: `given`
needs words the maker wrote. Kept as a limit on what "blind" means in this family of designs.
