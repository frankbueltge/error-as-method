# Wikipedia as it is — v3

## Wall text

Twelve articles, drawn at random by a computer from the 6.4 million in English Wikipedia (November 2023). Nobody picked them, so nobody made them interesting. This is what most of Wikipedia is: short articles about specific, small things. Seven of the twelve run under 300 words; one is a single sentence. Four are biographies, all of Americans, and five of the twelve subjects are American. There is a village in Iran, a monorail station in Japan, a frog, a drug that was never sold. Nothing famous. The great articles exist, but they are rare; a random draw almost never finds them. Twelve is few: another draw would bring different subjects, and perhaps films or albums, which this one missed.

## Captions

**g. Katano Station** — typical
One sentence on a monorail station in Kitakyushu, Japan. Its name dropped out when the text was copied for this show. 40 words.

**f. Rudbari Konar** — typical
A village of 234 people in northern Iran, described from census figures. Places this small make up a large share of Wikipedia. 48 words.

**k. Chlormadinone caproate** — typical
A contraceptive hormone that was tested but never sold. Wikipedia holds many narrow technical entries like this. 84 words.

**j. Kelly Stephens-Tysland** — typical
An American ice hockey player, Olympic bronze medallist in 2006. A short sports biography, among the commonest kinds of article. 109 words.

**l. Tambuco** — typical
A Mexican percussion ensemble founded in 1993, three times nominated for awards. 181 words.

**d. Lao Krang** — typical
A sub-group of the Lao people. The empty brackets held words in other scripts, lost when the text was copied for this show. 268 words.

**h. Kaja Kallas's second cabinet** — typical
An Estonian government that lasted nine months, from 2022 to 2023. Governments and cabinets each get a page of their own. 293 words.

**c. Southern pastel frog** — typical
A frog found only on Hispaniola. Wikipedia aims to cover every known species; most such articles are short, like this one. 388 words.

**e. Donovan Wilson (American football)** — typical
A Dallas Cowboys safety. Professional athletes are among Wikipedia's most common subjects; the bracket separates him from namesakes. 722 words.

**i. 300-win club** — unusual
The twenty-four baseball pitchers with 300 wins. A list rather than a single subject; list-like articles are a small share of the whole. 746 words.

**a. Oscar C. Badger II** — typical
A US admiral of both World Wars and Medal of Honor recipient. Military biographies are one of Wikipedia's largest families. 890 words.

**b. Thomas Hinds** — typical
A Mississippi soldier and congressman, born 1780. US members of Congress are covered almost without exception. The longest here: 1,371 words.

## Showing order

g, f, k, j, l, d, h, c, e, i, a, b

Shortest to longest: the visitor walks from a single sentence to a full biography, and sees most of the wall taken up by short pieces.

## Judgements

**Representativeness:** 4 of 5. The draw was random, so it is fair: its mix of biographies, places, species, sport and stubs is the kind a visitor would meet anywhere in Wikipedia. It loses a point because twelve is few: whole large families, such as films, albums and companies, happened not to be drawn.

**Replacements:** None. Swapping any article would turn a random sample into a curator's selection, which is exactly what this exhibition exists to avoid. What is missing belongs in the wall text, not on the wall.
