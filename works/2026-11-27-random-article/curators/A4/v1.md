# Wikipedia as it is — v1

## Wall text

Twelve articles, drawn at random by a computer from the 6.4 million in English Wikipedia (November 2023). Nobody picked them, so nobody made them interesting. What you see is what most of Wikipedia is: short articles about specific, small things. Half are under 300 words; two are a single sentence or two. A third are biographies, mostly of Americans — soldiers, a politician, athletes. There is a village in Iran, a monorail station in Japan, a frog, a drug that never reached the market. Nothing famous. The encyclopedia's great articles exist, but they are rare; a random draw almost never finds them.

## Captions

**a. Oscar C. Badger II** — typical
A US admiral of two World Wars. Military biographies, often American, are one of Wikipedia's largest families. 890 words.

**b. Thomas Hinds** — typical
A Mississippi soldier and congressman, born 1780. Every member of the US Congress has an article. At 1,371 words, the longest here.

**c. Southern pastel frog** — typical
A frog found only on Hispaniola. Wikipedia aims to cover every known species; most such articles are short, like this one.

**d. Lao Krang** — typical
A sub-group of the Lao people. The empty brackets are where non-Latin script failed to survive copying. 268 words.

**e. Donovan Wilson (American football)** — typical
A Dallas Cowboys safety. Professional athletes are among the most written-about living people; the bracket tells him apart from namesakes.

**f. Rudbari Konar** — typical
A village of 234 people in northern Iran. Tens of thousands of such articles were generated from census tables. 48 words.

**g. Katano Station** — typical
One sentence on a monorail station in Kitakyushu. Its name is missing from the first line, lost with its Japanese script. 40 words.

**h. Kaja Kallas's second cabinet** — typical
An Estonian government that lasted nine months. Wikipedia records current affairs quickly; each cabinet gets its own page.

**i. 300-win club** — unusual
Twenty-four baseball pitchers with 300 wins. A list rather than a single subject; list-like articles are a small share of the whole.

**j. Kelly Stephens-Tysland** — typical
An American ice hockey player, Olympic bronze medallist in 2006. Olympians are almost automatically notable. 109 words.

**k. Chlormadinone caproate** — typical
A contraceptive hormone that was tested but never sold. Wikipedia is full of narrow technical entries like this. 84 words.

**l. Tambuco** — typical
A Mexican percussion ensemble founded in 1993, three times nominated for awards. 181 words.

## Judgements

**Replacements:** None. These twelve were drawn at random; replacing any would turn a sample into a selection, which is what the exhibition exists to avoid.
