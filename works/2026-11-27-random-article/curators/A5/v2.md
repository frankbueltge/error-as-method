# Wikipedia as it is — v2

## Wall text

A computer drew these twelve at random from all 6.4 million articles of English Wikipedia (November 2023). Nobody chose them. They hang from shortest to longest.

Most are short and plain: a Polish village, a Japanese monorail station, a Mongolian mine, a 1945 film, a footballer. Places, people and works, stated in a few facts. That is the bulk of Wikipedia. Famous subjects are missing because they are a tiny share of the whole.

Notice, too, that every person here is a man, and that one list of manga characters is longer than the other eleven together. Wikipedia's coverage, and its effort, are spread very unevenly.

Twelve is a small sample: read it as a glimpse, not a census.

## Captions

- **a. Zimna Wódka** — A village in south-western Poland, located by distance from nearby towns. 65 words. *typical*
- **b. Katano Station** — One sentence on a monorail stop in Kitakyushu, Japan. Stations are a large family of short articles. *typical*
- **c. I Am Love (The Jackson 5 song)** — A 1975 single, its album and its chart peak. Popular music is heavily covered. *typical*
- **d. Arson Squad** — A 1945 American crime film, named with its director and writer, in 150 words. *typical*
- **e. Ongilog Lake mine** — A Mongolian phosphate deposit, given as tonnage and ore grade. At 39 words, the shortest here. *typical*
- **f. Croaker, Virginia** — An unincorporated community on the York River. Even very small American places have articles. *typical*
- **g. Masaichi Kondō** — A Japanese naval fighter ace of the Second World War, traced through his air groups. *typical*
- **h. Thomas Hinds** — A Mississippi soldier and congressman. At 1,371 words, fuller than most articles, as politicians' biographies often are. *typical*
- **i. List of Monster Musume characters** — The cast of a manga series, in 13,359 words: far longer than almost any article. Fan devotion made it. *unusual*
- **j. Exiles Bookshop** — A Sydney bookshop, 1979–1982, that hosted the city's poetry readings. A small local story, recorded. *typical*
- **k. Johannes Flum** — A former German professional footballer. Athletes are among Wikipedia's commonest biographies. *typical*
- **l. W. Wangyuh** — A member of India's 14th Lok Sabha. National legislators are covered as a set. *typical*

## Showing order

e, b, a, g, k, d, j, l, c, f, h, i

(Shortest to longest, so the wall itself shows how length is distributed.)

## Judgements

**How well these twelve represent English Wikipedia:** 4 of 5. The method is the right one — a random draw is unbiased — but twelve articles cannot show proportions reliably; whole kinds of article (science, species, women's biographies) are absent by chance.

**Replacements:** None. Swapping any of them for an article I consider more typical would put my expectations in place of the draw, which is the bias the draw exists to avoid. The gaps belong on the wall text, not on the wall.
