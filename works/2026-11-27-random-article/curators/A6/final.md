# Wikipedia as it is — v3

## Wall text

A computer drew these twelve at random from 6.4 million English Wikipedia articles. Nobody chose them. Most are short: the middle one runs about 240 words, and several are stubs. Half are biographies, mostly of minor figures. Pop culture is everywhere; places are small, a stream and a mountain lake. They hang from shortest to longest, and the last, a list of manga characters, holds four words in five on this wall. Twelve is a small handful. That there is no science here, and no woman, is chance, not proof. What it can show: Wikipedia is mostly short articles on modest subjects, with a few giants.

## Captions

Tag key: *typical* or *unusual* for English Wikipedia, judged by length and kind of subject together.

**l. List of presidents of the Legislative Council of Barbados** — unusual
Just 50 words, the opening only repeating the title. Lists usually run long; this frame was barely filled.

**g. Skalnaté Pleso (lake)** — typical
A Slovak mountain lake in 85 words. A stub, the commonest kind of article: a start waiting for more.

**i. Masaichi Kondō** — typical
A Japanese naval fighter ace of the Second World War. His name dropped out when the text was extracted, leaving "(born August 1917)".

**d. Grana del Monferrato** — typical
A small Italian stream feeding the Po. Rivers, villages and hills fill a large share of the encyclopedia.

**f. Puso ng Pasko: Artista Challenge** — typical
A 2010 Philippine Christmas reality show. The start of its first sentence was lost in extraction.

**h. W. Wangyuh** — typical
A member of India's 14th Lok Sabha, its lower house of parliament. Wikipedia treats every national legislator as worth an article.

**c. I Am Love (The Jackson 5 song)** — typical
The Jackson 5's last Top 20 hit. Wikipedia covers popular music single by single.

**j. Charles Ferdinand Bentinck** — typical
An Anglo-Dutch officer who governed Suriname, 1809–1811. Colonial administrators fill many short biographies.

**k. Tom Allom** — typical
The English producer behind records by Judas Priest and Def Leppard. Music's backroom figures get pages too.

**a. Pushkar Sahu** — typical
An Indian television writer of sitcoms. A working professional with a modest article.

**b. Gerald Murnane** — typical
An Australian novelist, "the greatest living English-language writer most people have never heard of". Longer than most; the nearest this wall comes to fame.

**e. List of Monster Musume characters** — unusual
Over 13,000 words on one manga's cast: nearly four times the other eleven combined. Very few articles grow this long.

## Showing order

l, g, i, d, f, h, c, j, k, a, b, e

Shortest to longest, so the walk itself shows the shape: many small articles, then one giant.

## Judgements

**Representation: 3 / 5.** The draw is fair, so nothing here is skewed by anyone's taste, and the shape (short articles, many biographies, one huge outlier) is true to Wikipedia. But twelve is too few to show its mix of subjects: science, women and many regions are missing by chance.

**Replacements: none.** Any swap would put my judgement where chance should be, and the wall's point is that nobody chose. The gaps are better named on the wall, as above, than filled.
