# Wikipedia as it is — v3

## Wall text

These twelve articles were drawn at random by computer from the 6.4 million in English Wikipedia (November 2023). Nobody chose them. They hang from shortest to longest.

Most of English Wikipedia is short: eight of these run under 300 words. Most of it is about the little-known: a Slovak lake, an Italian stream, an Indian MP, a Philippine reality show. Half are about people, few of them famous.

At the far end hangs the exception: 13,000 words on the characters of one manga. Length follows the enthusiasm of writers, not the importance of subjects.

Twelve is a small handful. By chance it misses villages, species, disambiguation pages and sports results, all common.

## Judgements

**How well these twelve represent English Wikipedia:** 3 / 5.

The lengths are about right: the twelve have a median near 240 words against about 210 for the 160, with one extreme outlier, as the 160 have. The mix of subjects is not: people are overdrawn and several common kinds are absent. That is what a draw of twelve does; it is not anyone's bias.

**Replacements, if allowed:**
- **i (Masaichi Kondō)** → a village or commune stub of about 50 words. Settlements are about one in eight of the 160; the wall has none.
- **j (Charles Ferdinand Bentinck)** → a disambiguation page ("X may refer to:"). About one in eleven of the 160, and central to how Wikipedia is navigated; none here.
- **k (Tom Allom)** → a species stub, such as a moth. Seven of the 160; none here.

Biographies are the ones to give up: six of twelve, against about one in five of the 160. But a wall rebalanced by hand is no longer a random draw, and the wall text would have to say so.
