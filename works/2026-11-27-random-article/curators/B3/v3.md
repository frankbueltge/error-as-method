# Wikipedia as it is — v3

## Wall text

A computer picked these twelve pages from all 6.4 million; nobody chose them. They show what English Wikipedia is mostly made of: short pages on specific, little-known things. Half run under 250 words. None of the subjects is famous. A village in Burma, a monorail stop in Japan, an English district abolished in 1974: each has a page simply because it exists. Three pages are mainly signposts to others sharing a name. Chance distorts too: signposts are really closer to one page in ten, and sport, about one page in eight, is missing entirely. The pages hang from shortest to longest.

## Captions

**f. Tom Allom** (330 words) — `typical`
English record producer for Judas Priest and Def Leppard. Biographies are about a fifth of Wikipedia; this one is ordinary in length and fame.

## Judgements

**How well these twelve represent English Wikipedia:** 3 of 5.

Lengths match the 160 closely (median about 245 words here, about 210 there), and the spread of places, people, media and stubs is right. But signpost pages (disambiguations and surname lists) are three of twelve here against about one in eleven in the 160, and there is no sport (about one page in eight in the 160) and no plant or animal species (about one in twenty). Twelve random pages will always miss something; the gap here is a big one. A deviation this size is not suspicious for a sample of twelve, but it is visible on a wall.

**What I would replace:**
- **i. Monteils** → a sports page, such as a minor athlete's biography or a club's season. Sport is the largest category with nothing on the wall, and Monteils is the thinnest of three signposts, so the swap corrects both distortions at once.

Only one swap: the wall's claim that nobody chose these pages is its main credential, and every replacement weakens it. If Monteils were swapped, the wall text would have to say so.
