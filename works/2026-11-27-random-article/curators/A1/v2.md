# Wikipedia as it is — v2

## Wall text

These twelve articles were drawn at random by a computer from the 6.4 million in English Wikipedia (November 2023). Nobody chose them, and that is the point. What you see is not the Wikipedia of famous people, wars and science, but the ordinary one: a Spanish village of 61 people, a lone Faroese swimmer, a Netflix documentary, a football player, a page that only points to other pages. Four are about people. None runs past 900 words; three are under 60. Some are rough: one opening sentence is missing a verb. They hang from shortest to longest.

## Captions

- **g. Elizabeth Stevenson** (31 words) — Not about anyone: a signpost to several people who share a name. A page type that exists to help readers find the right article. — `unusual`
- **l. Faroe Islands at the 1996 Summer Paralympics** (50 words) — One swimmer, one entry. Pages like this come in series, one per country per Games. — `typical`
- **i. Villar del Pozo** (58 words) — A Spanish municipality of 61 people, in two sentences. Short place articles like this are a staple of Wikipedia. — `typical`
- **c. Border poll** (139 words) — A large idea in 139 words, with a grammatical slip in its opening. Big subjects can get small articles. — `typical`
- **k. Pelé (2021 film)** (224 words) — A Netflix documentary about the footballer. Films, including recent streaming releases, routinely get their own pages. — `typical`
- **b. One World Media** (368 words) — A London media charity. Organisations fill many short, plain entries like this one. — `typical`
- **j. Pushkar Sahu** (474 words) — An Indian television writer, a living person known mainly to his industry and his viewers. — `typical`
- **e. Venus Envy (novel)** (588 words) — A 1993 novel by Rita Mae Brown, summed up in one opening sentence. Books, like films and albums, get pages of their own. — `typical`
- **h. Donovan Wilson (American football)** (722 words) — A Dallas Cowboys safety. Athletes are among the most frequent subjects of Wikipedia biographies. — `typical`
- **f. List of first minority male lawyers and judges in North Carolina** (823 words) — A list, not prose, on a very specific theme. Lists are a format of their own and a minority of pages. — `unusual`
- **a. Spotlight operator** (826 words) — The technician who steers the followspot in a theatre. A working trade, given a full article. — `typical`
- **d. Oscar C. Badger II** (890 words) — A US admiral who served in both World Wars and received the Medal of Honor. Military biography is a deep seam. — `typical`

## Showing order

g, l, i, c, k, b, j, e, h, f, a, d

Shortest to longest, by word count. The wall opens with the brief pages most visitors never notice and ends with the fullest of the twelve, which is still under 900 words.

## Judgements

**Representativeness: 4 / 5.** A random draw is the fairest picture available: it has no favourites. The limit is size. Twelve pages cannot show everything Wikipedia holds; there is no science, medicine or long, heavily edited article here, and the absence is itself honest about how rare such pages are.

**Replacements: None.** Any swap would turn a random sample into a chosen one, and bring back exactly the curatorial bias this exhibition sets out to avoid.
