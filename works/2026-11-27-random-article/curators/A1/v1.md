# Wikipedia as it is — v1

## Wall text

These twelve articles were drawn at random by a computer from the 6.4 million in English Wikipedia (November 2023). Nobody chose them. That is the point. Wikipedia is not mostly great men, wars and science. It is a sheriff's ledger of the world: a Spanish village of 61 people, a lone Faroese swimmer, a Netflix documentary, an American football safety, a page that only points to other pages. None runs past 900 words; three are under 60. Some are rough: one opening sentence is missing a verb. Look for what is common here, not what is famous.

## Captions

- **a. Spotlight operator** (826 words) — The person who steers the followspot. A working trade, explained in detail by someone who knows it. — `typical`
- **b. One World Media** (368 words) — A London media charity. Organisations like this fill many thousands of short, plain entries. — `typical`
- **c. Border poll** (139 words) — A large idea in 139 words, with a grammatical slip in the opening. Big subjects can get small articles. — `typical`
- **d. Oscar C. Badger II** (890 words) — A US admiral and Medal of Honor recipient. Military biography is one of Wikipedia's deepest seams. — `typical`
- **e. Venus Envy (novel)** (588 words) — A 1993 novel by Rita Mae Brown. Books, films and albums each get their own page. — `typical`
- **f. List of first minority male lawyers and judges in North Carolina** (823 words) — A list, not prose. Lists are a format of their own, and this one is very specific. — `unusual`
- **g. Elizabeth Stevenson** (31 words) — Not an article about anyone: a signpost to several people sharing a name. — `unusual`
- **h. Donovan Wilson (American football)** (722 words) — A current NFL player. Sportspeople are among the most numerous subjects on Wikipedia. — `typical`
- **i. Villar del Pozo** (58 words) — A Spanish municipality of 61 people. Nearly every place on the map has a stub like this. — `typical`
- **j. Pushkar Sahu** (474 words) — An Indian television writer. A living person known mainly to his industry and audience. — `typical`
- **k. Pelé (2021 film)** (224 words) — A Netflix documentary about the footballer. Recent media often gets an article quickly. — `typical`
- **l. Faroe Islands at the 1996 Summer Paralympics** (50 words) — One swimmer, one entry. Whole series of pages exist, one per country per Games. — `typical`

## Showing order

i, l, g, c, k, b, j, e, h, a, f, d

(From shortest-feeling to longest: the wall opens with the stubs most visitors never see and ends with the fullest pages.)

## Judgements

**Representativeness: 4 / 5.** A random draw is the fairest picture there is; the limit is size. Twelve cannot show the rare long, heavily edited articles, or every subject area (no science or medicine here).

**Replacements: None.** Any swap would turn a random sample into a chosen one, which is exactly what the exhibition is trying to avoid.
