# Wikipedia as it is — v1

## Wall text

Twelve articles, picked by a computer at random from 6.4 million. Nobody chose them for interest. Most of English Wikipedia is not famous things: it is a village in Iran, a monorail stop in Japan, a frog found on one mountain range, a hockey player with one Olympic medal. Many articles are a few sentences long; half of all articles are shorter than this text and its captions combined. People, places, species and sport fill much of it. The wall runs from the shortest article to the longest, so walking along it you move from stub to substance. Only a few articles, like the congressman at the end, are as long as the ones you usually read.

## Captions

**a. Oscar C. Badger II** (890 words) — `typical`
A US admiral with a Medal of Honor. Military biographies are a large, carefully maintained family on Wikipedia; this one is longer than most.

**b. Thomas Hinds** (1371 words) — `unusual`
An early Mississippi congressman. The subject is ordinary for Wikipedia; the length is not. Among the 160 articles this sample came from, few run longer.

**c. Southern pastel frog** (388 words) — `typical`
One frog species of one island. Wikipedia holds hundreds of thousands of species articles, often written from the same few scientific sources.

**d. Lao Krang** (268 words) — `unusual`
A sub-group of the Lao people. Articles on ethnic groups exist in numbers, but nothing like it appeared among the other 159.

**e. Donovan Wilson (American football)** (722 words) — `typical`
A current NFL safety. Professional athletes are among Wikipedia's most common subjects; the bracketed title shows another Donovan Wilson already exists.

**f. Rudbari Konar** (48 words) — `typical`
An Iranian village of 234 people, described in two sentences from a 2006 census. Thousands of such articles were made from the same template.

**g. Katano Station** (40 words) — `typical`
A monorail station, in one sentence. The shortest on this wall, and a fair picture of how brief a great many articles are.

**h. Kaja Kallas's second cabinet** (293 words) — `unusual`
Estonia's government for nine months in 2022–23. A current-affairs article about a recent event; nothing similar appeared in the wider sample.

**i. 300-win club** (746 words) — `typical`
Baseball pitchers who won 300 games: essentially a list. Sport and lists are both large parts of Wikipedia, and here they meet.

**j. Kelly Stephens-Tysland** (109 words) — `typical`
An ice hockey player with a 2006 Olympic bronze. Olympians get articles almost automatically, so short ones like this are very common.

**k. Chlormadinone caproate** (84 words) — `unusual`
A contraceptive drug that was tested but never sold. Specialist pharmacology, written for experts; the only article of its kind in the sample.

**l. Tambuco** (181 words) — `typical`
A Mexican percussion ensemble founded in 1993. Short articles about musicians and bands, outside the English-speaking world too, are everywhere.

## Judgements

**How well these twelve represent English Wikipedia:** 3 of 5.

Their lengths match the 160 well (three under 100 words; middle article around 280 words against about 210 in the 160). Their subjects do not quite: five of twelve are individual people, roughly double the share in the 160, and five are American. Nothing here is a film, album, song, book or TV show, though such articles make up about one in eight of the 160; and there is no disambiguation page ("X may refer to…"), about one in eleven of the 160.

**What I would replace, if allowed:**

- **a. Oscar C. Badger II** → a **disambiguation page**. Wikipedia is full of these navigation pages and the wall has none; Badger is one of five American biographies.
- **e. Donovan Wilson** → a **short article on an album or film**. Popular culture is a major share of Wikipedia and absent here; Wilson is a third sports item and another American person.
