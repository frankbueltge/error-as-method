# Wikipedia as it is — v3

## Wall text

A computer picked these twelve articles at random from the 6.4 million in English Wikipedia. Nobody chose them for interest. They show what the encyclopedia is mostly made of: short entries on an Austrian village, one college football season, a wartime pilot, a small island, a 1953 East German comedy. Seven of the twelve run under 200 words, as roughly half of all articles do. Famous subjects are rare. The articles hang from shortest to longest, so you walk along the encyclopedia's range of size, from a one-sentence municipality to the life of an Australian novelist. The long ones are the exceptions. Absent by the luck of the draw: a disambiguation page ("may refer to…") and a moth or snail.

## Captions

**a. Kamarupa inscriptions** (178 words) — *typical*
Sanskrit inscriptions from early medieval north-east India, covered in a few short paragraphs. Specialist subjects often get only this much.

**b. Xavier Ramonède** (605 words) — *typical*
A working French animator. Wikipedia holds many biographies of people known within their profession rather than to the public.

**c. 1974 Virginia Cavaliers football team** (63 words) — *typical*
One season of one college team, in a few sentences. Sports seasons are among Wikipedia's most numerous kinds of article.

**d. Langlee Island** (189 words) — *typical*
A small island in Boston Harbor: its size, its rock, its height. Geography is one of the encyclopedia's largest territories.

**e. Jonas Pate** (424 words) — *typical*
An American screenwriter and director. People from film and television are covered in great numbers.

**f. 2016 United States Senate election in North Dakota** (177 words) — *typical*
One of hundreds of election articles that follow the same pattern, state by state and year by year.

**g. Masaichi Kondō** (127 words) — *typical*
A Japanese navy fighter ace of the Second World War. Short military biographies, close to a service record, are common.

**h. Gerald Murnane** (1349 words) — *unusual*
An acclaimed Australian novelist, with a full article. Few subjects are this well known, and fewer than one article in ten is this long.

**i. ISO 3166-2:JP** (156 words) — *unusual*
Not really prose but a reference table: official codes for Japan's prefectures, one page in a series covering every country.

**j. Røyken Upper Secondary School** (1102 words) — *unusual*
A Norwegian secondary school given more words than nine articles in ten. On Wikipedia, length follows editors' attention, not importance.

**k. Wallern an der Trattnach** (40 words) — *typical*
An Austrian municipality in a single sentence. Stubs like this, on villages and small towns worldwide, are everywhere in Wikipedia.

**l. Swings or Roundabouts** (390 words) — *typical*
A 1953 East German comedy film. Little-known national cinema, recorded in English, fills a large, quiet corner of Wikipedia.

## Judgements

**Representativeness:** 4 out of 5. The mix of subjects and the spread of lengths match the 160 they were drawn from fairly closely. Very short stubs (under 100 words) are a little scarce here, two of twelve against nearly three in ten in the pool, and twelve is too few to catch every common kind of page.

**Replacements:** None. Swapping any article would turn a random sample into a selection, which is exactly what the exhibition sets out not to be. The gaps (no disambiguation page, no species stub) are named on the wall text instead.
