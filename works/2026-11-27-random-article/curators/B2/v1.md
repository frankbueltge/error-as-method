# Wikipedia as it is — v1

## Wall text

These twelve articles were picked by a computer, at random, from the 6.4 million in English Wikipedia. Nobody chose them for interest. What you see is what the encyclopedia mostly is: short entries on a village in Austria, a college football season, a Second World War pilot, an island in Boston Harbor, a 1953 East German comedy. Half are under 200 words. People and places dominate; famous subjects are rare. The articles hang from shortest to longest, so you can walk along the encyclopedia's size: from a two-sentence municipality to an essay-length life of an Australian novelist. The long ones are the exceptions.

## Captions

**a. Kamarupa inscriptions** (178 words) — *typical*
Ancient Sanskrit inscriptions from north-east India in a few paragraphs. Scholarly subjects often get only this much.

**b. Xavier Ramonède** (605 words) — *typical*
A working French animator. Wikipedia holds many biographies of people known within their profession rather than to the public.

**c. 1974 Virginia Cavaliers football team** (63 words) — *typical*
One season of one college team, in three sentences. Sport seasons are among Wikipedia's most numerous article types.

**d. Langlee Island** (189 words) — *typical*
A small island in Boston Harbor: size, rock, height. Geography is one of the encyclopedia's largest territories.

**e. Jonas Pate** (424 words) — *typical*
An American screenwriter and director. Film and television people are heavily covered, often through lists of credits.

**f. 2016 United States Senate election in North Dakota** (177 words) — *typical*
One of hundreds of near-identical election articles, built from a template and mostly results tables.

**g. Masaichi Kondō** (127 words) — *typical*
A Japanese navy fighter ace. Military biographies, short and service-record-like, are a common kind of entry.

**h. Gerald Murnane** (1349 words) — *unusual*
An acclaimed Australian novelist with a long, developed article. Fewer than one article in ten is this long.

**i. ISO 3166-2:JP** (156 words) — *unusual*
Not prose but a reference table: official codes for Japan's prefectures. One of a systematic series covering every country.

**j. Røyken Upper Secondary School** (1102 words) — *unusual*
A Norwegian secondary school described at unusual length, probably by people close to it.

**k. Wallern an der Trattnach** (40 words) — *typical*
An Austrian municipality in one sentence. Tens of thousands of such place stubs exist.

**l. Swings or Roundabouts** (390 words) — *typical*
A 1953 East German comedy film. Obscure national cinema, recorded in English, is a large quiet corner of Wikipedia.

## Judgements

**Representativeness:** 4 out of 5.

**Replacements:** None. Swapping any article would turn a random sample into a selection, which is the thing the exhibition sets out not to be. The twelve do miss two common kinds of page — a disambiguation page ("X may refer to:") and a species stub — but that is what a sample of twelve does.
