# Wikipedia as it is — v2

## Wall text

These twelve articles were drawn at random from English Wikipedia. Nobody chose them.

Most of the encyclopedia is small. Half of these run under 160 words: a Polish village, a monorail station, a Mongolian mine, a 1945 crime film. They are not the famous pages people read, but the bulk of what is there: places, people, songs, films, each recorded in a few lines. A few articles are long, and one is vast, a list of manga characters four times longer than the other eleven together.

The four people here are all men, as most of Wikipedia's biographies are. Missing by chance: plants and animals, and the pages that only point to other articles sharing a name.

## Captions

Tags: *typical* means the kind and size of article you would most likely land on at random; *unusual* means not.

**a. Zimna Wódka** — typical
A village in south-western Poland, in 65 words: a name, a district, and the way to the nearest towns. Villages are among Wikipedia's commonest subjects.

**b. Katano Station** — typical
A monorail station in Kitakyushu, Japan, in one sentence. Around a quarter of English Wikipedia's articles are under 80 words.

**c. I Am Love (The Jackson 5 song)** — typical
A single song with its own article: the Jackson 5's last Top 20 hit, 1975. Songs and albums are everywhere on Wikipedia.

**d. Arson Squad** — typical
A 1945 American crime film in 150 words: director, writer, and little more. Films are catalogued title by title, minor ones included.

**e. Ongilog Lake mine** — typical
A phosphate mine in Mongolia, in 39 words. A record of a place and a number, and nothing else: the classic stub.

**f. Croaker, Virginia** — typical
An unincorporated community on the York River, in 461 words. Small American places are thoroughly covered.

**g. Masaichi Kondō** — typical
A Japanese naval fighter ace of the Second World War. Soldiers and officers of the world wars fill many of Wikipedia's biographies.

**h. Thomas Hinds** — unusual
A Mississippi soldier and congressman, 1780–1840. The subject is ordinary for Wikipedia; the length, 1,371 words, is not. About one article in twenty is longer.

**i. List of Monster Musume characters** — unusual
13,359 words on the characters of one manga series. The longest of the 160 articles these twelve were drawn from, by a factor of four.

**j. Exiles Bookshop** — typical
A Sydney bookshop and poetry venue, open 1979 to 1982. Wikipedia keeps many small, local, closed institutions like this one.

**k. Johannes Flum** — typical
A German footballer, born 1987, in 148 words. Professional athletes are one of the largest groups of people on Wikipedia.

**l. W. Wangyuh** — typical
W. Wangyuh Konyak, a member of India's 14th Lok Sabha. Politicians, like athletes, appear in large numbers and often briefly.

## Showing order

e, b, a, g, k, d, j, l, c, f, h, i

Shortest to longest, left to right. The wall itself becomes the chart: a visitor walks past a long run of short articles before reaching the two long ones, and the last is out of all proportion.

## Judgements

**How well these twelve represent English Wikipedia:** 4 out of 5. Lengths and the mix of subjects match the 160 closely (middle length here about 165 words; in the 160 about 210). What is missing is what twelve random draws can be expected to miss: no disambiguation pages (about one in eleven of the 160) and no species (about one in twenty).

**What I would replace:** None. Any swap would make a chosen wall out of a random one, and the exhibition's claim rests on nobody choosing. The gaps are better named in the wall text than filled.
