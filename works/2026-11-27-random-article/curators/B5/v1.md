# Wikipedia as it is — v1

## Wall text

Most of English Wikipedia is small. Of these twelve articles, drawn at random, half run under 160 words: a Polish village, a monorail station, a Mongolian mine, a 1945 crime film. They are not the famous pages people read; they are what the encyclopedia is mostly made of — places, people, songs, films, each recorded in a few lines. A few articles are long, and one is vast: a list of characters from a Japanese manga, longer than the other eleven together several times over. The four people on the wall are all men, and most subjects are American, European or Japanese. Nobody chose these twelve. What you see is what the encyclopedia looks like when you open it anywhere.

## Captions

**a. Zimna Wódka** — typical
A village in south-western Poland, in 65 words. Thousands of villages enter Wikipedia this way: a name, a district, and directions to the nearest towns.

**b. Katano Station** — typical
One sentence: a monorail station in Kitakyushu, Japan. Transport stops are a large family of very short articles.

**c. I Am Love (The Jackson 5 song)** — typical
A single song gets its own article: the Jackson 5's last Top 20 hit, 1975. Songs and albums are among Wikipedia's most common subjects.

**d. Arson Squad** — typical
A 1945 American crime film, in 150 words: director, writer, little else. Old films are catalogued title by title.

**e. Ongilog Lake mine** — typical
A phosphate mine in Mongolia, in 39 words. Short articles on individual mines, often written in batches, are common.

**f. Croaker, Virginia** — typical
An unincorporated community on the York River. Small American places are well covered; this one gets 461 words.

**g. Masaichi Kondō** — typical
A Japanese naval fighter ace of the Second World War. Military biographies, especially of the world wars, are a large and devoted corner of Wikipedia.

**h. Thomas Hinds** — unusual
A Mississippi soldier and congressman, 1780–1840. His subject is ordinary for Wikipedia; his length, 1,371 words, is not. Few articles run this long.

**i. List of Monster Musume characters** — unusual
13,359 words on the characters of one manga series: the longest article in the 160 it was drawn from, and longer than all other eleven here combined.

**j. Exiles Bookshop** — typical
A Sydney bookshop and poetry venue, open 1979–1982. Wikipedia remembers many such small, local institutions, usually because someone cared to write them down.

**k. Johannes Flum** — typical
A German footballer, born 1987. Professional athletes are one of the largest groups of people on Wikipedia.

**l. W. Wangyuh** — typical
W. Wangyuh Konyak, member of India's 14th Lok Sabha. Every national legislator tends to get an article, however brief.

## Showing order

e, b, a, g, k, d, j, l, c, f, h, i

(Shortest to longest, left to right. The wall itself becomes a chart: the visitor walks past a long run of short articles before reaching the two long ones.)

## Judgements

**How well these twelve represent English Wikipedia:** 4 out of 5.

**What I would replace:** e (Ongilog Lake mine), with a disambiguation page — about one in eleven of the 160 are pages that only list other articles with the same name, and none came up here. And a, with a species article, since moths and snails appear often in the 160 and not at all on the wall.
