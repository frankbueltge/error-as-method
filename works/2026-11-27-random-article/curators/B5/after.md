# English Wikipedia as a whole: best estimates

Estimates are shares of all articles. Each one blends what I already believed with the counts in the 160-article pool, given in brackets.

1. About a person: **22%** (pool: about 35 of 160)
2. About a village, town, municipality or other settlement: **13%** (pool: about 22 of 160, counting settlements only, not rivers, lakes or mountains)
3. About a species or other taxon: **5%** (pool: 7 of 160, all animals; I expected a little more)
4. Disambiguation pages or lists: **12%** (pool: 14 disambiguation or name pages and about 6 lists, so 20 of 160)
5. Under 100 words long: **28%** (pool: 46 of 160)

Did these estimates play any part in the tags? Only loosely: a general sense that people, places, songs and films are common subjects stood behind tagging every subject on the wall "typical". The two "unusual" tags came from length alone (Hinds and the Monster Musume list are among the longest of the 160), not from any of these percentages.
