# Wikipedia as it is — v3

## 1. Wall text

Nobody chose these twelve. A computer drew them at random from the 6.4 million articles of English Wikipedia in November 2023.

They show what Wikipedia is mostly made of: short pages about specific, little-known things. A Burmese village, a Japanese monorail station, a record producer, an abolished Yorkshire district. Seven of twelve run under 300 words. Three are signposts, sorting pages that share a name. Only one explains a general idea.

They hang from shortest to longest. Notice how far you walk before anything resembles the encyclopedia entry you imagined.

Each caption is tagged typical or unusual for Wikipedia as a whole, not for this wall. Twelve is a small draw: chance can crowd in a rare kind of page.

## 2. Captions and tags

**a. Elizabeth Stevenson** (31 words) — `unusual`
A signpost page: several people share this name, and it points to each. It counts as an article, yet holds a single line.

**b. The Concise Guide to Sounding Smart at Parties** (205 words) — `typical`
A humorous trivia book by two Los Angeles screenwriters. Wikipedia records many modest books that most readers will never meet.

**c. Gwebin** (286 words) — `typical`
A village in Mogok Township, Burma. Places are one of Wikipedia's largest groups; many villages have a page and little more than a location.

**d. Mechanical joint** (569 words) — `typical`
The one page here that explains a general idea, in plain textbook prose. Ordinary on Wikipedia, though rarer than names, places and people.

**e. Oquendo** (99 words) — `unusual`
A surname of Basque origin, followed by a list of people who bear it. Another signpost page, sorting namesakes.

**f. Tom Allom** (330 words) — `typical`
An English record producer who worked with Judas Priest and Def Leppard. Biographies of people in music and film are everywhere on Wikipedia.

**g. List of first minority male lawyers and judges in North Carolina** (823 words) — `unusual`
The longest piece on the wall, and a list rather than prose: names and years of admission to the bar.

**h. Jonas Pate** (424 words) — `typical`
An American screenwriter and director. Another career in entertainment, told in a few hundred words.

**i. Monteils** (39 words) — `unusual`
Several French communes share this name. A signpost page: the article you want is one click further on.

**j. Katano Station** (40 words) — `typical`
A monorail station in Kitakyushu, Japan, in about forty words. Very short pages on single places like this are common.

**k. Máximo Macapobre** (366 words) — `typical`
A 19th-century founder of a town in Cebu, Philippines. "Little is known about his life" beyond stories kept by his descendants.

**l. Queensbury and Shelf Urban District** (124 words) — `typical`
A local government district in Yorkshire, 1937 to 1974. Wikipedia keeps pages for administrative units long after they are abolished.

## 4. Judgements

**How well these twelve represent English Wikipedia:** 4 out of 5.

A random draw is the fairest picture of Wikipedia that twelve pages can give: no curator's taste bent it toward the famous or the well written. What twelve cannot do is show rarer kinds of article, such as long, heavily edited pages on major subjects, which exist but make up a small share. I tagged four of the twelve unusual, mostly the three signpost pages, which I believe are a small minority of English Wikipedia. Drawing three in twelve is possible by chance, but it also suggests they are commoner than I assumed. A draw this size can only show the broad shape: short pages, specific subjects, many people and places.

**Which I would replace:** None. Swapping any of them would replace Wikipedia as it is with Wikipedia as I imagine it, which is the thing this exhibition exists to correct.
