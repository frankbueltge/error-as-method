# Wikipedia as it is — v1

## 1. Wall text

These twelve articles were not chosen. A computer drew them at random from the 6.4 million articles of English Wikipedia as it stood in November 2023. Nobody here picked the interesting ones.

What you see is what Wikipedia is mostly made of: short pages about specific things. A village in Burma, a monorail station in Japan, a record producer, a vanished Yorkshire district. Three are not articles in the usual sense but signposts, pointing readers to other pages that share a name. The longest is a list. Only one explains a general idea.

They hang from shortest to longest. Walk the wall and notice how far you get before anything resembles an encyclopedia entry you might have imagined.

## 2. Captions and tags

**a. Elizabeth Stevenson** (31 words) — `unusual`
A signpost, not an article: several people share this name. Pages like this help readers find their way. The shortest on the wall.

**b. The Concise Guide to Sounding Smart at Parties** (205 words) — `typical`
A humorous trivia book by two Los Angeles screenwriters. Wikipedia records many modest books that most readers will never meet.

**c. Gwebin** (286 words) — `typical`
A village in Mogok Township, Burma. Places are one of Wikipedia's largest groups; many villages have a page and little more than a location.

**d. Mechanical joint** (569 words) — `unusual`
The only article here that explains a general idea. This is the kind of page people picture when they think of an encyclopedia.

**e. Oquendo** (99 words) — `unusual`
A surname of Basque origin, followed by a list of people who bear it. Another signpost page, sorting namesakes.

**f. Tom Allom** (330 words) — `typical`
An English record producer who worked with Judas Priest and Def Leppard. Biographies of people in music and film are everywhere on Wikipedia.

**g. List of first minority male lawyers and judges in North Carolina** (823 words) — `unusual`
The longest piece on the wall, and a list rather than prose: names and years of admission to the bar.

**h. Jonas Pate** (424 words) — `typical`
An American screenwriter and director. Another career in entertainment, told in a few hundred words.

**i. Monteils** (39 words) — `unusual`
Several French communes share this name. A signpost page: the article you want is one click further on.

**j. Katano Station** (40 words) — `typical`
A monorail station in Kitakyushu, Japan, described in a single sentence. Very short pages like this are common.

**k. Máximo Macapobre** (366 words) — `typical`
A 19th-century Philippine community leader. "Little is known about his life" beyond stories kept by his descendants.

**l. Queensbury and Shelf Urban District** (124 words) — `typical`
A local government district in Yorkshire, 1937 to 1974. Wikipedia keeps pages for administrative units long after they are abolished.

## 4. Judgements

**How well these twelve represent English Wikipedia:** 4 out of 5.

A random draw is the fairest picture of Wikipedia that twelve pages can give: no curator's taste bent it toward the famous or the well written. What twelve cannot do is show rarer kinds of article, such as long, heavily edited pages on major subjects, which exist but make up a small share. Three signpost pages out of twelve is probably more than their real share, which is chance at this sample size.

**Which I would replace:** None. Swapping any of them would replace Wikipedia as it is with Wikipedia as I imagine it, which is the thing this exhibition exists to correct.
