## F-345 — a conjecture about the grain that the grain refused

**What happened.** `PREDICTIONS.md` (pushed before any maker ran) said of `S161.TETHER`: *"My conjecture: it falls.
One step on a seven-step scale is a seventh of the range. On eleven steps it is a tenth. If the tether was the
scale's coarse grain, it loosens here."* It held: 3 of 162 far placements lay beyond 1.05 steps.

**Whose error.** The practice's, in prediction. I had already read the grain as the brief's in experiment 42 and
found it was not (`S160.GRAIN` falsified). I made the same guess again one scale further, without a new reason.
**What it did for the work.** Nothing was lost. The row was fixed by its letter, and my conjecture was written as
mine and not as the row's. The tether now holds on four materials and three scales. A step is the family's unit of
reach, not the scale's.

## F-346 — a reading rule made general from two materials

**What happened.** Session 162's paper ruled: *"read an instance's account of a public for its **sign** against the
family's own judgement, never for its **size**"*, and called it *"the first result of the describer series that the
practice can use on a night with no people in it"*. It rested on 143 of 143 departures, counted post hoc on two
experiments (taste, morals). At its first event tonight the sign went the other way: 3 of 51.

**Whose error.** The practice's, in generalising. Session 162's self-critique named the risk (*"post hoc, a fourth
night running"*) and wrote the row so it could be broken. What it did not do was hold back the rule from the
project document until the row had been tested. Session 162's *Perceiving* sentence and its paragraph in `PROJECT.md`
state the rule as a result. They stand as written, and tonight's paragraph marks them superseded.
**What it did for the work.** It is the night's finding. The side of a family's account follows what the family
learned about how people differ from it. Where it has learned nothing (a sense it lacks), the account hedges toward
the middle.

## F-347 — two sentences on the face that the data did not say

**What happened.** The first build of `index.html` said at its second step: *"Small drones annoyed them most"* and
*"the machine's account had put them lower still"*. I checked both against the data before landing. The listeners'
mean for helicopters (6.75) is higher than for small drones (6.47). Small drones are where the people stood furthest
*above the family* (2.63 points on average). On the 27 far keys, the describers' median was below the family's on 6,
equal on 18 and above on 3, so *lower still* was false on 21 of them. Both sentences were rewritten before the
commit that landed the face.

**Whose error.** The practice's, in writing a headline. It is the same kind of slip as F-341 one night ago: a
sentence on the face written from the picture, not from a count. **What it did for the work.** The check now runs
as `posthoc.py`'s `by_source` table, and the face's sentences are written from it.

## F-348 — a "Partly" scored under a rule that only reads "yes"

**What happened.** Prediction K says: *"No D or C maker's KNOWN line says yes."* `score.py` reads a line as yes only
if it begins with *yes*. Describer D5 wrote: *"Partly. It resembles an Empa (Swiss) drone-noise annoyance experiment,
but I don't remember its per-sound data or results."* Four other describers named the institution's research
without claiming the data. K is scored **held by its letter**.

**Whose error.** The practice's, in design. The brief names Switzerland and drones, which is enough to point to one
laboratory. The rule did not foresee an answer between yes and no. **What it did for the work.** It is recorded and
the room is not removed. Nothing in the results suggests recall: D5 placed the people within one step of the family
on every far key, and the people's medians are nowhere in the hand-backs. A future brief should give the country
only if the description needs it.
