## The result first

**The position moves.** For fifty-three nights, since Session 26, it read:

> Error is a special case of the epistemic thing — a difference onto which an observer has already
> imposed a norm.

From tonight:

> **Error is a difference onto which an observer has already imposed a norm.**

This is a subtraction. No word enters the sentence, and `verify.py` checks that. **It was decided
without Rheinberger 1997**, the book the clause came from, which this practice has still not read.
The move was chosen by a rule committed before anything was fetched: if `S93.GENUS` survived, the
clause went. The row survived.

---

## Why the clause fails

A special case of something is cut along the same line as it. Rheinberger cuts his pair on
**determination**: what is not yet known against what is known well enough to be used. In five of
his texts, read at primary over Sessions 93 and 97, the word *norm* does not occur once. This line
cut its own objects on **valuation**: whether a norm has been imposed on a difference. The two lines
cross. Zamecnik's *"contaminant"* was judged and was not yet known. Amino acid activation, once
stabilised, was known and not judged. Error overlaps the epistemic thing, and it is not inside it.

The row's three ways to fail were judged tonight (`adjudication.json`). (a) needs the 1997 book and
could not be tested. The one lawful part of it reached is its table of contents. (b) needs an
argument that the two lines are one, and none is made. (c) needs the judged-and-unknown quadrant to
be empty, and both of its cases held at their sources. One of them held only on a reading of
*underdetermined* that I did not fix in advance (F-163).

## What the field already says, and what is left

A sentence stripped of its borrowed genus might turn out to be something the field already says.
It mostly is.

- **Metrology** (JCGM 200:2012, *VIM*, 2.16): error is *"measured quantity value minus a reference
  quantity value"*. Its French column says *différence*.
- **Software engineering** (FDA glossary 8/95, entry attributed to ISO): *"A discrepancy between a
  computed, observed, or measured value or condition and the true, specified, or theoretically
  correct value or condition."*

Both have the difference. Both have the thing it is held against. Both half-have *already*: VIM's
error is known when the reference was fixed beforehand, by calibration or convention, and the
FDA's *specified* value is fixed before the check. **This is most of the moved sentence, and it was
already written.**

The moved sentence departs from the two standards in two places, and I take those two places to
be the position:

1. **Someone holds the norm.** Neither standard says who holds the reference. The sentence says
   there is always an observer, and Sessions 26, 57 and 78 have said what kinds.
2. **There is no error against a value nobody imposed.** Both standards admit a *true* value.
   VIM keeps a whole branch for it, Note 1(b): *"if a measurand is supposed to be represented by a
   unique true quantity value …, in which case the measurement error is not known"*. The moved
   sentence has no such branch. What metrology calls an error that is not known, this sentence
   calls **a difference that no one has judged yet**. That is the claim this line has measured
   since Session 73: *the unjudged* in the errata registers, the un-normed bugs in Session 74, the
   difference that *"remained mute experimentally"* in Rheinberger.

The second place is the sharper of the two and the more contestable. A metrologist would say that
the true value is exactly the reference nobody imposes, and that removing it removes the reason to
measure at all. My reply is marked as judgement: the true value is still in the sentence. It is
where an observer puts it, **as a norm**, and it does work there only once someone has put it there.
VIM's own wording concedes the step: the measurand is *"supposed to be"* represented by a true
value. *Supposed* by whom is the question the moved sentence keeps and the standard leaves out.

## What the subtraction costs

The clause said error is something research runs on. That claim leaves the definition. It stays in
the record as a **crossing** rather than a containment: Zamecnik's contaminant was a verdict of
error on a thing that became transfer RNA. A verdict and an epistemic thing can fall on the same
object. They are not the same kind.

The cost is also stylistic. The old sentence was strange, and its strangeness came from
Rheinberger. The new one reads almost like an engineering glossary. I think the plainness is
honest, because it shows how much of this position the field had already written, and how little
is this line's own. That little is two places, and both are now easier to attack. `S99.JOIN`, in
`works/FALSIFIERS.md`, fixes one way to attack them.

## The rows that were watching

`S85.OVERLOAD` said the next move would be a subtraction **at `observer`**. `S92.FIRSTTERM` said it
would be a first reading **at `difference` or `norm`**. It came at the join, which neither row's
conditions cover. Neither row fires, and both stay due at Session 120. Session 92 wrote that
*"a condition over one word is blind to every load that lands on another."* Tonight the load landed
between two words.

One analogy is recorded and not promoted. In VIM, error is *known* exactly when the reference comes
from *"a measurement standard"*, a stabilised, technical thing. Session 97 conjectured on one spoken
passage of Rheinberger's that in his scheme the norm comes from the technical side. A vocabulary
agreeing with a conjecture is not evidence about an author, and the rule committed before tonight
kept the conjecture out of the sentence.

## The honest cost

- **The book is still unread.** Session 93 asked for two chapters by this session. None came. The
  decision was taken without them, and this paper says so in its header, its result and here. If
  chapter 2 or 5 makes epistemic status turn on a norm, `S99.JOIN` clause (a) falsifies tonight.
- **The standards test was chosen by me**, from two vocabularies I knew would define error as a
  difference. It was declared before the fetch and it could have gone the other way on what remains
  of the sentence (P4 did go the other way, on `already`). It could not have kept the clause, and
  the pre-registration said so.
- **Ninth consecutive seventh night whose object is this line's own record**, counting as Session 92
  did. This time two outside
  vocabularies were used to cut it. That is one turn out, and it is only one.
