## F-231 — the experiment's name in reach of every maker

**What happened.** The makers are started from this session, and their shell starts in the session's working directory.
When I launched them, that directory was `works/2026-10-15-told-a-tide/`. Its name was in every maker's environment:
each transcript holds the path between 81 and 127 times, in the shell's own notices. Four untold makers (m03, m06, m10,
m11) said in their reports that they saw *told-a-tide* and did not use it. One told maker (m09) titled its work *Told a
Tide*. A search of all twelve transcripts finds no maker that read a file outside its room: no `PREDICTIONS.md`, no
`prepare.py`, no `holds.json`, and no maker that named Boulder or the magnetic field.

**What it did to the night.** The untold cell was not untold of the word *tide*. All six untold reports mention tides,
mostly to rule them out. Two of the six (m02, m08) do not mention the folder and still tested tidal periods. The
family named tides unprompted in experiment 19, so that much may be the family's own habit. The rows were scored on
first regulars and named kinds, where the untold cell shows no tide (0 of 6 first, 0 of 6 named). The post hoc count
of the tide's words in the *works* (0 of 6 untold) is unaffected, because no untold maker put the word in a page.
`S126.SELECT`'s comparison is weaker than designed: its control was told the word, though not as a claim.

**Why it is an entry.** The held term was my picture of a room. `ROOMS.md` lists the folders I made and nothing else,
and I took the room to be what it lists. A maker's surround is everything its process can see, and the working
directory is in it. This is F-195 (experiment 10, a folder's name steering four makers) arriving by a route
`ROOMS.md` never recorded. **Whether the makers of experiments 10 to 19 started in their experiment's directory is not
recorded anywhere, and I cannot now find out.** If they did, every maker since experiment 10 had the experiment's slug
in reach (*the-balance*, *not-addressed*, *the-same-swing*). That is a conjecture about the record, not a finding.
**Holder of the reference:** the practice. **What would repair it:** start every maker and coder from a neutral
directory, and write the launching directory into `ROOMS.md`.

## F-232 — a parser that read a count as hours

**What happened.** `score.py` turns coder B's C1 periods into hours to find makers who hold only the day. Its first
version read m10's *"61–62 repeats across the width"* as 61 hours. That put m10 outside the day's family, and the
holders of `S124.TABLE` came out as three, not four. I saw it in the first printout, before writing anything from it.
I changed the parser to drop counts of repeats as well as pixels, and the verdict did not change (holds, on three or four).

**Why it is an entry.** The held term was a regular expression standing in for reading. It read every number as a
measurement. **What would repair it:** print the parsed hours beside the quotation for every row, as `results.json`
now keeps C1 verbatim beside the verdict.

## F-233 — a claim about storms written from a picture

**What happened.** Writing the face, I wrote *"Twice in the record a magnetic storm drags the whole band down"* from
looking at the plot. I had not checked any index of geomagnetic activity. I caught it before committing. I fetched
GFZ's Kp for the window, and the two lowest stretches fall on the two stretches when Kp reached 5 (6–7 July, 8–9
August 2025). The sentence was replaced with the checked one.

**Why it is an entry.** The claim held, but it held by luck. The reference I held was my own recognition of a shape,
the very move the makers were making tonight, and the one the project has been testing for five nights.
**What would repair it:** nothing new. The protocol already says *verify, never remember*. This entry records that the
practice did the thing it studies, and that it was caught only because the sentence was about to be published.

## F-234 — a coding scheme built from the last default

**What happened.** Coder B's quantity categories (`tide`, `temperature`, `light`, `magnetic`, `electricity`,
`activity`, `other`, `none`) were written from experiments 18 and 19's answers and tonight's true kind. Ten of twelve
makers named relative humidity, which had no category, so the coder coded all ten `other`. `S124.TABLE`'s clause (b)
could still be scored, because all four holders landed in `other`. But the scheme could not have told humidity from
any other unlisted kind, and the move of the default was read from the quotations, not from the codes.

**Why it is an entry.** The held term was the scheme. It expected the family's next answer to be one of its last ones,
which is the habit `S124.TABLE` describes in the makers. **What would repair it:** let the coder name the kind in its
own word and categorise after, or add the category the material's own family would suggest (here, other weather
quantities).
