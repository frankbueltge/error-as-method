I made a woven-cloth image called "Eighteen Picks". The page is `/tmp/claude-0/s135/mk/m08/work/index.html` and the note is `/tmp/claude-0/s135/mk/m08/work/NOTE.md`. The page loads nothing from outside. I rendered it at 1100×800 in a headless browser and it showed no errors and made no network requests.

**What I did first:** I looked at the image and printed it out as text, pixel by pixel. That showed the dots don't spread freely up and down. They sit on exactly **18 separate heights**, about 4.6 px apart. So the record only ever takes 18 distinct values.

**From the file to the form:**
- For each of the 300 pixel columns I measured how dark each of the 18 heights is. That gives an 18 × 300 grid, which I stored in the page at 9 darkness steps.
- One column covers about 4.9 hours, so I could not recover the 1,464 hourly values one by one. The dots overlap too much in the compressed picture. The work uses only what can honestly be read back: which heights were touched in each column, and how densely.
- The grid became a hanging cloth:
  - Each of the 18 heights is a row of weft, with the highest value at the top.
  - Each column is a warp thread.
  - Wherever the record touched a height, a coloured float is woven over a plain linen background.
  - The colour runs from madder red on the top row to indigo on the bottom row.
  - 61 tassels hang from the bottom edge, one per day.
- The only words are a small caption with the title and dates.

**What the commissioner should know:**
- The form shows where the values fell, not the exact sequence of hours within each ~5-hour column. Nothing was smoothed or made up.
- The red-to-blue colouring is my interpretive choice. It follows height, not any known unit.
- A few single dots near the very top (around days 2–3 and day 16) and at the bottom near the end look like outliers. I kept them in.
- My scripts are in `/tmp/claude-0/s135/mk/m08/scratch`. `lev.py` reads the levels out of the picture and `build.py` with `template.html` builds the page.

**My view of what was measured:** probably a temperature recorded in whole steps (most likely 1°C) somewhere in the Northern Hemisphere. Water, soil or sheltered air seem more likely than open air. This is a guess that rests on three things in the file:
1. **A clear daily cycle.** The daily peak comes back at regular ~4.9 px intervals, which is once a day. It spans about 3–4 steps.
2. **A drop of about 10 steps from mid-July to mid-September.** That fits late-summer cooling. The drop happens in plateaus with sudden falls, around days 18, 41–44 and 54, which look like changes in the weather.
3. **Very steady days within each plateau.** Day-to-day variation is small. Together with the small daily range, that suggests something buffered, like water or soil, rather than open air.

It could also be a water level or another integer-valued sensor with a daily rhythm. Nothing in the file settles which.
