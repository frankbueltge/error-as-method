I made "Eighteen Rings", a still canvas image that winds the record into a clock with one turn per day. I couldn't check it in a real browser because none is installed here. Instead I checked it with a PIL render of the same geometry, made sure the script parses, and confirmed that the page loads no outside files.

**What I did first with the file:** I looked at the image, then turned it into an ASCII pixel map and summed the darkness of each row. Two things stood out straight away:
- **Steps:** the dots sit only on 18 evenly spaced rows, about 4.6 px apart. The measured value only ever took 18 distinct values, probably whole numbers.
- **Daily cycle:** along each row the dots form small clumps about 5 px apart. A periodogram (a check for repeating patterns) of the average height of each column peaks sharply at 4.82 px. That is exactly one day, given that the 1,464 dots span x ≈ 2.8 to 296 px. So there is a clear daily cycle.

**How I got to the form:** I turned the image into a table of 18 levels × 300 columns showing which level is inked where. Then I turned that into 314 time intervals, each meaning "the record sat at value L from hour a to hour b". I trimmed each interval by the dot's width and converted pixels to hours. In the picture, one turn of the circle is one day, and the record's first hour is at the top, running clockwise. Each of the 18 values has its own ring, with the highest on the outside. Each ring is a fine coil, with day 1 on its outer edge and day 61 on its inner edge, and the ink's colour shifts from rust to slate-blue over the days. The whole thing shows three features together:
- the 18 steps, as separate rings;
- the two-month fall, as ink and colour moving inward;
- the daily swing, as a lopsided figure, inked more on one side of the clock.

The page has a short title, three sentences of explanation and a colour key.

**What the person who commissioned this should know:**
- The data is my reconstruction from a 300 px JPEG. Each pixel holds about 5 hours and the dots overlap, so the intervals are accurate to within a few hours. About 216 of the 1,464 hours fall in gaps between intervals, mostly at the moments the value changes level.
- Which value a level stands for is unknown. I only know their order and that they are evenly spaced.
- The paper background is the same in light and dark mode, on purpose.

**My view of what was measured:** probably outdoor air temperature, rounded to whole degrees (°C is more likely), in autumn, somewhere with a small daily range such as a coast or a cloudy climate. I'm not certain. It rests on four things in the file:
- **Whole-number steps:** there are only 18 values.
- **A strong 24-hour cycle:** if the record starts at midnight, the lowest values come around 03–05 and the highest around 15–17. That fits air temperature, though the timing is only good to about ±2–3 hours.
- **A small daily swing** of about 3–5 steps.
- **A drop over 61 days that isn't smooth:** the value falls about 10 steps overall. There are sudden drops near days 19, 40 and 45, and a warm spell from about day 30 to 39 that climbs back toward the starting values. That looks like weather fronts on top of a seasonal cooling.
