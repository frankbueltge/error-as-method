I made "The Afternoon Withdraws": a 24-hour dial with one thread that goes round it once a day for all 61 days, lit amber in July and pale blue by September. It is a single still canvas page, `/tmp/claude-0/s135/mk/m06/work/index.html`, and it loads nothing external; the material is embedded in it. The note is in `/tmp/claude-0/s135/mk/m06/work/NOTE.md`. I checked the page by rendering it at 1100 × 800 in headless Chromium.

**What I did first with the file.** I looked at it, then turned it into an ASCII map of its pixels. Two things stood out:
- **Few possible values.** The values sit on only 18 discrete horizontal levels, evenly spaced about 4.6 px apart. The quantity was recorded in whole steps.
- **A daily rhythm.** At each level the dots form small clumps about every 5 px, and 5 px is about one day.

**How I got from that to the form.**
- **Levels.** I fitted the 18-level grid (offset 5.46 px, spacing 4.595 px) and recorded which level was occupied in each pixel column. I trimmed the width of the 3 px plotted dots to find where they actually sat.
- **Time.** The dots run from x≈3 to x≈296, not edge to edge. With that mapping, a periodogram of the column-by-column ink centre peaks at exactly 24.00 h. Before I corrected for the margins it gave a misleading 23.5 h, which also ruled out a 24.8 h tidal period.
- **Timing of the cycle.** The phase is stable across the whole record. Assuming the series starts at 00:00 on 15 July, the low is around 05:00 and the high in the mid-to-late afternoon (about 14–17 h, depending on how it is measured).
- **Overall shape.** The daily range is about 3–5 steps. The whole cycle sinks about 10 steps over the period, in two fairly sharp drops (around days 18–22 and days 36–44) between flatter stretches.
- **An hourly series.** I rebuilt one from the ink centre of each column. Each plotted dot spans about 15 hours, which squeezes a 24 h swing to about 0.47 of its real size, so I doubled the daily component (around a 24 h running mean) and kept the trend as measured.
- **The dial.** Midnight is at the bottom, noon at the top, and higher values sit further out across 18 faint rings, one per step. Beads mark the steps as they were actually read. Every loop leans toward the late afternoon, and the loops contract inward as the season goes on. I tried and dropped two other versions first: concentric bands per level (unreadable) and an uncorrected thread (the daily swing was nearly invisible).

**What the commissioner should know.**
- **The thread is estimated.** In the picture the dots overlap by roughly 5 hours, so the individual hourly values can't be recovered exactly. The doubling of the daily swing is a deliberate correction derived from the dot width.
- **Time-of-day assumption.** It assumes the record begins at midnight.
- **Direction and scale.** "Higher = further out" follows the brief. Absolute values and units are unknown; the steps are only ranks 0–17.
- **Working files** are in `/tmp/claude-0/s135/mk/m06/scratch`: the extraction scripts, `core.json`, `hourly.json` and preview `shot.png`.

**My view of what was measured.** Most likely an air temperature (or something driven by the sun in the same way), rounded to whole units, at a Northern Hemisphere site. It rests on:
- **The cycle:** exactly 24 h, lowest near dawn and highest mid-to-late afternoon. That is the classic shape of a sun-heated quantity.
- **The trend:** a decline of about 10 steps from mid-July to mid-September, which fits late-summer cooling.
- **The drops:** abrupt step-downs over a few days, more like weather fronts than a smooth seasonal change.
- **The rounding:** strict whole steps, which suggests a sensor or log reporting whole degrees.

If one step is 1 °C, a daily range of 3–5° is small for open air. That points to a mild, cloudy or coastal place, or a sheltered sensor. This is inference only; nothing in the file names the quantity or the place.
