I made "Whole Steps Down", a single still SVG image. It is in `/tmp/claude-0/s135/mk/m09/work/index.html`, and the note is in `/tmp/claude-0/s135/mk/m09/work/NOTE.md`. The page loads nothing else; in a headless browser at 1100×800 it made no request but itself and drew exactly 1,464 dots.

**What I did first:** I looked at the image, then printed its pixels as text. That showed something the small picture hides: the dots do not spread freely but sit on 18 evenly spaced rows (about 4.6 px apart, from y≈5.5 to y≈83.5). So the quantity was recorded in whole steps of a fixed size.

**From file to form:**
- **Levels per column.** For each column of pixels I measured how dark each of the 18 levels was. The dots span x≈2–297, so about 4.9 hours share each pixel column.
- **Daily rhythm.** I tested the column-by-column average level for repetition and found a strong 24-hour cycle (peaks at lags of about 5, 9.5 and 14.5 px, against about 4.8 px per day).
- **Hours per step.** For each of the 61 days I shared out its 24 hours across the levels in proportion to their darkness, keeping each day's total at exactly 24.
- **Average day.** I folded all hours by 24 to get the shape of an average day.
- **The form.** The 18 steps are faint ruled lines, each day is a column, and each hour is one dot in a small cluster on its step. An inset in the empty upper-right corner shows the average day on the same step scale.

**What the commissioner should know:**
- **What is reliable:** which steps each day touched, the 18-level structure, the daily rhythm and the overall descent.
- **What is estimated:** how many hours each step held within a day. About five hours overlap in each pixel column and the JPEG blurs them, so the clusters probably make each day's spread look slightly wider than it really was. The caption on the page says this.
- **The time of day is unknown.** The inset counts hours from the first value, not clock time.
- **Labels only, no numbers.** There are no units or values anywhere, because none can be known from the file.

**My view of what was measured:** probably an hourly air temperature recorded in whole degrees, over an autumn (or the run into winter) somewhere in the mid-latitudes. It rests on four things in the file:
- a smooth 24-hour cycle about 4 steps from low to high, with the low point about 12 hours before the high;
- a slow fall of about 11 steps over the 61 days;
- spells of a few days that drop 2–3 steps and then recover, such as days 17–21, 29–31, 42–44 and 53–54, with a narrower daily range during some of them, which looks like weather fronts and overcast days;
- the whole-step resolution, which fits a sensor or log that rounds to the nearest degree.

If the record started at local midnight, the warmest point would come at about 17:00. That is a little late for air temperature, so shallow water or soil temperature, which share these features, are also plausible. I can't tell them apart from the file.
