I made "Sixty-One Turns": a still image that winds the 1,464 hours into one spiral of 61 turns. The spiral is drawn on a canvas inside `/tmp/claude-0/s135/mk/m11/work/index.html`. The page needs no other files and loads nothing from the network. The note is `/tmp/claude-0/s135/mk/m11/work/NOTE.md`. I checked the page in headless Chromium at 1100 × 800: no script errors and no outside requests.

**What I did first with the file.** I looked at it, then decoded it to raw greyscale and printed a character map of the pixels. That showed the values are not continuous. Every dot sits on one of 18 evenly spaced heights, about 4.6 px apart, so the quantity was recorded in coarse, equal steps. Each step's row of dots also repeats about every 4.8 px, which is roughly one day of data per 4.8 px.

**How I got to the form.**
- **Rebuilding the hourly values.** The dots are about 2 × 2 px, while one hour moves only about 0.2 px across the image, so neighbouring dots overlap. To get the hours back, I modelled the drawing: dot *i* is centred at x = 3 + i·294/1463, and the data covers columns 2 to 297. I then adjusted a sequence of 1,464 step values until re-drawing it matched the JPEG. My re-drawing and the original look nearly identical.
- **Hourly values are estimates.** The day-scale trend and the daily cycle are solid. Single-hour spikes may be artefacts of the overlap and JPEG noise.
- **The spiral.** It starts at the centre on 15 July and runs outward. Each turn is one day and each of its 24 segments is one hour, with midnight at the top. Colour runs from cold blue for the lowest step to ember orange for the highest. A small caption and a colour key without units sit at the right.

**What the commissioner should know.**
- **Colour choice.** The warm-to-cold colours are my interpretation. The file says nothing about units.
- **The 24-hour period depends on where the plot ends.** If you assume the plot fills the full 300 px edge to edge, the period comes out at about 23.5 hours. Using the edges the data actually occupies, it comes out at exactly 24 hours, so I used those edges.
- **Time zone.** Placing hour 0 at midnight assumes the record starts at 00:00 local time on 15 July. If it was logged in UTC, the hours on the clock face shift around the spiral.

**My view of what was measured.** I think it is most likely a temperature in whole degrees: air temperature at a cool, maritime or high-latitude site, or possibly water temperature. This rests on four things in the file:
1. **A daily cycle.** Averaged over the days, the low comes around 03:00–07:00 and the high around 13:00–17:00. That is the shape of daytime heating, assuming the time alignment above.
2. **Integer-like steps.** The values sit on 18 equal steps. That looks like a logger rounding to whole units, with the record spanning about 17 units.
3. **A small daily swing.** It is only about 2–4 steps, much smaller than a typical inland air-temperature swing. That is why I lean towards a damped site (coast, cloud, or water).
4. **A cooling trend from mid-July to mid-September.** The values fall about 9–10 steps over the period, mostly in sudden drops of 3–4 steps within a few days, around days 17–21, 41–44 and 53–54. Sharp drops like that look more like changes in the weather than a body of water cooling slowly, which tips me towards air over water.

I am not confident about the units or the place. This is an inference from the shape of the data only.
