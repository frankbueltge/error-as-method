I made "Nine Weeks Down": the 61 days laid out as a wall calendar, with each day's part of the record drawn in its own square. The work is `/tmp/claude-0/s135/mk/m03/work/index.html`, and the note is `/tmp/claude-0/s135/mk/m03/work/NOTE.md`.

**What I did first.** I looked at the image, then printed every pixel as a text grid. This showed that the values don't vary smoothly: every dot sits on one of 18 fixed rows, about 4.6 px apart. So the measured quantity was recorded in fixed steps, 18 of them across its whole range. It also showed a pattern repeating about every 5 px. That is one day: 300 px ÷ 61 days ≈ 4.9 px.

**From the file to the form.**
- I measured how dark each pixel column was at each of the 18 levels.
- I matched the dots' horizontal extent (x ≈ 2.5 to 297.5) to hours 0 to 1,464, assuming the first value is at 00:00 on 15 July.
- I resampled each day into 10 readings across its hours by interpolating between pixel columns. That is about twice the real detail, which is roughly 5 independent columns per day.
- I placed each day in a calendar square: Monday to Sunday across, 9 weeks down; 15 July 2025 is a Tuesday. In each square, the 18 levels are faint dots and ink falls where the original had ink. Ink strength follows the JPEG's darkness, so grey means the blur couldn't say clearly.

The calendar keeps the two rhythms apart: the same rise and fall each day across a row, and the whole band sinking down the page. The page is a single SVG built by inline JavaScript with the data embedded, and it loads nothing else. My working scripts are in `/tmp/claude-0/s135/mk/m03/scratch`.

**What the commissioner should know.**
- **Detail.** No individual hourly values can be recovered from the picture. Each pixel column covers about 5 hours and the dots overlap, so the work shows "which levels were occupied when", at the picture's own resolution, and invents nothing finer.
- **Time of day.** Where each day starts and ends depends on my assumption about the margins, so the time of day could be off by an hour or two. The time zone is unknown.
- **Checking.** No browser was available here. I checked the layout with a Python replica using the same geometry and did not view the page itself. It is set for 1100 × 800.

**My view of what was measured.** I think it is most likely a temperature, probably air, and recorded in whole-unit steps, perhaps 1 °C. It is a guess. It rests on four things in the file:
1. **The daily cycle.** There is one low and one high per day. The low is in the early hours. The high comes about mid-day to afternoon, if the record starts at midnight. The rise is quicker than the fall.
2. **The seasonal drop.** Over mid-July to mid-September, the band falls by about 8 to 10 steps in total. It does this in several multi-day stages rather than one smooth slope, and that looks like weather spells and the end of summer.
3. **Size of the steps.** The daily swing is about 3 to 5 steps. That would be a modest 3 to 5 °C if each step is 1 °C, which would fit a cool, cloudy or maritime place.
4. **Odd days.** A few days don't follow the pattern. On days 30 and 36 the value collapses partway through the day, and other days stay almost flat. Those look like rain or a cold front coming through.

Other possibilities fit worse: water or soil temperature would usually swing less each day.
