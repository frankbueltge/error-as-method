I made a single still work called **Sixty-One Lines**. It is in `/tmp/claude-0/s135/mk/m07/work/index.html` and fully self-contained (inline SVG built by plain JS, data embedded, about 3 KB). Its note is `/tmp/claude-0/s135/mk/m07/work/NOTE.md`. No browser was available here, so I have not seen the page itself. I checked it two other ways: I ran its script under node, and it produces exactly 1,464 dots inside the 1100-wide frame. I also drew a preview with PIL using the same layout, which is at `/tmp/claude-0/s135/mk/m07/scratch/preview.png`.

**What I did first:** I looked at the picture and then printed its pixels as text to see how the dots were laid out. Two things stood out:
- **Fixed levels:** the dots only sit at fixed heights, so the value is rounded to whole steps. A brightness profile of each pixel row confirmed **18 levels**, spaced about 4.6 px apart.
- **Ink extent:** the dots run from x=2 to x=297, which is about 4.9 px per day and about 5 hours per pixel column.

**From file to form:** a script in scratch (`extract.py`) does three things:
- It assigns each dark pixel to its nearest level and splits each pixel column between days by how much it overlaps each one.
- It turns the ink per day and level into an estimated number of hours, rounded so every day adds up to exactly 24.
- It saves the result as a 61 × 18 table, which is embedded in the page.

The work lays the record out as a page of text:
- Each day is one line, and each hour is one dot, so there are 1,464 dots, as in the original.
- Each dot sits at one of 18 faint vertical tab stops, with lower values to the left.
- The record holds, drops, holds and drops again, so read down the page the dots form three indented blocks that step to the left.

**What the commissioner should know:**
- **The hour counts are estimates.** At about 5 hours per pixel and with JPEG blur, they are about ±1 hour per cell.
- **The order of hours within a day is not in the work.** The dots in each line are sorted by value, not by time. The picture is too coarse to recover that order reliably.
- **Day boundaries are assumed.** I took each day as 1/61 of the plot width, starting from the first dot. I don't know whether the record starts at midnight.
- **The absolute values are lost.** With no axes, the 18 levels are only ranks. The work keeps the steps and the shape, not the units.

**My view of what was measured (a guess):** an air temperature, logged hourly and rounded to whole degrees (most likely °C), over about two months of cooling: late summer into autumn, or the matching season in the other hemisphere. It rests on four things in the file:
1. **A daily rhythm:** there is a clear high and low in every 24-hour block, with a typical daily range of 4 to 6 levels.
2. **Whole-number steps:** only 18 distinct values appear, which fits a sensor that reports whole units.
3. **A cooling trend in steps:** the values hold steady for about the first 18 days, drop sharply around days 19–22, and hold through about day 41. They drop again around days 42–45 and step down once more around day 55. Sudden drops followed by flat stretches look like weather changes, such as cold fronts, during a seasonal cool-down.
4. **The size of the daily swing:** 4 to 6 steps a day is too large for water or soil temperature, and about right for air temperature in °C.

I can't rule out another quantity with a daily cycle that is rounded to whole steps.
