# Sixty-One Lines

The work sets the record as a page: each of the 61 days becomes one line of type, and each line holds its 24 hourly values as 24 dots. Every dot sits at one of eighteen tab stops, because the source only ever takes eighteen distinct values, with lower values to the left. Within a day the dots are sorted by value rather than by hour, so each line shows how the day's hours were spread across the scale and not when they fell. I chose this form because two things in the picture outweigh the hour-by-hour wiggle. The first is the quantisation: the values sit on fixed shelves like a staircase. The second is the drift: the record holds steady, drops, holds, and drops again. Read down the page, those holds and drops become three indented paragraphs that step to the left. The same 1,464 dots as in the original picture are all there, only laid out again.
