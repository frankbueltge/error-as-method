# Whole Steps Down

The record turned out to move only in whole steps: its 1,464 values sit on just eighteen levels. It also has a clear 24-hour rhythm, and over two months it sinks about eleven of those steps. The form keeps the parts I could recover and does not pretend to know the rest. The eighteen levels are drawn as faint ruled lines, and each of the 61 days is a column. Every hour is one dot, placed on the step it kept, so the page holds exactly 1,464 dots. Within a day the dots gather into small clusters, so you can see how long each day stayed on each step. Taken together, the clusters show the slow, stair-like descent and the cold-snap plunges and recoveries along the way. A small inset in the empty upper corner folds all 61 days into one average day, on the same step scale, to show the rhythm that drives the spread within each column. I chose discrete dots on discrete lines because that is the nature of the data, a counted and stepped thing rather than a smooth curve. The caption also says that the number of hours on each step is an estimate, because it was read back from a 300-pixel compressed picture.
