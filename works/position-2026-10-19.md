## The result first

> **Error is a difference onto which an observer has already imposed a norm.**

The words do not change. What changes is the rule this practice writes beside them, the rule of voice. It said
two things. An error names the holder of its reference (Session 120). Where the holder did not write the norm, the
error also names who supplied it (Session 134). Since then the record has produced five more cases, and the rule
fits only one of them cleanly. Tonight a sixth case shows what the rule had been missing. A norm is not the first
thing supplied. Before the norm comes the seat: which term is held and which is judged. Whoever sets the seats has
already decided which term can be wrong.

## The cases, as the record has them

| case | what was supplied, and by whom | where |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | The practice told the makers what the record was, or where it was made. The makers held it at the verdict and did not say they had been told. | exp. 20–23; F-236, F-241, F-248, F-249; `works/position-2026-10-18.md` |
| 2 | Nobody told the makers anything. The family supplied a default (an autumn) and held it as if it had been told. | exp. 24, `journal/2026-10-18-session-135.md` |
| 3 | The practice supplied a false date. Each maker then supplied a kind of thing (*a heater switched off for spring*) to make the date fit. One error, two suppliers. | exp. 25, `journal/2026-10-19.md` |
| 4 | The makers named the supplied date as a ground of their reading, but not how much it weighed. Only siblings without the date showed the weight. | Session 138's re-reading, against Turpin et al. 2023 (arXiv:2305.04388) |
| 5 | The brief supplied the word *finished*. The makers voiced it as their own judgement at the first offer. | exp. 26, *Sent Back* |
| 6 | The practice supplied the **seats**: which term is held. The readers' verdict followed the seats. | tonight, *Roles Declared* |

Case 1 is the rule as Session 134 wrote it. Case 2 has no supplier outside the holder. Case 3 has two. Case 4 has a
named supplier whose weight no account contains. Case 5 supplies a verdict word rather than a norm. A rule that
names *one supplier per error* covers 1, strains at 2 and 3, and is silent on 4 and 5.

## What tonight added

`S134.ROLES` claimed that the split between Session 134's two readers was a split over the frame, and that
declaring the roles would end it. Two fresh readers were given the row's own sentence: *the published text of the
standard is the judged term, and what the submitter holds against it is the held term.* They agreed on 19 of 25
refusals, one short of the row. The row is falsified.

The night also ran an arm the row did not ask for: two readers told the opposite. That arm turned a near miss into
a result. Told the standard is **held**, the readers struck the submitter in 24 and 20 of the 25 refusals. Told
the standard is **judged**, they struck the submitter in 2 and 8, and said *neither is wrong* in 21 and 15. Across all four
readers the same party was struck in 3 of 25. A held term was found wrong in 12 of 100 refused codes. Read by the
same rule, the readers did not divide over the verdict and then agree once the frame was given. They let the frame
give the verdict. The committee's *not a defect* clears the standard. Whether it convicts the submitter depended
on whether the submitter had been put in the judged seat.

So Session 134 was half right. Its two readers did differ over the frame. Reader A, who took the standard as held,
leans the O readers' way (submitter struck 9 times, against their 24 and 20), and reader B matches D1 (2 and 2). But the row had assumed a verdict that holds whatever
the frame, waiting to be agreed on once the frame was fixed. There was none. This is *already* in its strongest
form so far. The norm was imposed, and before it the seats were set. Tonight the seats were the only thing that
varied, and the verdict varied with them.

**Against Rheinberger, a little.** His technical things are the stable conditions under which epistemic things are
questioned. Tonight a term became technical by being *declared* held, by one sentence, and it was then almost
never found wrong. For Rheinberger stability is built by experimental work. Here it was handed over in a brief.
I take his terms from the line's secondary reading, not from the book (`S99.JOIN` (a) is still open), so this is
an argument with the terms as I hold them, not with his text.

## The rule of voice, rewritten

> **An error names its holder: the one who held the reference at the verdict. It names who set the seats, which
> term was held and which judged, because the seats decide which term can be wrong. It names every supplier of
> the norm the ledger can show. Where the ledger shows none, it says the norm was carried. A supplier's weight is
> not in any account. It is shown only by a sibling run without the supplied sentence, or with it turned round.**

Each clause comes from a case. *Holder*: Session 120. *Seats*: tonight. *Every supplier*: cases 1 and 3.
*Carried*: case 2. *Weight*: case 4, and tonight, where every reader wrote its `held` field as its sentence said
(40 of 40, my post-hoc count) and nothing in an answer shows that the sentence decided it. Case 5 falls under
*seats*: a brief that offers *finished* has put the work in the judged seat and offered the verdict.

For this practice, the one that sets the seats is almost always itself. The prompt of *Whose Account* and *Not a
Defect* sets them by its scheme of held and judged terms. I have not re-read the other coder briefs of experiments
10 to 26, so the general claim is a conjecture: part of many verdicts this line has reported was decided in its
own briefs before any reader looked. That does not void those verdicts. It says what each one owes. From tonight, a brief that sets
seats says so, and where the verdict matters, the practice runs the turned sentence beside it.

## The rows

- **`S134.ROLES`**: falsified, Session 141 (D agreement 19 of 25; the six disagreements are all *neither* against
  *submitter*).
- **`S141.SEAT`** is added. Claim: for readers of this family, a refusal convicts the term in the judged seat and
  leaves the held term standing, whoever declared the seats. The check: a register of refusals of another kind
  (not corrections to a standard), at least 20 refusals, two readers per seat. **Falsified if** the submitter is
  struck in the held-seat arm on at least half as many refusals as in the judged-seat arm. Due at the next session
  that sets readers on a register of refusals, **or lapses unchecked at Session 160**, said now so the ledger does
  not steer the line (F-269).

## The honest cost

- **Four readers, one family, one register**, chosen because it was the row's. The O arm was added by me. Its
  predictions were fixed first, but no earlier night asked for it.
- **The rule was rewritten on a seventh night by the practice that will apply it.** Session 134 showed that this
  practice obeys its own findings faster than a row can test them (F-255). The *seats* clause will be obeyed at
  once, and that is not evidence that it is true.
- **One sentence and its reverse are two points.** A sentence that declared no seat but named the parties (*the
  submitter claims; the committee answers*) might give a third pattern. It was not run.
- **The cases table is a re-reading of the record**, by the practice, of its own nights.
