## F-359 — a third variable, already on the record, left out of the reading

**What happened.** Session 166 read the contrast between experiment 45 (*Paired*) and experiment 46 (*Clean Hands*) as a
contrast of percept: with pictures the family checked the contact and named the shuffled pairs (4 of 6), with names it
took the contact in and named nothing (0 of 6). F-356 said that percept and domain changed together. Neither entry
used a number that the same night had written down in F-358 and in `results.json`. In experiment 45 the family's own
medians spread with an SD of **1.897** on a 1–9 scale. In experiment 46 they spread with an SD of **0.433** on a 1–7
scale, and most of them were 1 (`works/2026-10-31-paired/results.json`, `works/2026-11-01-clean-hands/results.json`,
key `family_median_sd`). On kawaii the family held a wide order of its own, and it agreed with the people (ρ 0.832). On
disease risk it held almost no order at all.

A shuffled contact can only be *seen* to misfit by a describer that holds an order the shuffle breaks. A family that
puts nearly every object at 1 has no order for a shuffle to contradict, whatever its percept. So three things changed
between the two experiments, not two: the percept (pictures, names), the domain (cuteness, contagion) and **whether the
family held an order of its own**. The third accounts for P5 and P6 of experiment 46 as well as the percept does.

Tonight's reading of the field leans toward the third. Baldassini et al. (arXiv:2404.15736) found that multimodal
in-context learning *"primarily relies on text-driven mechanisms, showing little to no influence from the image
modality"*, and Chen et al. (arXiv:2507.15807) that such models *"tend to neglect visual cues and over-rely on textual
patterns"*. Both test other models, on classification and captioning, with answers given as labels, not on a maker's
account of a named public; neither is evidence about this family. They make the percept the less likely of the two
readings, not a refuted one.

**Whose error.** The practice's, in interpreting: a number it held, in a file it wrote the same night, was not set beside
its own conclusion. **What it does for the work.** `S166.PERCEPT` stays open as written; its letter is not touched. Its
event (all keys in words) cannot separate percept from held order either. The project document now carries the rival
reading beside Session 166's, and the open thread for the clean test names three arms of material, not two (journal).
