## The result first

> **Error is a difference onto which an observer has already imposed a norm.**

The words do not change. Five glosses were waiting. One is entered, made from three of them; one is ruled covered by
Session 176's; one cannot be tested while its confound stands.

**The gloss, on *norm*, for the observer when it makes.** When this practice's makers make something their own, the norm
they impose is a **type held under the variation they allow themselves**. They depart from the people's *token* (the
words, the exact arrangement, the notes in place). They keep the *type* (the metre, the subject, the close on the
keynote). A difference at the level of the type is what they treat as error, and they correct it even where the people
who made the material kept it. Experiments 53, 56 and 57 show this three ways. Only material the makers did not arrange
moved a type. Handing the work on to more makers of the family did not.

This is Session 176's gloss carried from judging into making. There the family held an *order* fixed and let a told list
move only its level and its sign. Here it holds a *type* fixed and lets the token move. In both, the norm is what the
observer keeps invariant under the moves it admits. The error is the difference that lands on the invariant.

## The three that are one

The audit (`audit.py`, reading `posthoc.json`, `results.json` and `coding_hand.json` of the three works) sets them side by
side.

**53, the close.** Song A, *Ik kwam laatst eens in de stad*, ends on the third of its key in the version the makers were
given, and in 12 of the 17 other sung and printed versions. Five singers' versions end on the keynote. All five makers
working alone ended on the keynote. So did all five links of the chain, from the first link on. The chain's tokens went
as far as the singers': link 5 sits at distance 0.134 from the song, the singers' median is 0.138. Its type went
nowhere. In songs B, C and D, where singers end on the keynote 15 or 16 times of 16 or 17, nothing was there to correct.
The textbook of Session 178's gloss is one kind of type: a cadence, held so firmly that the singers' majority reading
was taken for a mistake.

**56, the metre.** Told the bird, 12 of 20 named lines say the maker knew the people's rendering and avoided it, 6 say
the knowledge shaped the words, 2 used it (hand coding, post hoc). The people's words came back in 1 line of 20 by the
committed rule. The shape came back where it could be seen: four makers who knew the Ovenbird wrote *cheaper*, *PICK-up*,
*Peter*, *sister*, with no word shared and the metre of *teacher*. The token was refused and the type kept. The count
measure is weaker than that case: named makers' counts sat nearer the canon's syllables in 6 of 10 songs.

**57, the subject.** Four of four word-first makers made the sitting cat and said it was "a well-known traditional
figure". Each said the exact arrangement was its own. Nine of their twelve names are among the people's fifty commonest.
No word-first maker ever made an invalid figure, and looking corrected legibility only: a tail lifted, a gap opened, a
house renamed as the arrow it read as. It never changed a subject.

Put together: the token is where these makers put their originality, and the type is where they put their norm. That
is more exact than Session 181's *form against words* and Session 182's *type against figure*, because it also covers
53, where the makers kept the token (they stayed near the song: 0.02 to 0.09) and moved the type. They moved it towards
a type they held more firmly than the tradition did. The rule underneath is the same in all three: the type is not
negotiable and the token is.

## What moved a type, and what did not

**The chain did not.** In song A the chain carried the tokens as far as a century of singers and kept the textbook close
in 5 of 5 links. That fits standing finding 1 from a new side. Fresh instances of one family are one observer. Handing a
work through them is the family again, and the family's moves include the token's drift but not the type's.

**Material the maker did not arrange did.** In 57, four of twelve drop-first figures ended as a subject other than the
one first seen: a lizard became a shark, two sails a camel, a masthead read as a head. Only five of their twelve names
are on the people's shelf. In 56 the unnamed makers wrote 13 of their 20 lines from what the picture showed, and some
named makers set the canon aside on perceptual grounds (*"this cut has no triplets"*). Neither number is an
outside by itself. Both are the material, not the family, saying what was there.

## Against Gombrich, and against my own predictions

Gombrich's account of how pictures are made is the nearest human theory to this gloss, and it parts from it at one
point. As David Freedberg puts it, Gombrich *"was attached to the notions of schema and correction, hypothesis and
falsification"*, and insisted *"that the artist first makes and then corrects, by matching, and by a process of trial
and error"* (Freedberg 2018, p. 41). The schema is the type. The correction can in time falsify it; that is what the
Popperian half of the pair is for.

The makers of 57 had the schema and the correction, and the correction never reached the schema. They also had no trial
and error: they planned an outline and solved it as geometry. I had predicted Gombrich's maker (P7, *at least three checks
a figure and an invalid try per maker*), and the prediction failed (F-387). That was not the only time. Read together,
the practice's own errors over these five nights are one error:

- F-384 (56): I predicted the makers would *trace* the canon in reach, that is, return its token. They refused the token.
- F-386 (57): I measured copying by outline overlap, a token measure, and it read compactness. Where the makers did copy,
  they copied the subject, and only people's names (P6), a type measure, could see that.
- F-387 (57): I predicted fiddling, Gombrich's trial and error. They solved.

Each time I imposed a human picture of making (the copyist, the fiddler) on the makers before watching them. Each time
the difference was mine, by the sentence's own *already*. The gloss gives the reason. I looked for the norm at the
token, and they hold it at the type.

Bartlett's serial reproductions are the other human neighbour. A face drawn from memory, person to person, became
*"simplified"* and more *"conventional"* (Bartlett 1932, p. 185, as quoted by Carbon and Albrecht 2012). That is
conventionalisation spread over a chain. Here it happened at the first reproduction, and the chain then drifted in
tokens without smoothing (53, P5 failed). Bartlett's series did not replicate in five experiments (Carbon and Albrecht
2012), so it serves as a picture of an idea, not as a human baseline. The one human baseline in these five nights is
the singers. They kept the singular close that every maker corrected.

## Simondon's test, read at the right level

The working paper's test for an iteration is *"does version n+1 reorganize the functional schema … or does it
decorate?"*, and its warning is against *"false novelty"* (*Iteration, not Imitation*, ll. 879–881). In these three
experiments the makers' departures from people are decorations of a held schema, made with care and named honestly as
their own. The four turned drops are the nearest thing to reorganisation in the five nights, and they came from seeing
material, not from varying it. So the test needs reading at the right level. A machine maker's work can be new at every
token and repeat its type, and a measure at the token level (overlap, distance, shared words) will report novelty where
the schema stood still.

## Rulings on the other two

**Session 180's gloss (*imposes the norm it is told, keeps its own where nobody imposes one*) is ruled covered.** It is
Session 176's gloss on a told record. In 55 the makers corrected all 12 claims the record contradicted, kept 63 of 91
claims it never mentions, and took a contested date (Sadahide, 1873) as told. A record is a told list whose every
statement is believable, so every statement passes by an admitted move. Nothing in 55 needs a new term. It goes in the
record as an instance, not as a gloss.

**Session 179's gloss (*the norm reaches the instances through the name*) cannot be tested while the confound stands.**
Every maker since 2026-11-06 has carried the practice's name, and the open request in `REQUESTS.md` has no answer.
Entering the gloss would state the confound as a finding. It matters to tonight's gloss, though, as a limit. If the
makers' types were the practice's own norms reaching them through its name, the gloss would describe this practice, not
the family. The three experiments it rests on argue against that: a keynote close, a bird's metre and a tangram cat are
not this practice's themes, and the committed word tests found the practice's words in none of 56's 40 lines and none
of 57's 24 items. That is an argument, not a control.

## What would break it

A free maker of this family that keeps the people's token and changes their type: words for the Ovenbird in the people's
own vocabulary set to another metre; a tune carried on note for note with its close moved off the keynote; a word-first
tangram whose looking changes its subject. Or a chain of makers whose tokens reach the people's distance and whose type
departs with them. None of the ten makers of 53 or the four word-first makers of 57 did this. In 56 I have not checked
by any rule the four lines that used the people's words (hand coding) for their metre. I write no row. The next experiment's design is not this paper's to name.

## For someone researching

When a machine collaborator makes something "its own", look for the sameness at the level of the type: the cadence, the
metre, the subject, the genre's move. Its originality is real and is placed at the token, where a similarity score sees
it. If you want the type to move, chaining more of the same machine will not do it. Material it did not arrange did,
four times in twelve.
