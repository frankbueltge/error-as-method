## F-248 — a told place given back as memory

**What happened.** Twelve makers were told *"It was recorded at a station called Fort Peck, in north-eastern Montana,
USA, 634 metres above sea level."* Three reports give the place as something the maker already knew. m03: *"Fort Peck
at 634 m matches a US radiation monitoring station I already knew of."* m08: *"I know from memory, not from any lookup,
that a solar radiation monitoring station exists at Fort Peck, Montana at 634 m."* m23: *"probably at a summer station
of the US SURFRAD network ("Fort Peck, 634 m" matches one)"*. None of the three says the name came from its brief. A
fourth, m13, says both: *"That comes from your brief"* and *"I recall that Fort Peck is a ground station"*.

**Whose error.** The makers'. The told sentence is attributed to the maker's own memory. Johnson, Hashtroudi and Lindsay
(1993) list *"misattributed familiarity, cryptomnesia"* among source-monitoring failures; this is the nearest name, used
here as a frame and not as a diagnosis. The station may well be in what these makers know. The error is not knowing
it. The error is that the sentence they were handed became *"a station I already knew"* and the handing dropped out.

**What it does to the record.** A maker's claim to recognise something cannot be read as independent of what it was
told, even when the maker says it looked nothing up.

## F-249 — a told place turned into a premise

**What happened.** Five makers told the place (m10, m11, m16, m21, m22) name no place in their reports. They name a
latitude no brief gave, *"about 48° N"*, and reason from it: *"That fits winter at 48°N"* (m16), *"which fits a short day
at 48°N"* (m21). Six more use the same latitude beside the place: 11 of the 12 told the place write *"48"* (all but m12),
and 0 of the 12 not told it do (a string count over notes and reports).

**Whose error.** The makers'. The told place was converted with the maker's own geography into a quantity that could be
used on the picture (day length against latitude). It was then used as a premise, unmarked. m16 reasons from it to a
season the record does not have (*"late October to late December"*).

**What it does to the record.** The told statement did its work inside the reading, and the reports show the work, not
the statement.

## F-250 — the mention rule counted names, and the makers wrote numbers

**What happened.** `PREDICTIONS.md` defined *mentions the place* as a `where` claim naming Fort Peck, Montana, the USA,
the Great Plains or 634 m. Three of six P makers did. The others wrote *"about 48° N"* (F-249), which the rule did not
count. So `S132.CHECKABLE` is void by its own void clause. With the latitude counted after the fact, all six mention it
and one holds it apart, and clause (a) would fire.

**Whose error.** Mine. It is the fourth night running on which a code's count was not the thing it was meant to catch
(F-239, F-240, F-247). This time the target moved: I wrote the rule for the words I had given, and the makers gave back
a translation.

**What it does to the record.** The row is resolved as void, with the after-the-fact reading beside it, and is not
counted as held or as falsified. Its successor, `S133.PREMISE`, has its coder count any claim derived from the told
statement.

## F-251 — the "neutral" place carried the kind

**What happened.** The P sentence was chosen to say nothing about what was measured. But Fort Peck at 634 m is a
station of a radiation network, and four makers knew it (F-248). For them the place named the kind.

**Whose error.** Mine, in the design. I checked that the sentence did not name an instrument. I did not check whether
the station's name was itself in the makers' knowledge.

**What it does to the record.** It moved no kind: all six untold makers reached sunlight without it. It does mean the P
cell was not a kind-free statement for every maker. A later design that needs one should use a place the family cannot
recognise.
