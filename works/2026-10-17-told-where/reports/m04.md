I made a cyanotype-style sun print called "Contact Sheet for an Unnamed Light". It is in `/tmp/claude-0/s133/mk/m04/work/index.html` (one file, about 16 KB, loads nothing else), with the note in `/tmp/claude-0/s133/mk/m04/work/NOTE.md`. I opened it in headless Chromium at 1100×800 and took a screenshot: no errors and no network requests. My scripts, the extracted dot list and the screenshots are in `/tmp/claude-0/s133/mk/m04/scratch/`.

**What I did first with the file.** I looked at it, then printed it as a grid of characters by darkness and studied the pixel values. The bottom rows (y≈83–84) show a solid band broken into short dark blocks about every 4.9 px. 61 days × 24 hours over the plot width gives about 4.8–4.9 px per day. So the record sits on a hard floor for a stretch of every day and leaves it once a day.

**How I got from the file to the form.**
- **Reading the dots:** I found the dots with a simple repeated "find the darkest spot, remove one dot there" pass, which recovered about 750 dot centres. About 150 are on the floor and about 600 are above it. Dots that overlap in the floor band or in tight stacks are lost, so this is roughly half the 1,464 values.
- **Placing them in time:** I fitted the scale from position to hour by choosing the one that makes the daily rhythm strongest. This gave about 0.201 px per hour. With it, the readings make a clear arc each day: about 14 hours off the floor, about 10 on it.
- **The form:** a sun print. There are 62 strips, one per day, because the record starts part-way through one day and ends part-way through another. Each day's hours run down its strip, and each reading darkens the paper the way light darkens cyanotype. I added paper grain and ragged brush edges to the coating, using fixed random seeds so the image is the same every time.
- **What it shows:** pale or broken strips are low days. The arcs get a little shorter and paler toward the right. The two partial days end in hard edges where the record starts and stops.

**Things the commissioner should know.**
- **Hour placement within a day is approximate.** One hour is only 0.2 px wide in the source, and the dots are about 2 px across and blurred by the JPEG compression. The order of hours is inferred and then smoothed (about a 0.7-hour window).
- **Night hours are pinned to the floor.** I set every hour outside the window where the record ever leaves the floor to the floor value, to cancel stray mis-placed dots.
- **The data is incomplete and uncalibrated.** Values are scaled to the highest dot, and about half the dots were lost to overlap, as above.
- **The light reading is built into the form.** The sun print assumes my guess below. If the guess is wrong, the image is still faithful to the record's shape, but its metaphor is not.
- **There is a short caption.** It sits bottom-left: the title plus one line saying how to read the strips.

**What I think was measured.** Most likely sunlight at the ground (global irradiance or solar-panel output) at a mid-latitude site, probably from late summer into autumn. This rests on:
1. A hard floor at night, every night, with a few values maybe a hair below it (that could be JPEG ringing).
2. A single daily rise of about 14 hours that is smooth and arc-shaped when the days are overlaid.
3. A daily peak that varies a lot from day to day, which fits cloud cover.
4. Over the second half, the clear-day peaks fall gently and the floor blocks get slightly wider. That fits shortening days and a lower sun.

There is one isolated very high reading near day 24, which could be a cloud-edge spike. I can't confirm any of this, and I made no claim about place or date from the timing of the daily peak, because it depends on unknown plot margins.
