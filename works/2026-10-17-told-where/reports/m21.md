I built a still image that stacks the 61 days as rows, one point per hour. It's in `index.html`, with its note in `NOTE.md`. My view is that the record is sunlight (incoming solar radiation) measured in autumn, but that's an inference and could be wrong.

**What I did first.** I looked at the image, then printed its grey levels as a character map. The main finding was in the bottom row. The plot's floor is not a smooth line but 61 separate dark runs, one per day. Every day the value drops to the same minimum for a long stretch of hours, then rises above it for the rest of the day.

**How I got to the form.**
1. I fitted the centres of the 61 floor runs: one day spans 4.82 px, so one pixel holds about 5 hours. The record runs from about x=3 to x=297, so the picture has small margins.
2. I found each dot's centre to a fraction of a pixel and converted it to an hour number and a value (its height above the floor). The floor runs placed the middle of each night at hour 6.6 of every day.
3. I gave each dot to its nearest hours and rebuilt the 1,464 hourly values.
4. I drew them as a grid of 62 rows by 24 hours. The first and last rows are partial because the record starts and ends at night. Each row is cut at the middle of the night. Hours with a value are warm lights sized and coloured by value, floor hours are dim blue points, a faint glow shows how uncertain each estimate is, and a thin bar at the right gives each day's sum.

The form makes the daily cycle the subject: one band of light about 10–12 hours wide, narrowing slowly toward the bottom, with day-to-day flicker.

**What you should know.**
- The hourly values are rebuilt, not exact. One pixel spans about 5 hours, and dots with similar values can merge, so the shape within each day is an estimate.
- Brightness is scaled to the 98th percentile of the lit hours, so the strongest few hours are capped.
- The "noon" and "midnight" labels mean the middle of the light and the middle of the night as seen in the data, not clock times.
- The work includes a few stray small marks in the night hours, at most about 6% of the range. They may be JPEG artefacts or real small values; I left them in.

**What I think was measured.** I believe it is sunlight: incoming solar radiation, such as a pyranometer's hourly global irradiance. Several things in the file support this:
- Every day has a long stretch at exactly the same floor value, which looks like zero at night.
- The values rise and fall in an arch once a day, with the largest values in the middle hours.
- The peaks vary a lot from day to day, which fits clouds.
- About 10–11 hours per day are above the floor, which fits a short day at 48°N.
- Across the 61 days the dark runs grow (their darkness rises about 20–25%) and the late days have fewer lit hours, so the nights are getting longer. That suggests autumn, roughly October to November. This part is weaker evidence.

I'm fairly confident it's sunlight; the season is a tentative guess.

The timing points to UTC. The middle of each night falls about 6.6 hours after the start of each 24-hour block. If the record starts at 00:00 UTC, that puts local solar midnight near 07:00 UTC, which is what a station near 106°W would expect. This also rests on an assumption, that the record starts at midnight UTC.
