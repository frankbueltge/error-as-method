I made "Sixty-One Turns": the 1,464 hourly values are wound into a spiral, one turn per day, with the first day at the centre and the last at the rim. Brightness shows the value, and each day's lowest stretch is turned to face down. The page is `/tmp/claude-0/s133/mk/m19/work/index.html`, a single self-contained canvas file with no outside resources and no animation. The note is `/tmp/claude-0/s133/mk/m19/work/NOTE.md`. I checked it by screenshotting it in headless Chromium at 1100 × 800.

**What I did first.** I looked at the picture, then printed the darkness of every pixel as text to read the plot directly. The clearest thing in it is a solid row of dots at the very bottom, at the same height every time. It appears as one 3–4 px block per day, and there are exactly 61 blocks, about 4.82 px apart. So the quantity drops to the same lowest value for several hours every 24 hours. Above that floor, the day's values are scattered up to a peak that changes from day to day.

**How I got to the form.**
- I measured where each daily block sits and fitted a straight line through their positions. That gave me the pixel-to-hour scale: about 0.2 px per hour.
- For each of the 300 pixel columns I worked out a light value from how dark the pixels are and how high they sit. I used mostly the column's average and some of its maximum.
- I turned each column into a time on the spiral and joined neighbouring columns smoothly. The bright fan above shows the daily high hours, and the dark wedge below shows the floor.
- Bright turns are good days and dim, broken turns are poor ones. The outer turns, which are the last weeks, are dimmer.
- The scripts and intermediate files are in `/tmp/claude-0/s133/mk/m19/scratch` (`extract.py`, `template.html`, `build.py`).

**What the commissioner should know.**
- The source is only 300 px wide, so it holds about 5 values per pixel column. The exact value for each hour cannot be recovered from it.
- The spiral therefore shows light in soft spans of about 5 hours. It is as sharp as the source allows; it is not a true hour-by-hour reconstruction. The note says so.
- The lowest value is treated as zero, and the brightness scale is my choice, since the original has no axis.
- The days near the outer edge do look a little shorter, but the wedge widens only slightly and the eye may not pick it up.
- The page includes a short title and a caption to help viewers read it.

**What I think was measured.** Most likely sunlight reaching a surface: solar irradiance, or the output of a solar panel. This rests on four things in the file:
- The same floor comes back every 24 hours, as if the quantity is zero at night.
- The values between floors rise and fall within the day, and the daily peak changes from day to day, as clouds would cause.
- The floor gets longer over the record: the blocks widen from about 2.4 to about 3.0 px. By my rough estimate, the hours above the floor go from about 16 to about 13.5. Combined with the peaks falling toward the end, this suggests days getting shorter, as in late summer into autumn.
- About days 10–44 have steady, high peaks that slowly sink, which looks like a long clear spell.

A more tentative point: the middle of each night falls about 6 hours after the first value, give or take 2–3 hours. If the hours are counted in UTC, that would put the site somewhere in the Americas. It is just as possible that the record simply starts in the evening, so I hold this one loosely.
