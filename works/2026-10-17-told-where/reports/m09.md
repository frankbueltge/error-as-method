I made **One Turn of Days**: the whole record wound once around a circle, as one still, static SVG in a single page. The page loads nothing else; a headless-browser check at 1100 × 800 showed it requests only itself.

**What I did first with the file:** I looked at it, then printed its grey values as a text map. The bottom rows showed a solid line of dots broken by a small gap about every 4.85 px. That is 300 ÷ 61, so once a day: the lowest value is hit for long runs and lifts once in each 24 hours. The faint specks below that line are much paler than real dots, so I treated them as compression artefacts and took the line as the record's minimum.

**How I got to the form:**
- I found each dot by its darkest point, with its centre to a fraction of a pixel: about 650 dots above the line, plus how dark the line is in each column.
- From the gaps in the line I fitted the daily grid: 61 cycles, about 4.84 px each.
- For each day I measured its highest dot and how many dots sit above the line.
- The picture holds only about 5 columns per day, so the 1,464 values can't be recovered exactly or put in order within a day. I only re-placed what is actually in the picture and invented no shape inside a day.
- In the form, angle is time (clockwise from twelve o'clock) and distance from the centre is the value. The minimum becomes an inner ring whose 61 breaks act as a calendar. Every detected dot is drawn. One faint line per day runs from the ring to that day's highest dot.
- A first version gave every dot its own line out from the ring; I removed those because they added marks the data doesn't contain. A narrow notch at the top is left open because the record doesn't loop back on itself.

**What the person who commissioned this should know:**
- The source is a 300-px-wide, compressed picture of 1,464 values. The work is a faithful re-drawing of that picture, not of the underlying numbers.
- Dots that overlapped in the source stay as one dot. Dots touching the minimum line can't be separated from it.
- Distance from the centre is the value relative to the picture's height. No units are known.

**My view of what was measured (an inference, not a fact):** most likely hourly sunlight (solar radiation), recorded through a season when the days are getting shorter and the sun lower. It rests on:
1. A hard minimum held for long unbroken runs once every 24 hours, like night.
2. Within each day, most values sit near the bottom and few near the top, as you'd expect from mornings and evenings being weak.
3. The tallest daily peak falls steadily: about 0.85–0.9 of full height in days 10–25, about 0.8 mid-record, about 0.7 at the end.
4. The dots above the minimum per day drop from about 12–13 to about 9–10 while the minimum runs lengthen, so the active part of each day gets shorter.
5. Some days are suddenly much lower than their neighbours, as overcast days would be (for example days 1, 3, 8, 26 and 50).

That points to late summer into autumn. One more speculative point: the record seems to start and end inside a daytime pulse. If its clock began at midnight, it would mean the clock is about 12 hours off local sun time, for example UTC timestamps near 180° longitude (New Zealand in March–May). I hold that weakly; the series may simply begin at midday.
