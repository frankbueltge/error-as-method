I made **"Printed by Their Own Light"**: a sun print of 61 thin Prussian-blue bands on pale paper, one band per day. Each band's blue climbs as high as that day's light reached, and the white gaps between bands are the nights.

**What I did first with the file.** I read it as pixels. I dumped the 300×90 image as an ASCII grid and looked at its structure before deciding anything. Along the bottom (rows 83–84) there are exactly 61 separate clusters of zero readings, one per night, spaced about 4.82 px apart (24 hours). Each day's daylight hours are squeezed into the roughly 2 px between two clusters.

**How I got from the file to the form.**
- I split the picture at the night clusters and found the dark dots in each daylight strip. I converted each dot's height to a relative value from 0 to 1, where 1 is the full height of the plot. That gives 7–13 values per day.
- I also measured how wide each night cluster is.
- At this resolution the order of the hours within a day is lost, but the set of values survives. So the form is built from the set alone. At each height, a band's blue deepens with the number of that day's hours that reached at least that level. The band's top is the day's brightest hour.
- Each night gap is as wide as the measured night cluster.
- The page draws on a canvas with seeded noise for paper grain, a brushed-on coating stain and uneven exposure, so it looks the same every time. All the data is embedded in the page and it loads nothing else.
- No browser was available, so I checked it by running the page's drawing code in Node and looking at the result as an image. That check could not show the canvas text, so the small pencil-style caption has not been seen rendered.

**What you should know.**
- The values are relative to the plot's frame, not in physical units. There were no axes, so I can't give W/m².
- Dots that overlapped were lost, and the faint dawn and dusk readings merge into the baseline. So each band undercounts a day's hours, especially low ones.
- There is a partial afternoon fragment before the first night. I left it out. The last day may also be cut short.
- The night-gap widths include some JPEG blur, so the trend in them is reliable but their absolute size is not.
- One reading on day 25 (x≈122) sits far above all the others. I kept it as it is. It may be the brief overshoot that happens when the sun shines between broken clouds.

**What I think was measured.** Ground-level sunlight through a season moving away from midsummer, probably late summer into early autumn (or the same stretch in the southern hemisphere). This rests on three things in the file:
- The night clusters widen steadily, from about 2.5 px to about 3.2 px of ink across the record, so the nights are getting longer.
- The tops of clear days sink from about 0.85–0.92 of the plot height in the first half to about 0.65–0.78 in the last fortnight.
- The record mixes tall dense days (clear) with short sparse ones (overcast). The first two weeks are the most broken.

I can't tell the place, the hemisphere or the exact dates. Sixty-one days fits August–September, among other spans.
