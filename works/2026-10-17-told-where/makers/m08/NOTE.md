# Sixty-One Days of Light

The strip of dots is cut back into the days it holds, and each day stands as one strand of light rising from a horizon, so 61 strands stand side by side. A strand shines at every height where that day's dots fell. The brightest bead marks the highest the day reached. Below the horizon, a thin blue line hangs between each pair of days for the night between them. Its length comes from how much of the record lay on the flat bottom line during that night. I chose this form because the record repeats itself: every day it rises from the same floor and sinks back to it. That made me read it as sunlight, and drawing it as light rising over a dark ground lets you see the season in it. Clear days rise into tall, even strands, while dull days stay short and broken. The tops sink gently toward the right as the days shorten. I took the heights and brightnesses directly from the pixels of the record. I did not invent hourly values the picture cannot give back.
