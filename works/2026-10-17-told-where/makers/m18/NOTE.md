# Sixty-One Days Exposure

The work is a solargraph that no camera made. A solargraph is a pinhole photograph left open for weeks, in which the sun burns one arc across the paper each day. Here the record makes that burn. Every one of the 61 days is drawn as one sun-path arc over a dark horizon, and the light along it at each hour comes from the value measured at that hour. A clear day burns a full, pale-gold streak. A cloudy day leaves a thin, dim or broken thread. The nights are simply missing, because the sun is below the horizon. The arcs go down and get narrower in the order of the days, because the record itself shows this: the highest midday values fall by about a fifth and the lit part of each day shortens. I chose this form because the quantity is light falling on the ground, and the oldest way to keep a record of light is to let it mark a surface. The picture lets the season and the weather be seen as one exposure, not read as a graph.
