# Sixty-One Noons

The work bends the record into a circle: time runs clockwise from a hair-line at the top, and height above the zero line becomes distance from a small ring at the centre, so every night collapses onto that faint ring and every day rises off it as a burst of light, sixty-one of them side by side. Nothing is re-plotted from guessed numbers; the page carries all 300 × 90 pixels of the original plot and warps them, coloured from ember at ground level to pale white at the highest hours. I chose the circle because the material is made of returns, a day that goes to zero and comes back, again and again, and a straight strip hides that rhythm while a ring makes it the shape itself. Placed this way the record reads as what it measured: a sun drawn by the light it put on the ground, ragged where clouds broke the day up and thinning slightly as the weeks go round.
