# Gathered Light

The record rises from a flat floor once a day and sinks back to it, highest midway between floors, so I read it as daylight, and the work gives each day one ridge, stacked from the first day at the top to the last at the bottom, all centred on noon. The picture is too coarse to say which hour each dot belongs to, but it does show how many of each morning's and each afternoon's hours reached each height. So each half-day's hours are laid out from noon, the strongest nearest: a ridge is as wide as the time its day spent above the dark, and its outline tells how long it spent at every level. Nothing else is invented. The nights stay as cool, flat lines, and the light is coloured by height, so bright, clear days glow pale at the summit while overcast days stay low and ember-dark. Read down the stack, the change across the season shows without any axis: the ridges get lower and narrower, and one otherwise dim day sends up a single needle taller than any clear noon.
