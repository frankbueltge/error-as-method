# Sun Print

The record is rewritten as a cyanotype, the photographic process in which sunlight itself turns paper Prussian blue: each of the 61 rows is one day, its 24 hours run left to right with solar noon at the centre, and every hour is inked in proportion to the light it received, so night stays as bare paper and a bright hour prints deep blue. A narrow strip at the right prints each whole day's sum. I chose it because the measurement and the medium are the same thing — light falling on a level surface and leaving a mark — and because folding the line into a calendar of days lets the two things the dots only hint at become shapes: overcast days show up as pale bands across the print, and the blue body narrows and fades toward the bottom as the days shorten and the sun sits lower. The edges are left soft on purpose: the 300-pixel source cannot place a value to the exact hour, and the print does not pretend that it can.
