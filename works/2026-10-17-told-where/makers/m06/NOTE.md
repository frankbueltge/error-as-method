# Sixty-One Suns

The plot is cut at each midnight and folded into sixty-one half-dials, one per day, laid out in rows like a sheet of small horizons. Each hour becomes a 15° wedge, the angle the sun moves in an hour. Midnight is below the line, noon at the top, morning on the left. Each wedge reaches as far out as that hour's light, and the faint arc marks the brightest hour in the whole record. I chose this form because the flat strip hides what the record holds. In it the days run together, and the season disappears into a blur of dots. Once each day is folded into its own sunrise shape, the record shows two kinds of change. One is weather: a clear day opens as a full, even fan, and a cloudy day comes out short and ragged. The other is the season: across the sheet the fans grow narrower and shorter as the nights lengthen and the noon sun sinks.
