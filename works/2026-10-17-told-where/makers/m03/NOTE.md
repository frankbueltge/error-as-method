# Sun Wheel, Fort Peck

The work takes the strip of 1,464 hourly readings and bends its time axis into a wheel. The sixty-one days run clockwise from the top, and the opening at the top is where the record begins and ends. The zero line becomes an inner ring. Each night's run of zeros is one blue segment of that ring, and each dawn is a small break between segments. Every dot of light in the record keeps its place in time and its height, so a dot's distance from the ring shows how much sun reached the level ground, and its colour runs from ember at low values to white near the maximum. The few readings that dip just below zero at night appear as faint blue specks inside the ring. I chose a wheel because the record is ruled by one cycle, the day, repeated sixty-one times, and a circle lets that repetition be seen as rhythm instead of as a long line. The wheel also shows the slow drift across the whole record without any axes. Clear days near the start reach further out than clear days near the end, and the dawn breaks in the ring grow narrower as the nights lengthen. All marks are drawn from dots and night-band darkness measured directly from the supplied picture; nothing is interpolated into hours the picture cannot resolve.
