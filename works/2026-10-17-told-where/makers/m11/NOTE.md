# Sixty-One Turns

The 1,464 hourly values are laid along one spiral that makes a full turn every 24 hours. The first day is at the centre, the last at the rim, midnight points down and noon points up. Each hour is a stretch of band whose width and warmth follow the value, so the record reads like a sun-disc or a cut through a tree. I chose this form because the file is mostly a daily rhythm: every night the value drops to exactly the same floor, and on clear days the same hours return at the same heights. A spiral stacks those days so that the rhythm, and its slow change, show as a shape. Steady days become clean bright rings. Broken days become dim, ragged ones. The dark wedge where the nights fall widens toward the rim and the noon glow fades a little, which shows the days getting shorter and weaker over the two months. The values were traced back from the dots in the JPEG. Within each day, the order of the hours before and after noon is a reconstruction, but the night lengths, the daily peaks and the cloudy days come from the picture.
