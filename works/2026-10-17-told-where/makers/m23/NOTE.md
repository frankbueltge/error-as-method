# Sixty-One Turns

The 1,464 hourly values form one unbroken line, wound into a spiral with one turn per day. The first day is at the centre and the last is at the rim, and each hour's brightness is the amount of sunlight it held. The disc is laid out like a map seen from above, with north at the top. Each hour sits at the compass bearing the sun would have at that hour, so the light gathers at the bottom under the southern noon and night is a dark wedge at the top. I chose this form because the instrument behind such a record is itself a small level disc facing the sky, and because the spiral keeps the record's two rhythms at once: the days are consecutive, so the line never breaks, and they repeat, so each turn lies against the one before it. Cloudy hours show as dim or broken stretches. The way the days shorten shows as the night wedge slowly widening toward the edge.
