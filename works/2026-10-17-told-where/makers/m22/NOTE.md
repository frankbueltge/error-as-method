# What the Ground Received

The record is cut at local midnight into its days, and each day becomes one sun rising over a level line: every hour of light is a semicircular arc whose radius is that hour's strength, nested inside the others, and the hours that gave nothing lie as small points under the line. I chose concentric arcs because the picture I was given keeps how strong each hour was but blurs exactly which hour each dot belongs to (twenty-four values crowd into five pixels), and a nest of arcs does not depend on that order. It shows only what the record still holds: how many hours had light, how bright they got, and how they were spread. A clear day reads as a full, even fan with a bright rim where the noon hours crowd together. A grey day is a small, dim knot. Across the rows the full suns slowly shrink and the points of darkness beneath them grow in number as the season turns.
