# Sixty-One Exposures

The record is printed the way sunlight prints itself: as a cyanotype, where paper turns Prussian blue only where light has fallen. Each of the sixty-one days is one thin line, stacked top to bottom, running from dawn at the left to dusk at the right with solar noon in the middle; the more light an hour delivered, the deeper the blue, and the nights are left as bare paper. The ragged border is the brushed coating that sat in full sun around the image. I chose the form because the material is itself a measure of exposure, so letting it act as an exposure keeps the subject and the medium the same thing, and because stacking the days shows at a glance what a single line of dots hides: clear days as solid blue bars, cloudy days broken into fragments, and the lit part of each day growing slightly narrower and paler toward the bottom of the sheet.
