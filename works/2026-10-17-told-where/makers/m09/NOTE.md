# One Turn of Days

The record is wound once around a circle: angle is time, clockwise from twelve o'clock across all 1,464 hours, and distance from the centre is the measured value. The record's lowest value, where more than half the hours sit, becomes the inner ring. Its 61 small breaks are the moments each day when the value lifts, so the ring works as a calendar drawn by the data itself. The dots above the ring are the remaining hours, taken one by one from the picture I was given. One faint line per day joins the ring to that day's highest value. I chose a single turn because the material is one daily pulse repeated 61 times, slowly losing strength. A straight line hides that slow loss in the spacing; a circle lets the full reach of the first weeks be compared directly with the shrunken reach of the last ones. A narrow notch is left where the end meets the beginning, because the record does not close back on itself.
