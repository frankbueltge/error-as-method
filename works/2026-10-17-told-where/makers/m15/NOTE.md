# Printed by Their Own Light

The work is a sun print (cyanotype) made from the sunlight it records: sixty-one thin bands of Prussian blue on pale paper, one band for each day, left to right. Each band rises as high as that day's strongest hour reached. At any height its blue darkens with the number of that day's hours that were at least that bright, so the base, which every daylight hour passes, prints deepest, and the colour fades upward to the one or two hours that reached highest. The white between the bands is night, and each gap is as wide as that night's cluster of zero readings in the record, so the gaps widen and the bands narrow as the nights lengthen. I chose this form because in a cyanotype light is the thing that makes the mark, so the days in effect expose themselves. Also, the 300-pixel record packs each day's hours into about two pixels and loses their order. What it keeps reliably is each day's set of values, which is exactly what a "how many hours reached this level" exposure needs. Clear days come out as tall dark bands, overcast days as short pale ones, and the overall drift down and narrower toward the right is the season turning.
