# Contact Sheet for an Unnamed Light

The record rises off a hard floor once in every 24 hours and sinks back to it each night. That looks like daylight, so the work treats the readings as light falling on cyanotype paper. The 1,464 hours are cut into 62 strips, one per day, laid side by side, with each day's hours running down the strip. Each hour's reading is used as its exposure: where the reading was high the paper goes deep Prussian blue, and where it stayed on the floor the coating stays pale. Overcast days show as pale or broken strips. Toward the right, the days shorten and lose some depth. At either end the record breaks off part-way through a day, which shows as a hard edge. I chose a sun-print rather than a chart because if this is light, it can make its own mark. The form also keeps what the plot did not show: how each day was shaped, and how the days follow one another.
