# Sixty-One Days of Light

The dot plot is cut back into days and the days are stacked: each of the 62 rows is one day, midnight on the left, midnight on the right. Each hour is painted as light, running from night indigo through ember and amber up to near-white for the brightest hour in the record. The first and last rows are only partly filled because the record starts in an evening and stops in an afternoon. A thin rule to the right of each row gives that day's total. I chose this form because what was measured is light itself, so the work shows it as light rather than as a height. Laying the days under one another brings out what the original strip hides. The lit part of each row narrows and dims from top to bottom as the days shorten. Cloudy days show up as dark gaps cut into the shape. One isolated white hour, the record's maximum, sits on an otherwise broken day. The hourly values are my estimates, read back from the small, overlapping dots in the plot. They are not the original numbers.
