# Light Rings, Fort Peck

The 1,464 hourly values are wound into a spiral: one turn per day, noon at the top and midnight at the bottom, the first day at the centre and the last at the rim. Along each turn the colour gets warmer and paler as the value rises. The hours that read zero become a thin dark-blue thread, and small pale points mark each value where I found it in the source picture. I chose the spiral because the record's main feature is a daily rhythm: every day it falls flat to the same floor and then rises again. On a straight line that rhythm reads as a fence of repeats. Wound into turns, the days stack like growth rings, and two slower changes show up. The lit fan narrows toward the rim as the zero-hours widen from below, and the noon glow slowly cools from the centre outward. The few dim turns, where a day barely lit up, stand out as dark rings in the wood.
