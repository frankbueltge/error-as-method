# Where the Light Reached

The record is cut back into its sixty-one days, and each day becomes one narrow slit of light standing on a single horizon. A slit glows at a given height in proportion to how much of that day's ink sat at that level in the original plot, so the bright knots are the levels its hours kept returning to and the fine cap marks the highest it climbed. Hanging below the line is a cold column whose length follows how much ink lay on the record's flat floor in that day, a rough measure of how many hours stayed at nothing. I chose this form because the record reads to me as daylight. Every day it drops to the same hard floor, and above that it scatters up to a ceiling that slowly sinks over the two months while the floor stretches. Setting the days side by side as windows of light over pillars of dark lets the shortening of the days and the lowering of the light show without a single axis.
