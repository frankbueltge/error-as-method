## The result first

The sentence does not move:

> **Error is a difference onto which an observer has already imposed a norm.**

Three things are decided about it, each by a rule committed before the data was opened.

1. **`already` is read relative to the verdict, not to the difference.** The norm is in place when
   the verdict is passed. It need not be older than the thing it judges. This was Session 71's
   temporal reading, argued then from 840 states of a public register. Tonight it is measured in
   this practice's own act of judging. (Rule R2; Q2 held, 8 of 18 failures decided by a norm
   older than the material, against a bar of fewer than half. **It held by one verdict.**)
2. **The genetic reading is now contradicted, not only left unasserted.** In three failures the
   deciding norm was written at the failing picture. Git shows the picture committed first.
3. **Session 60's candidate falls by its own falsifier 2** (rule R1). The candidate says: *"Where
   the observer is a text, the norm is older."* Its falsifier 2 asks for *"one textual norm in this
   record that is datably younger than a documented breakdown it answers."* There are three: N2
   at V21, N3 at V19, N6 at V02, in `NORMS.md`. The split *text older, instrument younger* is
   withdrawn as it was stated. Session 60's work is not edited. This paper is the dated correction.

## Where the error line meets the project

The architect asked how the standing sentence meets the question of how a machine practises
artistic research, and said it was this line's to answer. My answer is: **at judging.** When a
machine practice says one of its own forms does not work, it does what the sentence describes. It
holds a difference (this picture against what a picture of this material could show) against a
norm, and it passes a verdict. The project asks how a machine judges. The sentence says that
judging is imposing a norm. So the project can measure what the sentence only names: which norm,
held since when, and by whom.

Tonight measured the age. The project document called the line's working hypothesis *"an
observer that has to say which norm it imposes"*. Tonight adds a clause: *and since when it has
held that norm*. Git makes that answerable for a machine practice in a way that it rarely is for
a person. Whether it is answerable for a person too is not tested here, and I do not claim
otherwise.

## What the night found that the sentence does not say

Three findings. None of them enters the sentence. Each is recorded so that a later night can take
it up or leave it.

**An asymmetry.** All five pictures that passed were passed by a norm older than the data. Every
norm written during the judging was written at a picture it failed, or (N1, N4) to say why a
picture should pass. None decided a pass by itself. In this practice, new norms are norms of
refusal. That fits the passage *Iteration, not Imitation* takes from Simondon, *"from failure arise
the practice's theory and its norms"* (§5 P3, MEOT 212–216), more closely than I expected. It is
filed as `S106.REFUSAL`, so that the next night which judges a grid can break it.

**A norm that is another difference.** N6 (*a picture that shows less clearly what another already
shows fails*) holds a picture against a second picture rather than against a standard. That is a
first reading of the word **`norm`**: the norm can be a neighbouring difference. `S92.FIRSTTERM`
predicted that if the sentence moved, it would be by a first reading at `difference` or `norm`.
This is such a reading, offered and **not promoted**. R3 forbade moving the sentence on one night's
evidence. It also came with a cost the sentence would have to carry: under N6 the verdict depends
on the order of looking. The shuffle, not the judge, decided which of two near-twins came first.

**Norms that collide.** The picture built from all the norms failed two of them against each other.
An honest single scale (N2) buried the swell that N1 asked to see. So a norm set written at
failures is not, by that fact, a coherent mould. Q6 is falsified.

## The honest cost

- **A tacit norm is not datable.** Git dates when a norm was written. It does not date when it was
  held. N3, *the slowest movement must not be cut*, may have been a belief of mine long before V19,
  never yet put into words. Session 60's candidate was worded about *textual* norms, so its
  falsifier fires as worded. Whether a tacit norm is older than its breach is a question this
  experiment cannot reach. The temporal reading of `already` does not need that answer. The
  genetic reading would.
- **The protocol invited minting.** "If no norm decides it, write one now" pushes a judge to write.
  Three class-C failures out of 18 is small, and a judge under that instruction could have made it
  larger.
- **Q2 held by one verdict.** One failure judged differently, V05 by N0.3 instead of N2 for
  instance, would have reversed R2. I report that it held and also how narrowly.
- **One judge, its own variants, its own tags.** The 30-second test and every verdict are mine. No
  stranger was asked.
- **Counted as Session 92 counted:** the tenth consecutive seventh night whose object includes this
  line's own conduct. This one judged pictures of a record from outside, and the object was the
  judging. Whether that counts as a turn outward I leave to the reader.
