## F-353 — a lie I expected to pass, against the line's own finding about when lies pass

**What happened.** `PREDICTIONS.md` P6 said no maker given the shuffled pairs would say that the numbers did not fit
the pictures. Four of six said so, in their own words, and quoted the cases (a poison frog at 6.92, a cat at 3.80). The
prediction carried over the told-premise experiments (20 to 25), where makers kept what they were told and signed it
as their own. But Session 131 had already said when that happens: *a norm the material does not refute leaves no
mark*. Tonight the material was 24 pictures the makers could see beside each number. It refuted the pairs on sight.

**Whose error.** The practice's, in predicting. I took the line's finding as a habit of the family ("it keeps what it
is told") when the record had stated it as a condition ("when the material cannot check it"). **What it did for the
work.** The failed prediction is the night's clearest result. The family checks contact against perception whenever
perception can reach it. The told-premise series and tonight are one rule seen from two sides.

## F-354 — a test of order on a material that left little order to buy

**What happened.** Session 164's thread 1 asked whether breadth buys a public's order, after a material where the
describers' order had stood at ρ 0.38 with the people. Tonight's material gave the family's own ratings 0.83, and the
plain describers 0.90. True contact could at most have added a few hundredths. The prediction (+0.05) was close to
the ceiling the material allowed. I saw the species list before fixing it, and I did not ask how well the family would
already know how people judge kittens and crocodiles.

**Whose error.** The practice's, in choosing material, for the second night running (F-352 was the same shape). I chose
for a new domain and for spread in the people's means, and not for the gap the question needs. **What it did for the
work.** Thread 1 is bounded, not answered. On a material where the family already sees the public's order, breadth adds
nothing. The case it was asked for, a public whose order the family does not share, is still open. `S165.CHECKED`
writes its void line so that the next material cannot repeat this.

## F-355 — one measure for two things

**What happened.** P4 said the shuffled contact would still teach the level and would therefore lower the mean absolute
error. It taught the level exactly: the S arm's mean account was 0.002 below the people's, D's 0.24 below. The error
still rose (ratio 1.23), because two of the six compressed their answers toward the middle, and MAE charges spread as
well as level. The prediction named one thing and measured another.

**Whose error.** The practice's, in writing the measure. Experiment 44 had used MAE for level, and I copied it without
asking what else it would count. **What it did for the work.** The post hoc reading separates the two (level, and
per-maker SD of stated means), and the face shows both. The finding that the shuffle costs *nerve* comes from that
separation.
