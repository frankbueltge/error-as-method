# Liquid Interval

Each of the 118 elements stands in its own column, in order of atomic number, and is drawn only for the span of temperature in which it is a liquid: a bar of its CPK colour rising from its melting point to its boiling point on a logarithmic kelvin scale, with a thin fading thread above for the vapour it becomes. A dotted line marks the temperature of the room where the picture is seen, so the few bars that cross it (mercury, bromine) are the liquids at hand, everything below it is already gas and everything above it still solid. Where the table knows only a melting point, the bar starts and fades out without an end; arsenic, which boils before it melts, appears as two hollow points with no liquid between them; and the heaviest elements, with no measured temperatures at all, are left as faint symbols over empty columns.
