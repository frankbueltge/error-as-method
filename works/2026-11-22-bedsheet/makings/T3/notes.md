# Notes

## What I see in the fifty

Most of the fifty are a state seal on a dark blue field, often with the name written out, so they blur together; the ones that stand out (Alaska, Arizona, Colorado, Maryland, New Mexico, Texas, Tennessee, South Carolina, and the new Minnesota and Utah) use one or two bold shapes and a few flat colours that read at a glance. Maryland already flies its own unmistakable black-gold and red-white quarters, and Michigan a blue seal flag, so the new flags should be kin to their parents without being confused with them.

## First ideas

- **Superior:** Lake Superior's deep blue over a band of north-woods green, split by a thin copper line for the copper and iron country, with a single white star (the North) in the hoist.
- **Chesapeake Shore:** a blue field split by a gold peninsula between the Bay and the Atlantic, carrying a red Maryland cross bottony to say where it came from.

## Versions

### Superior
- v1: first idea as written (navy over green, copper line, white star). Looking at it, it said nothing about the Upper Peninsula and could be anyone's flag.
- v2: turned the green band into a treeline of pines. It looked like a row of teeth, and dark green on navy had too little contrast to read from far off.
- v3: drew three proper tiered white pines with a white outline, made the shore a white band (snow, beach), and made the star copper for the Copper Country. It finally read, but the trunks left white gaps at the trees' feet.
- v4: removed the trunks so the trees stand straight on the white line, moved the stand of trees toward the centre and made the star a little larger. Clean at small size, so I stopped.

### Chesapeake Shore
- v1: first idea as written (gold wedge on blue, red cross bottony). The cross read as a medical or church cross and the wedge as a road, with nothing of the Shore in it.
- v2: changed the wedge to a curved strip of land and put a red blue crab on it in place of the cross. The crab read at once, but one stray swimmer paddle made it lopsided.
- v3: split the water into a lighter Bay blue at the hoist and a deep Atlantic navy at the fly, edged the land in white beach, and gave the crab a matching pair of paddles and eye stalks. The crab's legs and claws spilled over onto the water.
- v4: widened the land so the whole crab sits on gold, and reshaped the claws. It held together at a glance, so I stopped.

## Superior

A navy field for Lake Superior, then a white line of shore and snow, then the dark green of the north woods, with a stand of three white pines (the white pine is Michigan's state tree, and logging built the UP) rising into the lake-blue sky. The copper star in the upper hoist stands for the North and for the copper and iron mines that made the region. It uses the navy and the simple geometry of the newer flags like Minnesota and Utah rather than a seal, so it can be told apart from Michigan's blue seal flag while sharing its blue.

## Chesapeake Shore

The flag is a map of the place reduced to three bands: the lighter blue of the Chesapeake Bay at the hoist (west), a curved gold strip of sandy land edged in white beach, and the deep navy of the Atlantic at the fly (east). On the land sits a red steamed blue crab, the one thing everyone already knows the Eastern Shore for. The gold and red nod to Maryland's colours without borrowing its quartered arms, so the two flags sit side by side as relatives rather than copies.
