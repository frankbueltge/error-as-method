# Notes

## First ideas

- **Superior:** A deep lake-blue upper field over a dark pine-green lower band, split by a white wave line, with a copper-colored star in the upper hoist for the copper and iron country of the peninsula.
- **Chesapeake Shore:** A bay-blue field with a single white skipjack sail rising from the lower hoist, and a thin gold-and-black band along the bottom nodding to Maryland's heritage.

## Versions

### Superior

- **v1:** First build of the idea. Looking at it, the small copper eight-point star was low in contrast on the navy and read too much like Minnesota's star; the copper idea was lost.
- **v2:** Moved copper into its own stripe between the white shoreline and the green, and made the star a white four-point north star with a long upper ray. Better, but the many small ripples looked like a squiggle rather than a lake.
- **v3:** Replaced the ripples with one broad swell rising at the hoist and enlarged the star. The land now reads as a shore under a northern sky; this is the one I would hand over.

### Chesapeake Shore

- **v1:** First build. The boat floated above the stripes, the jib-and-main rig looked like a generic marina logo, and the thin gold/black bands were weak.
- **v2:** Tried a black-and-gold checked band for Maryland's colors and a raked skipjack rig on a white hull. The checks read as a racing or taxi flag, so they had to go.
- **v3:** Put the boat (now with a black hull) in front of a gold sun setting on the bay horizon, with white water lines below. Much stronger; the straight water lines looked like stripes and the two sails overlapped awkwardly.
- **v4:** Made the water lines into waves and opened a clear gap between jib and mainsail so the rig reads at a small size. This is the one I would hand over.

## What I made and why

**Superior.** A navy field for Lake Superior, which lies to the peninsula's north, over a dark pine-green land rising in one broad swell from the hoist. Between them run a white line (the shore, the surf, the snow) and a copper stripe for the Copper Country and iron ranges that built the place. A white four-point star with a long north ray sits above, for the northernmost land of the new state and the North Star over the lake. It uses four flat colors and no seal or lettering, so it can be drawn from memory and does not look like any of the fifty seal-on-blue flags.

**Chesapeake Shore.** A bay-blue field with a gold sun setting on the horizon, since from the Eastern Shore the sun goes down over the Chesapeake. In front of it sails a skipjack, the Bay's working oyster boat, with white sails and a black hull, over white waves. Gold and black are kept from Maryland's Calvert colors so the new state shows where it came from, without copying Maryland's busy quartered arms. The design is a single silhouette against a sunset, readable at a distance and unlike any existing state flag.
