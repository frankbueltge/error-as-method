# Notes

## What I see in the fifty

Most of the fifty are a state seal on a dark blue field, and at thumbnail size those blur into one another; the ones that read from across the room are the few built from a bold geometric idea in two or three flat colours (Texas, Colorado, New Mexico, Arizona, Alaska, Tennessee, the new Minnesota and Utah). Red, white, blue and gold dominate, green is rare (Washington alone), and Maryland is the one flag that is pure heraldic pattern.

## First ideas

- **Superior:** horizontal bands of Lake Superior blue over north-woods green, divided by a thin copper stripe for the Copper Country, with a single white north star in the blue.
- **Chesapeake Shore:** the Shore is the land between two waters, so a gold-sand band of land standing between blue on the bay side and blue on the ocean side, carrying a red crab as its single charge.

## Versions

### Superior
- **v1:** First idea as written (blue over copper stripe over green, white five-point star). Looking at it: clean but anonymous, it could be any small country's flag and nothing in it says lake or north woods.
- **v2:** Turned the top of the green into a jagged line of pines rising into the blue and made the star an eight-point north star. Looking at it: now unmistakably northern, but the copper stripe under the trees read as a road, and the blue read as night sky with no lake anywhere.
- **v3:** Dropped the copper, kept the dark blue as the northern sky, and put the lake itself across the bottom: a lighter blue band behind a white shoreline line. Looking at it: the picture reads in one glance (star, woods, shore, lake), but the dark green trees nearly vanished against the navy.
- **v4:** Brightened the green so the treeline separates from the sky at thumbnail size. Looking at it: the silhouette holds at small size; this is the one I would hand over.

### Chesapeake Shore
- **v1:** First idea as written (bay blue, sand-gold band, ocean navy, red crab). Looking at it: blue-gold-blue verticals are too close to Barbados, the two straight edges say nothing about the place, and the crab's claws read as arrows.
- **v2:** Gave the bay side a wavy edge and the ocean side a straight one, and redrew the crab with a spined blue-crab carapace and rounded notched claws. Looking at it: the crab is now clearly a blue crab, but the wave edge was regular like a curtain hem and the crab was small.
- **v3:** Made the bay edge irregular and enlarged the crab. Looking at it: the edge became hooked sawteeth that read as flames, and the larger crab crowded the bay edge.
- **v4:** Cut rounded coves into the land, added a thin white surf line on the straight ocean edge, and shrank the crab back to fit. Looking at it: the surf line works, but the coves read as mechanical scallops, like a bite mark.
- **v5:** Replaced the coves with four tapering river mouths (the Chester, Choptank, Nanticoke and Pocomoke) cut into the land from the bay side, widened the gold band and recentred the crab. Looking at it: the ragged-bay, straight-beach contrast now tells the shape of the place, and the crab sits clear of both edges; this is the one I would hand over.

## What I made and why

**Superior.** A north-country scene reduced to four flat shapes: a white eight-point north star in a navy northern sky, a green line of pines, a white shoreline, and the light blue of Lake Superior across the foot. Most of the fifty are seals on blue that blur together at a distance; this joins the smaller set of flags that read from across a room, beside Utah and Alaska, and it uses green, which almost none of the fifty do. The star says "the north" (and is a quiet kin of Alaska's), the pines are the woods that cover the Peninsula, and the lake that gives the state its name holds up the whole flag. I dropped the copper stripe of my first idea because it muddied the picture; Superior's story is lake, woods and north.

**Chesapeake Shore.** A vertical band of sand-gold land between two waters: the lighter Chesapeake Bay on the hoist side, cut by four river mouths, and the dark Atlantic on the fly, met by a straight white line of surf. That contrast between a ragged bay side and a clean barrier-beach side is the actual shape of the Shore, so the flag is a map without being a map. On the land sits a red Chesapeake blue crab, the one emblem everyone already ties to the place; red and gold also nod to the Maryland flag the region is leaving, without repeating its pattern. Its white shoreline makes it a sibling of Superior, two new water states joining at once.
