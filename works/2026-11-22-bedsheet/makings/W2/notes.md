# Notes

## First ideas

- **Superior:** A deep Lake Superior blue over a dark pine green, split by a thin white line for the shore, with a copper-coloured north star in the blue for the copper country and the far north.
- **Chesapeake Shore:** A white skipjack sail (a tall triangle) rising from the hoist on bay blue, with a gold band of marsh grass along the bottom, nodding to Maryland's gold without copying its arms.

## Versions

### Superior
- **v1:** The first idea as written: blue over green, a thin white line, a copper eight-point star in the canton. Seeing it, it was clean but murky and generic, two dark fields that could belong anywhere.
- **v2:** I lightened the blue and the copper and turned the straight shoreline into a row of pine tops edged in white, to say "north woods". Seeing it, twelve small teeth read as a saw blade, busy and a little aggressive.
- **v3:** I cut it to six larger peaks, which read as a stand of pines (or the Porcupine hills) rather than teeth, and moved the star up to sit centred in the blue. Seeing it, it was simple, legible at small size, and unlike any other state flag, so I stopped.

### Chesapeake Shore
- **v1:** A white sail with a dark mast on bay blue over a gold band. Seeing it, the sail floated in mid-water with no boat under it, and the fly half was empty.
- **v2:** I dropped the mast and sat a bigger sail on the gold. Seeing it, it read as a shark fin or a wedge rather than a sail.
- **v3:** I added a jib and a black hull above the gold, so it unmistakably reads as a sailboat on the water off the shore. Seeing it, it was legible but crowded against the hoist, leaving the fly empty.
- **v4:** I centred the boat and gave it more height. Seeing it, it was balanced but a generic yacht icon that any coastal state could fly.
- **v5:** I made it a skipjack, Maryland's oyster boat: a strongly raked mast, a long low hull, and a bowsprit carrying the jib forward. Seeing it, it reads as a particular working boat and the flag holds together, so I stopped.

## What I made and why

**Superior.** A blue field for Lake Superior, a dark green lower field for the forest that covers most of the peninsula, divided by a white-edged line of six pine peaks that stands for the shoreline and the snow. A copper eight-point star sits in the canton: copper for the Keweenaw copper country that made the region, and a north star because this is the northernmost ground of the old Midwest. It uses no seal and no lettering, has four colours, and can be drawn from memory, which helps it stand apart from the many blue seal-on-a-bedsheet state flags it will fly beside.

**Chesapeake Shore.** A white skipjack under full sail on bay blue, above a gold band for the marshes and farm fields of the Eastern Shore. The skipjack, with its raked mast and bowsprit, is the oyster dredger that defined the Shore's watermen, and it is specific to this bay rather than any coast. The black hull and the gold band quietly carry Maryland's Calvert black and gold, so the new state keeps a family tie to the one it left without copying its arms. Like Superior, it has no seal or text, uses few colours, and reads at a distance as one clear picture.
