# Notes

## What I see in the fifty

Most of the fifty are a seal or crest on a dark blue field, and at the size of a thumbnail they blur into one another; the ones that stand out (Texas, Colorado, New Mexico, Alabama, Maryland, Ohio, Arizona, Alaska, the new Minnesota and Utah) use two or three flat colours and one large, simple shape. Proportions vary (from nearly square to long), and red, white, blue and gold dominate, so green, copper or teal would stand apart.

## First ideas

- **Superior:** a big field of Lake Superior blue over a band of dark pine green, with a copper star (copper and iron country) high on the hoist; the lake and the forest are what the Upper Peninsula is.
- **Chesapeake Shore:** bay blue and marsh gold split by a low, flat shoreline, with the white sail of a skipjack, the Bay's oyster boat, standing on the water; a quiet nod to Maryland's black and gold without copying its quarters.

## Versions

### Superior

- **v1:** blue field, sawtooth green treeline, copper star. Looking at it, the dark blue and dark green ran together into a murky field and the sawtooth read as a saw blade, not trees, so I made the next one separate them.
- **v2:** lighter blue, a white horizon line, three stepped pines on the fly, brighter copper star. It read clearly at last; the white edging on the pines, made from scaled copies, left ragged flecks at the branch tips.
- **v3:** redid the white edging as a stroke. Each pine got its own outline, so the cluster looked like a cartoon logo with lines inside it.
- **v4:** dropped the outline on the pines and kept only the white horizon. Without it the green trees sank into the blue and became a blob, so the white edge was needed after all.
- **v5:** pines moved closer so they overlap, and the whole woods (ground and trees) got one white outline drawn as a single shape. It is one clean silhouette with no lines inside. I would hand this one over.

### Chesapeake Shore

- **v1:** gold over blue with a black shoreline stripe and a white sailboat. The white sail on gold had too little contrast, the black stripe looked like a stray band, and the boat was generic clip art.
- **v2:** dropped the stripe, made the boat a black silhouette on the gold (Maryland's black and gold), added wave lines. Much stronger, but the mast raked forward (a skipjack's rakes aft), the mast stuck up past the sail and the bowsprit looked like a loose stick.
- **v3:** redrew it as a skipjack: long low hull, mast raked aft, leg-of-mutton mainsail, jib run out to the bowsprit, one wave line. It read right, but the boat floated above the water instead of sitting in it.
- **v4:** raised the waterline so the hull sits in the bay and tidied the bowsprit. I would hand this one over.

## What I made and why

**Superior.** A field of Lake Superior blue over the dark green of the North Woods, divided by a white line that is both the shoreline and the snow. Three pines rise from the woods on the fly and break the horizon, and they share the same white edge, so that the land reads as one shape. In the upper hoist is a copper star for the Keweenaw copper country and for a state in the far north. Most of the fifty are blue fields with seals, and very few are green, so the green and copper set Superior apart and still sit easily beside them. The design is plain enough to draw from memory: blue, white line, green, three pines, a star.

**Chesapeake Shore.** A skipjack, the sailing oyster dredger that still works the Bay, in black on a gold field, sitting on a blue band of water crossed by one white wave. The black and gold are Maryland's Calvert colours, which keeps the tie to the state the Shore came from without copying Maryland's quartered flag. The flat gold over low water is the Eastern Shore itself: a level horizon and wide sky over marsh and bay. A single bold silhouette carries at a distance and is easy to tell apart from the seal-on-blue majority.
