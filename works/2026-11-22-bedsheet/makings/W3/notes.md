# Notes

## First ideas

- **Superior:** a deep lake-blue field over a dark pine-green band, with a single copper-colored star in the blue: the big lake, the north woods, and the copper country.
- **Chesapeake Shore:** a bay-blue field with a white skipjack sail rising from a low marsh-green shore, the flat land and the working boats of the Eastern Shore.

## Versions

### Superior
- v1: blue lake over green woods split by a white line, copper star in the canton. Looking at it: correct but plain, the straight line could be any flag, and the star was a touch dull on the dark blue.
- v2: turned the white line into a treeline and brightened and enlarged the star. Looking at it: the "trees" were bare spikes and read as teeth or a crown, not pines.
- v3: replaced the spikes with tiered spruces, a row of sixteen with a white edge. Looking at it: it finally read as north woods (the white edge even reads as snow), but sixteen small trees were busy and the white edge was uneven.
- v4: cut to ten larger spruces and drew the white edge as a uniform stroke. Looking at it: much cleaner, but a faint seam showed along the base of the trees and the rounded stroke corners looked soft and cartoonish.
- v5: overlapped the ground band into the trees to kill the seam and made the stroke corners sharp. Looking at it: clean, legible at small size; handing this over.

### Chesapeake Shore
- v1: white sail on bay blue above a wavy marsh-green shore. Looking at it: the boat was sitting on dry land, and a bare triangle reads as any sailboat.
- v2: moved the land to a green hoist bar, added a jib, hull and two wave lines. Looking at it: it became a generic yacht clip-art icon, and the green bar felt unrelated.
- v3: dropped the waves and the bar; drew a true skipjack (raked mast, big leg-of-mutton main with long boom, jib, bowsprit) on a white waterline over deeper water. Looking at it: the right boat at last, but too small in the field.
- v4: enlarged the boat. Looking at it: the masthead nearly touched the top edge, too cramped.
- v5: scaled the boat back to sit comfortably with room above. Looking at it: balanced and unmistakable; handing this over.

## Final flags

**Superior.** A dark lake-blue field for Lake Superior, the water the new state is named for, over a band of dark spruce green whose top edge is a jagged treeline outlined in white, the north woods under snow, which is what most of the Peninsula is for half the year. A single copper star in the canton stands for the copper (and iron) country that built the place, and for the north star over a far-northern state. Three colors plus white, no lettering, and among the fifty it is the only one that reads as a forest edge at a lake.

**Chesapeake Shore.** A skipjack under sail, white on bay blue, riding a white waterline above deeper water. The skipjack, with its raked mast and huge leg-of-mutton sail, is the oyster-dredging boat of the Eastern Shore, the last working sail fleet in the country, and its silhouette belongs to nowhere else. I left out crabs, marsh and the Maryland heraldry: the boat alone says the Bay and the watermen, and the two blues keep the flag calm and simple enough to draw from memory.
