# Notes

## First ideas

- **Superior:** a deep lake-blue field over a dark pine-green band, with a single copper-colored star for the north and the copper country of the Keweenaw.
- **Chesapeake Shore:** a white skipjack sail (a plain triangle) on bay blue, rising from a low gold band for the marsh grass and the flat farmland of the Shore.

## Versions

### Superior
- v1: blue over green split by a thin white line, eight-pointed copper star in the canton. First look: clean but dull; the dark blue and dark green sit together with little contrast and the star reads muddy.
- v2: replaced the straight shore with a sawtooth pine treeline and brightened the copper. After looking: the trees read as spikes, not pines, though the north-star-over-woods idea now works.
- v3: drew proper tiered pine silhouettes and lightened the blue so the green stands against it. After looking: clearly pines now, but evenly spaced and identical, like a picket fence.
- v4: varied the size and spacing of the pines into an overlapping, uneven treeline and enlarged the star slightly. After looking: reads as a real north-woods skyline at flag size; handed over.

### Chesapeake Shore
- v1: white sail triangles and a dark mast on bay blue over a flat gold band. First look: the boat sits on the gold as if beached, and the heavy mast splits the sail.
- v2: dropped the mast, added a hull, and put a white wave line between water and gold so the boat floats. After looking: the boat floats now, but the jib overlaps the mainsail in a messy notch.
- v3: separated jib and mainsail with a clean blue gap and added a bowsprit. After looking: tidy but a generic upright sailboat.
- v4: raked the mast aft and reshaped the hull with a raised bow, which is what makes a skipjack a skipjack. After looking: reads as a Chesapeake workboat, not any sailboat; handed over.

## Final flags

**Superior.** A copper north star over an uneven line of dark pines against Lake Superior blue. The Upper Peninsula is defined by three things — the big lake, the north woods, and the copper of the Keweenaw — and the flag holds just those three, in three colors, with no seal and no lettering. The star is the north, the copper and the old mining country at once; the treeline is the silhouette anyone who has driven the UP knows. Among fifty state flags, most of them seals on blue, the treeline and the copper star make it recognisable from a distance.

**Chesapeake Shore.** A white skipjack under sail on bay blue, riding a white wave line above a gold band. The skipjack, the raked-mast oyster boat still worked out of Shore harbors, is the region's most particular emblem; the gold stands for the marsh grass and flat grain fields of the Eastern Shore. It uses three colors and a single silhouette, avoids Maryland's Calvert pattern so it stands on its own, and still reads at small size.
