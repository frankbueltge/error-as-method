## F-158 — two audience questions counted as the author

**What happened.** Tonight counted a declared list of terms in the parts of two interviews spoken by
Hans-Jörg Rheinberger, and excluded the interviewers. The script that split the 2014 *Dynamis*
conversation into turns recognised a new speaker by a label of the form `Name:`. Two turns are
labelled differently: *"From the audience, Emilio Muñoz:"* and *"From the audience, Richard
Burian:"*. The comma put them outside the pattern, so the script kept attributing to Rheinberger
until the next turn it recognised. The first count gave Rheinberger **one** occurrence of `judg`. It
was Muñoz's: *"how do you judge your relationship with them"*.

**What the correction changed.** With the comma admitted to the label pattern, the Rheinberger turns
drop from 5,084 words to 4,910, `judg` from 1 to 0, and nothing else moves. The count that would
have mattered, a `judg` word in the author's own voice, was false. It was caught because the
occurrence was printed with its context before any count was written down.

**Why it is an entry.** A count attributed to an author is a claim about the author. This one
attributed a question to the person it was put to. The only safeguard that worked was reading every
hit in context, and that should be the rule for any term count this line publishes about a named
person, not a habit.

## F-159 — a term counted that was not declared

**What happened.** The pre-registration, committed as `63f572d` before any text was fetched,
declared ten term families. While reading I also counted `wrong`, because the 2025 interview says
*"something going wrong"* and the passage bears on the question. `wrong` is not on the declared
list.

**What is done about it.** The journal reports `wrong` separately and marks it as added after the
reading. The ten declared counts are reported as declared. Nothing is scored on `wrong`.

**Why it is an entry.** It is a minor departure, and it is the kind a reader cannot see unless it
is written down. A count added after the reading is chosen by what the reading found, and it is not
worth more than a quotation.
