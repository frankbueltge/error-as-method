## F-332 — a pattern that counted a sentence it should not have

**What happened.** `posthoc.py` counts the self-describers whose HOW line says that some instances would give the
neutral 5. Its first pattern matched *"5"* or *"neutral"*. It counted all six. O1's line says foods would sit *"near
neutral for bland or processed ones"*, which is about bland foods, not about instances refusing. Reading the six lines
beside the count caught it before anything was written. The pattern now matches *default* or *disclaim*, and it
counts five of six.

**Whose error.** The practice's, in coding. A word list stood in for reading. It was caught because the six lines
were printed beside the count. Had the count gone into the text unread, *six of six predicted the refusal* would have
stood.

## F-333 — a row that counted sides when the answer was a distance

**What happened.** `S158.CENTRE` was fixed on Session 158 from a count of sides: on each diverging key, is the
describers' centre nearer the people's or the family's? Tonight that count came out 23 against 23, with 2 ties, and
the row held by its letter, one key short of falsification. The proportion of the gap that the describers crossed,
computed afterwards, was 0.52. The row could only report *held* or *falsified*. The finding is that the describers
stood half-way, and the row's form could not express it. Session 158's own data, read the same way, would have
given the proportion as well. That was not computed then.

**Whose error.** The practice's, twice. Session 158 fixed the row in the form its post-hoc question had taken. Tonight
`PREDICTIONS.md` carried the row unchanged, as it should, but added no measure of distance beside it. `S159.HALFWAY`
is stated as a distance. Session 158's data are not rescored here, so that a past night's result is not revised from
outside its own predictions. That rescoring is an open thread.

## F-334 — a scaled threshold that made almost every key diverge

**What happened.** The row's rule, 2 points on a 0–100 scale, was scaled by range to the 1–9 scale: 0.16 points. A
median of whole ratings moves in halves, so the rule became *any difference at all*. 48 of 67 foods diverged. That is
enough keys, but many of them differ by half a point. On those, *nearer* and *tied* turn on rounding. `PREDICTIONS.md`
saw that the rule would move in halves. It did not see how many keys that would admit, or how close the count would
then run.

**Whose error.** The practice's, in fixing a measure. The rule was scaled faithfully, so the verdict is the row's
own. A test at a full point was reported beside it and gave 22 of 46, also one short. Both counts sit within one key
of the threshold. The verdict says *held*, but on this evidence it cannot tell the claim from its opposite. That is
recorded here, and the verdict is not changed.
