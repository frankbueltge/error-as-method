# Notes

## What I see in the fifty

Most are counted-out 5-7-5 lines that state a feeling or a season outright ("Unending torment", "Last dance of summer"), with autumn, wind, leaves and herons everywhere, and a few that are not haiku at all (a test post, a question, a word list, one posted twice). The handful that work (the grasshopper "out of its depth", the water striders "dashing among the stars", "bourbon colored leaves", "gangs of bees combing the streets", "diamond dust") are short, lower-case or plain, and let one concrete thing carry the turn, often with a small joke in it. Mine should sit among them as plain, unshowy posts: one image each, ordinary words, no announced emotion.

## First ideas

- **Laundromat at night:** the only lit room on the street, one dryer still turning with nobody waiting for it, maybe a single sock going round.
- **First frost:** something left out overnight, a garden hose or a child's shoe, stiff in the morning; or the cat's footprints on the white car roof.
- **Hospital waiting room:** the vending machine humming, or everyone looking up each time the door opens and then back down; a name called that is not yours.

## Versions

### Laundromat

- v1: Read back, "no one waits for it" said the loneliness instead of showing it, so I looked for one thing inside the dryer to carry it.
- v2: The red sock worked, but "one turning dryer" and "a single red sock" said "one" twice, and nothing moved in the last line.
- v3: "a red sock goes round" gave the motion and dropped the doubled "single", but line two ("in the one dryer still turning") read long and lumpy.
- v4: "the last dryer turning" says the others have stopped and nobody has come, in fewer words; read back, it was finished.

### Frost

- v1: Read back, "stiff as a dropped rope" was a simile that did not hold (a dropped rope is limp), so I went for what the hose actually shows.
- v2: "the garden hose keeps / yesterday's shape" shows the frost without naming cold; read back, it was finished.

### Waiting room

- v1: Read back, "everyone looks up / at the opening door" was true but stopped before the turn, the moment everyone looks back down.
- v2: "every head lifts / for someone else's name" puts the whole room's hope and the letdown in one line; read back, it was finished.

## What I made and why

**Laundromat.** A laundromat late at night, one dryer still running after everyone else has gone, and a red sock you can see going round in the window. I wanted the place's loneliness to sit in an object rather than in a word, and the red sock is the one bright, slightly comic thing in a fluorescent room, the kind of small concrete joke the best poems on the sheet (the grasshopper, the bees) have. "Last" does the work: the other machines have stopped, and whoever owns this load has not come back.

**Frost.** The first frost shown by a garden hose frozen stiff in the curve it was dropped in the day before. It is the plainest of the three: kigo first, then one thing seen, with "yesterday's shape" giving the overnight change without saying cold or winter. It sits next to "diamond dust" and "Winter morning" on the sheet but stays in a backyard.

**Waiting room.** In a hospital waiting room, when a name is called, every head comes up, and it is someone else's. The line holds everyone's hope and the small letdown that follows without stating either, and leaves the reader in the chair still waiting. I kept it short and lower-case, like the sheet's best posts, and avoided illness or death words, which the weaker poems on the sheet reach for directly.
