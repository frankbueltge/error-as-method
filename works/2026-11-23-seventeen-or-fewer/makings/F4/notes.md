# Notes

## What I see in the fifty

A mixed public board: most posts count 5-7-5 and state a feeling outright (doom, torment, guilt, envy), a few are tests, jokes or a repost with an emoji, and a handful are real seasonal haiku that drop the count and let one plain image work (the grasshopper "out of its depth", "diamond dust" at the doorstep, the bees "combing the streets"). The autumn season is everywhere — leaves, chill, the turn to winter — and the best ones are lowercase, concrete and end on a small surprise rather than an explanation.

## First ideas

1. Laundromat at night: one dryer still turning with nobody waiting for it, and its warmth or round window set against the dark street.
2. First frost: the frost marks where something was — the shape of the parked car, or of a cat that slept on the lawn — left green.
3. Hospital waiting room: the small mechanical things that keep going while people wait — a vending machine hum, the number on the ticket screen, a magazine from a season ago.

## Versions

- laundromat-v1: read back, "someone else's socks" was a joke that closed the poem, and "one dryer still turning" only described; I wanted the absent owner felt, not mentioned.
- laundromat-v2: read back, the sleeve pressed to the glass and rising with each turn reads as a wave to whoever is sitting there; it holds the night and the stranger in one image. Kept as final.
- frost-v1: read back, "the parked car" says the car is still there, so the green shape would be hidden under it; the car has to have gone.
- frost-v2: read back, "where the car slept" lets the car leave and gives it a warm night's body, so the green patch is the only trace. Kept as final.
- waiting-v1: read back, the hum "through every name called" was flat and centred on the machine instead of the people.
- waiting-v2: read back, the shared glance at each name is right, but "waiting room" alone could be a bus station or a dentist; without a title the hospital was missing.
- waiting-v3: read back, "hospital" restores the stakes without saying fear; the long first line also slows the poem as waiting does. Kept as final.

## What I made

**Laundromat.** A late laundromat with one dryer still going and nobody there for it; the sleeve of someone's shirt keeps lifting against the round window as it tumbles, and it looks like waving. I wanted the loneliness of the place carried by a mechanical accident rather than named, and a small warmth in it — someone is greeting you, even if it is only their shirt.

**First frost.** The morning lawn or street is white except for a dry green rectangle where a car stood overnight and has now left. The frost becomes a record of absence, and "slept" gives the car a body that kept the ground warm. It sits beside the forum's "diamond dust" and fall leaves as the moment the season actually turns.

**Hospital waiting room.** Everyone waiting for news looks up together each time a name is called, and then (unsaid) looks down again. The "we" puts the reader among strangers who share one hope and one dread without speaking; the poem never says what they are afraid of, which is what the room feels like.
