# Notes

## What I see in the fifty

Almost none count 5-7-5. Most are three short lines, lowercase, very little punctuation (an ellipsis or a dash at most), with a few one-liners. Nearly all work by a cut: a short seasonal or situational phrase on line one ("drought year", "crowded bus", "new prescription"), then a small concrete image that answers it sideways, often with a dry, quiet turn of wit (water from a tailpipe, rain inside the bus, syllables hard to swallow), not with a stated feeling.

## First ideas

1. Laundromat at night: one person left, and the dryers still turning someone else's clothes; the warmth of a stranger's load.
2. First frost: the garden hose stiff in the morning, or the tomatoes picked green the night before lined up on the sill.
3. Hospital waiting room: the TV playing a cooking show nobody watches, or the vending machine and its coil holding the last of something.

## Versions: what I changed after reading each back

- laundromat-v1: read back, "isn't mine" landed as a punchline and stayed with looking; I moved to touch, so the stranger is felt through the heat of their load (v2).
- laundromat-v2: "late laundromat" sounded awkward and vague; "all-night" is the real sign-word of the place and sets the hour without saying it (v3).
- laundromat-v3: read back and left as it is; handed over.
- frost-v1: "keeps its coil" was accurate but only a picture; I let the person in, so the frost fixes a shape someone left behind at the end of the season (v2).
- frost-v2: read back and left as it is; handed over.
- waiting-v1: "no one is watching" said the point flatly and "waiting/watching" was too neat; I kept the TV but gave it one precise silent action (v2).
- waiting-v2: "waiting room" alone could be a dentist's; "ER" places it in the hospital and raises the stakes without adding a word of feeling (v3).
- waiting-v3: read back and left as it is; handed over.

## What I made and why

**Laundromat.** "all-night laundromat / warming my hands / on a stranger's dryer." The night and the loneliness are carried by the place-name and the cold hands; the only company is the warmth of someone else's clothes turning. Like many of the fifty, it lets a plain act stand in for a feeling, and the small intimacy with an absent stranger is the turn.

**Frost.** "first frost / the hose keeps the shape / I left it in." The season-word on line one, then one object answering it, as in "drought year / water drips / from a tailpipe". The frozen hose holds the last careless gesture of summer, and "the shape I left it in" can be read about more than the hose.

**Waiting room.** "ER waiting room / on mute, the chef / tastes and adds salt." The room is all suspension; on the silent screen someone calmly corrects and goes on. It keeps the dry, sidelong humour of "new prescription / all those syllables / hard to swallow", and the salt leaves tears unspoken.
