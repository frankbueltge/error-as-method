# Notes

## First ideas

- **Laundromat at night:** One dryer still turning with nobody waiting for it; someone's clothes tumbling alone under fluorescent light.
- **First frost:** The garden hose left out overnight, stiff in the morning; the change you notice through touch before sight.
- **Hospital waiting room:** The muted TV and the clock; everyone pretending to read while listening for one name.

## Versions: what I changed after reading each back

### Laundromat
- v1: Read back, "laundry tumbling in the dryer" just said what dryers do. There was no turn, so I went looking for one detail instead.
- v2: Narrowed it to a single sleeve against the glass. The image was sharper but it sat still, with none of the motion or the loneliness I wanted.
- v3: Let the sleeve "wave" and changed "glass" to "window" so it reads as a wave from a window. At 2 a.m. the only greeting comes from a stranger's clothes. Read back, I would hand this over: final.

### First frost
- v1: "Stiff where I left it" worked, but "where" only gave a place. The poem needed to show yesterday's careless gesture frozen in place.
- v2: Changed it to "in the shape I left it" and moved "stiff" to the end of line 2 for weight. Read back, it holds: final.

### Waiting room
- v1: "All of us look up" was true but expected. It showed waiting without showing what waiting costs.
- v2: Changed it to a nurse calling a name and everyone else breathing out: relief that it isn't theirs, with a trace of guilt in it. "Nurse" sets the scene, so I dropped "waiting room —". Read back: final.

## What I made and why

**Laundromat at night.** I first pictured an unattended dryer and kept the loneliness, but put it into one small motion: a sleeve rising against the round window as the drum turns, which looks like someone waving. The time, "two a.m.", does the night-work, so the poem can be about being alone in a public place where the only company is the evidence of other people.

**The first frost.** I wanted the season to arrive through touch and through something ordinary I had neglected. The hose, frozen in the loose coil I dropped it in, holds my own gesture from yesterday, so the frost marks the change and also the moment I didn't notice it coming.

**The hospital waiting room.** Everyone there is waiting for the same thing, a name. The poem turns on the breath the others let out when the name isn't theirs. It's a small, honest, slightly shameful relief, and I think it says more about that room than the clock or the TV would.
