# Notes

## First ideas

- **Laundromat at night:** One person alone with a row of dryers, one of which is still turning a stranger's clothes; the warmth and the loneliness together.
- **First frost:** The garden hose stiff on the lawn in the morning, or the last tomatoes blackened overnight; something summer left out that the cold has claimed.
- **Hospital waiting room:** The vending machine hum and a cup of coffee going cold in someone's hands while they watch a door; time measured by the coffee, not the clock.

## Versions

- **laundromat-v1:** Read back, "in the only light" was false (the whole laundromat is lit) and vague, so v2 gives the shirt something to do through the dryer glass instead.
- **laundromat-v2:** Read back, a whole shirt doesn't wave; a sleeve does, so v3 swaps "shirt" for "sleeve" and "at me" for the gentler "to me".
- **laundromat-v3:** Read back and kept; nothing I wanted to change.
- **frost-v1:** Read back, the snake-skin simile was decoration (shed skin is brittle, not stiff) and pulled away from the frost, so v2 lets the frozen hose simply keep the shape it was dropped in.
- **frost-v2:** Read back and kept; the cold shows itself through the hose without being named twice.
- **waiting-v1:** Read back, the cold coffee was the first, most expected image, and "before the door opens" explained the wait, so v2 tried a different measure of time.
- **waiting-v2:** Read back, the floor tiles worked but "by heart" was a pun that winked at the hospital, and the poem was only about one person, so v3 moved to the moment everyone shares.
- **waiting-v3:** Read back and kept.

## What I made and why

**Laundromat.** A late, lit room where the only company is someone else's laundry. The sleeve flapping against the dryer window looks like a wave, which makes the absent stranger briefly present and leaves the speaker's loneliness unsaid. I kept "midnight" up front so the hour does the setting work, and let the last line place the image exactly.

**First frost.** I wanted the frost seen through one ordinary thing summer left out. A frozen garden hose holds whatever curve it was dropped in, so "the shape I left it in" records the speaker's carelessness from yesterday, fixed overnight. The cold is never described; it shows as stillness.

**Waiting room.** Instead of one person's private wait, the poem catches the instant a name is called and every head rises, because each person hopes and fears it is theirs. The dash's cut after "waiting room" sets the place, and the plain last line carries the whole room's suspense without naming it.
