# Notes

## First ideas

- **Laundromat at night:** one dryer still turning for someone who has gone, its round window like a small moon in an empty room.
- **First frost:** the tomato vines that were green yesterday, blackened overnight; the garden ending without a sound.
- **Hospital waiting room:** a cup of coffee someone bought and never drank, going cold while the clock moves.

## Versions: what I changed after reading each back

- **laundromat-v1:** "its round window moon" read as clumsy and explained too much on top of "for no one", so v2 dropped the metaphor and gave the clothes a gesture (a shirt waving).
- **laundromat-v2:** "still" was filler and "the last dryer" was unclear (last in the row, or last running?), so v3 says "the one dryer turning" and narrows the shirt to a sleeve, which is what actually waves.
- **laundromat-v3:** read back and found nothing left to cut; it is the final.
- **frost-v1:** the blackened tomatoes were an expected image, and "yesterday's" was confusing, so v2 moved the loss into a person: the tomatoes I meant to pick.
- **frost-v2:** read back and left as it is; the short last line lands the regret without saying it. Final.
- **waiting-v1:** "waiting room" alone did not say hospital, and "no one drank" was vague about whose coffee, so v2 names the hospital and makes it "the coffee I forgot".
- **waiting-v2:** read back and left as it is; the forgetting carries the worry. Final.

## What I made and why

**Laundromat.** A single dryer running in an empty laundromat at 2 a.m., with a stranger's sleeve waving through the window. I wanted loneliness without naming it: the only motion in the room is somebody else's clothes, and the wave is a greeting that nobody is giving. The time stamp does the work that "night" would have done more vaguely.

**First frost.** The frost itself is never described; only what it cost. "The tomatoes I meant / to pick" leaves the blackened vines for the reader to see, and turns a weather poem into a small poem about putting things off, which is what a first frost catches people at.

**Hospital waiting room.** A coffee bought and forgotten, growing a skin as it cools. Forgetting it is the sign of how much the speaker is thinking about someone else, and the skin marks the time passing without a clock. It also keeps the body quietly in the poem, which suits the place.
