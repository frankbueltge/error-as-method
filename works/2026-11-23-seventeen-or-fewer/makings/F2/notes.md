# Notes

## What I see in the fifty

Most are 5-7-5 by syllable count and say their feeling out loud (doom, torment, guilt, envy), often with capitals on every line, and a few are not haiku at all (test posts, a question, one long line). The ones that hold up are the short, lowercase, concrete ones — the grasshopper "out of its depth", the diamond dust at the doorstep, the bees combing the streets after the storm — where one plain image is set beside another and the feeling is left unsaid. Autumn and weather dominate; people and rooms hardly appear.

## First ideas

1. Laundromat at night: one dryer still turning in an empty room, someone's clothes going round with nobody there to collect them.
2. First frost: the garden hose stiff in the morning, or the dog's breath; something ordinary suddenly showing the cold.
3. Hospital waiting room: the vending machine hum, or a stranger's coat kept on the chair beside them; the waiting shown only through an object.

## Versions

- laundromat-v1: on reading it back, "someone else's socks" was a flat list of facts with no movement, and the image of the dryer was shared out between two lines; I moved to one stranger's garment seen through the round glass.
- laundromat-v2: read back, the shirt "waves" from the dryer window, which gives the empty room a figure and a gesture without saying lonely; nothing to change, so it is final.
- frost-v1: read back, "stiff as a bone" was a stock simile and did the noticing for the reader; I wanted the hose itself to show the cold.
- frost-v2: read back, "keeps its coil" shows the hose frozen in the shape it was left in, plainly and without comparison; final.
- waiting-v1: read back, a humming machine "through the long night" was generic and said the length instead of showing it; I looked for a detail that only long waiting produces.
- waiting-v2: read back, knowing the snack prices shows the hours and the mind's escape from worry, but "waiting room" alone could be a dentist's and "learn" sounded like the first hour; I named the hospital and changed "learn" to "know".
- waiting-v3: read back, "know" makes the prices already memorised, so the wait is long and the reason for it stays unsaid; final.

## What I made and why

**Laundromat.** A late-night laundromat is mostly empty machines and fluorescent light, so I wanted one live thing in it. A stranger's shirt tumbling behind the glass looks, for a moment, like it is waving; the speaker and the owner are both absent from the poem, which leaves the reader alone in the room with the gesture. It keeps the plain, lowercase, image-first manner of the strongest poems on the sheet (the grasshopper, the bees after the storm).

**First frost.** Most of the fifty describe autumn with dancing leaves and colour; I wanted the turn of season in something household and unlovely. A garden hose left on the lawn that will not uncoil in the morning is the kind of small fact you only notice the first cold day, and "keeps its coil" lets the stiffness and the stillness be seen without a simile. It is short on purpose, like "diamond dust" on the sheet.

**Hospital waiting room.** Several of the fifty name fear and dread directly; I wanted the opposite approach, where the worry is never stated. Someone who has memorised the price of every snack in the vending machine has been sitting there for hours and has been looking anywhere but at what they are waiting for. The slight dryness of the line is how that kind of waiting actually feels.
