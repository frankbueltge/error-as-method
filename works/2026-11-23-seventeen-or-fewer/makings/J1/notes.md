# Notes

## What I see in the fifty

Almost none count syllables: they are short, lowercase, mostly three lines (with a few one-liners), built as a short setting phrase (often a season word or an occasion — "drought year", "Mother's Day", "slushy sidewalk") cut against a plain concrete observation, with only dashes and ellipses for punctuation. The best of them let the juxtaposition carry the feeling without naming it ("crowded bus / some of the rain / is inside", "after goodbye— / the bowl of pears / begins to bruise"), and many have a dry, sidelong humor or a small turn in the last line.

## First ideas

- **Laundromat at night:** one dryer still turning after everyone has gone, a stranger's clothes rising and falling in it — the loneliness of something kept warm for nobody watching.
- **First frost:** the dog's water bowl frozen in the morning, or the shape of the dog's body left unfrosted on the lawn where it slept; something present only by what the frost didn't touch.
- **Hospital waiting room:** the fish tank, or a stranger's name called and everyone looking up; the small involuntary hope each time the door opens.

## Versions

- **laundromat-v1:** Read back, it sounded like a counted 5-7-5 postcard, and the red sweater was decoration with no turn; I dropped the lonely-dryer idea for a moment of accidental intimacy instead.
- **laundromat-v2:** Read back, the stranger's sock still warm in my own load did the work without comment; I wondered whether "things" was too vague and tried a stronger last line.
- **laundromat-v3:** Read back, "warm in my arms" turned a sock into a baby and tipped into sentiment and unintended comedy; I went back to v2 as final.
- **frost-v1:** Read back, the dog's unfrosted shape worked, but a dog sleeping all night on the lawn is unlikely and "still" leaned on pathos; I moved to a deer, which really does bed down in yards, and ended on the absent animal.
- **frost-v2:** Read back, it was plain and exact, the green present only where the frost was not; I would hand it over as it stands.
- **waiting-v1:** Read back, "we all look up when the door opens" was true but familiar and general; I looked for one small concrete thing that holds the helplessness.
- **waiting-v2:** Read back, the vending machine returning the dollar again and again held the waiting, the repetition and the sense that nothing here will take what you offer; "giving back" kept a gentle double sense, so I left it.

## What I made

**Laundromat (final = v2).** "all-night laundromat / a stranger's sock / warm among my things." The night laundromat is a place of strangers who never speak, and the sock is the one contact between them, still carrying the warmth of the shared dryer. I kept the last line understated, like most of the fifty, so the closeness stays small and slightly awkward rather than tender.

**Frost (final = v2).** "first frost / the green shape / where the deer slept." The season word stands alone as in many of the fifty, and the image shows the frost by its absence: a green patch the size of an animal that is already gone. Ending on "slept" leaves the deer's warmth and its leaving in the reader's mind, with no comment.

**Waiting room (final = v2).** "ER waiting room / the vending machine / keeps giving back my dollar." Like "questions Alexa / can't answer" and the prescription poems in the sheet, it uses an ordinary, slightly absurd machine to carry something heavier: waiting with nothing to do and nothing accepted. "keeps" gives the duration of the wait, and "giving back" has a faint note of the machine being kind.
