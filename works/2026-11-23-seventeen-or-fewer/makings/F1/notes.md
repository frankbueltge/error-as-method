# Notes

## What I see in the fifty

The sheet is very uneven: most are 5-7-5 counted on the fingers, autumn weather and abstract feeling ("torment", "guilt", "doom") stated outright, with a few lines that are only tests or questions. The ones that hold up (the grasshopper "out of its depth", the bees "combing the streets", the diamond dust at the doorstep) are short, lower-case or loosely counted, and rest on one concrete thing with a small turn. My three should sit among them as plain, concrete poems in that better register, not as showpieces and not as stated emotion.

## First ideas

1. Laundromat at night: one dryer still turning after everyone has gone, with someone's clothes in it; the fluorescent light and the round window.
2. First frost: the garden hose stiff in the morning, or breath on the windshield; something that was soft yesterday is set hard today.
3. Hospital waiting room: the vending machine's hum and a person holding someone else's coat; the waiting is shown by the coat, not said.

## Versions, after reading each back

- laundromat-v1: "someone else's shirts" read flat; it only noted a stranger's laundry, with no relation to me. Rewrote around the minutes a stranger leaves on a dryer.
- laundromat-v2: read back as finished; the kindness is unstated and the last line turns the poem. No change.
- frost-v1: "stiff as yesterday's words" was a simile pushing in a feeling the frost did not earn, the very habit of the sheet I meant to avoid. Kept the hose, dropped the simile.
- frost-v2: "keeps the curve of the lawn" did not make sense on reading (the curve is the hose's, not the lawn's). Changed it to the shape I left it in.
- frost-v3: read back as finished; the stiffness is shown, and a trace of yesterday's person is in it. No change.
- waiting-v1: "folded, then unfolded" showed fidgeting but stayed on the hands; it did not show how long the wait had been. Moved to the coat losing its warmth.
- waiting-v2: read back as finished; time passing and the absent person are both in "no longer warm". No change.

## What I made and why

**Laundromat.** "late laundromat / my shirts dry on the minutes / a stranger left." Laundromats at night are mostly about being alone, and the cliche is the lone dryer turning. I wanted the one small way strangers reach each other there: the coins' worth of time left on a machine. The poem says nothing about kindness; the last line lets the reader find it.

**Frost.** "first frost / the garden hose holds the shape / I left it in." The first frost is the night soft things set hard. The hose, frozen in the loop it was dropped in, shows that without naming cold, and "I left it in" puts yesterday's careless person into the morning. It is loosely counted, like the better poems on the sheet.

**Waiting room.** "waiting room / his coat on my lap / no longer warm." The person in the waiting room usually holds something for the person who has gone in. The coat cooling measures the hours, and it carries the fear without saying it, so the poem stays open between a long wait and something worse.
