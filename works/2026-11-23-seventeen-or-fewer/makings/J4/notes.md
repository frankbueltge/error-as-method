# Notes

## What I see in the fifty

They are short, usually well under 17 syllables, mostly lowercase and lightly punctuated (a dash or a spaced ellipsis at most), and nearly all work by a cut: a short fragment, often a season or occasion word ("false spring", "Mother's Day", "drought year"), set against a small, plain observation that tilts it. The tone is dry, often wry or punning ("small / potatoes", "tariff free"), and the feeling is left in the gap, not stated; a handful are single-line monoku. Illness and medicine turn up several times (oxygen tube, second med, new prescription), so a hospital poem has to avoid sounding like those.

## First ideas

- **Laundromat at night:** someone else's clothes still turning in a dryer after everyone has left, a fragment like "night shift" or "insomnia" set against it.
- **First frost:** the hose left out, or the dog's water bowl, stiff by morning; perhaps keep it to a single image with "first frost" as the opening fragment.
- **Hospital waiting room:** the vending machine or the TV nobody watches; the number on a ticket being called, and the person stands up for a number that isn't theirs.

## Versions

- **laundromat-v1** ("insomnia / a stranger's shirts / still tumbling"): read back, "insomnia" names the feeling outright and the tumbling just mirrors it, and nothing places it in a laundromat; I dropped it for a new image.
- **laundromat-v2** ("all-night laundromat / I fold the warmth / out of my shirts"): read back, the setting is now in the first line, and the folding does the work that "insomnia" was doing; I left it as it was.
- **frost-v1** ("first frost / the garden hose / holds its shape"): read back, "holds its shape" sounded flat, a description with no turn in it; I went for an image with something at stake.
- **frost-v2** ("first frost / the tomatoes / we meant to pick"): read back, it is plain, and the regret is left in "meant to", with "we" quietly bringing in a second person; I left it as it was.
- **waiting-v1** ("waiting room / I stand up / for someone else's name"): read back, the pun on "stand up for" worked, but "waiting room" on its own could be a dentist's or a station's; I needed the hospital.
- **waiting-v2** ("family waiting room / I stand up / for someone else's name"): read back, "family waiting room" is what the sign by a surgical ward says, and "family" brings in whoever is being waited for; I left it as it was.

## What I made

**Laundromat.** An all-night laundromat and one person folding. Folding drains the dryer heat out of the clothes, so the only warmth in the place is spent while you watch. I wanted the loneliness to sit in that action, not in a word for it. That is the move the sheet makes over and over: a setting line, then a small physical act that tilts it.

**First frost.** The season word stands alone, then the tomatoes left on the vine, which the frost will now kill. "We meant to pick" holds the whole feeling, a small, ordinary failure to get round to something, with a hint at a shared life. I kept it very plain, close to the sheet's "mildew on the mint" and "thinning the carrots".

**Waiting room.** Someone in a hospital's family waiting room, so on edge that they get up when the wrong name is called. "Stand up for" also means to defend or speak for, and that second meaning brushes against whoever is in surgery, without the poem saying so. The sheet already has three poems about medicine (the oxygen tube, the second med, the new prescription). To stay clear of them I kept every clinical object out and the poem on the person waiting.
