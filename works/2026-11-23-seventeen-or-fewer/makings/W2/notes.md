# Notes

## First ideas

- **Laundromat at night:** One dryer still turning in an empty laundromat, someone's clothes tumbling with nobody there to claim them.
- **First frost:** The garden hose stiff on the lawn in the morning, so the frost shows up in something ordinary going rigid.
- **Hospital waiting room:** A vending machine humming while someone waits, and the coins in their hand warm from being held too long.

## Versions: what I changed after reading each one back

### Laundromat
- v1: On reading it back, "no one waits for it" told the loneliness instead of showing it, and the poem had no one in it. I rewrote it around one image, a stranger's shirt that seems to wave through the dryer glass as it tumbles.
- v2: The image worked and the count was 5-7-5, but it was the only one of the three without a dash after its first line, which would look uneven on a shared page. I added the dash.
- v3: Read back, nothing more I wanted to change. Final.

### First frost
- v1: "stiff" and "where I left it coiled" said the same thing twice, and the last line was slack. I cut it down so the frost is shown by the hose keeping yesterday's shape, which also carries the sense of time stopped overnight.
- v2: Read back, it was short and exact. Final.

### Waiting room
- v1: "the coins for the machine" was vague, and "warm" did not say how long they had been held. I named the vending machine and changed "warm" to "gone warm", so the line holds the long wait.
- v2: Read back, nothing more I wanted to change. Final.

## What I made and why

**Laundromat at night.** A person alone with machines at night, and the only thing that greets them is someone else's shirt, tumbling so that its sleeve seems to wave through the glass. I wanted company and its absence in one image, a little funny and a little lonely, without saying either word.

**The first frost.** The hose is still lying in the coil I left it in yesterday, but now it is frozen there. The frost isn't described at all. It shows in an everyday object that can no longer move, and the poem lets "yesterday" mark the night that has passed.

**A hospital waiting room.** Someone holds coins meant for the vending machine and never gets up to use them, until the coins take on their body heat. The poem doesn't name the fear or the person being waited for. The warmth of the coins measures how long the wait has been and how tightly they are holding on.
