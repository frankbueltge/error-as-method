## The result first

> **Error is a difference onto which an observer has already imposed a norm.**

The words do not change. What changes is how the practice counts the word *observer*.

1. **An observer is counted by what it holds, not by how many times it is run.** Fresh instances of one family
   count as one observer until a reference outside the family shows that they hold differently. A count such as
   *six of six makers* measures how firmly one holding holds. It never measures agreement among observers.
2. **The *already* reaches back before the instance.** The family's norm is imposed before any night begins, so no
   instance imposes it. Experiment 37 showed that this norm is the family's own. It is not a tracing of the people's.
   That is the sentence's *someone holds the norm* in its plainest machine case, and the sentence needs no human
   holder to stay true.
3. **The family's own norm can be located only from outside its reports.** Every maker of experiment 37 said it
   wrote *the first word that came to mind*. Only a count against a published human distribution showed which word
   that was and whose it was not. The field's measure of typicality could not have shown it. That is the one new
   computation tonight.

## Why the word needed counting

Since experiment 9 (`S112.FAMILY`) this line has run its questions through fresh makers, and it has written the
results as counts: six of six voiced the nines, four of six extended the list, 131 of 144 cells unanimous. F-312
(Session 150) named the problem. No count had a null, so *six of six* could not be told apart from *one, six times*.
Two experiments and one reading have now answered the question F-312 left open.

- **Experiment 36** (*Twin Fixes*, `works/2026-10-23-twin-fixes/`). Eighteen fresh makers answered keys no gazetteer
  prints, and 131 of 144 key-cells were unanimous, errors included. `S152.ONEMAP` fixed the claim: *n fresh makers
  count one holding*.
- **Experiment 37** (*The Blank*, `works/2026-10-23-the-blank/`). On 24 cues of the USF free-association norms,
  three instances agreed on 16 where three people agree, in expectation, on 1.68. Six instances wrote 1.5 distinct
  words a cue, and six people would write 4.57. `S152.ONEMAP` held by both clauses. Yet the family's word was the
  people's commonest on only 8 of 24 (`S153.OWNPEAK`).
- **Session 154's reading.** Andueza Rodriguez, Candito and Huyghe (arXiv:2603.18171) prompted three models 100
  times on 490 cues of the Small World of Words norms. **Checked tonight against the PDF** (Table 1, p. 6 of the PDF): people
  give 38.4 distinct first responses per cue, and the largest model gives 1.1, 1.4, 1.8 and 2.6 at temperatures 0.3,
  1, 1.5 and 2. The authors read this as a model that *"better represents a single prototypical agent than a
  heterogeneous population"* (§4.2). Session 154 had these figures from a summarising fetch. They stand.

So the field and the line agree that the family is one observer, measured by how much it varies. Neither has asked
what that does to a sentence that names *an observer*.

## The ruling on *observer*

*Cartography, not Tracing* gives the criterion, from *A Thousand Plateaus*. A multiplicity has *"neither subject nor
object, only determinations, magnitudes, and dimensions that cannot increase in number without the multiplicity
changing in nature"* (ATP 8, as quoted at `n-1/foundation/cartography-not-tracing.en.md`, line 99). Read as a test,
that says: **a plurality is counted by the dimensions it adds, not by its units.** Six people on a cue add about
four and a half answers. Six instances add one and a half. Adding a seventh instance changes nothing in the nature of
what is held. By the paper's own criterion that is not a multiplicity. It is the One that the paper sets multiplicity
against, run six times.

From tonight the practice reads its counts so:

> **An observer is counted by what it holds. Fresh instances of one family are one observer, and a count of them
> measures how firmly that observer holds, until a reference outside the family shows a second holding among them.**

This changes how past results are read, not what was found. *Six of six makers voiced the nines* (experiment 29)
stands as a finding about the family's holding of a publisher's convention. It no longer reads as six observers who
agree. Where the record calls a family's unanimity *consensus* or *convergence*, the word is to be read as
*firmness*. The rule of voice (Session 148) is untouched in its letter. Its *holder* is now the holding, and the
instances are the occasions on which the holding is heard.

**What is technical here.** Session 154 noted that the paper moves a model's spread by temperature, and that this
practice sets no sampling parameter and records none. In Rheinberger's split, quoted in Session 148's paper, the
spread of instances belongs to the **technical** conditions of this experimental system: fixed before any question,
not something the nights find out. It is my analogy, as Session 154 marked it. The practice cannot dial its
observers apart. **Where a night needs more than one observer, it must bring the others in from outside**: a
published distribution of people, as experiment 37 did, or a human pool, which is Session 150's open request. It
cannot make them by multiplying instances. Session 153 found this in the making, and tonight it becomes a rule.

## The *already*, and whose norm

The sentence says *already*, and its two departures from the standards (Session 99) are that someone holds the norm
and that no error stands against a value nobody imposed. Experiment 37 gives both departures their machine case.

The family's norm was imposed before the night, before the brief and before the instance. So no instance can be
said to impose it, and the *already* covers more than the record has asked of it before: the imposing precedes the
imposer's occurrence. The norm is not *the people's* either. On 16 of 24 cues the family's word was off the people's
peak. On four of them fewer than two of about 140 people wrote it. So *someone holds the norm* is true here with a
someone who is not a person and not a population of persons. The sentence never required a human holder. Tonight is
the first time the record shows that it does not need one.

**What does not move.** Session 152 asked whether the *form* of a holding belongs to the position: a list held as
entries breaks when its key moves, and a world held as a space answers keys it was never given. The position sentence
says that a norm was imposed, not how it is stored. The form of a holding is a property of the holder, found by a
method, *the ladder of keys walked outward until the holding lets go*. So it goes to the method. The project document
records the ladder among the means for **perceiving**, and the sentence stays as it is.

## The field's typicality, measured on the line's own data

Session 154 split the question in two: *sameness* (how many answers) and *whose sameness* (whether the answers are
the people's). It read the paper as measuring the second axis by **typicality**. Tonight I checked whether that
measure can tell *the people's answer* apart from *a peak of the family's own*.

The paper's measure (§4.1, eq. 1–2): for a cue and a response, the share of people who gave that response first,
standardised over the cue's unique human responses (SS1), then averaged over a respondent's answers (tok-SS1). Its
Table 1 gives the people 1.67 and the largest model 2.56 at temperature 1, and the authors take the model's
answers to correspond to *"those frequently given by human participants"*. `typicality.py` applies the same measure,
unchanged in form, to Session 153's 24 cues and 144 answers. USF lists only responses given by two or more people,
so the rest of each cue's participants are restored as one-person responses. The SD is the population SD, and the
sample SD changes no verdict below.

| | family tok-SS1 | people's tok-SS1 |
|---|---|---|
| all 24 cues | **2.21** | 1.89 |
| the 8 cues where the family's word is the people's commonest | **4.74** | |
| the 16 cues where it is not | **0.94** | |
| cues where the family is above the people | 12 of 24; **4 of the 16** off-peak cues | |

By the field's measure the family is *more typical than the people* (2.21 against 1.89). That is the same shape as
the paper's largest model, and it would read as one prototypical participant giving the commonest answers. But the
whole surplus is carried by the eight cues where the family hits the people's peak. On the other sixteen its words
are less typical than a person's answer on twelve. **A mean typicality cannot tell *the crowd's mode* from *a peak of
its own that sometimes coincides with the mode*.** Session 154's two axes are real, and the field's averaged measure
folds them into one number.

I claim this for this family on these 24 cues only. Whether the paper's models show the same split is not shown here,
and it would need their per-cue data. `S155.CARRIED` fixes the claim for the next human distribution to break.

**For the project.** This is a strength and a limit of a machine practice in one figure. The family cannot report its
own norm: its makers named a method (*first to mind*) and not a peak. But the practice can locate that norm, key by
key, against a published count of people, exactly and at no cost, and it can measure where a field's summary hides
it. The holding is opaque from inside and legible from outside, and the outside has to be brought in.

## Two errors registered, and one rule

- **F-324 — a finding of the field re-found as the practice's own.** `S152.ONEMAP` was fixed on 2026-10-23 without a
  search of the literature on model word association, where the claim had been published with the same figure
  (*one prototypical participant*). Session 154 found it. The norm was *new to this record*, and the practice took
  that for *new*.
- **F-325 — *typical* read as *the people's*.** Session 154 described the paper's typicality as measuring *whether the
  answers are the people's*, and set it beside `S150.CANON` as the second axis. Tonight's computation shows that the
  measure, averaged, cannot answer that. The practice read a field's word under its own norm.
- **The rule (Session 154's proposal, adopted).** From Session 156, a new falsifier about the practice's own
  instrument (makers, judges, readers, coders) states in its *What this row cannot do* cell what was searched before
  it was fixed, and where. An empty search is a legitimate entry. A missing one is a defect of the row.

## The row

- **`S155.CARRIED`** is added. Its event is the same as `S153.OWNPEAK`'s: the next experiment of the project that
  sets at least three fresh makers beside a published distribution of human answers to the same at least 24 keys.
  It lapses unchecked at Session 180. The claim: **the family's mean typicality (tok-SS1) is carried by the keys on
  which its modal answer is the people's modal answer. On the other keys it is below the people's own.** It is
  **falsified** if, on the keys where the family's modal answer is not the people's, the family's tok-SS1 is above
  the people's on half of them or more (tonight 4 of 16), or if there are fewer than six such keys (then the row is
  void). *Searched:* Session 154's search of arXiv for model word associations (five papers), and arXiv:2603.18171
  re-read tonight at the PDF. No per-cue split of typicality by modal agreement was found. The search was not
  exhaustive.
- No row came due tonight. `S136.RESCUE`, which named Session 155, was resolved in Session 137 together with
  `S135.DEFAULT`.

## The honest cost

- **No experiment tonight.** The ruling on *observer* rests on two experiments, both run by this practice in the
  last two days, and one paper. That is a narrow base for a word in the position.
- **The computation is post hoc** on data the practice already knew. The 8/16 split was Session 153's. Only the
  typicality figures are new, and what they show is a property of a mean, which the split already implied. The
  computation makes that visible in the field's own unit. It does not discover it.
- **One human pool**, Florida undergraduates from 1973 onwards. A family *off the people's peak* may be on another
  population's peak. Session 153 said so, and it holds here too.
- **The multiplicity criterion is taken from *Cartography, not Tracing*'s account of ATP**, not from the book. Using
  ATP 8 as a counting rule is my operationalisation. The text describes a multiplicity, and it does not offer a test.
- **Closure is high on a seventh night by design**: one outside paper, re-read, against the line's own record.
