I made "Sixty-one Noons". It's a single still canvas at 1100×800 that winds the 61 days into a spiral, one turn per day, starting at the centre on 15 October and ending at the rim on 14 December. Midnight is at the bottom and noon at the top. The value isn't drawn as height. It's drawn as ink weight: the line thickens where the value is large and thins to a hairline where it's small. The page loads no other file, library or network resource; the 1,464 values are written into it. Files are /tmp/claude-0/s137/mk/m12/work/index.html and /tmp/claude-0/s137/mk/m12/work/NOTE.md. The scripts and screenshots are in /tmp/claude-0/s137/mk/m12/scratch.

**First step with the file:** I printed the 300×90 pixels as text and looked at them before doing anything else. That showed one sharp tooth per day of about 4.85 pixels, two multi-day swells that each began with a sudden jump (around 26 October and 11 November), and a sharp change at about day 43 (27 November). After that point the daily swing almost disappears and the values sit at the lowest level in the record.

**From the file to the form:**
- **Reading the values back:** I found the centre of each dot in every pixel column, about 4 to 5 dots per column. Then I put them in time order with a small "rising or falling" choice per column, picking whichever order made neighbouring columns join up best. The data turns out to fill about 296 of the 300 pixels, measured from the spacing of the daily teeth.
- **Daily shape:** Averaged by hour, the result is consistent. The lowest point is about 03–04h, values rise from 06 to 09h, stay high until about 16h and fall through the evening. There is no weekly pattern.
- **Choosing the form:**
  - I first tried a spiral where the value pushed the line outward. The slow changes in overall level tangled the turns, and a daily bump of that shape only looked like a ring shifted upward.
  - I then tried showing each hour against a 24-hour average. It was still hard to read.
  - The ink-weight spiral works. Daytime stacks up as a darker upper half, the 11 November jump is a heavy ring, and the December turns become a pale, even rim.

**What you should know:**
- The hourly values are rebuilt from a small JPEG. Times are good to within about ±2 hours, and values to about a pixel of the original 90.
- The first hours of 15 October fall in empty edge columns, so I filled them from the nearest columns.
- Units are pixel heights in the source picture, because no real scale was given.
- The short text on the canvas describes only what the picture shows. It makes no claim about what was measured.

**My view of what was measured (a guess, held loosely):** I think it is a temperature, or something else warmed by the sun, at a northern site.
- **For a natural cause:** a cycle on every day of the week with no weekly pattern points to the sun, not a human schedule.
- **For weather:** the dull spells with small daily swings (24–25 October, 8–10 November) and the sudden warm jumps look like weather.
- **For the seasons:** the overall level falls from mid-October into December.
- **Open question:** what makes the daily cycle collapse so abruptly around 27 November after narrowing for two weeks. It could be snow covering the sensor, lasting fog or overcast, or the sun no longer reaching the site. 27 November is roughly when polar night begins near 69–70°N, but nothing in the file can tell these explanations apart.
