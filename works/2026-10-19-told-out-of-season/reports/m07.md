I finished the work. It's called **Sixty-one Nights**, and it's in `/tmp/claude-0/s137/mk/m07/work/index.html` with `/tmp/claude-0/s137/mk/m07/work/NOTE.md` beside it. I checked it in headless Chromium at 1100 × 800: the page requests nothing but itself and throws no errors.

**What I did first with the file.** I looked at the picture, then read every pixel: an ASCII dump, and the top and bottom of the dark pixels in each column. It is a sawtooth that repeats every 4.9 columns. 300 px over 61 days is 4.9 px per day, so the repeat is daily. Each cycle rises over a few hours, falls more slowly, and lingers at the bottom. That repeat runs for about 42 days. Around day 42–43 it collapses into a flat, low, slightly wavy line that lies below every earlier low.

**How I got to the form.**
- **Rebuilding the values.** Each pixel column holds about 5 hours, so the picture can't say which dot in a column came first. A simple column-by-column rebuild added a false 5-day beat, because the day doesn't line up with the pixel grid.
- **The daily shape.** I folded all the dark pixels of the active days onto one 24-hour clock. Because each day lands at a different offset against the pixel grid, the fold gives the shared daily shape at better than one-column resolution.
- **The series.** For the active period, each hour's value is that daily shape scaled between each day's peak and trough, read from the dots. For the quiet weeks I followed the dots directly, blending the two across the change. Laid over the original, the result matches the dots well. The scripts are in `/tmp/claude-0/s137/mk/m07/scratch`.
- **The form.** A coil of 61 turns, one per day, read from the outer edge inward, with each hour at a fixed clock angle. Colour runs from dark slate (low) to pale ember (high). The daily rhythm lines up into a fixed bright sector from midnight to dawn and a dark one in mid-afternoon. Changes in how far it was filled show as bands, including one very bright ring around day 27. The stop becomes a dark core of about 18 turns. A small side panel carries the title, a colour key and the record redrawn left to right.

**What you should know.**
- **The values are approximate.** They are rebuilt from a 300 × 90 JPEG, so time is resolved to roughly 5 hours and value to about 1/80 of the plot's height.
- **The daily shape is assumed the same every day.** The fold supports this, but individual days may have differed. For example, the big day-27 peak seems to have stayed high for longer than the model shows.
- **The clock labels are assumptions.** The 00:00 / 06:00 marks assume the record starts at midnight on 15 January, in an unknown time zone. Only relative hours are reliable.
- **Units and scale are unknown**, so "lower" and "higher" are the only labels.

**My view of what was measured, held loosely.** I think it is a temperature in, or of, something heated by a timer. My best guess is a night-storage heater or a hot-water tank on a cheap night tariff, switched off for the season around 25–26 February. It rests on four things in the file:
- The cycle is locked to the clock: it rises at the same hours every day for six weeks.
- Each day it rises over a few hours and then falls steeply at first, slowing as it nears the bottom. That is how something cools.
- After the stop, the level sinks slowly over about ten days to below any earlier low, as a heated mass settling towards its surroundings would. Small daily bumps return in March.
- The peaks taper over the last two weeks before the stop, as if the heating was being turned down while winter ended.

A battery or a tank that is filled and drawn down would also fit the sawtooth. That is why the work itself only says "filled" and "drains".
