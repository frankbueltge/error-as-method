The work is called **Each Night, Less**. It's a single still SVG page with one glowing bar for each of the 61 days. All the data is embedded in the page and it loads no other file, script or network resource. I rendered it headless at 1100 × 800: no script errors and no outside requests.

**What I did first with the file.** I looked at the image, then read its pixels and listed the dark rows in each column. That showed the structure right away. Through about x≈210 there is a regular sawtooth with a period of about 4.84 px, which is 296 px ÷ 61, so one tooth per day. Each tooth climbs over a few hours and then falls for the rest of the day. After that point the swing collapses to a small ripple that keeps the same daily rhythm.

**From the file to the form.**
- I turned the dark pixels into about 1,330 dot centres, each weighted by darkness. Long dark runs were split into dots of about 2 px. Fewer than 1,464 come back because dots overlap in a picture this small.
- I cut the strip into 61 equal day bins. I shifted the bins by about 0.8 px so each one starts just before its daily climb rather than at the image edge.
- For each day I kept the highest and lowest value, plus all of its dots with their position within the day.
- Each bar runs from the day's top to its bottom. It glows amber at the top, where the day is refilled, and darkens downward as the value is drawn down. The recovered readings sit inside as pale specks. A hairline drops from each bar to a shared floor to show what was left.
- The only words on the page are four sparse date marks, the title and one subtitle line.

**What you should know.**
- The resolution is about 5 hourly readings per pixel column. So the hourly values can't be recovered, only each day's span and its rough shape.
- The day boundaries are inferred. The page tells you nothing about clock time or time zone, so each bar is a ~24-hour slice set to the refill rhythm, not necessarily midnight to midnight.
- There is no absolute scale. Heights are relative to the original picture.
- The dates on the page come from the brief, not the image.

**What I think was measured.** This is a guess, and I didn't look anything up. My best guess is the stored heat of something charged once a night: probably the core of a night-storage heater, or possibly an off-peak hot-water tank. Here is what that rests on:
- **Timing of the climb.** If the series starts at 00:00 on 15 January, the climb falls in the small hours, roughly 01:00–05:00. It lasts about 3–5 hours and is followed by about 19 hours of fairly steady decline. That is a charge-then-discharge shape, not a sun-driven one.
- **The fills fade out.** After a very large fill on 11 February, the nightly fills get smaller almost every night until about 27–28 February. After that only a small nightly bump remains, still at the same hour. A charge controller cutting back as the nights warm into March fits this, and so does someone turning the heater down.
- **Weak spells.** The two weak spells, around 24–25 January and 8–10 February, look like milder weather or an absence.

Other explanations are possible, such as a battery charged on a night tariff or a tank refilled overnight. Nothing in the file proves which one it is.
