I made the work and called it "Sixty-One Spans". It's in `/tmp/claude-0/s137/mk/m18/work/index.html`, with `/tmp/claude-0/s137/mk/m18/work/NOTE.md` beside it. The page is one self-contained file using a canvas and plain JavaScript, with the data written into it. It loads nothing else.

**What I did first with the file:** I looked at it, then printed it out as text characters, one per pixel, to read it closely. Then I measured how much dark ink each pixel column and row held. That showed the plot covers about x 2–297 and y 3–87. Each day is about 4.8 pixels wide, so each pixel column holds about five hours of values. Single hours can't be recovered from a picture this small, but the range and spread of values within each day can.

**How I got to the form:** I cut the picture into 61 day-slices, and each day into 6 strips of about 4 hours. For each strip I kept how dark the ink is at every height. I turned each day on its side, so it becomes one horizontal band. Low values are on the left and high values on the right, and day 1 is at the top, day 61 at the bottom. The ink is the ink from the original picture, grouped by day and stretched, so there is no fitted curve and nothing invented. What you see:
- Broad, wandering days at first.
- Two short narrowings that drop to lower values, around days 9–11 and 25–26.
- One day, day 28, that reaches much higher than any other.
- From there the days fall steadily, and after about day 43 they shrink to a thin thread at the low end.

The only extra marks are small ticks at days 1, 10, 20 … 61, a title, and a one-line key.

**Things you should know:**
- No headless browser was available, so I checked the layout with a Python render that copies the page's drawing logic, not the browser itself. That render is `/tmp/claude-0/s137/mk/m18/scratch/preview.png`.
- The horizontal axis is stretched about 11 times, so dots show as short horizontal dashes. I kept that because it reads like a weave, but it is a side effect of the stretch, not something in the data.
- The value axis is relative only, because the picture has no units.
- The build script is `/tmp/claude-0/s137/mk/m18/scratch/build.py`. It regenerates the page from the JPEG and `template.html`.

**My view of what was measured (a guess):** I think this is probably the hourly discharge of a stream fed by snow or a glacier, in late summer and autumn. This rests on:
- **Each day's shape:** the values cluster densely low, with sparse dots high up. That means short daily peaks and long slow declines, which is how meltwater behaves.
- **Days 29–43:** the daily level falls smoothly and steadily after the day-28 peak, like flow dropping off after a rain or warm spell.
- **After about day 43:** the large daily swing almost disappears, leaving a low, nearly flat line with small ripples, as when melting stops for the season.
- **The two dips:** these look like cold spells.

Another reading is ground temperature that becomes muffled once snow covers the sensor. The leftover ripples and the smooth decline make me favour discharge, but I hold this loosely.
