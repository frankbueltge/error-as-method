I made a still piece called **Sixty-one Breaths**. It's in `/tmp/claude-0/s137/mk/m15/work/index.html` (about 30 KB, loads nothing else), with `/tmp/claude-0/s137/mk/m15/work/NOTE.md` beside it. I couldn't open the page in a browser because none is installed. Instead I ran the page's own script in Node and redrew its marks with my own small renderer. Text wasn't in those previews, so I haven't seen the title, labels and caption in place. The script runs and draws 2,788 marks.

**What I did first.** I looked at the image, then measured it pixel by pixel. The data fills columns 2–297, which is 296 pixels for 1,464 hours, so about 4.85 pixels per day and 5 hours per pixel. In zoomed crops each day is a slanted streak: a sharp rise, a high stretch, then a slower fall. The record has four phases:
- **15–23 Oct:** a large daily swing.
- **About 23–26 Oct:** a few lower days with a smaller swing.
- **27 Oct–10 Nov:** large swings again. Then a sudden step to the highest values of the whole record.
- **10 Nov–14 Dec:** both the level and the swing shrink steadily until about 26 Nov. After that the line is almost flat and low, with only small daily bumps and a slow sag.

**How I got to the form.** The strongest thing in the plot is the daily swing fading out, and a 300-pixel strip hides it. So I turned the plot on its side and cut it into days:
- Each day is one row, top to bottom.
- A value becomes a sideways position: larger is further right.
- Within each row the hours drop slightly, so time reads down the page.
- A thin red line joins each day's centre.

The result is a ragged, wing-like shape. It narrows into a tight dark thread at the bottom, and the October dip and the 10 November jump show as clear sideways shifts.

**Worth knowing:**
- Every mark is a pixel of the supplied JPEG at its own darkness. I removed only the faint compression halo and invented no values.
- I didn't try to recover the 1,464 values one by one. The picture can't resolve single hours, so I used it as it is.
- The dates are estimated from pixel positions and are accurate to roughly a day.
- The piece has no value scale, because the source has none.
- My working files are in `/tmp/claude-0/s137/mk/m15/scratch`.

**What I think was measured (a guess, not established).** Most likely a temperature near the ground, or something else driven by the sun, at a northern-hemisphere site going into winter. My reasons:
- There is one strong cycle per day, shaped like a heating curve: a fast rise and a slow fall.
- The swing gets smaller through November as the days shorten.
- The almost total loss of the swing from about 26 November looks like a sensor that became covered or insulated, for example by snow, or a long spell of overcast or fog.
- The 10 November jump would fit a sudden warm spell.

A solar-charged battery voltage is a credible alternative: it charges by day, sags at night, and collapses when the panel is covered. I couldn't tell which local hour the daily peak falls at, because the pixel positions are too coarse for that.
