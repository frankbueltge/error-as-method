I've finished the work and its note. It's a single still canvas called **Sixty-One Beats**, in `index.html`, with `NOTE.md` beside it in `/tmp/claude-0/s137/mk/m14/work`. The page loads no other files or network resources, and a headless render at 1100 × 800 showed no errors or requests.

**What I did first with the file.** I looked at the picture, then dumped its pixels as text and measured each column: where the dark dots start and stop, and how much ink there is. The dots run from x≈2 to x≈297, so each pixel column holds about 5 hours and one day is about 4.84 px. The marks repeat at that spacing, so the dominant rhythm is one tooth per day. Each tooth rises over a few hours and falls over most of the rest of the day.

**How I got to the form.**
- **Reading the values back:** sorting the dots inside each column directly gave false double peaks, because at 5 hours per pixel the order of values within a column can't be recovered. Instead I found each day's peak and trough (60 daily peaks were detected) and filled the hours between them with a smooth curve. That gave 1,464 approximate hourly values, which are embedded in the page.
- **What that means for accuracy:** the daily highs, daily lows, the trend and the step changes are reliable. The exact shape within each day, and the hour of each peak, are only good to roughly ±3–5 hours. You can see this in the work: the rise drifts slightly across the rows.
- **The form:** the record is cut into 24-hour strips, each starting at the daily low. The strips are stacked down the page like ledger lines, each one partly covering the line above. Every line holds one beat: a quick rise on the left and a long fall to the right. The record starts and ends part-way through a strip, and the work shows those partial strips as they are. Small dates in the left margin and a short caption on the right are my additions; the source has no words.
- **What the stack shows:**
  - The daily beat sinks steadily from 15 January to about 25 January.
  - It jumps up suddenly around 26 January, sinks again, then jumps to its highest point of the whole record around 10–11 February.
  - It fades from about 20 February and stops around 27–28 February.
  - The last roughly 16 days are low and almost flat, with only faint daily ripples and a slight further drop.

**What was measured, as an opinion only.** My guess is a store that was topped up about once a day and drained in between: perhaps a battery's charge or voltage, a heated store such as a hot-water tank or storage heater, or a tank's level. Then, around the end of February, whatever drove the daily top-up stopped. This rests on four things in the file:
- one sharp-rise, slow-fall tooth per day, with no other strong rhythm;
- a slow downward drift, as if more was taken out than put in;
- two sudden one-off jumps that look like a manual refill or a strong outside event, not a gradual change;
- an abrupt end to the daily cycle, after which the value sits near its floor.

I can't tell these candidates apart, and the time of day of the rise is uncertain because the start time and time zone of the record are unknown.
