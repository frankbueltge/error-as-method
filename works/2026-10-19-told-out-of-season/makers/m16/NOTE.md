# Sixty-One Wicks

The record is cut into its 61 days, and each day is stood upright as one column, read downward through its 24 hours. Each hour's value sets both the column's width and its colour, from cold slate to ember orange to pale gold, so a day becomes a single flame-like silhouette: it swells, pinches to a thread around the same hour every day, and swells again. Side by side, the columns show what the original long, thin plot hides. The daily rhythm is regular enough to line up as horizontal bands. The forms dim through November, one warm night on 10–11 November flares out of the row, and from 27 November the columns burn down to bare grey wicks for the last eighteen days. I chose folding over unrolling because the material is built around one dominant rhythm, the day. Giving each day its own body turns a decline you would otherwise squint at into a row of things going out. The thin line at the foot is the same 1,464 hours, unfolded, left there so the source stays in view.
