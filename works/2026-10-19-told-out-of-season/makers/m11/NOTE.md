# Run-out Groove

The 1,464 hours are cut into a single spiral groove on a dark disc. The groove makes one full turn per day and runs from the rim inward, the way a stylus travels, so the 61 days become 61 turns. Each hour sets how wide and how bright the groove is at that point: high values make it broad and ivory, low values make it a thin, dim thread. Because every turn takes exactly 24 hours, the daily pulse that drives the record lines up into one paler side of the disc, opposite a darker side where the low hours fall. The days where the record runs high build up into bright bands like the tracks on a record. A single bright ring marks the one surge. The last eighteen days, when the daily pulse nearly stops, spiral into the quiet centre the way an LP's run-out groove does after the music has ended. I chose the record because the material is a rhythm with a fixed period, and the spiral is a form that keeps both kinds of time at once: going round the disc you read the hours of a day, and moving inward you read the days of the record.
