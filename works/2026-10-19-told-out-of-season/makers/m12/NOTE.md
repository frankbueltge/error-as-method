# Sixty-one Noons

The 1,464 hourly values are wound into a spiral: each turn is one day, starting at the centre on 15 October and ending at the outer edge on 14 December, with midnight at the bottom and noon at the top. The value is not shown as height. It is shown as the weight of the ink, so the line thickens where the measurement is large and thins to a hairline where it is small. I chose this because the strongest thing in the picture is a rhythm tied to the clock. Every day rises in the morning, holds until afternoon and sinks through the night, and the larger runs of days lie on top of that rhythm. In a flat line those days blur into a picket fence. Wound so that every noon falls in the same place, they stack up, and the upper half of the disc darkens. You can then see the change the work is about: after about 27 November the turns stop swelling and the disc ends in an even, pale rim of quiet days. The hourly values were read back from a 300-pixel-wide JPEG, so the hour of each value is approximate (to within roughly two hours), but the daily swell and its disappearance are clearly in the source.
