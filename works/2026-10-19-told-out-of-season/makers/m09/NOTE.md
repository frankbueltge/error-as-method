# Sixty-one Strokes

The record runs at the pace of a day: each day it climbs steeply and then falls away slowly, until near the end of February the climbing almost stops. The form keeps that day as its unit. Each of the 61 days becomes a single tapered ink stroke, broad where the day was highest and thinning to a point at its lowest, so the shape of the stroke repeats the shape of the day: a hard press, then the pen lifting. Behind the strokes, every dark pixel of the original plot is kept as a faint mark, so the hourly record stays visible under the gesture drawn from it. I chose a row of strokes rather than a line because what matters here is not the slow drift of the values but the daily repetition, and how it weakens. As tally marks, the end of the record reads the way it feels: a hand that strikes strongly for six weeks, falters, and ends making small marks.
