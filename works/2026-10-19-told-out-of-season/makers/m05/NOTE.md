# Sixty-One Falls

The record is cut into 24-hour lengths, and the days are stacked like the ridgelines of a range or layers of sediment. The first day is at the top and the last at the bottom. Each cut is made at the usual daily low, so each line reads the same way: it lifts, crests and slides down again. Each day partly hides the day before it, which shows through faintly. I chose this form because the strongest thing in the record is a pulse that comes back every 24 hours, and on the original long strip it blurs into hatching. Folding the strip at its own period puts the days on top of one another, so they can be compared. The larger story is then read from top to bottom: weeks of tall swells, a short slack spell, a surge and its long ebb, and then about eighteen nearly flat days at the bottom, where the pulse has almost stopped.
