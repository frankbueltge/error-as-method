# Sixty-one Breaths

The plot is turned on its side and cut into days. Each of the 61 days becomes one row, read from top to bottom. Each row is that day's dots, laid out sideways, so a day's values spread out from left to right, with larger values further right. Inside a row, the hours drop a few pixels so that time keeps running down the page. A thin red line joins each day's centre. In the original strip the most striking change is easy to miss: for six weeks the record swings hard every day, then around 26 November the swing almost stops. Stacking the days makes that change the shape of the whole piece. Wide, ragged rows of daily swing narrow into a tight, dark thread near the bottom, and the record's two other events show up as clear lateral jumps: the low, narrow days around 23–26 October and the sudden rise around 10 November. No values were invented. Every mark is a pixel of the supplied image, drawn with its own darkness.
