# Sixty-One Measures

The work folds the record's 1,464 hours into 61 figures, one per day, standing side by side from 15 October to 14 December. Each figure covers the full range the value moved through that day. Its width at any height shows how much of the day's ink sits at that level, and every figure holds the same 24 hours, so all 61 have the same area. A day that swung widely becomes a tall, thin needle. A day that barely moved becomes a squat, dark seed. In the source plot the eye follows a falling line. I chose this form because the record's real event is something else: for six weeks the value rose and fell sharply every day, then in late November that daily swing almost stopped. Giving every day the same amount of "time-ink" makes that change visible as a change of shape, from thread to knot, and it lets the one spike day (10 November) stand as the tallest needle. A faint thread joins each day's median level, and nothing else is added.
