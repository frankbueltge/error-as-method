# Once Around

The work wraps the whole record clockwise in a single turn, from 15 October at the top back round to 14 December. Larger values sit further out. Because one day is a sixtieth of the circle, each daily swing stands out as a spike, and for six weeks the record forms a ruff of 61 teeth. There is a gap where a cold spell flattened them in late October, and one tall spike in mid-November. In the last eighteen days the ruff wears down to a thin, quiet edge. A ring of short strokes inside repeats each day's swing, so you can count down to the stillness. I chose a circle over a line for three reasons. It makes the daily rhythm repeat like a cog. It turns the change from pulse to calm into a change of outline you can see from across a room. And it brings the end of the record back round to meet its beginning. The dots are not redrawn from a guessed series. They are the inked pixels of the original picture, each one scattered inside its own small cell of time and value, so the work shows exactly the detail the file holds and no more.
