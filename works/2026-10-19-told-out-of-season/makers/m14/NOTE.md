# Sixty-One Beats

The original picture packs the record into one strip, so its most regular feature disappears into a fine comb. That feature is a single daily pulse: the value climbs over a few hours, then falls for most of a day. This work cuts the record into 24-hour strips, each starting at the daily low, and stacks them down the page like lines in a ledger. Each line then holds one beat, with the rise on the left and the long fall to the right. Each strip covers the one above it, so the page reads like layered paper. Across the stack you can see three things the strip hides: the beats sink a little day by day, they jump up twice (about 26 January and 11 February), and from the last days of February they stop, leaving sixteen nearly flat lines. I chose the ledger because it treats the record as days someone lived through, not as one long trace, and because a flat line needs no explaining when it sits under forty lines that have a pulse.
