# Sixty-one Nights

The record is wound into a coil of 61 turns, one turn per day, read from the outer edge inward. Each hour sits at the same clock angle every day, and colour stands for the value, from dark slate (low) to pale ember (high). I chose the coil because the strongest thing in the picture is a daily rhythm locked to the clock: a rise each evening and a long fall to a low by the next afternoon. Laid on a clock face, those six weeks of repetition line up into a bright sector that never moves. Each change in how far it was filled shows as its own band, and the moment it stops shows as a dark core: about eighteen turns where the rhythm is gone and the level sits below every earlier low. The values were rebuilt from the 300-pixel picture, so they are approximate. Each day's shape comes from folding all 42 active days onto one 24-hour clock, scaled to each day's peak and trough as read from the dots. The quiet final weeks follow the dots directly.
