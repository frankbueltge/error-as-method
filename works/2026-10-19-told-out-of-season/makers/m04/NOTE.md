# Wound Down

The record turned out to be a 24-hour sawtooth: almost every day the value jumps up late in the evening and then falls steadily until the next jump. A long line squeezes that rhythm into a grey blur, so this work lays the 61 days out as a wall calendar, Monday to Sunday, one cell per day. Each cell is drawn directly from that day's slice of the original image, with each pixel column's ink stretched into a horizontal stripe. Read this way, every cell shows one fall. The weeks also show three longer runs, each reset by a large jump on 26 October and 11 November. In the last three weeks the falls stop and only faint ripples are left. I chose the calendar because the thing that matters here, the daily reset, happens where one day meets the next, and a calendar puts those boundaries in plain view. I kept the source's own ink rather than redrawing a smooth line because the image cannot resolve single hours: each stripe is a pixel column covering about five hours, and the work shows that limit instead of hiding it. The unbroken record runs along the foot of the page for comparison.
