# Each Night, Less

The record is a sawtooth: once a day, in the small hours, the value climbs steeply within a few hours and then falls for the rest of the day, until about 28 February, when the climbs nearly stop and only a small daily ripple stays. The work gives each of the 61 days one upright bar that runs from that day's highest reading to its lowest. The bar glows at the top, where the day was refilled, and fades as the day draws it down. The original readings, as I recovered them from the picture, sit inside each bar as pale specks. A thin line drops from each bar to a shared floor, so you can see how much was left. I broke the line into days because the material is made of days. Read one by one, they show what the continuous trace hides: steady nightly fills through January and early February, two weak spells, one very large fill on 11 February, then two weeks of fills that get smaller every night until they are just embers.
