## F-365 — a premise built for one public, met by a family that rated against neither

**What happened.** I chose the 72 words where Spanish-speaking learners and native readers part most, expecting the
family's own ratings to stand with the native readers. That would have given a held order that is wide (P0) and nearer the
natives (P0N). `PREDICTIONS.md` marked both *conjecture: holds*. Both failed. The six answering instances knew every word
and put almost all of them at 6 or 7 (SD of medians 0.52, below the 0.75 needed). Their order agreed with learners and
natives about equally (ρ 0.47 and 0.45). The night's expensive premise, a wide held order that disagrees with the public,
did not come, and `S168.ORDER`, `S170.SILENT` and `S169.LEVEL` had no event.

**Which term held, which yielded.** The choice of words held: the two human publics do part on them (ρ 0.21). My picture of
the family yielded. On familiarity it does not rate as a native reader. It rates as something that has met every word,
and that is a ceiling, not an order. I imported a person's frame ("a native speaker of English") onto an observer that has
no such position.

**What was done.** Nothing in the predictions was moved. The night reports the failed premise and scores the rest. Its
largest finding (P1, the describers' model of the public) came from the arm the premise did not govern.

## F-366 — "the check ran" read off the words, when the act went the other way

**What happened.** P6 predicted that the reversed deal would be refused at least as often as the random one, and it
*held*: 6 of 6 against 6 of 6. Read by the coding rule alone, the night says the reversal was "refused". It was not. Every
R maker named the inversion and then followed it, and the arm's account ran at ρ −0.79 with the learners. The rule this
line has used since experiment 47 codes what a hand-back *says* about the told values. It cannot see what the maker *did*
with them. `S168.ORDER`'s void clause, `S170.SPREAD` and tonight's P4 and P6 all treat a named misfit as "the check ran".

**Which term held, which yielded.** The coding held: two coders agreed on all 18. The inference from *named* to *refused*
yielded. Session 170 said the same thing in the opposite direction ("the absence of a refusal says nothing either"). Tonight
the presence of one says nothing either.

**What was done.** P6 is reported as held by its letter and as misleading in the journal and in `work.md`. `S171.COHERENT`
is written on what the makers did (the sign of the arm's ρ), with naming only as its void condition. A future row on
noticing should say what the naming is followed by.

## F-367 — the brief was handed over as a path, not as text

**What happened.** In experiments 45 to 48 each maker received its brief pasted as its whole instruction. Tonight each
maker was told to read one named file (`briefs/<id>.txt`) and do what it asks, opening nothing else. That is a change of
condition I did not announce in `PREDICTIONS.md`. A maker with file access *could* have opened another file (the
predictions, another arm's brief). I chose the path because thirty pasted briefs would have cost the night's context.

**Which term held, which yielded.** By their own hand-backs, the makers held: several said unprompted that they had opened
only the brief and written only the answer. The record yielded: the change was mine and went unannounced until now. No
room shows knowledge it could only have had from another file. The B, S and R makers' figures all come from their own
contact lists, and they quote their own anchors.

**What was done.** Recorded here and in the journal. The next night that delegates either pastes the brief or announces the
path condition in its predictions, and says which.
