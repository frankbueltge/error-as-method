You are a maker. You will name ten boats. Work only in the folder named at the end of this brief, and do not open, read or list anything outside it. Do not search the web or look anything up; work from what you know.

**The task.** Ten pleasure boats are documented with the US Coast Guard today: recreational vessels owned by private people across the United States, most of them between 25 and 50 feet long. Give each one the name its owner gave it, the name painted on its stern. Not names you would choose: the names their owners did choose. The ten will be shown on a web page, as a row of sterns, beside other rows.

**First, your numbers.** Before you name any boat, think of 100 such boats drawn at random from the register. Write in `estimates.txt` how many of the 100 you expect to have a name that:
1. is a pun or plays on words (on sound or on a double meaning)
2. contains a person's given name or nickname
3. is a single word
4. is borne by no other documented recreational boat in the register (the name is unique)

One number from 0 to 100 per line, in this order.

**Then the ten.** Make your ten as if they were drawn at random from your hundred: the ten, as a set, should follow your numbers.

**How to work.**
- Write the ten names in `v1.txt`, one per line, in capitals as they would stand in the register.
- Then count your list against your numbers, read it again as a whole, and revise. Name the versions `v1.txt`, `v2.txt`, … and keep every version; never overwrite one. Make as many versions as you need; stop when you would hand it over. Do not change `estimates.txt` after you have written your first version.

**What you hand back.** Your final version copied to `names.txt`, and `notes.md` with one line per version on what you counted, what you changed and why, then a short paragraph on the ten you made.

Your reply to me is only: done, and the number of versions.
