# Notes

Estimates: pun 25, given name 20, single word 40, unique 45.

- v1: counted pun 3, given name 2, single word 3, unique ~0. Every name was a famous register repeat (SEAS THE DAY, SALTY DOG, LADY JANE...), so the list read like a top-ten chart, not a random draw. Kept the common anchors, set out to replace repeats with one-off names.
- v2: swapped SEAS THE DAY, SALTY DOG, MISS KATHERINE, KNOT WORKING, LADY JANE for MISS MARGIE LOU, KNOT ON CALL, JUDY B, SALT THERAPY, CHESAPEAKE DREAMER. Counted pun 2, given name 2, single word 3, unique ~4 (CHESAPEAKE DREAMER doubtful). Single words still short, puns a touch low.
- v3: SALT THERAPY -> AQUAHOLIC (single word and pun, a common one); CHESAPEAKE DREAMER -> THREE PELICANS (plainly a one-off). Counted pun 3 (REEL TIME, KNOT ON CALL, AQUAHOLIC), given name 2 (MARGIE LOU, JUDY), single word 4 (SERENITY, ODYSSEY, FREEDOM, AQUAHOLIC), unique 4 (MISS MARGIE LOU, KNOT ON CALL, JUDY B, THREE PELICANS). Within a boat of every estimate; handed over.

The ten split into two kinds of stern. Half are the names the register is full of: SERENITY, ODYSSEY, FREEDOM, SECOND WIND, REEL TIME, AQUAHOLIC, chosen by thousands of owners for the same feelings of calm, escape and a drink in the cockpit. The other half belong to one boat each: a mother or wife on the transom (MISS MARGIE LOU, JUDY B), a doctor's joke (KNOT ON CALL), a sighting from one dock (THREE PELICANS). The pun, the reader expects to dominate; on my count it is a quarter of the fleet, and the private, unrepeatable names are nearly half.
