# Notes

- v1: First pass, written straight from memory of what sterns say. Every line was a pun or slogan.
- v2: Reading it back, it looked like a "top ten boat names" listicle, not ten owners. A real register mixes puns with a wife's or mother's name, a family blend of first names, an in-joke about a job or a divorce, and a plain bird or animal. I kept the puns that really do turn up all the time (SEAS THE DAY, REEL THERAPY, SECOND WIND, PIER PRESSURE) and replaced the rest with MISS JUDY, JOMAR, KNOT ON CALL, SHE GOT THE HOUSE, SALTY DOG and BLUE HERON.
- v3: Roman numerals are common on registered names, because owners reuse a name on the next boat. SECOND WIND made the same point a second time, so I replaced it with KATHY ANN II.

The ten are a cross-section, not my favourites: four puns of the kind people actually paint on boats (sea/see, reel/real, knot/not, pier/peer), two women's names, one of them on a second boat, a two-name blend, one joke about the cost of a divorce, and two plain names taken from the water and the dock. Nothing is clever beyond what an owner would think up over a beer, and that is the point: they should read like names that are really in the register, not like names someone invented.
