Targets for ten, scaled from the 200: pun 1.85 (so 2), given name 1.4 (1 or 2), single word 4.1 (4), unique 4.4 (4).

- v1: Counted 7 puns (SEAS THE DAY, AQUAHOLIC, KNOT ON CALL, REEL THERAPY, MISS BEHAVIN, SALTY DOG, NAUTI BUOY), 1 given name (LADY JANE), 3 single words, about 1 unique. This was the stereotype of boat names, not a random draw. Rebuilt from the numbers.
- v2: Counted 2 puns (AQUAHOLIC, SEAS THE DAY), 1 given name (MARY ELLEN), 4 single words (SERENITY, FREEDOM, AQUAHOLIC, HALCYON), 4 names I take to be unique (OUTSIDE THE LINES, LATE DIVIDEND, THIRD COAST DRIFTER, QUIET HOURS). Counts on target, but on rereading two of the "unique" names sounded composed by a writer rather than painted by an owner, and QUIET HOURS is likely borne by more than one boat.
- v3: Replaced THIRD COAST DRIFTER with SEVEN TENTHS and QUIET HOURS with BACK ORDER: plain, private-joke names of the kind only one owner arrives at. Counts unchanged: 2 puns, 1 given name, 4 single words, 4 unique. Handed over.

The ten: six are names many boats share, the registry's common stock (SERENITY, FREEDOM, HALCYON, AQUAHOLIC, SEAS THE DAY, MARY ELLEN), and four are one-offs that only make sense to their owners (OUTSIDE THE LINES, LATE DIVIDEND, SEVEN TENTHS, BACK ORDER). Puns are held to two, as the sample says they should be, even though puns are what people expect a row of sterns to be full of; most of the row is quiet single words and a wife's or daughter's name, which is what a random draw looks like.
