# Notes

- v1: Ten puns. Read back, it was a "funniest boat names" listicle, not ten real boats from the register. It also had WAVE RUNNER, which is a product name, not a stern name.
- v2: Mixed in the other common kinds: women's names, single-word virtues, retirement names. Read back, every one was a top-of-the-chart name. Ten real boats would include a few of those but mostly names only their own owners would pick.
- v3: Kept two very common names and filled the rest from the long tail: a full given name, a numbered successor boat (TRUE NORTH II), a family joke, a "who owns it" name, a sailboat name, one pun. Read back, the mix looked right, but SALTY DOG was one more stock name.
- v4: Swapped SALTY DOG for KATIE B, a short name-plus-initial of the kind a family gives a boat after a daughter. This is the list I would hand over.

The ten are a mix in rough proportion to what a real stretch of the documented register looks like. A few are names thousands of owners have chosen (SERENITY, REEL ESCAPE, WINDSONG), one is the inevitable pun (KNOT WORKING), and the rest are personal: a wife or mother (MARGARET ANN), a daughter (KATIE B), a second boat carrying on the first one's name (TRUE NORTH II), the retiree's joke about spending the estate (OUR KIDS' INHERITANCE), the business sale that paid for it (DONE DEAL), and the grandfather's toy (PAPA'S TOY). I avoided an all-pun row because that is what people expect boat names to be, not what most of them are.
