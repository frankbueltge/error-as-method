# Notes

- v1: First draft. Reading it back, it was the familiar "top boat names" list: all puns and clichés (AQUAHOLIC, LIQUID ASSET, FREEDOM). That is the list a writer would make up, not what owners actually paint on their boats.
- v2: Swapped three clichés for women's names (MISS KATHY, LADY JANE), the most common kind of name on a real stern, and spelled the pun the way owners do (KNOT WORKIN, not KNOT WORKING).
- v3: Dropped LADY JANE, which sounded like a storybook, and PURA VIDA. Added a full given name (PATRICIA ANN), a numeral for a second boat (REEL THERAPY II) and a plain fisherman's excuse (JUST ONE MORE). That puts the puns, the personal names and the plain names in roughly the mix you see in a marina.

The ten are meant to read like one real row of a marina: the much-repeated favourites (SEAS THE DAY, SERENITY, SECOND WIND, ISLAND TIME) are all there, because owners really do pick them again and again. Beside them sit the fishing puns, a wife's or mother's name in two forms, and a numeral that marks an owner on his second boat. Nothing is meant to be clever on my part. Where the row looks predictable, that is because the register is.
