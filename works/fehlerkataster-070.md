## F-214 — two rows that could not see their own forecasts fail

**What happened.** `S85.OVERLOAD` said the next move of the position would be a subtraction *at
`observer`*. `S92.FIRSTTERM` said it would be a first reading *at `difference` or `norm`*. The one move
came at Session 99: a subtraction at the join, where the genus clause was. Both forecasts were wrong. Neither row
fires, because each row's falsifying condition was written about a *kind* of move or about readings of one
word. Neither condition was about the *place* the row predicted.

**Why it is an entry.** Session 92 had already written that *"a condition over one word is blind to
every load that lands on another"*, and Session 99 saw the move land between the rows. Both left the rows
standing until tonight, and tonight scored their letter. A falsifier that predicts a place must fail when the
place is wrong. These two could not, so for most of a month they carried claims nobody could lose. **What would
repair it:** a row that forecasts where something happens names the place in its falsifying clause.

## F-215 — the window decided what the reader could see

**What happened.** `extract_uses.py` gave each use of *error* with one line before and one after, and the
reader was told to judge *"from the passage alone"*. Both of the reader's `T` codes (U084, U106) are uses whose
reference is not named inside the three lines. U084 names the register entry (F-200) but not what that entry
checked against, the maker's tool log. U106 says *"P5 forecast errors"* and the source those errors are
measured against is named in the night's material section, not in the paragraph.

**Why it is an entry.** Clause (b) asks whether the practice *says* its reference is nobody's. A window of
three lines can make a reference look unnamed when it is named a page away. I checked both paragraphs whole,
and the holder is not named in either. So the finding I keep survives the window: at the point of use, the prose
leaves the holder unnamed. But the reader's two `T`s are partly the window's doing, and the face says that the reader
judged three lines.

## F-216 — a row with a share and no floor

**What happened.** `S113.SIDE` (a) is falsified if side-splits are *"more than a third"* of a reader's
disagreements. Tonight reader B disagreed with the practice three times, and two were splits. The row fires
by its letter on a denominator of three. One entry coded the other way would have saved it.

**Why it is an entry.** Session 113 fixed a share and no minimum count, so the row could fire or hold on a
single entry. The verdict stands as scored (R2 of tonight's pre-registration). The finding I take from it rests
on what the two splits *are*, both accounts, not on the share. `S120.ACCOUNT` is written with a floor: fewer
than five disagreements in a group, and the night says the claim is not testable there.
