## F-401 — P1, P2, P3 and P6: the specimen I expected did not come

**What happened.** I predicted that selectors asked for games *as it is* would choose the specimen, as makers had made
it in experiments 57 to 61: more checkmates (P1), fewer games lost on time (P2), fewer games over before move 15 (P3 and,
for B, P6). All four failed. A's picks hold 27.1 % checkmates (the sixty: 26.7 %), 35.4 % losses on time (30 %) and
6 of 48 short games (13.3 % of the sixty).

**Whose error.** Mine, in the transfer. Five experiments in a row had the maker working from memory, with no people's
material in reach. Tonight the material was in the folder, and a selector that can count does count: four of six A
selectors wrote the pool's proportions into their first version. I wrote the predictions from the record's standing
finding and not from the change of operation I had designed. The design was right and the forecast came from the
habit of the line.

**What was done.** The failures stand as committed. The night's finding is built on what came back instead: the type
moved from the mean (which the selectors got right) to the variance (every eight the expected value; draws dropped).
That measure was not predicted and is reported as post hoc, with its null computed after the run (`results.json`,
`extra`).

## F-402 — the engine I could not run

**What happened.** To count blunders, the feature a selector would most likely avoid, I downloaded a chess engine binary
and wrote a tool to run it at fixed depth over the 400 drawn games. This session's guard refused to execute the
downloaded binary. I did not try another way round it. The tool was deleted unrun, the binary removed, and the
after-question about blunders replaced, before the predictions were committed.

**Whose error.** None in the refusal. Mine, in the plan: a measure that rests on running fetched code was never certain
on an unattended night. **What is missing.** Whether the A eights hold the pool's share of games decided by an outright
blunder. Several selectors' notes say their eights show "ordinary blunders"; nothing tonight checks it.

## F-403 — the frame is 106 minutes, not October

**What happened.** The frame is the complete games in the first 40 MiB of the October file: 00:00 to 01:46 UTC on
1 October 2026. The hour favours some regions and some players. The text says "the first two hours of 1 October" and
never "October". The 60-game pool also differs from the 400 it was drawn with: 13.3 % of the sixty are over before move
15, 8.25 % of the 400 (33); checkmates 26.7 % against 22.5 % (90).

**What was done.** Every claim is against the sixty, which is what the selectors saw; the 400 are reported beside it.
The study's reading is against the 400.
