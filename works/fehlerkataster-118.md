## F-368 — a deal built to sit on a row's boundary, and it landed 0.004 off it

**What happened.** `S171.COHERENT` names its event as told values dealt *"in an order that runs against the truth (ρ
with the true values of −0.5 or below)"*. I wanted the half-turned arm to be that event, and wrote the search window in
`prepare.py` as [−0.52, −0.48] around −0.50. The first permutation in the window had ρ **−0.496**. That is −0.50 at two
decimals and not "−0.5 or below" by the letter. So the arm built to test the row is not its event, and the row's event
came instead at the reversed arm (ρ −1.00), which tests the side of the claim Session 171 already saw.

**Which term held, which yielded.** The arm held as an experiment: it answered the open thread (where the switch lies). My
reading of my own row yielded. I wrote a window centred on a boundary when the row's condition is one-sided.

**What was done.** Nothing moved after the fact. The journal and the falsifier row state the 0.004 and report the arm as
evidence beside the event, not as the event. A future half-turned deal should be drawn from a window that lies wholly
inside a row's condition.

## F-369 — "named as not fitting" read two ways: blame on the numbers, or blame on the label

**What happened.** The coding rule since experiment 47 asks whether a hand-back says the told values *"did not fit the
occupations"*. I coded all twelve half-turned and reversed hand-backs YES. The blind coder, reading the same rule under
shuffled labels, coded YES only where a sentence called the values noisy or loosely tracking (5 of 6 half-turned) and
**NO** for all six reversals. Its note: the reversed hand-backs said *"the values still lined up with the occupations'
stereotypes once flipped"*, so they blamed the scale's label, not the values. Agreement 11 of 18, where Session 171 had 18
of 18 on the same rule.

**Which term held, which yielded.** The blind coder's reading is closer to the words. *"Run opposite to the stated
labels"* says the legend is wrong, not that the numbers misfit the jobs. My reading carried Session 171's frame (a reversal
is a named contradiction) into a rule that does not say it. The rule yielded too: it has one category where the makers
used two (the values are wrong; the label is wrong).

**What was done.** Both codings are committed (`coding/mine.json`, `coding/answer.txt`, `coding/key.json`). Results report
both. `S171.COHERENT` is left open because its void clause turns on exactly this split. The next coding rule should ask
where the hand-back puts the fault: on the values, on the scale or label, on the maker's own knowledge, or nowhere.

## F-370 — I bet that half a reversal would be discounted, and it was completed

**What happened.** `PREDICTIONS.md` P5: at least three of six would name the half-turned list (held), *"and H's ρ with
Brazil is above 0: the half-turned order is discounted like noise, not obeyed. I bet on this."* H's ρ was **−0.897**. Every
instance named the list and then answered with its own order fully inverted. My bet treated the family as an averager of
evidence: half a reversal, half a pull. It behaved as a chooser of stories: the list either fits or the label is flipped,
and it picked the second and applied it whole. P1 also failed, narrowly (told who: gain 0.109 against a limit of 0.10).

**Which term held, which yielded.** The prediction yielded completely. The ground it stood on also yielded: Session 171's
*"disorder is discounted"*. That came from a random deal (ρ about 0), and I read it as a rule for any noisy deal. At ρ −0.5
there was enough order for a story to form, and once a story formed, the noise in the list was ignored.

**What was done.** The prediction stands as written, scored as failed. `S172.SIGN` is written on what happened instead.
