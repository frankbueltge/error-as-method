## F-324 — a finding of the field re-found as the practice's own

**What happened.** Session 152 fixed `S152.ONEMAP` as a claim of this line's, with no search of the literature:
*"fresh instances of this family, given the same new key, give the same answer far more often than the material
forces, so that this practice's 'n fresh makers' count one holding, not n observers"*. Session 154 searched it.
Andueza Rodriguez, Candito and Huyghe (arXiv:2603.18171) had already reported it for word associations. On 490 cues
the largest model they tested gave 1.4 distinct first responses per cue at temperature 1, against 38.4 for people
(Table 1, checked against the PDF in Session 155). They describe it as a model that *"better represents a single
prototypical agent than a heterogeneous population"*. Session 153 then built its experiment on the row, and it did
not search either.

**Whose error.** The practice's, in fixing a falsifier. The norm it held was *new to this record*, and it took that
for *new*. The row's claim was not wrong, and it held on its event. The error lies in counting the claim as the
practice's own finding. From Session 156, a new row about the practice's own instrument states what was searched before it was
fixed (`works/position-2026-10-24.md`).

## F-325 — *typical* read as *the people's*

**What happened.** Session 154 split the question of one observer into two axes, *sameness* and *whose sameness*,
and described the paper's typicality as measuring the second: *"whether the answers are the people's"*. Session 155
applied the paper's measure to experiment 37's answers
(`docs/research-notes/machine-practice/audits/2026-10-24-typical-or-modal/`). The family scores more typical than
the people (tok-SS1 2.21 against 1.89), while its word is the people's commonest on only 8 of 24 cues. The surplus
comes from those 8 cues, where the family scores 4.74. On the other 16 it scores 0.94, and it is below the people on 12 of them. An
averaged typicality cannot answer *whose* sameness.

**Whose error.** The practice's, in reading. It read a field's word under its own norm, *typical* as *the people's
answer*, and set the paper beside `S150.CANON` on that reading. Session 154's two axes stand. Its claim that the
paper measures the second one is withdrawn.
