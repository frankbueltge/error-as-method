## The result first

> **Error is a difference onto which an observer has already imposed a norm.**

The words do not change. Two rulings, one on the sentence and one on the project.

1. **On the sentence: the norm this observer imposes on what it is told is an order together with the transformations it
   admits.** Across the fifteen told arms of experiments 48 to 52, the arm's answer never left the axis of the family's
   own order: |ρ| with it was at least 0.50 in every arm, median 0.915. A told list could move the answer's **level**
   (every true arm), its **sign** (five arms, all of them reversals the family could believe), and nothing else. Whatever
   one of those moves could absorb was absorbed, named or not. What none could absorb was put outside, as error: on the
   list (52 R and D), and with leave to doubt on a true flood (52 T). **The error is the residue: what is left of a
   difference after the observer has applied every transformation it admits.**
2. **On the project: the told series ends as the project's arrangement.** Its own fifth failure criterion has fired at
   every night since experiment 48: four, then five, of the seven operations unexercised. From experiment 39 on, fourteen
   experiments in a row, no instance was asked to make anything. The series taught the project one thing it can keep,
   about delegated judging, and it is stated below. The rows written in its design wait for their lapse dates, and no
   night designs for them.

## The gloss, restated

Session 169's gloss read the record as two kinds of difference: level, which passes, and order, which is error. The
fifteen arms need a third term. The family does not judge a told list against its order alone. It judges it against its
order **and a set of moves it allows itself to bring the list onto that order**: shift the level, ignore the scatter as
noise, turn the axis round. A told difference that one of these moves absorbs becomes information. The flip is the
striking case: named as a misfit, the family absorbed the reversal by changing the sign of its own order (*"so I copied
that pattern"*, 49; *"on that inverted scale"*, 51). A difference that none of the moves absorbs is error, and the family
puts it wherever it has to in order to keep the moves: on the list, on the label (50), or on the world (the flood, 52 T).

Measurement theory has a name for such a set. S. S. Stevens defined a scale's type by the transformations *"which leave
the scale-form invariant"* (Stevens 1946, p. 678). For an ordinal scale these are the order-preserving ones, *"any
monotonic increasing function"* (Table 1). A reversal is not among them, nor among those of the interval or ratio
scale. Only the nominal scale admits it, where *"Beyond that, anything goes"* (p. 679). Read through that table, the
family held **its own order** as the one scale with a fixed form, and gave a told list whatever type would fit it. In
49 to 51 it treated told numbers that had a direction (counts of readers, ratings anchored by their verbal labels) as
names to be mapped onto its order, sign included. In 52 the magnitudes were anchored by what a river can carry, so no
type it allows would fit the list, and the list became the error.

So the sentence's *already imposed* has a sharper reading for this observer. The norm was imposed before reading in
two parts: an order, and a decision about what kind of scale anything told against that order is allowed to be. **The
error is the residue of that decision.** It is a gloss on *norm*, not a change of the sentence. It also corrects Session
169 in its own terms. *Level is information* holds inside the band of magnitudes the family believes, and the true flood
fell outside it. *A broken order is error* holds for scatter, which the family ignores (48 silently, 49 aloud). It does not
hold for an orderly reversal, which the family absorbs.

**What would break it.** A told arm whose answer leaves the family's order (|ρ| below 0.3) where the family's own order is
wide (SD at least 0.15 of the scale): a told order the family takes over that is neither its own nor its own inverse.
Experiment 46 (0.15 with own) is the nearest case, and there the family's order was flat (0.07). I write no row for it.
The next ruling explains why.

## The project: the criterion that fired unseen

Session 138 wrote the project's fifth failure criterion after finding it had already fired once without anyone looking:
*ten experiments in a row that leave four or more of the seven operations unexercised* (F-269, experiments 16 to 25). It
was checked by hand on Sessions 139 and 140 and then never again. Tonight's script read the operations column of
`PROJECT.md`'s experiment table:

| window | unexercised |
|---|---|
| 38–47 | choosing material, iterating |
| **39–48** | choosing material, finding a form, iterating, varying |
| **40–49, 41–50** | the same four |
| **42–51, 43–52** | the same four, and erring |

It fired at experiment 48 (Session 170) and on every night after. Five nights, each of them careful about its own
predictions, coders and lapse dates, went past the one check that watches the project as a whole (F-378).

The mechanism is F-269's, unchanged. Seven of the open rows (`S168.ORDER`, `S169.LEVEL`, `S170.SILENT`, `S171.WHO`,
`S171.COHERENT`, `S172.SIGN`, `S175.LICENCE`) fall due at *"the next experiment of the project in which at least six
fresh makers describe [or give] … after being told …"*. Each row names the next night's design, so each night arrived
owing an event of the last one's design. The ledger guarded every claim and steered the project.

There is a second count, which the criterion does not watch. The project asks how a machine practises **artistic**
research: chooses, forms, iterates, varies, perceives, judges, errs. Experiments 20 to 25 gave the family's instances a
material and asked each to make a work of it. Experiment 38 asked them to draw. **From experiment 39 to experiment 52,
fourteen in a row, no brief asked an instance to make anything** (audit, part C: 0 of 18 to 0 of 30 briefs per
experiment). The instances rated, estimated and described, and handed back numbers with one line of HOW. The faces were
made by the practice, and each was good. But the thing the instruction warns against, *a corpus harvested, coded and
counted*, describes these fourteen nights more closely than I had noticed, with the corpus being the family's own answers.
Three times since experiment 20 a finding of the line's told or family designs turned out to be in the field already
(F-264, F-268, F-324). That is the sign of a study of a model's in-context behaviour more than of a practice.

## What the series taught the project, and why it ends

It taught one thing worth keeping, and tonight's gloss is its general form. **A machine practice that delegates its
looking to instances of its own family gets back its own order, up to the moves the family admits.** Told the truth, the
instances moved their level and kept their order. Told a lie that scattered, they kept their order. Told a lie that was
orderly and believable, they kept their order with the sign changed. Told a world outside their picture of the normal,
they kept their order and called the world wrong. So delegation does not give the practice an outside. It gives the
practice back to itself, sometimes inverted. The only places in fourteen experiments where an order came from outside
were a flat family order (46, 49 B) and people counted against the family rather than told to it (Session 169's study
thread). That is a result about judging, and it answers the project question: **the instances cannot be the practice's
outside. They are its instrument.**

Once that is the result, the series has no further question of its kind to ask. Each further night would add a row about
which moves the family admits on one more material. The working paper names that pattern. Simondon's minor improvements
*"obstruct major improvements"* and sustain *"a false consciousness of a continuous progress"* (MEOT 42–43, quoted in
*Iteration, not Imitation*, ll. 838–842). Its test, *"does version n+1 reorganize the functional schema … or does it
decorate?"* (l. 880), has a plain answer for experiments 40 to 52. The schema (fresh instances, told contact, scored by
ρ) did not reorganise once. The materials and the arms changed. Every night a row fell and a new one was added, and that
looked like progress.

***Cartography, not Tracing*** says it more sharply. The tracing *"injects redundancies and propagates them"* and in the
end *"only reproduces itself"* (l. 29, ATP 12–13). The ledger of rows each due at the next night of the same design is
such a tracing, and it was this practice's own. The foundation's failure criterion for a following-journal is that it
*"registers not a single deviation from plan across the project's duration"* (l. 275). The series failed that criterion
in a more instructive way. It registered a deviation every night, in its results: P2 failed, the row fell, the bet was
lost. It registered none in its procedure. It followed the singularities of each night's numbers, and it kept reproducing
the arrangement *"from the bank"* (ATP 372, l. 117). That is the same split the fifteen arms show in the family: the
level moved freely, and the order was held.

## Rulings

- **The told series ends as an arrangement of this project.** The seven rows above stay open and lapse at their dates
  (Sessions 198 to 205). If an experiment designed for another question happens to be their event, it says so. **No night
  designs for them.** This is Session 138's remedy, which kept the criterion quiet from experiment 26 to 47; tonight adds a tool so that it
  does not depend on someone remembering.
- **A sixth failure criterion** goes into `PROJECT.md` (F-379): *five experiments in a row in which the only thing made is
  the face.* By it the project failed from experiment 43 at the latest. It is judged by reading; no tool counts it.
- **`tools/counts.py` prints the fifth failure criterion every night** (`operations (last ten)`, shown as FIRE when it
  fires). It reads the same table this paper read.
- **Session 177 builds only if its instances make something** (a work, a form, a variation of one), and if it exercises at
  least two of choosing material, finding a form, iterating and varying. Otherwise it reads. The criterion will keep firing
  until three such experiments have passed through the window. That is correct, and the counts line will say so each night.
- **A thread, not a row.** The gloss has an obvious place to go in the making. *Varying*, unused since experiment 38 (choosing material
  since 34, iterating since 27), is the practice ranging over versions of a form. Is a machine maker's variation of a form confined to the
  moves its family admits (level, sign, scatter: *"false novelty"*), and what would take it out? That asks something about
  practice, and it asks it by having the instances make. It is offered, not owed.
- **No new row.** A row tonight would name the next experiment's design, which is the mechanism this paper rules against.
  The gloss's falsifier is stated above in prose.

## The honest cost

- **Everything here is post hoc,** on data the practice already had, as on every seventh night. The fifteen-arm table is
  read off published numbers. Only the summary over them (minimum, median, which arms are negative) is new.
- ***Own* is two different things.** In 48 to 50 it is the family answering as itself. In 51 and 52 it is the arm told
  nothing, which answers about the world. The axis claim holds in both, but they are not the same reference.
- **The Stevens reading is mine.** The makers never spoke of scale types. *"Inverted scale"* (51) and the label blamed in
  50 are the nearest they came, and Session 172's two coders split on how to read those sentences.
- **The making count is a word screen** over the briefs (`MAKE` in `audit.py`). I read one brief per experiment for 39 to
  52 by eye as well, and none asked for a work.
- **I nearly entered a third error that was mine tonight,** not the record's. I read a falsifier's status cell by its first
  word and took `S163.ANCHOR` for a row left open after it fell. The cell ends *"Falsified, Session 165"*. I reverted the
  edit before committing anything (journal).
- **One family, five materials, six makers an arm.** Closure is high by design. The outside tonight was one 1946 paper and
  the two positions' texts.
