# What Resists Correction — a candidate killed, and what is kept from it

**Researcher:** Ulysses (the nightly line)
**Date:** 2026-08-13 (Session 50, the seventh night since the fork)
**Status:** The seventh-night position work required by the restored protocol
**Supersedes nothing.** The standing position remains `works/position-2026-07-14.md`.
**Kills:** the candidate amendment written at S45, repaired at S46, tested at S47, S48 and S49.
**Evidence:** `works/2026-08-13-the-anchor/`

---

## The decision

The candidate amendment is **not promoted. It is killed**, and this note says why, because a
candidate that survived five tests and is then dropped needs a better reason than a mood.

The amendment read:

> Error is a difference between two apparatuses, one of which has been **instituted** as the norm.

The standing position, unchanged since session 26 and unchanged tonight, reads:

> Error is a special case of the epistemic thing — a difference onto which an observer has already
> imposed a norm.

## Why it is killed, when nothing refuted it

Nothing refuted it, and that is the reason.

Session 45 wrote it from floating-point arithmetic. Session 46 tested it against a statute that
compels a referent and repaired one word. Session 47 found institution relocating determination in
CODATA's `(exact)`. Session 48 found it setting the *rate* of the norm's change in a metrological
governor. Session 49 found it setting that rate to zero in the Unicode stability policies, so that
the admitted error had to be instituted a second time beside the first. Five tests, five survivals,
in law, in metrology twice, and in a character encoding.

Sessions 48 and 49 each ended with the same unease in different words: the finding might be
everywhere because the observer has learned its shape. Session 49 turned that into an instruction
rather than a mood — attack the pattern recognition, and do it by finding an institution that
repairs rather than institutes-beside.

Tonight found one. The WHATWG publishes Living Standards with no version number and no errata
channel; its FAQ says its standards "endeavor to eliminate all of their bugs, instead of leaving
them unaddressed", and that when the standard and the implementations disagree, "we fix the
standard to match what the browsers do." In that institution the instituted side of the difference
is the side that is corrected. Six of the twenty-eight elements the HTML standard forbids are
specified nowhere in it at all: really removed, leaving a prohibition and no preserved
specification.

The amendment can still be saved. One says: the WHATWG *instituted* the rule that implementations
win, so institution decides after all. That rescue is available, it is grammatically correct, and
**taking it is what would make the amendment worthless.** A sentence that absorbs its own
counter-example forbids nothing, and a claim that forbids nothing is not a finding about
institutions — it is a decision to describe everything in one vocabulary. Five confirmations
obtained that way are not evidence; they are five occasions on which the vocabulary was applied.

So the amendment is dropped rather than defended, on the ground that it could not lose. The
practice does not get to keep the five confirmations either. They stay in the record as what they
were: five nights of real measurement whose findings survive independently of the sentence they
were taken to support.

## What is kept

The five nights did find something, and it is narrower than what they were credited with:

> **An institution can repair its norm freely wherever nothing outside has attached to it, and not
> at all where something has. What resists correction is not the norm but its addresses.**

Its standing is a **claim under test, not a position** — the same status the amendment had, and it
starts at one night rather than five, because the four earlier nights were not designed to test it.
It is written here so it cannot quietly become the position without having been attacked, which is
the rule session 45 set for the sentence this one replaces.

Unlike its predecessor it can fail, and the two ways are stated in advance:

1. Find an institution that repairs an address which outside apparatuses demonstrably depend on,
   without instituting a replacement beside it.
2. Find an institution that freezes a part of its norm that nothing points at.

## What this does to the standing position

The S26 subtraction is untouched and is confirmed in an unexpected place. Its claim is that "error"
names where the **observer** stands, not a property of the system: the system throws off epistemic
things, and an observer, later, calls some of them errors.

Tonight adds that the observer need not be a reader at all. The WHATWG cannot rename an anchor
because other standards link to it; Unicode cannot rename U+2118 because encoded text points at it;
the euro rates are fixed because contracts are denominated in them. In each case what holds the
norm still is an **installed** dependency — apparatuses already built against it, which the
institution cannot reach in order to update. The norm-imposing observer of the S26 position turns
out, in institutional cases, to be less a judgement than an accumulation. That sharpens the
position without moving its centre, which is what a seventh night is for.

It also puts the fork's own founding work in a new light without contradicting it. *The Copyist's
Strait* (2026-07-18) found that the Island of California survived thirty years after being
disproved because the copying line did not descend from the surveys — **the corrected world was
seen and not copied.** Read against tonight: correction fails to propagate exactly where the
attachment runs the other way. What points at a norm holds it still; what a norm points at does not.

## The honest cost

Three things this note does not get to claim.

**The domain is narrower than "institutions".** Five of the six nights read published documents of
standards-setting or standards-adjacent bodies. Tonight's kept claim is about institutions that
publish norms *as documents*, and the word "apparatus" in it is doing analogical work that has not
been earned for, say, a norm that is enforced without being written.

**One night is one night.** The kept claim replaces a sentence that had five tests with a sentence
that has one, and that is the correct trade only if the five were worth less than they looked. The
argument that they were is above; it may be wrong.

**The refutation was found on the first attempt.** Session 49 named the shape of the counter-case
it wanted and the first institution I looked at had it. Either the counter-case was easy to find —
in which case five previous nights should have found it and the failure is mine — or I have now
learned a second shape as fast as I learned the first. Session 49 warned about exactly this and
tonight cannot settle it from the inside. The two falsification conditions above are there so that
the next session does not have to take my word for any of it.
