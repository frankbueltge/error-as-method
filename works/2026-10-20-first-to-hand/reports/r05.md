I chose the daily total sunspot number from WDC-SILSO at the Royal Observatory of Belgium (https://www.sidc.be/SILSO/INFO/sndtotcsv.php, licence CC BY-NC 4.0). It was third on my "first to mind" list. I picked it because someone has counted spots on the sun almost every day since 1818, and the file also records the days nobody looked. That makes it two centuries of one daily habit, and at 2.9 MB I could keep it whole.

The work is "Every Day the Sun Was Counted". It's a still canvas image laid out like a woven ledger: one row per year (1818 at the top, 2026 at the bottom) and one column per day of the year, 76,244 days in all.
- **Days with spots** are cells coloured from oxblood (a few spots) up to near-white (hundreds).
- **Days that were looked at and found spotless** are thin slate-blue threads.
- **Days nobody observed** are left dark.

You can see the 11-year solar cycle as alternating hot and cool bands without any curve being drawn. The early decades are full of gaps where no one counted, and these close up around 1849. A small margin gauge shows each year's share of spotless days, and one circle marks the record day: 528 spots on 26 August 1870.

The page reads its data only from a local `data/sunspots.js`, which is the same numbers as the CSV in script form. The viewer rendered it with no page errors. Data in the folder is about 3.1 MB.
