I chose Old Faithful's eruption times for 2025. They come from GeyserTimes, a community database of geyser eruptions that publishes its data under the Open Database License (ODbL). I fetched one year in a single request to their public API: https://www.geysertimes.org/api/v5/entries/1735714800/1767250800/2. The geyser was the first of the three materials I listed. The other two were sunspot numbers and the dates a lake freezes and thaws, and they sit close to "geomagnetic activity" and "Arctic sea ice" on the list of used materials. A geyser's rhythm is clearly a separate domain.

The work, "Faithful", is a single still image drawn on a canvas. It has 365 thin rows, one per day, running from midnight to midnight in local time. Each eruption is a black tick, and a thread runs from it to the next eruption. The thread is rust when the geyser came back in under 83 minutes and slate blue when it took longer. Waits over 150 minutes are left blank, because an eruption was probably missed. A small histogram at the foot shows the geyser's two kinds of wait as two humps. The outline of the threads also shows the people who watched: the band widens in summer and narrows in winter, and the nights stay almost empty because hardly anyone sees the geyser in the dark. I rendered it three times; the final picture is `renders/03.png`. It has no page errors and no overlapping text.
