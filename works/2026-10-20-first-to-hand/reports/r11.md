I chose the eruption log of Old Faithful geyser in Yellowstone from the GeyserTimes archive (https://geysertimes.org/archive/geysers/Old_Faithful_eruptions.tsv.gz). It has about 205,000 logged eruptions from 1970 to now, licensed ODbL, fetched 2026-10-20. It was the second thing that came to mind. I passed over the first, sunspots, because it is too close to geomagnetic activity, which another work has already used. Old Faithful is clearly its own domain, and the archive has the whole record rather than the 272-row textbook sample.

The work is "The Waiting at Old Faithful", a single canvas still at 1100 × 800. It is 682 thin rows, one per month from 1970 at the bottom to October 2026 at the top. Each row shows how long that month's waits between eruptions were, on a scale from 35 to 130 minutes, and glows brighter where more eruptions fell. Read upward, the "steam" moves right. In the 1970s the waits spread from about 45 to 90 minutes. They are wide in the 1990s. From about 2001 they gather into one bright column near 90 minutes. The 1979–1991 band is left empty and labelled, because the archive has too few logged eruptions from those years to draw.

The viewer renders it with no page errors (`renders/02.png`). `CHOICE.md` and `NOTE.md` are written. The data folder is 4.4 MB: the raw .tsv.gz plus a derived `data/intervals.js`, which the page loads with a script tag, so it needs no network.
