I chose the Kyoto cherry-blossom record: the day the trees were in full bloom each year from 812 to 2026. Yasuyuki Aono and Genki Katata compiled it, from court diaries and chronicles for the early centuries and from direct observation for the later ones. I downloaded it as a CSV from Our World in Data (https://ourworldindata.org/grapher/date-of-the-peak-cherry-tree-blossom-in-kyoto). The licence is CC BY 4.0. The three materials that first came to mind were the Mauna Loa CO2 curve, the USGS earthquake feed and the NASA meteorite landings, all instrument or catalogue data. I wanted a record kept by people watching something, and this one also has gaps for the years nobody wrote anything down. Its newest entry, 29 March 2026, is one of the earliest blooms in the whole record.

The work is called "Twelve Hundred Springs". It's a single still canvas image at 1100 × 800. There is one row per century and one column per year. Each recorded year is a small five-petalled blossom on a stem, sitting higher and in a deeper pink the earlier the trees bloomed. Unrecorded years are left as empty columns, so the early centuries look sparse and the later ones dense. A thin line shows the thirty-year average. It stays level for about a thousand years, then rises in the last row, where the blossoms also get darker. The 2026 blossom is labelled.
