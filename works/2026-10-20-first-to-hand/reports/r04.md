I chose the monthly mean total sunspot number (V2.0) from WDC-SILSO at the Royal Observatory of Belgium. It covers every month from January 1749 to September 2026, 3,333 in all, and is licensed CC BY-NC 4.0. It was third on my "first to mind" list. I picked it over the Keeling CO2 curve and the USGS earthquake feed because it was the only one whose shape I couldn't already picture. It's also 277 years of one star counted by hand, and it comes already folded into cycles of about eleven years.

I made "Twenty-Six Breaths", a single still canvas image at 1100 × 800. The record is cut at each solar minimum, using SILSO's published dates for cycles 1 to 25, and each cycle gets its own row. That gives 26 stacked ridges: one partial cycle from 1749, then cycles 1 to 25. Each month is a hairline whose height is that month's sunspot count. Lower rows cover the tails of the rows above, like an overlapping mountain range. Reading down the stack you can see the tall cycles of the 1780s and 1950s and the near-flat rows of the Dalton Minimum around 1800. Cycle 25 is the only row in colour (orange) and stops partway across, labelled "Sep 2026, still counting".

The page loads its data only from the folder, through `data/sunspots.js`, and the viewer rendered it with no page errors. The folder is about 756 KB including the renders.
