I chose the lights of the major lighthouses around Britain, Ireland and the Channel. They come from OpenStreetMap/OpenSeaMap: 206 nodes tagged `seamark:type=light_major`, fetched on 2026-10-20 through an Overpass mirror under the ODbL licence. I picked them because a lighthouse is identified at night only by its rhythm of flashes, and the domain is unlike any on the excluded list. The other two first ideas were close to excluded domains: sunspots sit next to geomagnetic activity, and ship-log weather sits among the atmospheric sets.

The work is called "One Minute of Coast". It is a single still canvas with one thin row per light (199 of the 206 have a rhythm on record). The rows run from Shetland at the top to the Channel at the bottom, and every light is switched on at the same instant and drawn for sixty seconds.
- **Bright marks** follow the exact on/off sequence where the map records one.
- **Dimmer marks** are rhythms I rebuilt from the light's type, group and period, where no exact sequence was given.
- **Coloured sectors** split a light's row into stripes.

Because all the lights start together, lights with the same period line up into faint vertical seams that drift apart again. Across the middle runs a solid band of 19 steady lights. These are fixed lights near the Forth bridges, mapped as major lights alongside Fastnet and the Eddystone. I kept that band as it is and labelled it on the image.

The viewer renders the final version with no page errors; it is `renders/02.png`.
