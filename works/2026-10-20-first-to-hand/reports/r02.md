I used the SILSO monthly mean total sunspot number (version 2.0, January 1749 to September 2026) from the Royal Observatory of Belgium (https://www.sidc.be/SILSO/DATA/SN_m_tot_V2.0.csv). The SILSO data page gives the licence as CC BY-NC 4.0. It was the third of my first-to-mind list. The other two were the Mauna Loa CO2 record and the USGS earthquake feed. I chose it because it is nearly three centuries of people counting spots on the Sun every month. It also has a rhythm of about 11 years that never repeats exactly. The CO2 curve is too familiar to really be looked at, and a month of earthquakes is mostly scatter.

I made "Twenty-Five Suns", a still canvas image at 1100 × 800. The record is cut at each solar minimum and the cycles are stacked as overlapping ridges, one per line, oldest at the top. They all start at the same left edge, so strong cycles can be compared with weak ones and long cycles with short ones. The strong cycles spill over the lines above them, and the weak Dalton-minimum cycles (1798–1823) lie almost flat. The first line is the fragment observed before cycle 1, so it starts partway in. The last line, cycle 25, ends square at September 2026 because the cycle is still going.

- **Data loading:** the viewer could not load the CSV directly ("Failed to fetch"). I kept the CSV as downloaded and added a copy of the same text wrapped as a script (`data/sn.js`), which the page reads. Nothing is loaded from the network.
- **Cycle start dates:** I typed the cycle start dates into the page from memory of SILSO's published dates. I did not check them against a SILSO table or work them out from the data.

The folder is about 268 KB, and the last render (`renders/05.png`) showed no page errors.
