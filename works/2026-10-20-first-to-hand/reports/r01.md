I chose NOAA's monthly mean CO2 record from Mauna Loa (Global Monitoring Laboratory, with Scripps data for 1958 to 1974): https://gml.noaa.gov/webdata/ccgg/trends/co2/co2_mm_mlo.csv. It was second on my "First to mind" list and I knew its address, so I fetched it directly without searching. I picked it because it is one continuous line kept at a single place for nearly seventy years. It has a seasonal "breath" in it, and it has a gap: the months in 2022–23 when the eruption cut off the observatory and readings were taken on Maunakea instead.

The work is **"Every Breath Wider"**, a single still image drawn on a canvas. All 823 monthly means form one spiral. Each turn is a year, with January at the top going clockwise, and the distance from the centre is the CO2 level in parts per million. Each turn wobbles with the seasons and none closes on itself, so the coil only widens. Its colour shifts from ash grey in 1958 to ember red in 2026. The Maunakea months (December 2022 to June 2023) are a cold blue arc on the outer rings. A side column explains how to read it and gives the rise: +115.1 ppm with the seasonal cycle removed.

It renders through the viewer with no page errors. The data comes only from the folder: the CSV plus a JS copy of it, about 77 KB together. `CHOICE.md` and `NOTE.md` are written as you specified. NOTE.md gives the licence as I found it: the file names no formal licence, only a statement that the data are free to use and a request to credit GML.
