My material is the US National Geospatial-Intelligence Agency's (NGA) List of Lights, Publication 114. It covers 3,002 lighted aids to navigation around the British Isles, the English Channel and the North Sea. For each light it gives the position, colour, range and the exact flash and eclipse timings. I chose lighthouses because they were the first thing on my "First to mind" list. My first try was OpenStreetMap through the Overpass API, but that failed: the main server reset the connection and two mirror servers returned errors. So I took the data from NGA's own JSON API instead, at https://msi.nga.mil/api/publications/ngalol/lights-buoys?volume=114&includeRemovals=false&output=json. No licence is stated with the data; it is a US Government work that anyone can download.

I made **"Long Exposure"**, a still canvas image that shows the coasts as a whole night's exposure would record them.
- **What is drawn:** each of the 2,851 lights I kept glows in its own colour. The glow reaches out to the light's charted range, drawn at true scale.
- **How bright:** each light's brightness is set by the share of its period that it is actually lit. The glows are added together and toned like a long-exposure photograph.
- **What it shows:** the coastline appears made only of light, but inside out. Small, steady red and green harbour lights burn in strongly. Great lighthouses like Bishop Rock, which flashes for a tenth of a second every fifteen seconds, leave only a faint haze over the water.

**Assumptions in the image:**
- Fixed lights count as always on.
- Where the list gives no flash timings, I assumed a typical value for that kind of rhythm.
- Lights with no stated range are drawn at one nautical mile.

I left out radar beacons and the few entries with no position. The viewer rendered the page with no errors. The folder holds about 2.9 MB in all.
