I picked SILSO's monthly mean total sunspot number (version 2.0) from the Royal Observatory of Belgium. It has one value per month from January 1749 to September 2026, 3,333 months in all. It came from https://www.sidc.be/SILSO/datafiles and is licensed CC BY-NC 4.0. It was the third of my first three ideas. The other two were the USGS earthquake feed and the Mauna Loa CO2 record. I chose it because it has the longest unbroken record, and its cycle repeats a little differently each time.

The work is "Twenty-five Turns", a single still image. I cut the record at each solar minimum and stacked the 25 cycles one under another, all starting from the same left edge.
- **Each row:** a pale red field and outline show the raw monthly counts, and a dark line shows the 13-month smoothed mean. Each row's length in years is written where it ends.
- **Overlap:** strong cycles rise into the rows above them, like the peaks of 398 in May 1778 and 359 in October 1957. The weak cycles 5 and 6 (Dalton minimum, early 1800s) lie almost flat.
- **The last row:** cycle 25 stops in September 2026 with a dot marked "still turning".

The page reads the data only from local files and uses no libraries. The viewer rendered it at 1100 × 800 with no page errors.
