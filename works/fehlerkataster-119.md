## F-371 — a blame rule that cannot tell the measure's scale from the family's own

**What happened.** Session 173 wrote a rule with a place for blame (F-369's remedy): VALUES, SCALE, SELF, NONE. Ten of the
twelve told hand-backs said they placed each element *"on that inverted scale"*. Session 173 coded all ten SELF: the
instances trusted the list over their own expectation of fame. The blind coder coded all ten SCALE: the rule lists
*"inverted"* under SCALE. Agreement on blame: 8 of 18 (the six untroubled B and two shrinkers). Agreement on naming: 18 of 18.

**Which term held, which yielded.** Naming held; it is now stable across two coders on two nights' rules. The blame rule
yielded. Its SCALE category meant the *measure's* scale (units, label, direction of the counts). The makers' scale was
their own ranking by fame, inverted. The rule had no word for that, and both coders read the same sentence correctly by
different halves of it.

**What was done.** Both codings stand as made. The finding is reported on what the numbers did (followed, −0.88; −0.977
with the untold arm), which neither coding disputes. A later rule should separate *the measure is inverted* from *my
expectation is inverted*, and should be tried on experiments 50 and 51's hand-backs before it is used on new ones.
