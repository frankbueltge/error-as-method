## F-270 — the working paper's norm, forecast as a stranger's eye

**What happened.** P5 forecast that in at least 2 of the 3 K lineages a blind coder would not choose v6 as the
version that gives a stranger the most. The coder chose v6 in two lineages and v4 in one (`results.json`). P5 failed.

**Whose error.** The practice's. P5 was written from *Iteration, not Imitation* (§6 I2, after Simondon, MEOT 35 and
42–43): steps that add without reorganising are *"minor improvements"* that make *"false novelty"*. Coder S then coded
the K steps 10 *add*, 2 *trim* and 3 *reorganise*. On that norm, the late versions should have been no better. The
forecast took a norm about the *technical object's genesis* and turned it into a forecast of a *stranger's
preference*. The two parted. The coder preferred the most added-to version in both lineages that only added. It
marked down only the lineage that reorganised (*Sediment*, where v5 coded distance as brightness and the coder wrote
that the dimming *"muddies the small planets"*). This is F-185's kind again (Session 111): a norm the practice holds,
put in place of the readers' own.

**What it does to the record.** Nothing in the working paper is refuted by one coder of the makers' family, three
lineages and one material. What fails is the use of I2 as a forecast of reception. The project document says it as
a finding about judging: I2 names a norm a stranger's preference does not share.

## F-271 — a schema coder given the chain

**What happened.** Coder S was meant to code each step on its own, *"in no particular order"*. `code.py` copied each
step's two renders under the names `before.png` and `after.png`, and every *after* is the *before* of the next step
in its lineage. The coder noticed. In its own words, *"Every after-picture except T15's is the before-picture of the
next folder in its chain, so I could read each chain in order"*. It reconstructed all three K lineages in their true
order.

**Whose error.** The practice's, in its design of the packet. The shuffle hid the order of the folders and left the
order in the pictures. The coder could therefore code a step knowing what came before and after it. Its one
judgement that leans on that (T05, the hollow rings, *add* with *reorganise* second) does not change any prediction,
and P4 holds whether T05 counts as *add* or as *reorganise* (7 or 6 of 9; it needed 6).

**What it does to the record.** P4's margin is one step. A coder who sees steps without their chain needs the pairs
taken from different lineages, or the two pictures re-encoded so they cannot be matched byte for byte. That is a note
for the next packet, not a row.
