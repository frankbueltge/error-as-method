# notes

## The person and the first idea

Ed Kowalczyk, 56, a rural mail carrier outside Norwalk, Ohio, who in 1997 put up a twelve-room aluminum purple martin house in the back yard and has been keeping a log of the colony ever since; his daughter set up the GeoCities account for him over Christmas. My first idea is the page he would make in spring 1999: a heading, a "photo" of the martin house, a welcome paragraph in his own voice, a table of first-arrival dates by year, a few tips, links, a counter and a guestbook, a bit crowded and a bit off-center.

## What I noticed in the twenty pages

Most of them are not about the person at all but about one thing they love or sell (a TV show's fan fiction, a band, Jerry Springer, shark teeth, Transformers), and the "about me" is a sentence or two tucked inside. The furniture repeats everywhere: visitor counters with a start date, "last updated", guestbook sign/view links, webrings, e-mail links, notices that the page has moved or is closed, apologies for not updating. The HTML is careless and layered (unclosed tags, stray markup, font tags everywhere, editor-generated tables), the spelling slips, colors are loud against black or a tiled background, and several are only a frameset or a doorway to somewhere else.

## Versions

- v1: first full page (title, snapshot of the martin house, welcome in Ed's voice, colony log table, what's new, tips, links, counter, guestbook, mailbox). Looking at it, the bird silhouette in the heading read as a shark, the "under construction" sign was cut off, and the whole thing felt too neat and self-contained for a 1999 hobby page.
- v2: redrew the martin as a swept-wing bird seen from below and fixed the sign text (the shared images were regenerated, so v1's screenshot is the record of the old bird); added a webring box like the ones in the sheet and a caption apologizing for the throwaway-camera photo, because these pages always explain their own pictures and belong to a ring.
- v3: added what a page that has been kept for a season would accumulate: the GeoCities server comments, the first "Kim got the page working" entry, a loose un-indented "From last year" block of 1998 log lines he never deleted, one more link, and a "click here" line that runs into the divider. Looking at it, it now reads like something added to over months rather than laid out once, so I stopped.

## What I made

A Netscape Composer page for Ed, a rural mail carrier in Ohio who keeps purple martins, last updated in April 1999. Like most of the twenty pages it is not really about him but about the one thing he cares about, with the "about me" folded into the welcome; it carries the same furniture (counter with a start date, guestbook, webring, mailto, last-updated line, an under-construction promise) and the same small slips of spelling and markup. I kept it plain and earnest rather than loud, because a careful man in his fifties keeping records would make a tidy page with a table in it, and let the mess come from accretion: a log that grows at the top, last year's notes left at the bottom, a photo his daughter took. All images (paper background, bird, divider, snapshot, counter, sign, mailbox) are drawn by `make_images.js` on a canvas in headless Chromium.
