# Notes

Dale Kowalczyk, 59, retired in 1997 after 31 years as a signal maintainer for the Pennsylvania Railroad and then Conrail, lives in Altoona PA, and is building an HO-scale model of the Horseshoe Curve in his basement. His grandson put him on GeoCities (Heartland/Prairie) over Christmas 1998 and Dale has been adding to it ever since, one evening at a time. First idea: a long single page with a tiled background, a big centered title, a little moving train he got from "a clip art place" (I'll draw it), a layout progress report with dates, a photo-less "pictures coming soon" spot because the scanner isn't hooked up yet, a few links, a guestbook that is really a mailto, and a counter.

## Versions

- v1: First full page: title, animated train, intro, construction signs, dated progress log, Paint track plan, real-curve facts, links, mailto guestbook, counter. When I looked at it, it was too neat and too all-of-a-piece (every paragraph in Arial, nothing that wasn't designed), and the "NOT DONE" label ran off the edge of the track plan.
- v2: Moved the body text to the browser's default Times so only the headings are styled, the way most editor-made pages looked. Added what the render made me notice was missing for a 1999 hobby page: a scrolling marquee, a bordered roster table, a "Coming Soon" list he'll never finish, a webring box, a hidden MIDI of "I've Been Working on the Railroad", and the Heartland/GeoCities footer. Redrew the plan (plan2.gif) so the labels fit.
- v3: The roster table stretched past the 600-pixel text column and looked like a different page, so I set it to 600 wide and trimmed one comment. Looked at it again and handed it over.

Dale Kowalczyk is a retired Pennsy/Conrail signal maintainer in Altoona whose grandson set him up on GeoCities at Christmas 1998. The page is his: one long centered column on a speckled tan tile, with home-drawn GIFs (a little steam engine that runs along a strip of track, track-tie dividers, a blinking NEW!, construction signs, an odometer counter), a track plan he drew in Paint with the furnace in the corner, and a progress log in his voice about ballast, a mirror reservoir and glue in his good sweater. I tried to keep it as sincere and plain as he'd have made it, and not as a joke about old web pages: it's the stuff he's proud of, in roughly the order he added it, with the "pictures coming soon" that never came. All images and the MIDI were drawn or written by scripts in this folder (make_images.js with image_specs.js, and make_midi.js). Python's PIL was broken here, so the GIFs are drawn on a canvas in headless Chromium and encoded by hand.
