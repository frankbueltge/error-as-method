# notes

## Who and the first idea

Gordon "Gordy" Pfaff, 63, retired after thirty-one years carrying mail in Fond du Lac, Wisconsin. His grandson put
Netscape on the computer his daughter gave him for Christmas 1997, and over the next two winters he made himself a
page on GeoCities (Heartland) about ice fishing on Lake Winnebago, his shanty, the sturgeon spearing season, his
bowling league and his late dog. First idea: one long page on a pale blue tiled background, a homemade title GIF, a
picture of the shanty he drew himself, a list of "my catches", a guestbook link, a counter, "last updated" at the
bottom, and the plain, slightly formal voice of a man who writes letters.

## What I noticed in the twenty pages

Most of them are not much: frames that point elsewhere, a "this page has moved / been closed" notice, an empty
directory listing, a redirect to a new address. Where there is a real page, the images carried the design and the
text is short, earnest, typo-ridden and often apologetic about not updating ("i know i haven't updated in along
time"); people address the visitor directly, ask to be emailed, and say exactly when they started counting visitors.
The furniture recurs everywhere — hit counters with a "since" date, guestbooks (sign / view), webrings, "last
updated", GeoCities' own injected banner and pop-ups — and the HTML is messy: FONT tags on every line, CENTER left
unclosed, tables used for layout, generator tags from FrontPage, PageBuilder and Netscape.

## Versions

- v1: first full page — Composer-style HTML, homemade title, drawn shanty, ice report, catch table, sturgeon,
  Duke, counter, guestbook. Looking at it, it read too composed: Arial everywhere like a designer's choice, no
  typos though he apologises for them, and a couple of writerly jokes (the wife with binoculars, the TV-set-off
  quip) that belonged to me, not him.
- v2: let the body fall back to the browser's default Times as most people left it, kept Arial only on the headings
  he styled; plainer sentences with his real slips ("Alot", "there shanties", "Didnt", "brothers"), cut the jokes,
  added a NEW! by the ice report, a promised picture he never found, and the GeoCities "hosted by" line. Looking at
  it, it now reads like a man's page rather than a page about a man, and I would hand it over.

## What I made

A retired Wisconsin mail carrier's home page from January 1999, the winter after his first, made in Netscape
Composer the way his grandson showed him: a pale ice-blue tiled background, a shadowed title GIF, a picture of his
red shanty, an ice report that is clearly not going to be kept up, a table of what he caught, a paragraph on
sturgeon spearing, a snapshot of the dog that died, his bowling average, three links, an under-construction sign,
a counter with its start date, a guestbook and a mailto. The twenty pages showed me that the people were mostly
earnest, specific and a little apologetic, and that the furniture (counter, guestbook, last updated, hosted-by) was
half of what a page was; so I kept his voice flat and practical, let the useful things he knows (ice thickness,
where the crack is) carry the page, and left the gaps a real page has — the stale report, the missing sturgeon
photo, the scans waiting on Tyler. All images are drawn by make_gifs.py in this folder.
