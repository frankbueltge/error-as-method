# notes

## Before v1

The person: Wendell Haack, 61, a retired rural mail carrier who lives on an acreage outside a small town in eastern
Nebraska. He has read the rain gauge on the fence post by his garden every morning at 7 since 1971 and writes it in a
ledger; his grandson showed him GeoCities in 1998 and he put the numbers up "so they are somewhere besides my kitchen
drawer." First idea: a plain Netscape Composer page, light background, a big heading, a short welcome in his own
voice, this year's monthly totals in a bordered table next to his 28-year average, a few remarks about weather events,
a homemade picture of the gauge, counter, guestbook, last-updated date.

What I noticed in the twenty: most are not "about me" pages at all but a page about the one thing the person cares
about (a TV show, a band, shark teeth, Transformers, Hitchhiker's Guide), with the person showing up sideways in
apologies, asides and spelling ("i know i haven't updated in along time"). The furniture is constant and broken: a
counter with a start date, a guestbook on some outside service, "last updated", webrings, GeoCities banners and pop-up
ads, generator meta tags from FrontPage or Composer, unclosed tags, and images that are now missing. Many are
half-finished, closed, or just a frameset, and they are laid out with center tags, font tags and tables, not CSS.

## Versions
- v1: first full page (heading, welcome beside a photo of the gauge, 1999 table against his average, margin notes,
  other years, links, guestbook and counter). Looking at it, it was too tidy and too much like a designed article, and
  the drawn photo was big and read as clip-art rather than a snapshot.
- v2: replaced the photo with a small, blurred, warm, date-stamped drugstore-print scan floated right in the text; let
  his voice get plainer and more particular (mail route, Farm King gauge, Dale's beans, snow melted on the stove);
  added the grandson's touches (marquee, server comment, pale blue background) and the GeoCities hosted-by line.
  Looking at it, the "^ The gauge" caption landed centred under the text, pointing at nothing.
- v3: moved the caption into a small parenthetical paragraph in the text, the way someone without a caption tool would
  do it. Looking at it, the first screen reads as his page and nothing else needed changing, so I stopped.

What I made: the GeoCities page of Wendell Haack, a retired rural mail carrier outside Hooper, Nebraska, who has
written down his rain gauge every morning since 1971. Like most of the twenty pages, it is not really about him but
about the one thing he cares about, and he comes through in the asides: the neighbour who forgot to read the gauge,
the tomato starts lost to hail, the Y2K joke, the ledgers he types up in the evenings. The table is the heart of it
because a careful man would put his numbers first. The rest is the usual furniture of the time, made with Netscape
Composer and font tags: a counter with a start date, a guestbook, a last-updated line, links to the weather service,
a link to the grandson who set it up, and a marquee he probably added. All the images (the snapshot, the animated rain
cloud, the raindrop bullet and the counter) were drawn by script in this folder.
