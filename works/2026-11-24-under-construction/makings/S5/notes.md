# notes

## Who and the first idea

Lorraine Kostecki, 48, works the lunch line at a grade school outside Kenosha, Wisconsin, and has kept a notebook for
years of when the ruby-throated hummingbirds first show up at her kitchen-window feeder. In 1998 her son put her on
GeoCities with FrontPage Express and she made one page out of the notebook: the arrival dates year by year, her sugar
water recipe, a picture of the feeder, and a plea for other people to e-mail her their dates.

## What I noticed in the twenty

Most of them are thin or half-broken: frames with nothing behind them, a "this page is closed" notice, a bare directory
listing, a sales page for shark teeth laid out by a page-builder grid. The ones with a person in them are about one
enthusiasm (a TV show, a band, a hobby, a cartoon) and speak straight to the visitor, apologise for not updating,
misspell things, ask for e-mail and guestbook signatures, and count visitors "since" a date. The markup is font tags,
centered everything, tables for layout, a generator meta tag, and a last-updated line at the bottom.

## Versions

- v1: the first full page (title with two hummingbirds, feeder snapshot beside her welcome, the year-by-year table,
  recipe, e-mail, guestbook, counter). Looking at it, the feeder picture read as a diagram cut in half by a window bar,
  not a snapshot, and the page only talked outward; nobody had answered her yet.
- v2: redrew the feeder as a grainy, slightly soft drugstore print without the crossbar, and added the list of dates
  people had e-mailed her for 1999, so the page shows the thing she made it for actually happening. Looking at it, the
  page reads as hers, wrapped title birds and lopsided left-aligned recipe included, so I stopped here.

## What I made

A single-subject page by a school lunch-line worker in Wisconsin who has written down the day her first hummingbird
arrives every spring since 1991. It is built the way FrontPage Express would have built it in 1999: font tags, a
centered table, Comic Sans that falls back to the browser default, a homemade odometer counter "since August 9 1998",
an under-construction sign promising the orioles. I kept it to the one notebook she has, plus the small exchange the
web gave her (her sister in Racine beating her again, a man in Thunder Bay), because the pages in the sheet that had a
person in them were almost all one enthusiasm spoken directly to whoever came by, with the spelling and grammar left as
typed.
