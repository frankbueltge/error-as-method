# First to mind

1. The weekly Billboard Hot 100 chart dates (a record that is supposed to appear every seven days).
2. Daily US Treasury "Debt to the Penny" (total public debt outstanding, published each business day).
3. A long daily weather-station temperature record (e.g. a GHCN-Daily station).

# Chosen

The list of issue dates of every Billboard Hot 100 chart, 1958 to the present, from the community-maintained
"Historic Billboard Hot 100 Data" repository by mhollingshead (valid_dates.json):
https://raw.githubusercontent.com/mhollingshead/billboard-hot-100/main/valid_dates.json
(repository: https://github.com/mhollingshead/billboard-hot-100).

How I came to it: it was the first thing on my list. GAPS asks whether a record keeps its own cadence, and a
weekly chart is a record whose whole identity is a cadence: one chart every seven days. Music charts are not
among the domains already used, and this repository publishes the chart dates as one clean list, so the
instrument would read the chart's own calendar rather than my preparation of it.

# Expected

I expect a cadence of 7 days and almost no breaks, except for a handful of irregular steps (shorter than a
week) where Billboard moved the weekday its issues are dated, most likely around 1962 when the dating changed
from Mondays to Saturdays; I do not expect any real gaps.
