# Chosen

Fortnightly reported measles cases in Cambridge, England, 1944-1966, one column of the "60 cities" measles
dataset of England and Wales (Registrar General's reports) published by Ottar Bjørnstad's lab at Penn State:
https://ento.psu.edu/research/labs/ottar-bjornstad/ottar-lab-abstracts/60measles.txt
(page: https://ento.psu.edu/research/labs/ottar-bjornstad/ottar-lab-abstracts/tsir-analysis-of-measles-in-england-and-wales).
Pre-vaccine measles was the first thing on my list; a web search for the England and Wales city series led
to this page. I already know that London's measles came in a two-year rhythm, so I picked a small city,
Cambridge (about 90,000 people, below the often-cited "critical community size"), whose rhythm I do not know.

# Expected

I expect a strong peak near 26 fortnights (one year, the school-term seasonality) and probably one near 52
fortnights (two years), but less clean than London's biennial cycle, since in a small city measles fades out
between epidemics and returns irregularly; long-period power may also be large.
