# First to mind

1. Monthly water levels of the Great Lakes (NOAA GLERL / USACE, since 1918)
2. Weekly US retail gasoline prices (US Energy Information Administration)
3. The Titanic passenger list (age and fare of each passenger)

# Chosen

Monthly mean water levels of Lake Michigan-Huron, 1918 to September 2026 (metres above IGLD 1985),
published by the Coordinating Committee on Great Lakes Basic Hydraulic and Hydrologic Data:
"Lake Michigan-Huron Monthly Mean Water Levels 1918 to 2026", listed at
https://www.greatlakescc.org/en/coordinating-committee-products-and-datasets/ (file served from
https://drive.google.com/file/d/1z5010rdGBEmyFrVPOEOu-1PpxP8u5Hta/view).

How I came to it: the first item that came to mind, lake levels, is not in the list of used domains
(river discharge and tides are, but a closed-basin lake level is neither). The old NOAA GLERL page I
remembered now returns 404; a web search led to GLISA's note that the data moved to the Coordinating
Committee site, where I picked Michigan-Huron, the lake with the famous swing from the record low of
2013 to the record high of 2020.

# Expected

A line of about 1,300 months with a fine yearly sawtooth (summer high, winter low, roughly 0.3 m)
riding on slow multi-year swings of over a metre: lows in the mid-1930s and mid-1960s, highs around
1952, 1973 and 1986, a long low stretch 1999 to 2013, the sharp rise to the 2020 peak, and a decline
since.
