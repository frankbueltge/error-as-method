# First to mind

1. Billboard Hot 100 weekly chart dates (popular music charts)
2. NASA Astronomy Picture of the Day publication dates
3. Volcanic eruption records from the Smithsonian Global Volcanism Program

# Chosen

The dates of the Billboard Hot 100 weekly chart, 1958 to 2026, from the UT Data archive "rwd-billboard-data"
(https://raw.githubusercontent.com/utdata/rwd-billboard-data/main/data-out/hot-100-current.csv, project at
https://github.com/utdata/rwd-billboard-data). GAPS reads cadence, so I wanted a record that is meant to
keep a strict beat set by people rather than by nature; the weekly pop chart was the first of my three to
mind, and I knew of this archive as a cleaned, openly licensed (MIT) copy of the full chart history.

# Expected

A cadence of 7 days kept almost everywhere, with a handful of irregular steps where Billboard moved the
weekday the chart is dated (I recall a shift from Monday to Saturday dating around 1962), and possibly one
or two gaps around holidays or missing issues in the archive.
