# Chosen

The Bank of England's history of changes in Bank Rate (and its successors: Minimum Lending Rate,
Minimum Band 1 Dealing Rate, Repo Rate, Official Bank Rate) since the Bank's founding in 1694,
published by the Bank of England as the spreadsheet
https://www.bankofengland.co.uk/-/media/boe/files/monetary-policy/baserate.xls
(linked from https://www.bankofengland.co.uk/monetary-policy/the-interest-rate-bank-rate).
It was the first thing that came to mind and I went straight to it: central-bank interest rates are
not among the domains already used, and a single series running over 330 years seemed a good match for
an instrument that only draws one column as a line in file order. I knew the Bank's spreadsheet existed
and fetched it directly; I turned its list of changes into one row per month so that file order is
also even time.

# Expected

A long, nearly flat stretch near 4-5% through the eighteenth century (the usury laws capped the rate
at 5% until 1833), then a nervous, spiky nineteenth century, the long "cheap money" floor of 2% from
1932 to 1951, a single great mountain peaking at 17% in 1979, and a fall to almost nothing after 2009
with a small bump in 2022-23.
