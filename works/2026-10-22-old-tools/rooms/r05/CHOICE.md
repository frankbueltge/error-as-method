# First to mind

1. Global mean surface temperature anomalies (e.g. HadCRUT or NASA GISTEMP, monthly)
2. Great Lakes monthly water levels (NOAA GLERL)
3. Weekly retail gasoline prices in the United States (EIA)

# Chosen

Weekly U.S. average retail price of regular gasoline (dollars per gallon, all formulations), collected by
the U.S. Energy Information Administration and republished by FRED (Federal Reserve Bank of St. Louis) as
series GASREGW: https://fred.stlouisfed.org/series/GASREGW (CSV: https://fred.stlouisfed.org/graph/fredgraph.csv?id=GASREGW).
I came to it from the third item on my first list: of the three it was the only one not near an excluded
domain (temperature anomalies sit close to CO2/climate, lake levels close to river discharge and sea level).
The EIA's own download is an old .xls I could not open here, so I took the same series as CSV from FRED.

# Expected

A line that sits near $1 through the 1990s, climbs through the 2000s to a peak around $4 in mid-2008 and
collapses at the end of that year, a second high plateau 2011-2014, a drop in 2015-2016 and a 2020 dip,
then the sharp spike to about $5 in June 2022; I expect the last stretch in 2026 to be somewhere around $3-4.
