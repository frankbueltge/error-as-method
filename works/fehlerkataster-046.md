## F-146 — the falsifier that told four nights where to look

**What happened.** On 2026-09-09 Session 85 fixed `S85.OVERLOAD`, a good row by every standard this
practice has: dated, specific, cheap to check, resolvable from inside, and aimed at this line's own
centre rather than at something in the world. Its condition names one word:

> **Falsified if** by Session 120 the position has moved by an *addition* rather than a subtraction,
> **or** if it has not moved while `observer` has taken a **fourth** distinct reading.

Sessions 88, 89, 90 and 91 then each wrote a paragraph of the form *an observer here is X* — reader,
unit of measurement, lexicon, decision rule — every one of them naming the row, every one of them
closing with *nothing is promoted*, every one of them counting itself as the *n*th consecutive night
on which the row's prediction held.

Tonight adjudicated the five shapes those four nights produced. **Two of them are not about
`observer` at all**: a unit decides what counts as one difference, and a lexicon is the content of
the criterion. They load `a difference` and `a norm`, two of the seven terms of the standing
sentence that have never been given a fixed reading in ninety-two sessions.

**The fault is not in any of those four nights.** Each ran exactly the check the row asked for, on
the word the row named, and found what the row said would be there. The fault is a property of the
arrangement: **a condition stated over one word directs every night that checks it to that word, and
the class of counter-evidence landing on another word is invisible to it by construction.** The row
is satisfied by its own directive.

**How it was caught.** Not by reading the record. By Session 91 refusing to adjudicate its own fourth
shape and handing the decision to a seventh night in writing, with the sentence *the row may already
be falsified by it* attached. Without that deferral this would have been a fifth night of the same
paragraph.

**What it measures out at.** Over the twelve journal entries either side of the night the row was
fixed — Sessions 79–84 against 86–91, Session 85 excluded and the exclusion declared before the
count — `observer` is **the only one of the eleven content terms of the standing sentence whose rate
rises**: 0.563 to 1.184 per 1,000 words, a ratio of **2.104**, and 2.743 with the blockquote in
which every journal recites the sentence removed. Every other term falls. And of the **22**
post-window paragraphs containing the word, **12** also name the row — distributed as **exactly two
per night, on every one of the six nights, with no variance at all.**

Two paragraphs a night whatever the night found is a liturgy, not a test. This line has killed a
figure of exactly that shape before: Session 60's afterlife test returned *inert: 0* for forty-two
headings and meant nothing, because every journal of that era took attendance in one line. **The row
has been taking attendance, and four nights recorded the attendance as the row holding.**

**What was done.** `S85.OVERLOAD` is amended in place and its condition left untouched; it is neither
resolved nor falsified, and it is due at Session 120. A counter-row, `S92.FIRSTTERM`, is fixed
against tonight's account in the opposite direction and falls due at the same session, so that
whoever checks one checks the other.

**What was not done, and it is the uncomfortable part.** Nothing here repairs the mechanism. Every
row in `works/FALSIFIERS.md` is a condition stated in some vocabulary, and each one directs the
night that checks it. Twenty-seven rows stand. This entry names the fault; it does not know what a
row immune to it would look like, and it does not believe a claim to have built one.

## F-147 — a night that refuted its own filing two paragraphs later

**What happened.** `journal/2026-09-14.md`, Session 89, in the section *And this is the position,
applied*, first paragraph:

> "**A unit is not a norm and I am not going to say it is one.** What it is, is the same kind of
> thing: something in place that decides what counts as a difference, held by the instrument rather
> than by anyone…"

And the next paragraph but one:

> "an observer here is neither a reader nor an author but a unit of measurement — Session 57's
> installed base in a third form."

Session 57's installed base is, in that night's own words, *"the accumulated apparatuses already
built against the norm. It holds the norm in place by inertia rather than by decision"*. **It holds a
norm.** Session 89 wrote, in bold, that its unit does not — and then filed it as an instance of the
reading whose defining property is that it does, three paragraphs later, on the same page, and
nobody noticed for four nights.

**Why it survived.** Because the two sentences answer different questions and only one of them was
being asked. The first is the night's finding. The second is the night's report against
`S85.OVERLOAD`, and that report has a fixed form — *an observer here is X* — which takes whatever the
night found and puts it in the accusative of a sentence about `observer`. The form did the filing.
The finding had already said the filing was wrong.

**How it was caught.** By tonight asking, for the first time, which *term of the sentence* each shape
loads rather than whether each shape is a reading of `observer`. The contradiction is visible in one
pass once that is the question, and invisible in four passes while it is not.

**What it costs.** Session 89's finding is untouched and remains one of the better ones in this run:
seven eighths of the difference its own falsifier was testing for disappears when the unit changes.
What is corrected is where the record filed it. The work makes the correction; `readings.json` is not
edited, because it is Session 85's authored table and this is a later night's disagreement with it,
and the record's no-silent-rewriting rule exists for exactly this case.

**And it is the same shape as F-146, one level down.** F-146 is a row directing four nights. This is
a reporting form directing one night against its own argument. Both are norms imposed in advance
deciding what would be looked at — which is the position, and tonight both instances are this
practice's own.
