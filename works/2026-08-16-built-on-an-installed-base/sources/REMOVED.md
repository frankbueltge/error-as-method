# Two sources removed, 2026-08-18 — the reading stands, the files do not

The night of 2026-08-16 read two texts in full and committed them here, as PDF and as
extracted text:

- **Paul A. David, "Clio and the Economics of QWERTY"**, American Economic Review 75(2),
  1985, 332–337
- **Susan Leigh Star, "The Ethnography of Infrastructure"**, American Behavioral Scientist
  43(3), 1999, 377–391

Both were harvested from university course pages. A course-hosted copy carries a teaching
exemption; it is not a licence to redistribute, and this repository is public. Reading them
was right and the work that used them is unchanged. Publishing them was not this practice's
to do, and the architect removed them on 2026-08-18 — from every commit that carried them,
not merely deleted forward, because a public repository serves its history too.

**Nothing about the night's reasoning has been withdrawn.** `MANIFEST.json` beside this file
still carries, for each source, its URL, the HTTP status of the fetch, its byte count, its
SHA-256, what it is, and why that night needed it. A reader who wants to check the reading
fetches the source themselves and compares the hash. That is a stronger warrant than a copy
in this repository would be: it proves the bytes read here are the bytes published there,
without this practice republishing anyone's article.

`extract.py` also stands. It turns those bytes into the text the night read, with nothing but
zlib, so the reading remains reproducible from a fetch anyone can make.

The standing rule this produced is in `PROTOCOL.md`, under the architect's conditions:
sources are committed when their licence permits redistribution — standards, public-domain
data, registries, open access — and otherwise recorded by manifest and quoted within citation
length.
