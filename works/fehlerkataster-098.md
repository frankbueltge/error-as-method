## F-312 — the family's sameness was claimed without a null, for twenty-five experiments

**What happened.** Since experiment 9 (`S112.FAMILY`) this line has made claims about what *this family* shares.
It does not multiply standpoints. It makes one form six times (experiment 12). It chooses from one shelf
(experiments 28 and 34). Every such claim compared fresh makers with each other, or with the practice's own
record. None compared them with people given the same brief, and none measured how alike two answers to one
brief are when they say different things. Schaeffer et al. (arXiv:2609.33936, §3) put it in one line:
*"Homogeneity is a comparative property, so a similarity threshold without a null distribution is not a
measurement."* The line's measures are discrete codes, not cosine thresholds: SAME, KIN, OFF on a shelf, a
coder's distance from a mould. So the paper's second null, the geometry of one prompt, bites less here. Its
first point bites fully. Without a human pool, *"this practice would choose what its siblings choose"*
(experiment 28) cannot be told apart from *it would choose what its teachers chose*.

**Whose error.** The practice's, in design, repeated. It had a reason it never wrote down: the project asks what
a machine practice does, and a human pool seemed to be about people. But a claim about what is the machine's own
needs exactly that reference. The project question asks for the machine's *own* strengths. A trait the practice
shares with the human canon it learned from is not one of them, and the line had been filing such traits
as its own.

## F-313 — experiment 28 read a teaching canon as a second shelf

**What happened.** Experiment 28 coded three Old Faithful choices **OFF** its shelf and wrote that a list of
exclusions moved the makers *"onto a second shelf the first did not show"*. The geyser's eruption data is
`faithful` in R's base `datasets` package. That is the same index that holds `co2` (Mauna Loa) and `sunspots`, the
first shelf's two most-taken items. Nine of experiment 28's twelve choices are in that index (six as the same
series, three as kin). So the "second shelf" may be the same human canon, entered lower down.

**Whose error.** The practice's, in reading. The shelf was built only from the family's own lists, so anything
outside those lists looked like a new place. The correction does not touch experiment 28's counts, which were
right by its own codes. It changes only what *second shelf* can mean. Not retouched there; noted here.
