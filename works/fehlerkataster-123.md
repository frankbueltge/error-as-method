## F-385 — the makers started where the conductor stood

**What happened.** Each maker's brief named its own scratch folder and forbade opening any file outside it. A maker's
shell starts in the conductor's working directory, which at launch was the experiment's own folder in this repository.
Three of four drop-first makers (c2, c3, c4) ran their first `python3 tangram.py check` there; the repository's copy of
`tangram.py` ran and failed at once (no drop file), writing nothing. c2's command also listed the folder, so its context
held the file names, `predictions.md` among them. c4 tried to open two pictures there that did not exist. All three
reported it in `files_opened` or a note; none read a file's contents.

**Whose error.** The practice's: the arrangement put the makers' starting point inside the record they were not to see.
**What was done.** Not excluded: `predictions.md` set no exclusion rule for this case, and no maker read a file. Named in
the journal and here. A next night starts its makers from a neutral folder (`cd` to the scratch area before launching)
or names the folder in every command of the brief.

## F-386 — a measure of nearness that measures gathering

**What happened.** P1 and P2 were scored on silhouette overlap with the nearest of KILOGRAM's 1,013 book figures. Both
held. Post hoc, overlap correlates with compactness (area over convex-hull area) at r = 0.81 across the 24 figures and
0.74 across the 300 chance drops; the book's figures are compact (median 0.68), drops loose (0.48), word-first figures
between (0.62), drop-first at the drops' value (0.48). So P1 and P2 mostly say that word-first figures are gathered like
the book's and drop-first ones scattered like chance.

**Whose error.** The practice's, in an instrument written on the night: overlap after centring and scaling rewards any
compact blob against a canon of compact figures. **What was done.** P1 and P2 stand as scored (the rule was committed);
the work, the face and the journal read them as compactness, not copying. The evidence for tracing the night keeps is
the makers' own words (four of four: the traditional cat) and P6, which used people's names, not outlines.

## F-387 — the prediction that making is trial and error

**What happened.** P7 predicted at least three checks a figure and at least one invalid figure per word-first maker. The
median was two, and no word-first maker ever made an invalid figure. All four wrote their own programs (`fit.py`,
`make.py`, `build.py`) that turn a planned outline into piece positions; they drew on a grid, solved, then looked.

**Whose error.** The practice's. It imposed a human picture of tangram play (fiddling pieces until they fit) as the norm
of making before watching a machine make. The makers did something else, and it is the night's clearest view of a
machine's own means: geometry as calculation, so that the eye is used only to judge legibility (a tail lifted, a gap
opened between sails, a house renamed as the arrow it reads as).

## F-388 — the cap that became a target

**What happened.** P8 predicted that drop-first makers would change at most one piece in the median. They changed two,
the cap, in 8 of 12 figures, one in 2, and twice left the drop as it fell.

**Whose error.** The practice's. "At most two" was meant as a limit and read as an allowance. The brief cannot separate
the makers' disposition to use what is given from their seeing; c4 left one drop unchanged because "moving the loose small
triangles did not seem to make it clearer". **What was done.** Scored as failed; nothing re-read.
