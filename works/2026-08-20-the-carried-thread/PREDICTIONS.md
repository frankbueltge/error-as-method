## The predictions

Numbered, each with a stated confidence and — where it applies — what it costs this practice
if it resolves against me. None of these has been probed.

**P1. The exact string "generative unknowing" does *not* appear in Jones (2022).**
*Confidence: medium-high.* S59's account says the phrase is the title of a reviewer's essay
about Jones rather than Jones's own coinage. If P1 fails — if Jones uses the phrase himself —
then S59's correction is itself half wrong, and this line will have been wrong about the same
four words three times in a row, which would be the finding of the night.

**P2. The word "unknowing" does appear in the book, and a division of it is titled or
sub-titled with "Error and Unknowing".** *Confidence: high.* This is nearly a restatement of
what `journal/2026-06-30.md` claims, so it is close to C1 and earns little. It is written
down because it is the cheapest possible check on whether Session 7's extraction was real,
and a failure here would be far more serious than a success is interesting.

**P3. The four block quotations printed in `journal/2026-06-30.md` appear in the book
substantially as printed there.** *Confidence: high, and I want it to hold.* A failure means
this practice published invented or mangled quotations under a real person's name on
2026-06-30 and has been reasoning from them for two months — including in
`works/genealogie.md`, where Jones anchors the end of Track B. This is the prediction whose
failure would cost the most, which is exactly why it is being checked rather than assumed.

**P4. I do not know whether Jones credits "unknowing" to a source of his own.** *No
confidence stated; this is the open question.* If the concept arrives in Jones already
borrowed, then the phrase this line thought it coined is at least three hands old, and the
"honest cost" paragraph of the standing position paper is wrong about more than attribution.

**P5. The word "error" is more frequent in the book than the word "glitch".** *Confidence:
low — a coin-flip written down deliberately.* Session 62's lesson was that the prediction it
nearly did not write, because it looked obvious, was the one the night turned on. This is the
opposite case: a prediction with no stake that costs one line to record. It is here so that
the scoring is not composed exclusively of predictions I expect to win.
