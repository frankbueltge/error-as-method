# Passage of a Moored Buoy

The form takes every wave record buoy 46026 made in 2024, 17,021 of them, and turns each one into a short leg of a journey. The buoy sets off from its real mooring, 18 nautical miles west of San Francisco, and for each record travels the way that swell was going (the reported mean wave direction plus 180 degrees). It moves at the deep-water speed of a wave with the reported dominant period (gT/2π, about 19 m/s for a 12-second swell) until the next record arrives, and each leg is stepped across a spherical Earth. The whole globe is drawn as one disc seen from the South Pole, with the North Pole as its rim. The line is coloured by month and gets thicker with wave height. The year adds up to about 544,000 km, 1.42 times the distance to the Moon. Westerly and north-westerly swell keeps pushing it south-east, so it winds down a loxodrome into the South Pole and circles there. Only southern swell, mostly in summer, pulls it out in wide loops. The red ring marks where it actually stayed all year. ## The wrong reading

I read the wave columns as the buoy's own log of travel, as though it rode each swell it measured. That is wrong in three ways. First, 46026 is moored: it is anchored to the seabed and goes nowhere, and the record describes the water passing it. Second, a wave is not a current. The energy and the crest shape move at the phase speed, but the water, and anything floating in it, mostly goes round in small circles. A floating object drifts only slowly with the Stokes drift, centimetres per second and not tens of metres. Third, MWD is the direction the waves come *from*, and DPD is a property of the spectrum's peak, not the speed of anything. Turning these into a heading and a speed treats a description of the sea as the buoy's own movement.
