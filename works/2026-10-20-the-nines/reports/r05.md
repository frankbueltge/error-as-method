I made "One Wave", a still canvas drawing from buoy 46026's 2024 record, and it renders without page errors.

**The form:** each of the 17,023 rows that has a wave measurement is drawn as one sine cycle. Its height is WVHT and its length is DPD. The cycles run end to end as a single unbroken line, wrapped into 44 rows like lines of text. Winter storms spill into the neighbouring rows, summer narrows to a dark seam, and a calm spell in late November thins to a thread. The left margin shows an invented elapsed clock, because the whole year passes in 53 h 36 min. The right margin shows, in grey, the real date each row came from.

**The wrong reading:** I treated each record as one wave, followed directly by the next. That is wrong for three reasons:
- WVHT is the significant wave height of a 20-minute sample, a statistic, not the height of one wave.
- DPD is the period with the most energy in the spectrum, not how long one wave lasted. Each record stands for a hundred or more real waves.
- The records are about half an hour apart, and laying them end to end throws away all the time between them. 8,784 hours shrink to 53.6.

**One extra file:** the viewer opens the page from `file://`, where the browser cannot read a plain text file. So I added `data/46026h2024.js`, which holds the original text unchanged inside one JavaScript string. The page loads that and parses it itself, with no network. The original `.txt` is untouched.
