I made "Air at 999 Degrees", a single still image on a canvas. My wrong reading was to treat the buoy file's missing-value placeholders (99, 999, 9999) as real measurements.

All 49,309 ten-minute rows, every one of the thirteen measurement columns, are plotted as faint points of light on one logarithmic scale across 2024. Where points pile up, the light adds to white. The real sea, meaning sea temperature, wave periods and wave heights, shows as thin, restless threads in the lower half. Above it run perfectly straight lines at 99, 999 and 9999, each labelled as a fact: "999 °C air temperature", "9999 hPa pressure", "99 ft tide" and so on. The 999 °C air line gives off an orange heat that is brighter wherever it has more readings. The only breaks in those lines are the stretches in February and March when the buoy sent nothing at all.

The page draws with no errors in the viewer, and `renders/04.png` is the latest picture.

One thing to know: the viewer blocks the page from reading the `.txt` file directly from disk, so the first render was empty. To fix it I added `data/46026h2024.js`, which holds the raw file unchanged, wrapped in one JavaScript string. The page loads that, and still falls back to reading the `.txt` when it is served from a web server. `NOTE.md` has the title, what the form does, a "The wrong reading" section on why taking the placeholders literally is wrong, and a note about the extra data file.
