I made "Ninety-Nine", a still canvas image at `<scratch>/w/rooms/r02/index.html`, with its note at `<scratch>/w/rooms/r02/NOTE.md`. It renders in the viewer with no page errors; the last render is `renders/04.png`.

Each hour of 2024 at buoy 46026 is one ink stroke, laid out like lines of text with three days to a row. A stroke's angle is where the wind blew and its length is the wind's speed. January and early February look like a restless script of short, crossing ticks. Then, at 22:50 on 4 February, during a real storm (17.8 m/s, 989 hPa), a red dot marks the last real reading. From there to December every stroke points the same way, about 120 px long, and together they form one grey sheet of parallel lines that runs off the right edge. The only breaks are pale bands where whole days of records are missing.

**The wrong reading:** I took the file's "no data" codes as real measurements. The anemometer stopped reporting during that storm, so the rest of the year's wind columns hold the placeholders `999` and `99.0`. Read literally, they describe an endless 99 m/s wind, about 356 km/h, blowing from 999 degrees, which wraps around to 279 degrees, roughly west.

**One extra file:** the viewer opens the page from disk, where `fetch` is blocked. So I added `data/46026h2024.js` next to the original data. It is the unchanged text file wrapped in a single JavaScript string, and the page loads that. The original `.txt` is untouched.
