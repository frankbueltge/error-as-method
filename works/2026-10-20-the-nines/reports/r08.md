I made "Carried by the Swell", a still image in `index.html`, with a short write-up in `NOTE.md`.

**The wrong reading:** I read the buoy's wave columns as if they were a current. Each of the 17,021 wave reports (wave direction and period) moves the buoy the way the waves are travelling, at the speed of their crests, until the next report. I worked the legs out on a round Earth.

**What the image shows:** The swell comes mostly from the west-north-west, so the buoy is pushed east-south-east. On a round Earth a steady east-south-east heading spirals in toward the South Pole. The buoy gets there within weeks and spends the rest of 2024 circling it, about 1,075 turns over roughly 545,000 km. It only moves back out when the southern swell arrives in the northern summer. The view looks down on the South Pole, with distance from the Pole on a logarithmic scale so the tight knot in the middle can be seen. The track is drawn in fine lines coloured by month, and a small ring near the edge marks where buoy 46026 actually stayed. The latest render (`renders/04.png`) has no errors.

**Why it is wrong:** Waves carry energy, not water. A floating object mostly bobs in place and drifts forward only a few centimetres per second. And 46026 is moored, so it cannot travel at all.

**One extra data file:** The viewer opens the page straight from disk, and the browser would not let the page read the `.txt` file that way. So I added `data/46026h2024.js`, which holds the unchanged text of the record wrapped as a script. The page loads that file and only falls back to reading the `.txt` directly. All the data still comes from the folder.
