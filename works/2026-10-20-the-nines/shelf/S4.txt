1. Units swap — reads every value in the wrong unit (meters as years, degrees Celsius as dollars, counts as kilograms), so the quantities keep their numbers but change what they mean.
2. Column shift — offsets every row by one field, so each value lands under its neighbour's header.
3. Encoding misread (mojibake) — decodes the bytes with the wrong character set (UTF-8 as Latin-1, text as raw pixels or audio samples), turning the record into glyph noise, image or sound.
4. Endianness flip — reads multi-byte numbers in reversed byte order, scattering plausible values into wild magnitudes.
5. Correlation as causation — treats every co-occurrence in the data as one thing causing another and builds a causal story or diagram from it.
6. Ecological fallacy — attributes aggregate or regional figures to each individual, inventing people who embody averages.
7. Sorting by the wrong key — orders records alphabetically by name, by ID, or by file size instead of by time or magnitude, and presents that order as a sequence or ranking.
8. Index as timeline — takes the row number or primary key for chronology, so database insertion order becomes history.
9. Missing values as zero — reads blanks, NaN, null or sentinel codes (-999, 9999) as real measurements, so absences become extreme events.
10. Sentinel as signal — treats error codes and placeholder values as the most important data points and makes them the subject of the work.
11. Spurious precision — takes all decimal places as meaningful and amplifies rounding noise or float artifacts into structure.
12. Overfitting / pareidolia — fits a high-degree curve or seeks faces, figures and messages in noise, and reports the pattern found as a discovery.
13. Extrapolation to infinity — extends a short trend far past its range (centuries, negative populations), turning a local slope into a prophecy.
14. Simpson's reversal — aggregates over a confounding group so the overall trend points the opposite way from every subgroup, and takes the reversed trend as the truth.
15. Survivorship reading — reads only the records that remain (survivors, published items, surviving objects) as the whole population.
16. Base-rate neglect — ranks by raw counts without normalising (per capita, per area), so big places and big categories always "win".
17. Geographic misprojection — plots coordinates with swapped latitude and longitude, the wrong datum or a flipped sign, relocating places into the sea or onto other continents.
18. Literal reading of codes — reads category codes, ZIP codes, ISBNs, phone numbers or classification numbers as quantities to add, average and chart.
19. Numerology / gematria — reads numbers as letters, notes or omens, turning measurements into text, music or prophecy.
20. Metadata as content — ignores the data and works only from the timestamps, file names, licence fields, column headers or revision history.
21. Uniform weighting — gives every row equal weight regardless of survey weights, sample sizes or confidence intervals, so outliers and tiny samples speak as loudly as the majority.
22. Anthropomorphic reading — reads time series as moods, biographies or diaries, so a stock index "grieves" and a sensor "sleeps".
23. Cherry-picked window — crops the time range or subset to the one stretch that tells the story you want, and presents it as the whole.
24. Proxy literalism — takes a proxy for the thing itself (night lights as wealth, search queries as illness, tree rings as weather), then reads it the other way round.
25. Cross-dataset join on a false key — merges two unrelated public datasets on a shared-looking field (same year, same row number, same name), and reads the merged table as one world.
