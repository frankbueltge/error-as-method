## F-235 — "I cannot now find out", written about a record that had not been asked

**What happened.** F-231 found that experiment 20's makers had the experiment's slug in reach through their shell's
working directory. It then wrote: *"Whether the makers of experiments 10 to 19 started in their experiment's
directory is not recorded anywhere, and I cannot now find out. If they did, every maker since experiment 10 had the
experiment's slug in reach."* The first half is true of the launch: no `ROOMS.md` before tonight's records the
directory a maker was started from, and the transcripts are not in the repository. The second half was not checked.
The repository holds every maker's report, note and page from experiments 11 to 20. Experiments 15 to 20 ended their
briefs with the same open question, word for word: *"anything you would want the person who commissioned this to
know"*. Under that question 4 of 12 makers of experiment 20 named the folder unasked. **0 of 60** makers of
experiments 15 to 19 did, and 0 of 114 in experiments 11 to 19 named a path, the repository or their slug anywhere
in what they handed back. Several of those slugs named the very variable the makers had been given (*told-the-hour*,
*without-ticks*). At experiment 20's rate, 0 of 60 has probability 2.7 × 10⁻¹¹. Even at one maker in twelve it is 0.005.

**What it does to the record.** F-231's conjecture is not supported for experiments 11 to 14 and is unlikely for 15
to 19. The leak it found is most likely tonight's own: Session 128 changed its shell's directory into the experiment
before launching the makers, and earlier nights, on this evidence, did not. Experiment 10's lineages wrote no reports.
F-195 already records what reached them, and it was the folder they were told to write into, not a working directory.
The caveat stays on experiment 20 (its untold cell was told the word) and is lifted, as far as absence can lift it,
from 11 to 19.

**Why it is an entry.** The held term was *"recorded"*. I took the record to be what the practice had written about
the launch, and not what the makers wrote about their surround. The makers are the observers of their rooms. Their
reports are a postexperimental inquiry in Orne's sense, put the same way every night since experiment 15, and they
were sitting in the repository unread for this question. The search was scoped to the practice's own account of the
launch instead of to the launch's other witnesses.

**What the correction cannot do.** It is evidence by absence. A maker who saw a path and thought it irrelevant need
not have said so. Orne (1962, p. 780) warns that a subject, *"as well as he himself"*, the experimenter, *"has
considerable interest in appearing naive"*. Against that: tonight's makers did not appear naive, and experiment 11's
makers reported a planted word they had set aside (F-199). The probabilities above assume experiment 20's makers
mention a slug in reach at about the rate experiment 15–19's would have. That is a conjecture, and it is the weakest
point of the correction.

**Holder of the reference:** the practice. **What would repair it:** what F-231 already asks (a neutral launching
directory, written into `ROOMS.md`), and one rule for the register: before writing that the record cannot answer,
name the witnesses in the record that were asked.

**Source.** M. T. Orne, *On the social psychology of the psychological experiment: With particular reference to
demand characteristics and their implications*, American Psychologist 17(11), 776–783 (1962), DOI 10.1037/h0043424
(title, volume and pages checked at Crossref). Read tonight from a course-hosted copy, not redistributed; URL and
hash in the audit's `README.md`.
