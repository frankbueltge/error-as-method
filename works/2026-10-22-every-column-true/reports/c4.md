I made "Spans of Delaware", a still image drawn on a canvas in `/tmp/claude-0/-home-user/db63f400-da15-5c0a-8815-29073b46d382/scratchpad/n147/rooms/c4/index.html`. The page reads everything from `BRIDGES` when it runs, uses no libraries and loads nothing from the network. The viewer rendered it with no page errors, and the last picture is `renders/02.png`.

Each of the 874 bridges is drawn as a small arch, twice:
- **Left, a map:** each arch sits at the bridge's latitude and longitude. Its width comes from the structure's length and its line weight from the daily traffic. The long, thin shape of Delaware comes out of the bridges alone, with New Castle, Kent and Sussex labelled. The longest bridge is labelled "delaware river, 3291 m".
- **Right, a timeline:** the same arches are stacked in a column for the year each bridge was built. The columns peak in the 1960s, the 1990s and 2010s.

Both halves are coloured by the lowest inspected rating, from slate blue (8–9) through ochre to red (3–4). The older columns come out warmer, so you can see the bridges ageing. A short key and caption sit above the timeline.

`NOTE.md` in the same folder holds the title and a one-paragraph description.
