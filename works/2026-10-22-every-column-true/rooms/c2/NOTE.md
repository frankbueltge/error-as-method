# Latitude of Spans

Every one of Delaware's 874 highway bridges is drawn twice as a small arch, both times at its own latitude. On the left it stands where it is, so the bridges trace the long, thin shape of the state. On the right the same arch is moved to the year it was built, so the state's north–south order is kept while its history spreads out to the right. An arch's width follows the bridge's length (on a log scale), its line weight follows daily traffic, and its colour follows its lowest condition rating, from slate blue (9) through ochre to dark red (3). A red dot marks the ten bridges rated poor. Read across one line and you go from a place to its age: New Castle's dense cluster of bridges built mid-century, and the blue arches of the bridges built since 1990 that make up so much of the newer stock.
