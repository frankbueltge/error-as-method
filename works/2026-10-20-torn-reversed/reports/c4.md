I made "The Turning Year". It is a single still SVG image in `index.html`, with its note in `NOTE.md`. It loads only `data/selborne.js`, uses no libraries, and the viewer rendered it with no page errors (latest render: `renders/04.png`).

The page reads the text itself. It splits out the 110 letters (44 to Pennant, 66 to Barrington) and pulls the date line from each. Every dated letter becomes a dot on a spiral that turns once per year, January at the top, with 1767 at the centre and 1787 at the rim:
- **Dot size** shows how long the letter is.
- **Colour** shows who it went to: rust for Pennant, green for Barrington.
- **Open dots** mark letters written away from Selborne, at Ringmer and Fyfield.

A fine thread runs from each dot out to the edge of the wheel. It ends in the letter's numeral and one word: the word that letter uses most and the other letters use least, such as tortoise, swifts, echo, shrew-ash or snow. The 25 letters with no full date (24 with no month at all, plus 2 dated only by year) sit in two side columns, one per correspondent.

The one-word labels are scored automatically, but I added a hand-made list of words to skip, such as month names and filler like "twelfth" or "voice", so a few labels reflect my choices. Read as a whole, the image shows a crowded middle period, Pennant's letters ending in 1780, and a six-year gap before the last letter in June 1787.
