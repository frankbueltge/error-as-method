I made *Annus Historico-naturalis*, a single still page at 1100 × 800. It builds the twelve-month calendar that White says in his last letter he meant to add and then didn't.

**How it works:** the page runs entirely in the browser. It reads the text from `data/selborne.js`, with no network and no libraries, and finds every place the book names a month, which comes to 318 passages. It leaves out the dates the letters were written on, and skips "May" where it's the verb.

**What you see:**
- Each passage becomes a short fragment cut from its own clause, with the month word in red italic.
- The fragments sit on their month's row, ordered by the day of the month where the text gives one. Passages with no day sit at mid-month, in book order.
- The type is the same size on every row, so a row's depth shows how often White wrote about that month. April is deep with arrivals (54 passages). January is thick with his rainfall tables. December is a thin strip of six.

**Rough edges:** the January row opens with a long run of repeated rain-table lines, which I kept because that is what the text says. A few fragments still include letter headings, such as "Letter XII To Thomas Pennant, Esquire", where my filter missed them.

I looked at the page in the viewer twice (the second render is the final one) and it showed no page errors.
