# The Order of Reading

The same 110 letters, read out of the same text, set in two orders at once. Across the top they hang in the order the book prints them, Pennant's 44 and then Barrington's 66, each a column carrying its number, the one word it uses most and the other letters use least, and a bar for its length. From every bar a thread falls to a time line of 1767 to 1787, landing on the day the letter was dated (an open ring where it was dated away from Selborne). Because the book prints the two correspondences one after the other while White was writing them side by side, the threads of the two series sweep across each other in one broad weave over 1769 to 1780; the few threads that cross within a series show where the book printed a letter out of its date, and the long rust thread from the last Pennant letter reaches past almost all of Barrington's. Letters with only a year end in a dashed thread and a bracket over that year; the many letters with no date at all drop a short dotted thread and stop short of the calendar.
