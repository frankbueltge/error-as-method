# The Year Within

The earlier work set each letter of *The Natural History of Selborne* on the day White wrote it; this one keeps the wheel of the year, January at the top, but asks what months White wrote *about*. It reads the letters sentence by sentence, leaving out the datelines, and finds every sentence that names a month (treating "may" and "march" as months only where the wording makes them so). The inner ring is a tally with one stroke for each of those sentences, so April, the month of the swallows' return, has fifty and December has six. Outside the tally, each month's sector holds the creatures, plants, weather and seasonal events named in those sentences or in the sentence after, matched against a short naturalist's vocabulary. The words are set in rings with the most frequent nearest the centre, each word's size shows how often it comes with that month, and its colour shows its kind. Read around the wheel, the book's own calendar appears: frost and hellebore in winter, swallows and martins arriving in April, nests, eggs and broods through summer, and martins and migration again in October and November.
