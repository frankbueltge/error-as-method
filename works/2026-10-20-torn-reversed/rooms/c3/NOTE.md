# The Selborne Year

White's letters were written over twenty years and are full of exact days: the first swallow, the last swift, a frost in January. This piece reads the whole text when the page loads and folds those twenty years onto one calendar wheel. Inside the ring, each dated letter is a stroke on the day White wrote it (red for Pennant, blue for Barrington), and its length shows how long the letter is. Outside the ring, every sentence that names a day, a month or a feast day such as Michaelmas leaves a faint tick on that day. Where the sentence also names a creature, plant or kind of weather, those words are written outward along the ray. Spring arrivals are recorded so much more often than anything else that April and May grow into a thick fan of *swallow · swift · house-martin*, while midwinter holds only *frost*, *thaw* and *rain*. The shape of the wheel is the shape of the book's attention through the year.
