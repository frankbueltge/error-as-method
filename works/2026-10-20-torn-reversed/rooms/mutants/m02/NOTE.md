# The Turning Year

*The Natural History of Selborne* is a book of letters, and most of them carry a date. This work reads the 110 letters out of the text and lays each one on a spiral that makes one turn per year, with January at the top. 1767 sits at the centre and 1787 at the rim. A letter is a dot on the day it was written: its size shows the letter's length, its colour shows who it went to (Pennant in rust, Barrington in green), and it is drawn open when it was written away from Selborne. A thread runs from each dot to the edge of the wheel, where it ends in a single word: the word that letter uses most and the other letters use least (tortoise, swifts, echo, shrew-ash, snow). The page shows how the book was paced: a dense middle period, Pennant falling silent after 1780, and the long gap before the last letter in 1787. The letters White left undated cannot be placed in the year, so they stand in columns at the two sides.
