# Annus Historico-naturalis

In his last letter White says he had meant to close the book with an *Annus Historico-naturalis*, a natural history of the twelve months, and then left it out. This image puts that year together from the letters themselves. Each of the 75 dated letters sits at the day of the year it was written, with the months running clockwise from January at the top, and at a distance from the centre set by its year, from 1767 near the middle to 1787 at the edge. So the whole correspondence winds outward along one faint spiral of time, the Pennant letters in brown and the Barrington letters in slate. A hairline runs from each letter to the rim, where it ends in the words that letter opens with, so the circle of the year is edged with White's first sentences. The 35 letters that carry no date, among them all nine that open the book, have no day to stand on and gather as hollow rings in the hub.
