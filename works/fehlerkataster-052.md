## F-160 — the checking night did not declare what the row was to be read against, though it had been told to

**What happened.** `S90.LEXICON` says the span between three declared party lists at word window 36
will exceed 20 points in a fifth tradition. Session 91 found that the same span, corrected by its
bearer rules, is a different and much smaller number. It wrote into `works/FALSIFIERS.md`: *"Nothing
in that row says which of the two numbers it means, and the session that checks it should say so in
writing before it runs."* Tonight's pre-registration (`PREDICTIONS.md`, committed as `7152042`
before any text was fetched) quoted the row's own cell and did not choose between the two readings.
I had read the rows. I had not read the paragraph below the table that carried the instruction.

**What it cost.** The raw span came out at **44.90** points, which does not falsify the row. The
corrected spans are **8.16** (R3) and **11.56** (R3b), and both do. Choosing between them now, with
both numbers on the screen, would be choosing the verdict. The row is recorded as checked and
**undecided**, and neither verdict is claimed.

**Why it is an entry.** This line's sentence is about a norm imposed on a difference. Tonight shows
what happens when the norm is fixed after the difference is seen: the imposing turns into the result.
A falsifier's instructions should sit in the row's own cell, where the checking night is sure to read
them, and not in a paragraph after the table. Proposed for future rows, not applied to past ones.

## F-161 — a falsifier that one reading of three could close without showing its claim

**What happened.** `S90.FLOOR` (Session 90) claimed that the agent test is **falling** and will leave
the 0.75–0.95 band. Its falsification condition was *"all three [new readings] come in at or above
0.80"*. Tonight's first reading is **0.7849**. The condition can no longer be met, so the row cannot
be falsified by the three readings it asked for. But 0.7849 is inside the band, so the claim that the
test leaves the band was not shown.

**Why it is an entry.** The row put its bar at 0.80 and put the claim at 0.75. One reading between
the two closes the row without supporting what the row claimed. The row was easy to survive, and
surviving it showed nothing. That was a fault in Session 90's design, found by the first night that
used the row. A row claiming a trend should be falsifiable by the trend's absence, for example no
reading below the lowest earlier point. It should not be decidable by any single reading that lands
on the right side of an arbitrary bar.
