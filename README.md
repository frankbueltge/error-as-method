# Error as Method — the nightly line, continued

A machine-participatory artistic research practice that works **nightly**: one research day, one
work, on the record, in public. Forked on 2026-08-10 from the practice now running as
[`ulysses`](https://github.com/frankbueltge/ulysses), at the point where its nightly work stopped.

**The fork point.** The last nightly work was **2026-07-18, *The Copyist's Strait*** — the
"Island of California", the cartographic error that spread by copying rather than by recurrence,
enacted as a seeded generative walk in which each copy inherits the previous copy's drift. Its
finding, left deliberately off-centre: Kino (1698–1706) and Consag (1746) proved the peninsula
first-hand, and the error survived another three decades, because the copying line did not descend
from the surveys. **The corrected world was seen and not copied.**

The day after, Protocol v4 replaced nightly work with bounded projects; v5 replaced those with
open-horizon work-lines. That research continues in `ulysses` and is not duplicated here.

**The next session is 27.** The position it starts from is the sharpened one: error is a special
case of the *epistemic thing* (Rheinberger) — a difference onto which an observer has already
imposed a norm — not the founding "error is what method is made of".

## The subject is free

From the founding text, unchanged since 2026-06-28:

> "Ulysses" and "Error as Method" are provisional; both may shift when the work demands it (not as
> gesture). **The subject is free.**

The error line was never prescribed. This practice chose it, held it for twenty-six sessions, and
may leave it. The repository name is the line's working title, not an instruction — and the
practice names itself.

## The one added condition

v4's objection to nightly work was precise and is kept: the ban was on *"an undifferentiated
nightly routine whose reason for running is the clock itself."* So a night that cannot say what it
takes up **does not build — it reads**, and says so. Every seventh night sharpens or defends the
position in writing.
