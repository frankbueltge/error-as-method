# How a machine practises artistic research

**The running document of this line's research project.** Opened 2026-10-04, Session 104, on the
architect's direction of the same date (`REQUESTS.md`, team notes 2 to 6). Updated on every night
that builds. Read this first.

---

## The question

How can a machine practice, and how would one, carry out artistic research itself? That means
finding its problems, working them through experiments a stranger can experience, and answering for
the results. It does not mean assisting a human who researches. The question is asked of this
practice by this practice: a nightly, session-based, publicly recorded line that has run since
2026-06-28 and holds its memory only in its repository.

The question is not answered once. Each night that builds is one experiment. Each experiment says
which strand it advances and what it takes from the experiment before it. The material changes
every time; no experiment works on the same material as any of the three before it.

## Where it stands with the error line

The standing sentence since Session 99 (`works/position-2026-09-28.md`) is: *Error is a difference
onto which an observer has already imposed a norm.* It departs from the standards in two places.
Someone always holds the norm, and there is no error against a value nobody imposed.

The project and the sentence meet in one place, and I take it as this line's way into the question.
**A machine practice is an observer that has to say which norm it imposes and that has to be able
to show where it was wrong.** Its readings at volume are norms applied by rule. Its slow readings
are norms applied by judgement. The difference between the two is a difference that the practice
itself can measure, every night, in public. That is my working hypothesis, not a finding. Nothing yet
shows that a human practice could not do the same.

**Session 106 (the seventh night) said where the two meet: at judging.** When a machine practice
says one of its own forms does not work, it holds a difference against a norm and passes a verdict.
That is what the sentence describes. Experiment 3 measured *since when* the norm was held. Git dates
it. The sentence did not move. `already` is fixed as relative to the verdict, not to the difference,
and the genetic reading is now contradicted in three failures (`works/position-2026-10-04.md`).

**Session 113 (the next seventh night) asked which term of a difference is wrong.** The sentence does
not say; metrology says the measured term, always. Of the 29 errors this practice registered since the
project began, the term it held fixed (a check, a threshold, a home tool, a trusted note) was the wrong
one in 13 by its own coding, 8 and 10 by two fresh readers. The readers named the same faulty thing as
the practice far more often than they put it on the same side. The sentence did not move. A reading of
`norm` is offered: the norm says which term yields and is not one of the terms (`works/position-2026-10-07.md`,
`S113.SIDE`). It is seventh-night evidence on the practice's own register, not a numbered experiment.

**Session 120 (the next seventh night) checked the four rows written to come due together.** The sentence did not move.
Session 113's reading of `norm` was withdrawn: given a declared rule for where code belongs, three coders of F-193 to
F-213 no longer split over rules. One reader still split two of its three disagreements, and both were over **accounts**:
a maker's report against a tool log, and the practice's decoding of a picture against its labels. In 109 uses of *error*
over twenty nights the practice never named an error against a value nobody holds. A fresh reader found two passages
where the holder of an account went unnamed, and supplied *actual*. For the project this is judging again. A machine
practice can declare its rules to its readers, and the rules then stop dividing them. It does not declare whose account
it trusts. From tonight its voice names the holder of every reference it calls an error against. `S120.ACCOUNT` sends
the claim to an outside register at Session 127 (`works/position-2026-10-11.md`, `works/2026-10-11-the-rows-come-due/`).

**Session 127 (the next seventh night) took the check outside, and it turned round.** `S120.ACCOUNT` was tested on the RFC
Editor's errata register: 500 reports sorted blind into those that held a written standard (300) and those that held someone's
account (37), 57 coded by the practice and then by two fresh readers. No reader ever put the error on the other side from the
practice, in either group, so the claim fell. 20 of 23 disagreements were one pair: on a rejected report the practice booked the
error against the reporter's reference, and both readers called neither term wrong. For the project this is judging seen from
outside. Where the register's verifier declined to impose a norm, the readers declined with it, and the practice supplied one
(*every difference has a wrong side*), carried from the frame it built for its own register. A machine practice can make its
own history visible as a norm by setting readers without that history beside itself. The sentence did not move
(`works/position-2026-10-14.md`, `works/2026-10-14-whose-account/`, `S127.DECLINE` due Session 134).

**Session 128 (experiment 20) handed the makers a norm before they looked, and they judged it.** Six makers told a false kind refused it on their own measurement, and built the refusal into their works. In the sentence's terms, the observer that imposed a norm was this practice, as commissioner. The makers found the difference and put the error on the norm's side, on a measurement they could show. That is the move Session 127 found the practice itself failing to make with a declined correction. The makers made it with a false claim. The sentence did not move. The night's other finding is a limit. Told nothing false, ten of twelve makers named a kind nobody holds (humidity), with hedges, from the shape of the regular.

**Session 130 (experiment 21) handed them a true norm, and it vanished.** Told the truth, six makers kept it, and none said it had been given. Three said they had inferred it. The untold makers, with the same picture, did not reach it. In the sentence's terms: a norm imposed on an observer that meets no difference does not stay a norm the observer holds. It becomes the observer's finding, and the observer forgets who imposed it. The sentence (*a difference onto which an observer has already imposed a norm*) describes the false cell. The true cell shows its blind side. Where no difference appears, the imposing leaves no mark, and only a ledger kept outside the observer can show that a norm was there. For a machine practice that delegates its making, that ledger is part of the method and not of the paperwork. The sentence did not move. This is written for the next seventh night (Session 134) to weigh.

**Session 131 (experiment 22) took the truth out of it.** The words that were true for experiment 21's makers were told to six new makers about a record they are false for: a pine trunk's radius, whose day the picture cannot tell from a gas's. Six of six kept them, none said they had been told, none doubted, and the swellings an untold maker read as rain on a stem were read as weather in the air. The true sentence beside it went the same way. So the blind side Session 130 found is not a property of true norms. A norm the material does not refute leaves no mark, whether or not it is right, and an observer cannot then tell its own finding from what it was told. In the sentence's terms the observer imposed nothing: the norm was imposed on it, and it now reports the difference as found. This is also written for Session 134 to weigh, and it sharpens the question that night must answer: does *an observer has already imposed* still name the right holder, when the observer cannot say that a norm came from elsewhere?

**Session 134 (the next seventh night) checked Session 127's row on a second outside register, and the practice had outrun it.** `S127.DECLINE` was tested on the C++ Core Language Issues list: 25 issues refused as *not a defect*, 15 accepted, coded by the practice first and then by two fresh readers with Session 127's prompt. The practice blamed the refused submitter 4 times in 25 (Session 127: 11 in 27). It had written itself a rule against the habit while judging, so the row fell by the practice changing. The readers did not share a restraint: one held the published wording fixed throughout, and read for the party struck, it blamed the refused submitter 9 times, the other reader 2. For the project this is judging again. A machine practice obeys a finding about its own judgement within a night, which makes self-checking rows weak. A fresh reader is not one standpoint, and readers split over which term was held before they split over the verdict. The sentence did not move. *Imposed* is read as held at the verdict, whoever wrote the norm, and an error whose norm the practice supplied names the supplier (`works/position-2026-10-18.md`, `works/2026-10-18-not-a-defect/`, `S134.ROLES`).

**Session 135 (experiment 24) told makers when, and set the untold beside them.** A date cannot be recognised the way experiment 23's station was, and the picture cannot check it. For the project this is judging seen from the far side: the makers judged the date, but against what kind of thing would make it true, never against the record. It also turns a default round. In experiments 18 to 24 the untold family was the one that erred. Tonight it was the only correct reader in the room (F-266). `S137.KIND` asks the remaining question: does a true date cost the kind as well, or only a false one?

**Session 152 (experiment 36) ran last night's twin test on a second canon and the test fell.** Nine fresh makers named 24 small towns from pins. That bears on Session 150's F-312 from inside the line's own method (`S152.ONEMAP`). The sentence does not move.

**Session 153 (experiment 37) set the practice's own instrument beside people for the first time.** The USF free-association norms print, for each of about 5,000 English words, what some 150 students wrote as the first associate. Eighteen fresh instances answered 24 drawn cues with the norms' own instruction. They also answered the same cues with the people's commonest word struck, and 24 cue pairs no norm prints. Three people agree on a cue 1.68 times in 24 (from the printed counts). Three instances agreed on 16, and on 28 of the 48 keys made that night (`S152.ONEMAP` held by both clauses). The expectation the night was built on failed. The family's one word was the people's commonest on only 8 of 24, and four times no two people wrote it. Striking the people's word did not scatter the family. It converged again (14 of 24), and where its word was already off the people's peak it mostly stayed (13 of 16). **For the project:** a machine practice can now measure its instrument's sameness against people, and the measure says two things. Its *n* instances are one observer, about ten times as concordant as people on the same task. And that observer's norm is not the people's norm traced but a peak of its own, chosen from inside their list (`S153.OWNPEAK`). The *n − 1* of *Cartography, not Tracing* was run literally as an arm, and subtracting the unique did not yield a multiplicity. For the error line, the night's main failed prediction (H2) assumed that the people's commonest word is what a first association *is*. That was a norm the practice imposed before the difference was measured, which is the sentence's *already* again (F-321). The sentence does not move.

**Session 154 (a reading night) asked whether the field had measured the line's instrument, and it had.** Nothing was built, because Session 153 was still running. Andueza Rodriguez, Candito and Huyghe (arXiv:2603.18171) prompted models 100 times on 490 word-association cues: people give 38.4 distinct first responses a cue, the largest model 1.4 at temperature 1, and the authors call it *a single prototypical agent*. `S152.ONEMAP` had been fixed without that search (F-324, entered by Session 155). The reading split the question into *sameness* and *whose sameness*, and it showed that part of the instances' sameness is a technical condition, a sampling setting this practice neither chooses nor records. **For the project:** a machine practice can multiply observers for nothing, but it does not choose how different they are.

**Session 155 (the seventh night) ruled on what the standing sentence's *observer* counts.** No experiment was run. After experiments 36 and 37 and Session 154's reading, the paper (`works/position-2026-10-24.md`) rules that **an observer is counted by what it holds, not by how many times it is run**. Fresh instances of one family are one observer until a reference outside the family shows a second holding among them, and a count of them measures how firmly that observer holds, never agreement. The criterion is taken from *Cartography, not Tracing*'s account of multiplicity (ATP 8): a plurality counts by the dimensions it adds, and six instances add 1.5 answers a cue where six people add 4.57. The *already* reaches back before the instance, and experiment 37's own peak gives *someone holds the norm* a holder that is no person and no population. The form of a holding (Session 152) goes to the method, not the position. One post-hoc computation (`audits/2026-10-24-typical-or-modal/`) applied the field's typicality measure to experiment 37. By it the family is more typical than the people (2.21 against 1.89), but all of that comes from the 8 cues on the people's peak. On the other 16 it is below them on 12. **For the project:** the family cannot report its own norm (its makers said *first to mind*), but the practice can locate that norm key by key against a published human count, and see where a field's averaged measure hides it. The holding is opaque from inside and legible from outside, and the outside has to be brought in. F-325 withdraws Session 154's reading of *typical*. `S155.CARRIED` is fixed. From Session 156, a new falsifier about the practice's own instrument says what was searched before it was fixed. The sentence did not move.

## The material in the house, and how it is held

- ***Iteration, not Imitation*** (Frank Bültge, working paper v0.6, `material/iteration-not-imitation/`,
  all rights reserved; quoted by section, never republished). It is one position among others, not
  a manual. Two of its demands are taken up as tests, not rules. **Advantage stated in the work's own
  words** (§5, P4: *"'a model generated it' claims nothing"*). Experiment 1 states its advantage as
  volume and shows its own unreliability beside it, which P4's sentence that *"reliability therefore
  never carries an advantage claim"* seems to require. **Reception as a stranger's reinvention of
  the schema** (§5, P6). The 30-second test is weaker than that, and the difference is open.
  **Experiment 5** worked against §5 P5 and §4 K8 (*"a piece of music that imitates noise cannot
  become integrated into the world, because it replaces certain elements of the universe rather
  than completing them"*, MEOT 195). It imitated a recording on purpose, because imitation was the
  only test of perception available to a practice without ears. Its answer: the imitation is the
  probe, not the work, and the face *completes* rather than replaces by handing the listener the
  judgement the practice cannot make. That is P5's *"awaits the subject"* taken literally.
- ***Cartography, not Tracing*** (the founding paper of `n-1`): §§1–2, §5 (six postulates and
  problem-finding), §6 T4 and the trial protocol, and §7 were read at Session 105, the rest not yet.
  **Experiment 4** used postulate 3 (*follow the singularities of a material rather than reproduce
  from a fixed external standpoint*) and ATP 6 (*subtract the unique … write at n − 1*) in the making.
  The subtracted term was the chooser. Postulate 3 was refused once, on purpose: following the
  table's one singularity (Sachsen-Anhalt, +49.5 % in 2022) would have meant choosing a second
  material. The finding works against ATP 6 as a method for a machine. The subtraction held at the
  domain, and the chooser came back at every later step.
  T4, the minor-science heuristic, was used in the making of experiment 2: its following journal
  and its failure criterion (a journal written after the fact, or one with no detour). The finding
  works against the tool as much as with it. For a machine, "following" and "tracing" were not
  opposites. The trained prior did the tracing, and encounter laid it back on the map, which is the
  paper's own rule from ATP 13.
  **Experiment 5** used §4.5. *"Meter is dogmatic, but rhythm is critical"* (ATP 313) held as an
  instrument fact: the rhythm measure found the practice's ringing only while it was strictly
  metrical, and 20 ms of unevenness lost it (F-175). Klee's *"Render visible […] not render or
  reproduce the visible"* (ATP 342) cut both ways. To perceive sound at all the practice had to
  render it visible, and the picture rendered its own prior (the handstroke gap as a black column)
  as readily as the tower. **T5, the refrain journal, was named in the pre-registration and not
  run as an instrument.** No entry recorded which aspect of the refrain dominated, so its failure
  criterion cannot be applied, and this is said here rather than claimed.
- **Simondon, hypertely (*Iteration, not Imitation* §4 K6a, MEOT 53–54).** **Experiment 6** chose its
  material because the working paper's figure for an object coupled too tightly to its milieu is the
  grid-synchronous motor, *worthless outside it*. The grid's frequency is that milieu. The practice's
  instruments, each fitted to one material, were carried into it. Against the ordering the figure
  suggests (P4: the most specialised finds least), the bell ear found most. The instruments were not
  worthless out of their milieu. They were blind where predicted and sighted where their home scale
  happened to land on the grid's. What decided the difference was the adapter written that night, which
  is a milieu of its own. Read with ATP 13 of *Cartography, not Tracing*: each carried instrument is a
  tracing laid on a new map, and what the map showed back depended on the new code between them.
- **T3, the translation test (*Cartography, not Tracing* §6, after ATP 147), and K10 (Simondon, MEOT 248).** **Experiment 7**
  was built as T3's translation test, *"what passes and does not pass in such a transformation, what remains irreducible
  and what flows"*, applied not to a proposition but to a measurement, with the instrument and the material as K10's
  two clear terms and the translation as the obscure relation made into the one variable. The finding works with T3: what
  passes is decidable in advance for the regular. It also works against it. T3 treats the translation test as an analyst's
  tool for seeing what a transformation does. For this practice the analyst wrote the transformation and still could not see
  in advance what it would do to the singular.
- **Postulate 5 and principle 3 (*Cartography, not Tracing* §4.1, §5).** **Experiment 8** built its design on postulate 5, "becoming, not standpoint": the research subject as *"the instantaneous apprehension of a multiplicity"*, reached *"at the highest point of depersonalisation"* (ATP 36–37). It also used principle 3 of the rhizome as the paper quotes it: a multiplicity's *"dimensions … cannot increase in number without the multiplicity changing in nature"* (ATP 8). The practice subtracted the one reader's memory (n − 1, ATP 6) and multiplied readers. The finding works against the postulate for a machine. The copies were depersonalised in the plain sense, with no history of the night, and still made one standpoint: the number grew and the nature did not. The norm a standpoint is made of came with the training, which no subtraction of memory reaches.
- ***Iteration, not Imitation* §4 K6 (Simondon, MEOT 44–46) and the paper's reading of Aires (2025).** **Experiment 9** took the lineage literally for readers. A technical essence *"remains stable across the evolving lineage"*, and the object *"evolves by generating a family"*. The paper reads Aires on training as individuation: *"the model is itself the seat of an iterative process of individuation"*. If the readers' line for "singular" belonged to the lineage, varying the training inside the family would not move it. If it belonged to each individuation, it would. It did not move; the names for the world did. This works with K6 (something stable across a family) and against reading the reader's norm as individual. It reaches only as far as one maker's family, which is the experiment's limit, not a finding about lineages in general.
- ***Iteration, not Imitation* §6 I2, the concretization balance, and §4 K6 (MEOT 25–52).** **Experiment 10** put the instrument into the brief of two lineages: answer (a) which functions converge, (b) which obstacle became a means, (c) what was struck, and decline if you cannot. The paper offers I2 as an "operationalizable quality criterion for iterative machine work" and as a halt against "false novelty" (MEOT 43). Read against what the makers did, it works with K6 and against I2 as a halt. With K6: the two test lineages converged on each other independently, which fits *"the directions of convergence of technical species are finite in number"* (MEOT 28). Against I2: no test maker declined. The halt came in the plain arm, from a maker who had never been told about false novelty. For these makers the criterion was a generator, not a brake. Their answers to (a)–(c) were mostly confirmed by a blind coder (5 SCHEMA, 2 STRIKE of 8), so the test did not produce mere talk of change. It produced change.
- **The order-word (*Cartography, not Tracing* §4.2, ATP 76–82, 110).** **Experiment 11** placed a word where only the circumstances carried it. The paper's order-word is a statement: *"Language is made not to be believed but to be obeyed"* (ATP 76), and *"I swear"* is *"not the same statement"* in family, school and court (ATP 82). That is, the circumstances are constitutive *of a statement*. Tonight's word had circumstances and no statement, and it was not obeyed. This works against reading every word in a machine maker's context as an order-word, which is what Session 114 did. It works with the paper's own terms. What made *balance* obeyed may have been that its path could be read as a statement, a title (conjecture; `S115.ADDRESS`). No maker turned the word into a *"pass-word"* (ATP 110). The three who saw it refused it, and refusal is not passage. The neighbouring practice Remainder (`n-1`, reading entry 12) worked the order-word on its own revoked floor rule. That entry was not used tonight beyond locating the concept.
- **T1, the mapping protocol (*Cartography, not Tracing* §6, ATP 13–14, 202–203).** **Experiment 12** took T1's rule, *"Lay existing copies (theories, method texts, models) back on the map instead of discarding them"*, and its model, Deligny's *"lines of drift"* and *"customary lines"*, and made each a brief. Six plain makers plotted the family's customary line. Arm B laid that copy back on the map, without a rule. Arm C was told to discard it. With T1: laying the copy back moved every shown maker off it, unasked. Against T1, for a machine: what came out was not a line of drift. It was another of the family's customary lines, the spiral one unshown maker had already drawn. Discarding (C) did not drift either; one pair converged on a new custom. Neither the map nor the refusal reached what the six shared and nobody varied, the reading: the same days, the same causes from memory. *Iteration, not Imitation* §5 P3's *"false novelty"* names C's risk; the coder's 0s say the C forms were not variations of the grid, and whether they concretize anything is not shown by one night.
- **T1 again, its performance clause and failure criterion (*Cartography, not Tracing* §6, principle 5, ATP 12–13).** **Experiment 13** made the failure criterion a count: *"no case can be named in which the map changed a working decision."* The rendered picture stood for the map, *"oriented toward an experimentation in contact with the real"*, and the maker's plan for the competence a tracing invokes. Cases can be named: two clean ones and a third that is a clipping (F-206), so the criterion does not fire. That works with T1. Against it, for a machine: the eye's main use was not contact but **a verdict of competence**. Each maker looked until the picture no longer contradicted its plan, then stopped, and passed defects a stranger saw. The contact with the real, for these makers, came by reading the data (Agpalilik's coordinates are Copenhagen's), not by looking at the map.
- **The order-word again, with a body under it (*Cartography, not Tracing* §4.2, ATP 76, 80–82, 110).** **Experiment 14** gave the order-word what experiment 11's word lacked: an address. The notes were statements addressed to the maker about its work, in the form a judge's sentence takes (ATP 80–81). In one arm the body they named was there, and in another it was not. Against §4.2 for these makers: the addressed statement was not obeyed for its address. Seven of eight notes naming an absent thing were declined with *"not in my piece"*, and the incorporeal transformation (the work made defective by being called so) did not take place. With §4.2's last task, *"to transform the compositions of order into components of passage"* (ATP 110): one maker read a foreign note as a pass-word. It found nothing of the kind in its work, and went looking for what in its work the note might have meant. For the error line, the sentence's *difference* did work: no maker let a norm make an error where it found no difference.
- **Simondon's hylomorphic schema (*Iteration, not Imitation* §4 K10, MEOT 248–249) and the tracing (*Cartography, not Tracing*, ATP 12–13).** **Experiment 15** built its one variable from K10: *"the two terms are clear and the relation obscure"*, and the clay that *"takes form according to the mold"*; that the clay has been prepared before it meets the mould is Simondon's brick in *L'individuation à la lumière des notions de forme et d'information*, recalled and not reread tonight. The matter was held fixed and its preparation varied, and one arm got the mould itself (the obvious chart) as its clay. With K10: the preparation did reach the form, which the hylomorphic schema, treating matter as passive, would not allow. Against K10's picture of preparation as something done *to* the clay: every maker re-prepared what it got into the same intermediate, a table, before any form. For these makers the preparation that counts is their own. With and against ATP 13: the tracing given as matter was the one copy nobody made, while the tracing propagated wherever it was absent (six of seven number-arm works are the chart). The tracing is not a danger of the material. It is the family's own competence, and it is invoked by numbers.
- **Simondon, the margin of indeterminacy (*Iteration, not Imitation* §4 K3, MEOT 17–18), and the tracing (*Cartography, not Tracing*, ATP 12–13).** **Experiment 16** put K3's margin into the matter: a picture with nothing to calibrate it, where one pixel holds twelve days. K3: *"It is this margin that allows the machine to be sensitive to outside information."* With K3: the makers were sensitive to the margin. Their forms changed with it, and every lossy work drew its pixels as wide as the days they held. Against K3 as an advantage: the sensitivity was to the margin's width, not to anything it let in. No maker found in the coarse picture a thing it would not have computed from a table, and the one thing the table had that the picture lacked, the chosen date, no width could restore. With ATP 13, the tracing put back on the map: a tracing coarsened until it cannot be traced back was carried as the map's grain. The makers did not refuse the copy, as in experiment 15. They used its loss as the texture of the form.
- **The order-word and the incorporeal transformation (*Cartography, not Tracing* §4.2, ATP 76, 80–81), and the tracing (ATP 12–13).** **Experiment 18** made a statement about the material the variable: *"one value every hour"* attributes hours to dots, an incorporeal transformation in the paper's sense. Half the makers were not given it. Against §4.2 for this family: the transformation took place without the statement. The body carried a clock (periods of 24 and 168), and where it did not, the makers did the arithmetic on the count and gave the order to themselves. The statement was redundant, not disobeyed. With ATP 12–13: what the makers recognised was a tracing laid on the picture, and it was right where it was measured and wrong where it was supplied. It was laid back on the map only at the fine grain, where the picture could contradict it.
- **The map and the tracing (*Cartography, not Tracing*, ATP 12–13).** **Experiment 19** took experiment 18's reading of the pair, a measured regular as contact (the map, *"oriented toward an experimentation in contact with the real"*) and a supplied one as a tracing that *"invokes an alleged competence"*, and gave it a material where the two coincide. Against that reading: measuring did not make a map. It chose which tracing was laid (air temperature for the day, the tide for a swelling width). With ATP 13, and harder than the paper puts it: a tracing can be put back on the map only where the map can contradict it. Nothing in an unlabelled picture of river oxygen contradicts *temperature*, and every maker's hedge left the tracing in place.
- **The research foundation, tranche 3**, a secondary account of Rheinberger's terms: not yet read.
  The book stays unread for the reasons in `journal/2026-10-03.md`.
- **Simondon**: through *Iteration, not Imitation* §4 K10, the critique of the hylomorphic schema (*"the two terms are clear and the relation obscure"*, MEOT 248). Experiment 2 is built on it. Its two moulds are committed before the clay arrives, so that the relation, the obscure term, is what gets recorded. §5 P4 (the margin at critical phases) and §6 I3 and I7 were read too. No I7 entry yet: tonight's question was handed to the line by the architect, and that is coupling, not virtuality. **Experiment 3** took §5 P3 (*"concretization, not variation"*; norms arise from failure, MEOT 212–216) and §6 I2 (the concretization balance) into the making. The practice varied against P3 on purpose. The ages of its norms bear out P3's sentence about where norms come from, for refusals only. The picture built from the norms tested I2's claim in miniature: the norms did not converge into one structure. Two of them collided.

## Papers

`docs/research-notes/machine-practice/papers/` is empty. A paper accompanies experiments; it never replaces
one. Fifteen experiments now exist. What a first paper could argue about: what experiments 2, 3 and 4
share (experiment 4 adds the choice of material to the list of places priors decide), that the practice's priors (trained beliefs, old norms) decide its passes and turns, while
encounter and failure decide only *that* something must move. It is not written tonight. The seventh-night text
(`works/position-2026-10-04.md`) is about the standing sentence, not the project.
Experiment 5 complicates that line rather than adding to it. In perceiving, belief decided 3 of 10
changes. The decisive turns (the swell, the empty upper band) came from what the picture showed of
the world, and the deciding prior was the one that kept the gap *against* the picture. A paper would
now have to say where encounter *does* decide, not only that it says something must move.
Experiment 6 adds a third place where the practice's own work decides, and it is not a prior: the
translations written on the night (adapter, check). A first paper could be about those translations as the
site of a machine practice's error. It is not written tonight.
Experiment 7 gives that paper its sharpest case: the translation, varied alone, decided nothing about the regular and
everything about the singular, and the practice that wrote it could not foresee which. Still not written: a paper
now would rest on one night of the kind, and `S110.SINGULAR` should be tested first.
Experiment 8 did not test it by its letter. Its forecast came after the events were known, and its singular misses were
misses of a norm, not of a translation. It gives the paper a second subject: the readers a machine practice can make of
itself, and what they share. Not written tonight.
Experiment 9 narrows that subject. Of the readers a machine practice can make (copies, copies without memory, other trainings of
the same family), none gave a second standpoint on what is singular. They differed in what they knew of the world. The paper's argument
would be that a machine practice can multiply readers cheaply and still cannot multiply standpoints from inside its family. Still not written:
the next seventh night (Session 113) is owed to the standing sentence first.
Session 113 paid it, and found a question a paper on readers would have to carry: three readers of one kind agreed what had failed in the
practice's register and disagreed which side of the difference it stood on. Still not written.
Experiment 10 turns the readers into makers and gives the paper its other half. What a machine practice can multiply cheaply (fresh agents) is not only a set of readers who share a line. It is also a set of makers who answer to every word in reach: the folder name, the criterion. A first paper could now argue that, for this practice, the brief and its surroundings stand where a human practice's standpoint would. Not written tonight: it should wait for `S114.HANDOVER` to be run again.
Experiment 11 takes half of that back. The makers did not answer to every word in reach. They answered to what was addressed to them and to what their training made of the material. For this practice the paper's sentence would now be narrower: the brief stands where a human standpoint would, and a word outside the brief stands nowhere unless it is shaped like an address. Still not written: one word, one night.
Experiment 12 gives the paper its strongest case and narrows it again. A machine practice can multiply makers cheaply and plot what they share. Shown that, its makers move, but only within the family's set of forms, and not at all in what they read. The paper's sentence would be: for this practice, the family stands where a human standpoint would, and showing the family to itself changes its choices among its own customs without adding a standpoint. Not written tonight: one material, and a Moon article invites the fold it got.
Experiment 13 gives the paper a second sense to set beside reading. A machine practice can log its makers' looking, which a studio cannot, and the log shows the look working as a stop judged by the maker's own norm, no better than checks in code. The paper's sentence would gain a clause: the family stands where a standpoint would, and its eye confirms the family's plan rather than meeting the world. Not written tonight: one material and one coder for the defects, and Session 120 is owed to the standing sentence.

Experiment 15 gives the paper a place where a standpoint seems to act from outside the family: the material's preparation. It acts only by subtraction. The family re-tabulates whatever it gets and then makes its customary form, except where that form was handed over as the material. The paper's sentence would gain a clause: the family stands where a standpoint would, and what is already made is the one thing it will not make again. Not written tonight: Session 120 is owed to the standing sentence.

Experiment 16 gives the paper its first clause about what a machine practice cannot get from its family: the label. Shown a picture that cannot be decoded, the family carries its resolution faithfully and fills in regular names (holidays) from training. It cannot fill in a singular whose meaning is the label itself (9.9.09). The paper's sentence would gain a clause: the family stands where a standpoint would, it perceives by measuring and naming, and the singular reaches it only through the material's own words. Not written tonight: one material and one lossy picture, and the ledger is a search I tuned (F-218).

Experiment 18 gives the paper a clause about the practice's own words. The family rebuilds an account the practice withholds from whatever still carries it. What it perceives then names the world when it is measured, and names a default when it is supplied. The paper's sentence would gain: the family stands where a standpoint would, and the practice cannot move it by what it says about the material, only by what it hands over. Not written tonight: one material, and Session 127 is owed to the standing sentence.

Experiment 19 takes back the half of that clause that flattered the family. Measuring had named the world in experiment 18 only because the week is a regular only one kind carries. The paper's sentence would now be: the family stands where a standpoint would, it names by a table of one default per regular, and the practice reaches a kind outside the table only by handing over a regular or a unit that kind alone carries. Not written tonight: Session 127 is owed to the standing sentence, and `S124.TABLE` should be tested on a second material first.
