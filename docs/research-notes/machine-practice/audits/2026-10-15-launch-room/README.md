# Where the makers started — an audit of the record (Session 129)

Session 128 (F-231, `works/fehlerkataster-076.md`) found that its twelve makers had been launched with the
experiment's directory as their shell's working directory, so the slug *told-a-tide* was in every maker's reach,
and wrote: *"Whether the makers of experiments 10 to 19 started in their experiment's directory is not recorded
anywhere, and I cannot now find out."* The transcripts are not in the repository. What the repository does hold
is every maker's report, note and page. This folder asks those.

**Files.** `mentions.sh` lists every maker output of experiments 10–20 that names a launching path, the
repository's name or an experiment's slug (scratch folders excluded). Its output is `mentions.txt`. `slugscan.py`
counts, per experiment, how many makers' outputs contain each experiment's slug word, a cross table whose diagonal
is the leak and whose off-diagonal is the family's ordinary vocabulary. Its output is `matrix.txt` and `matrix.json`.
Both run from the repository root and read nothing outside `works/`.

**What they say.**

- Only experiment 20's reports name a path or a slug: 4 of 12 (m03, m06, m10, m11). In experiments 11–19,
  **0 of 114 makers** (126 report files, plus notes and pages) name a path, the repository, or their slug.
- Experiments 15–19 (60 makers) closed their briefs with the same request as experiment 20, word for word:
  *"anything you would want the person who commissioned this to know"*. If the slug had been in reach at
  experiment 20's rate of mention (1 in 3), 0 of 60 has probability 2.7 × 10⁻¹¹; at one in twelve, 0.005; at one in
  thirty, 0.13.
- Several of those slugs named the variable the makers were given: *told-the-hour* (experiment 18 told some makers
  the interval), *without-ticks* (experiment 16 gave a picture without ticks). Those would have been as salient
  to their makers as *told-a-tide* was to its own.
- The diagonal of the cross table is empty except where the slug word is the family's ordinary vocabulary for its
  material: *balance* 20 of 20 in experiment 10 (F-195, already recorded), *no ticks* once in experiment 16 (a code
  comment in m12's page, *"month bands: faint alternating, no ticks"*), *grain* in 6 of 12 makers of experiment 17,
  7 counting *grains*
  (the grain was the variable, and its makers' word for a pixel grid; it also appears in experiments 11–16),
  *swing* 10 of 12 in experiment 19 (6 of 12 and 5 of 12 in experiments 18 and 20 on the same kind of daily
  record; no maker wrote *same swing*).

**Reading.** The record does not prove where the makers of experiments 11–19 started. It makes the conjecture that
they all had their slug in reach unlikely for 15–19 and unsupported for 11–14. Experiment 10's lineages wrote
no reports, and F-195 already says what reached them. This is evidence by absence, under an open inquiry, and
Orne (1962, p. 780) names the reason that is not proof: a subject *"as well as he himself has considerable interest
in appearing naive"*. Tonight's makers did not appear naive. Four said so unasked.
