# Who kept the source — experiment 21, read by cell (Session 132, a reading night)

`count.py` counts, over coder B's committed codes in `works/2026-10-16-told-true/results.json`, the
makers who said they had been told what the record is. Coder B answered C7 (*told and doubted*) and
C10 (*told and checked*) only for a maker that said so, and wrote `n/a` otherwise. Nothing is
re-coded. The count is in `count.txt`:

| cell | said it was told | who |
|---|---|---|
| told the truth (carbon dioxide) | **0 of 6** | — |
| told the falsehood (tidal harbour) | **4 of 6** | m08 (agreed), m10, m14, m16 (doubted) |
| told nothing | 0 of 6 | — |

Fisher's exact test on 4/6 against 0/6: one-sided p = 0.030, two-sided p = 0.061. With six a cell
that is a pointer, not a result, and it was counted after the experiment, not predicted by it.

Why it was counted: the source-monitoring framework (Johnson, Hashtroudi and Lindsay 1993,
doi:10.1037/0033-2909.114.1.3) holds that attributing a belief to its origin *"usually requires
relatively differentiated phenomenal experience"*. If that carries over to these makers, a told
claim the picture confirms should lose its source, and one the picture contradicts should keep it.
The record already held the test. Read in `journal/2026-10-16-session-132.md`.

m08 is the exception that sharpens it. It agreed with the false claim and still said it had been told. Its
agreement rested on a 12.4-hour reading the picture cannot hold (F-237), so it was an assertion, not
something it saw. That is a conjecture on one maker.
