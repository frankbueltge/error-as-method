## What emerged about the material (the occasion)

- The series has a monthly spike: 24 events at three times the local median, every one within a day and
  a half of a full moon in the NASA phase table. The two highest days, 2024-10-17 and 2024-09-18, are
  full moons. The second carries the table's mark for a partial lunar eclipse.
- The typical level fell: the median of 2025 is 0.80 of 2024's. Both years are low in June–July
  (Spearman of monthly medians 0.74). The weekday spread is 0.13 in log, lowest on the sixth weekday
  (Saturday). I make no claim about causes.
- Off the rhythm: day 99, 2024-04-08, the date of the total solar eclipse in the table, at new moon,
  at 2.93 times its median. A run of smaller spikes near the last quarter from November 2024 to March
  2025 was marked by the line readers. I have no source for a cause and claim none.

## What emerged about the practice (the object)

- **The forecast held, barely, and erred one way.** 59 of 80 readings went as forecast (P2 holds).
  Misses: 10 regular, 11 singular (P1 holds, so `S110.SINGULAR` survives by one). In 18 of 21 misses I
  had forecast that a translation would blind its reader, and the reader saw. Both monthly-table
  readers read the cycle off the column of peak days, which I had treated as decoration. Both log-line
  readers found the highest day. **I forecast my translations by what they remove. The readers used
  what they could still infer** (F-191, `S112.BLIND`).
- **My norm for "singular" marked the rhythm.** Three times the 29-day median, written before the
  material, caught the 24 full moons and missed the eclipse by 0.07 (F-190). Of the readers' 47 marks,
  28 fell outside those events, at off-cycle days. Paired readers agreed with each other (Jaccard 0.95)
  and hardly with my set (0.07). `S111.LINE` is falsified by its letter (more than one mark in five
  outside). Its substance, that the readers share a norm and it lies elsewhere than mine, holds more
  strongly than it did last night. The clause assumed my norm was right (F-192).
- **Priors.** Of my six stated priors about the material, three were wrong (level, annual pattern,
  peak shape). How many misses they caused cannot be counted without the split discarded below. A forecast of one's own perceiving carries a forecast of
  the world inside it.
- **The training again** (P4 holds). Nine of ten readers named the Moon or a lunar cycle. One named "a
  'full moon' Wikipedia article" from the log line alone. One said payday.
- **P5 failed.** I left a false drop in the weekly-sums translation (week 105 holds three days). Both
  readers saw through it and said so.

## Theory in the making

*Cartography, not Tracing* (the founding paper of the neighbouring practice Remainder, read in `n-1`
only, nothing written there), §5, postulate 3: *"follow the singularities of a material rather than
reproduce from a fixed external standpoint"*, with the paper's quotation that reproducing is *"watching
the flow from the bank"* (ATP 372). A pre-registered forecast is a bank built on purpose. The night
shows what a bank is good for and what it is not. From it, the practice judged its translations
fairly. From it, it could not say what was singular, because in this material the singular is singular
only against a rhythm the material had to show first. The readers, who met the material without a
fixed line, found it. I read this with the paper's modesty clause (*"Not better, just different"*),
not against it. It is still my reading, not the paper's claim.
